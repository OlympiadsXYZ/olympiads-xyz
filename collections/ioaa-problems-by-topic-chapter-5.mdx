Luminous Blue Variable (LBV) stars greatly vary in visual brightness; however, their bolometric magnitude remains constant. Imagine a LBV star with a black body temperature of 5000 K at its maximum visual brightness, and 30,000 K at its minimum visual brightness. Calculate the ratio of the star radius between both situations above.




<p>Условие: <a href="/collections/ioaa-problems-by-topic/source/711ac8d837c5f8d9a39467d3a734940e193d151edef342c2c232a543d4b48062.pdf#page=47">PDF 47</a></p>

<details id="c5-sp-t04-key">

<summary>Печатно решение / форма</summary>



The bolometric magnitude of the star remaining constant, which means its luminosity remains constant. As stars can be approximated as black bodies,

$$
L_\star=4\pi R_\star^2\sigma T_\star^4\tag{15.14}
$$


$$
4\pi R_1^2\times\sigma T_1^4=4\pi R_2^2\times\sigma T_2^4
$$


$$
R_1^2T_1^4=R_2^2T_2^4
$$


$$
\frac{R_1}{R_2}=\frac{T_2^2}{T_1}\tag{15.15}
$$


$$
\frac{R_{5000\mathrm K}}{R_{30000\mathrm K}}=\left(\frac{30000}{5000}\right)^2=36\tag{15.16}
$$

### 5.1 Theory · 9

<p>I08 - T09 - B</p>



<h4 id="c5-sp-t09-question">Условие</h4>



The radiation incoming to the Earth from the Sun must penetrate the Earth’s atmosphere before reaching the earth surface. The Earth also releases radiation to its environment and this radiation must penetrate the Earth’s atmosphere before going out to the outer space. In general, the transmittance $(t_1)$ of the Sun radiation during its penetration to the Earth’s atmosphere $(t_1)$ is higher than that of the radiation from the Earth $(t_2)$. Let $T_\odot$ be the effective temperature of the Sun, $R_\odot$ the radius of the Sun, $R_\oplus$ the radius of the Earth, and $d_{\oplus-\odot}$ the distance between the Sun and the Earth. Derive the temperature of the Earth’s surface as a function of the aforementioned parameters.




<p>Условие: <a href="/collections/ioaa-problems-by-topic/source/711ac8d837c5f8d9a39467d3a734940e193d151edef342c2c232a543d4b48062.pdf#page=48">PDF 48</a></p>

<details id="c5-sp-t09-key">

<summary>Печатно решение / форма</summary>



As the Earth is in thermal equilibrium, Power of radiation received by the Earth from the Sun will be equal to the power transmitted by the earth to outer space.

$$
P_r=F_\odot At_1=\frac{L_\odot}{4\pi d_{\oplus-\odot}^2}\pi R_\oplus^2t_1\tag{15.27}
$$


$$
=\frac{4\pi R_\odot^2\sigma T_\odot^4}{4\pi d_{\oplus-\odot}^2}\pi R_\oplus^2t_1\tag{15.28}
$$


$$
P_r=\left(\frac{R_\odot R_\oplus}{d_{\oplus-\odot}}\right)^2\pi\sigma T_\odot^4t_1\tag{15.29}
$$


$$
P_t=\sigma T_\oplus^4\,4\pi R_\oplus^2t_2\tag{15.30}
$$


$$
P_t=P_r\tag{15.31}
$$


$$
\sigma T_\oplus^4\,4\pi R_\oplus^2t_2=\left(\frac{R_\odot R_\oplus}{d_{\oplus-\odot}}\right)^2\pi\sigma T_\odot^4t_1\tag{15.32}
$$


$$
T_\oplus^4=\left(\frac{R_\odot}{d_{\oplus-\odot}}\right)^2\times\frac{t_1}{4t_2}T_\odot^4\tag{15.33}
$$


$$
T_\oplus=\sqrt{\frac{R_\odot}{2d_{\oplus-\odot}}\sqrt{\frac{t_1}{t_2}}}\,T_\odot\tag{15.34}
$$

<h4 id="c5-sp-t11-question">Условие</h4>



Suppose a star has a mass of $20M_\odot$. If 20% of the star’s mass is now in the form of helium, calculate the helium-burning lifetime of this star. Assume that the luminosity of the star is $100L_\odot$, in which 30% is contributed by helium burning.Assume no additional helium is being produced in this time and all helium is available for burning.is 12.000000 amu. The fusion of helium into carbon by triple-α process is given by

$$
3\,{}^4\mathrm{He}\to{}^{12}\mathrm C+\gamma
$$



<p><a href="/collections/ioaa-problems-by-topic/assets/957db7bcd6002d40ff474b191a498c7855986019628b1f084cdd018fad8436ea.png">Source detail, PDF 48</a></p>

<details id="c5-sp-t11-key">

<summary>Печатно решение / форма</summary>



The mass of three helium nuclei = $3\times4.002603$ amu = 12.007809 amu. The mass converted to energy is the difference between the sum of the masses of three helium nuclei and the mass of the resulting carbon nucleus:

$$
12.007809\mathrm{amu}-12.000000\mathrm{amu}=7.809\times10^{-3}\mathrm{amu}
$$


This represents a fractional loss of mass of the original mass.

$$
m_\mathrm{frac}\ \frac{7.809\times10^{-3}}{12.007809}=6.503\times10^{-4}
$$


Let the stellar mass be $M$, luminosity $L$ and helium-burning time $t$.

$$
t=\frac E{0.3L}=\frac{\triangle mc^2}{0.3\times100L_\odot}\tag{15.39}
$$


$$
=\frac{0.2Mm_\mathrm{frac}c^2}{0.3\times100L_\odot}=\frac{0.2\times20M_\odot m_\mathrm{frac}c^2}{0.3\times100L_\odot}\tag{15.40}
$$


$$
=\frac{0.2\times20\times1.989\times10^{30}\times6.503\times10^{-4}(3\times10^8)^2}{0.3\times100\times3.826\times10^{26}}\tag{15.41}
$$


$$
=4.06\times10^{16}\text{ seconds}\tag{15.42}
$$


$$
=1.29\times10^9\text{ years}\tag{15.43}
$$


Thus, it will take 1.3 billion years for the star to burn all that helium. However, in real stars not all helium is available for burning and luminosity of a massive star in a helium-burning phase would be much higher. Thus, helium-buring time scales are much shorter.

<h4 id="c5-sp-t12-question">Условие</h4>



A main sequence star at a distance of 20 pc is barely visible through a certain space-based telescope which can record all wavelengths. The star will eventually move up along the giant branch, during which time its temperature drops by a factor of 3 and its radius increases 100-fold. What is the new maximum distance at which the star can still be (barely) visible using the same telescope?

<details id="c5-sp-t12-key">

<summary>Печатно решение / форма</summary>



To keep same brightness, the flux of the star should remain the same. Using the radius-luminosity-temperature relation,

$$
L\propto R^2T^4\tag{15.44}
$$


$$
\frac{L_\mathrm G}{L_\mathrm{MS}}=\frac{R_\mathrm G^2T_\mathrm G^4}{R_\mathrm{MS}^2T_\mathrm{MS}^4}\tag{15.45}
$$


$$
=\left(\frac{R_\mathrm G}{R_\mathrm{MS}}\right)^2\left(\frac T{T_\mathrm{MS}}\right)^4\tag{15.46}
$$


$$
=(100)^2\left(\frac13\right)^4\approx11.1^2=123.5\tag{15.47}
$$


$$
\text{Now }F=\frac L{4\pi d^2}\tag{15.48}
$$


$$
\frac{F_\mathrm G}{F_\mathrm{MS}}=\frac{L_\mathrm G}{L_\mathrm{MS}}\left(\frac{d_\mathrm{MS}}{d_\mathrm G}\right)^2=1\tag{15.49}
$$


$$
d_\mathrm G=\sqrt{\frac{L_\mathrm G}{L_\mathrm{MS}}}\times20\mathrm{pc}\tag{15.50}
$$


$$
\approx\sqrt{11.1^2}\times20\mathrm{pc}\tag{15.51}
$$


$$
\approx220\mathrm{pc}\tag{15.52}
$$


Thus, at 220 pc it would be barely visible.

<h4 id="c5-sp-t13-question">Условие</h4>



Estimate the number of solar neutrinos which should pass through a $1\mathrm{m}^2$ area of the Earth’s surface perpendicular to the Sun every second. Use the fact that each fusion reaction in the Sun produces 26.8 MeV of energy and 2 neutrinos.




<p>Условие: <a href="/collections/ioaa-problems-by-topic/source/711ac8d837c5f8d9a39467d3a734940e193d151edef342c2c232a543d4b48062.pdf#page=48">PDF 48</a></p>

<details id="c5-sp-t13-key">

<summary>Печатно решение / форма</summary>



We can compare energy of each fusion reaction to the Solar constant.

$$
N_\mathrm{neutrino}=2N_\mathrm{fusion}
$$


$$
=2\frac S{E_\mathrm{fusion}}
$$


$$
=\frac{2\times1366}{26.8\times1.6\times10^{-13}}
$$


$$

– Their brightness, observed at a given instant from Earth, depends on the surface area illuminated by the Sun and the part of the asteroid which is visible to the observer. Both vary as the asteroid moves.

– The way the sunlight is reflected by the surface of the asteroid depends on its texture and on the angle between the Sun, the asteroid and the observer (phase angle), which varies as the Earth and the asteroid move along their orbits. ### 5.2 Data Analysis · 2

<p>I08 - D03 - C</p>



<h4 id="c5-sp-d02-question">Условие</h4>



The basic equation of radioactive decay can be written as $N(t)=N_0e^{-\lambda t}$, where $N(t)$ and $N_0$ are the number of remaining atoms of the radioactive isotope (or parent isotope) at time $t$ and its initial number at $t=0$, respectively, while $\lambda$ is the decay constant. The decay of the parent produces daughter nuclides $D(t)$, or radiogenics, which is defined as $D(t)=N_0-N(t)$.

A group of astronomers investigated a number of meteorite samples to determine their ages. They took samples from two meteorites, one containing Allende chondrites (A) and the other one containing basaltic Achondrite (B). By examining these samples, they measured the abundance of ${}^{87}\mathrm{Rb}$ and ${}^{87}\mathrm{Sr}$ assuming that ${}^{87}\mathrm{Sr}$ was entirely produced by the decay of ${}^{87}\mathrm{Rb}$. The value of $\lambda$ is $1.42\times10^{-11}$ per year for this isotopic decay of ${}^{87}\mathrm{Rb}$. In addition, quantities non-radiogenic element ${}^{86}\mathrm{Sr}$ are also measured. The results of measurement are given in the table below, expressed in ppm (part per million).

| Sample | Type | ${}^{86}\mathrm{Sr}$ (ppm) | ${}^{87}\mathrm{Rb}$ (ppm) | ${}^{87}\mathrm{Sr}$ (ppm) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A | 29.6 | 0.3 | 20.7 |
| 2 | B | 58.7 | 68.5 | 44.7 |
| 3 | B | 74.2 | 14.4 | 52.9 |
| 4 | A | 40.2 | 7.0 | 28.6 |
| 5 | A | 19.7 | 0.4 | 13.8 |
| 6 | B | 37.9 | 31.6 | 28.4 |
| 7 | A | 33.4 | 4.0 | 23.6 |
| 8 | B | 29.8 | 105.0 | 26.4 |
| 9 | A | 9.8 | 0.8 | 6.9 |
| 10 | B | 18.5 | 44.0 | 15.4 |

(a) Express the time $t$ in term of $\frac{D(t)}{N(t)}$.

(b) Determine the half-life $T_{1/2}$, i.e. the time required to obtain a half number of parents after decay.

(c) Knowledge of the ratio between two isotopes is more valuable than just the absolute abundance of each isotop. It is quite likely that there was some initial strontium present. by taking $\frac{{}^{87}\mathrm{Rb}}{{}^{86}\mathrm{Sr}}$ as an independent variable and $\frac{{}^{87}\mathrm{Sr}}{{}^{86}\mathrm{Sr}}$ as dependent variable, estimate a simple linear regression model that describes the data.

(d) Plot $\frac{{}^{87}\mathrm{Rb}}{{}^{86}\mathrm{Sr}}$ versus $\frac{{}^{87}\mathrm{Sr}}{{}^{86}\mathrm{Sr}}$ and also the regression line for each type of the meteorites.

(e) Subsequently, help the astronomers to determine the age of each type of the meteorites and error in its estimation. Which type is older?

(f) Determine the initial value of $\left(\frac{{}^{87}\mathrm{Sr}}{{}^{86}\mathrm{Sr}}\right)_0$ for each type of meteorites along with errors.
