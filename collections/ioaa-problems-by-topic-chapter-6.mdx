In a binary system, the apperant magnitude of the primary star is 1.0 and that of the secondary star is 2.0 Find the maximum combined magnitude of this system. (I10 - T01 - A)

Source â€” exact edited IOAA compilation, native 57, printed 43. [native 57](/collections/ioaa-problems-by-topic/source/711ac8d837c5f8d9a39467d3a734940e193d151edef342c2c232a543d4b48062.pdf#page=57).




<p>Условие: <a href="/collections/ioaa-problems-by-topic/source/711ac8d837c5f8d9a39467d3a734940e193d151edef342c2c232a543d4b48062.pdf#page=57">PDF 57</a></p>

<details id="c6-t1-key">

<summary>Печатно решение / форма</summary>



Let $F_1,F_2$ and $F_c$ be the flux of the first star, the second star and the combined flux, respectively.

$$
\delta m=-2.5\log\left(\frac{F_1}{F_2}\right)\tag{16.1}
$$

$$
\frac{F_1}{F_2}=10^{(2-1)/2.5}=10^{0.4}\tag{16.2}
$$

$$
F_c=F_1+F_2\tag{16.3}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}m_c&=m_2-2.5\log\left(\frac{F_1+F_2}{F_2}\right)\\&=2-2.5\log\left(1+\frac{F_1}{F_2}\right)\\&=2-2.5\log(1+10^{0.4})\\&=2.0-1.36\end{aligned}\tag{16.4}
$$

$$
m_c=0.64\mathrm{mag}\tag{16.5}
$$

\begin{aligned}\therefore d&=10^{10/2}\mathrm{A.U.}=10^5\mathrm{A.U.}\\&=1.496\times10^{16}\mathrm m=\frac{1.496}{3.0856}\mathrm{pc}\\&\approx0.5\mathrm{pc}\end{aligned}\tag{16.7}

Determination of stellar masses in a visual binary system: The star α-Centauri (Rigel Kentaurus) is a triple star consists of two main-sequence stars α-Centauri A and α-Centauri B representing a visual binary system, and the third star, called Proxima Centauri, which is smaller, fainter and farther than the other two stars. The angular distance between α-Centauri A and α-Centauri B is 17.59″. The binary system has an orbital period of 79.24 years. The visual magnitudes of α-Centauri A and α-Centauri B are−0.01 and1.34 respectively. Their color indices are 0.65 and 0.85 respectively. Use the data below to answer the following questions.

Notes:

– Bolometric correction (BC) is a correction that must be made to the apparent magnitude of an object in order to convert an object’s visible band magnitudes to its bolometric magnitudes, i.e.,

$$
BC=M_v-M_{bol}=m_v-m_{bol}\tag{6.1}
$$


– Mass-Luminosity relation:

$$
M_{bol}=-10.2\log\left(\frac{M}{M_\odot}\right)+4.9\tag{6.2}
$$


where $M$ and $M_\odot$ are masses of the star and the Sun respectively.

(c) Orbital period of α-Centauri is $T=79.24$years and its angular semi-major axis is $\alpha=17.59''$. If $d$ is the distance to the star in parsec, $M_A$ and $M_B$ are masses of two components in solar masses and $a$ is their orbital radius in A.U., using Kepler’s third law we get,

$$
T^2=\frac{a^3}{(M_A+M_B)}\tag{16.116}
$$

$$
=\frac{(\alpha d)^3}{(M_A+M_B)}
$$

$$
d^3=\frac{(M_A+M_B)T^2}{\alpha^3}\tag{16.117}
$$


The implicit trick here is the fact that the Earth goes around one solar mass star in one year with orbital radius of 1 A.U. So by taking quantities in those units will automatically eliminate $\frac{4\pi^2}G$. From here we can proceed by two methods:

i. We find the masses by iterative method. 1st iteration: We first assume

$$
M_A+M_B=2M_\odot\tag{16.118}
$$

$$
d^3=\frac{(M_A+M_B)T^2}{\alpha^3}
$$

$$
d=\sqrt[3]{\frac{2\times79.24^2}{17.59^3}}=\sqrt[3]{2.3074}
$$

$$
d=1.3214\mathrm{pc}\tag{16.119}
$$


We determine the absolute bolometric magnitude.

$$
M_{bol}=m_{bol}+5-5\log d\tag{16.120}
$$

$$
M_{bol-A}=-0.1+5-5\log(1.3214)
$$

$$
M_{bol-A}=4.9-0.6052=4.29\tag{16.121}
$$

$$
M_{bol-B}=1.11+5-5\log(1.3214)
$$

$$
M_{bol-B}=6.11-0.6052=5.50\tag{16.122}
$$


Now, we determine mass of both stars using luminosity mass relation.

$$
M_{bol}=-10.2\log\left(\frac M{M_\odot}\right)+4.9
$$

$$
\therefore M=10^{(4.9-M_{bol})/10.2}M_\odot\tag{16.123}
$$

$$
M_A=10^{(4.9-4.29)/10.2}M_\odot=10^{0.61/10.2}M_\odot
$$

$$
M_A=1.1476M_\odot\tag{16.124}
$$

$$
M_B=10^{(4.9-5.50)/10.2}M_\odot=10^{-0.6/10.2}M_\odot
$$

$$
M_B=0.8733M_\odot\tag{16.125}
$$

$$
\therefore M_A+M_B=(1.1476+0.8733)M_\odot
$$

$$
M_A+M_B=2.0209M_\odot\tag{16.126}
$$


2nd iteration: Repeating the steps above and using the previous resulting total mass from the first iteration,

$$
d=\sqrt[3]{\frac{2.0209\times(79.24)^2}{(17.59)^3}}
$$

$$
d=1.3260\mathrm{pc}\tag{16.127}
$$

$$
M_{bol-A}=-0.1+5-5\log(1.3260)
$$

$$
M_{bol-A}=4.29\tag{16.128}
$$

$$
M_{bol-B}=1.11+5-5\log(1.3260)
$$

$$
M_{bol-B}=5.50\tag{16.129}
$$


We can note that within the accuracy limits of magnitudes, the absolute bolometric magnitudes in the second iteration are exactly same as the ones in the first iteration. Thus, masses determined would again be exactly the same. Thus,

$$
\therefore M_A=1.15M_\odot\tag{16.130}
$$

$$
\&\ M_B=0.87M_\odot\tag{16.131}
$$


ii. Alternatively, we can substitute $d$ in terms of mass.

$$
d^3=\frac{(M_A+M_B)T^2}{\alpha^3}
$$

$$
\therefore\frac{(M_A+M_B)}{d^3}=\frac{\alpha^3}{T^2}=\frac{17.59^3}{79.24^2}\tag{16.132}
$$

$$
=0.8668M_\odot/\mathrm{pc}^3\tag{16.133}
$$

$$
M_{bol}=m_{bol}+5-5\log d
$$

$$
M_{bol}=-10.2\log\left(\frac M{M_\odot}\right)+4.9
$$

$$
\therefore10.2\log\left(\frac M{M_\odot}\right)=5\log d-m_{bol}-5+4.9
$$

$$
\log M=\frac{5\log d-m_{bol}-0.1}{10.2}\tag{16.134}
$$

$$
\log M_A=0.49\log d\tag{16.135}
$$

$$
\log M_B=0.49\log d-\frac{1.21}{10.2}\tag{16.136}
$$


Combining the two equations, we get,

$$
=\log M_A-\frac{1.21}{10.2}
$$

$$
\therefore M_B=10^{-1.21/10.2}M_A
$$

$$
M_B=0.7610M_A\tag{16.137}
$$

$$
M_A+M_B=1.7610M_A
$$

$$
\therefore0.8668d^3=1.7610d^{0.49}\tag{16.138}
$$

$$
d=\left(\frac{1.7610}{0.8668}\right)^{1/2.51}
$$

$$
d=1.3260\mathrm{pc}\tag{16.139}
$$

$$
M_A=d^{0.49}=1.15M_\odot\tag{16.140}
$$

$$
M_B=0.7610\times1.1483M_\odot
$$

$$
M_B=0.87M_\odot\tag{16.141}
$$
