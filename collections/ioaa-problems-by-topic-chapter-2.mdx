Two persons, on the equator of the Earth separated by $180^\circ$ in longitude, observe the Moon’s position with respect to the background star field at the same time. If the declination of the Moon is zero, sketch the situation and calculate the difference in apparent right ascension seen by those two persons. (I08 - T01 - A)

Source: exact IOAA edited compilation, physical native pages 21; printed 7.




<p>Условие: <a href="/collections/ioaa-problems-by-topic/source/711ac8d837c5f8d9a39467d3a734940e193d151edef342c2c232a543d4b48062.pdf#page=21">PDF 21</a></p>

<details id="c2-theory-01-key">

<summary>Печатно решение / форма</summary>



$$
\alpha=\frac{\text{Diameter of Earth}}{\text{Distance to Moon}}\tag{12.1}
$$

$$
=\frac{D_\oplus}{d_{e-m}}\ \text{radians}=\frac{D_\oplus}{d_{e-m}}\times\frac{180\times60}{\pi}\ \text{arcminutes}\tag{12.2}

$$

$$

$$
=116'=\frac{116}{15}\ \text{minutes of RA}\tag{12.4}
$$


Thus, the difference in apparent right ascension is 7.73 minutes.

### 2.1 Theory · 2

<p>I07-T03-A</p>



<h4 id="c2-theory-02-question">Условие</h4>



The time interval between noon of 1 July and noon of 31 December is 183 solar days. What is this interval in sidereal days? (I07 - T03 - A)

Source: exact IOAA edited compilation, physical native pages 21; printed 7.




<p>Условие: <a href="/collections/ioaa-problems-by-topic/source/711ac8d837c5f8d9a39467d3a734940e193d151edef342c2c232a543d4b48062.pdf#page=21">PDF 21</a></p>

<details id="c2-theory-02-key">

<summary>Печатно решение / форма</summary>



As the solar day is measured from noon to noon, it is slightly longer than the rotation period of the Earth (sidereal day). Over the course of one solar year, this small time is accumulated to one additional sidereal day. Thus, in exactly half a year, it would have accumulated to 0.5 extra days.

Thus, there will be 183.5 sidereal days from the noon of 1 July to the noon of 31 December.

Mathematically, the sidereal day is $3^m56^s$ shorter than the solar day.

$$
\therefore183\ \text{solar days}=183^d+183\times(3^m56^s)
$$

$$
=183^d11^h50^m48^s
$$

$$
\approx183.5\ \text{days}\tag{12.5}
$$

Thirdly, atmospheric refraction at the horizon ($\alpha$) is about 34’.

$$
\cos\theta=\frac{R}{R+h}=\frac{6370.8}{6370.8+5.6}\tag{12.15}
$$

$$
\theta=2.40^\circ=2^\circ24'\tag{12.16}
$$

$$
\therefore\delta=90^\circ-\phi-\theta-\alpha\tag{12.17}
$$

$$
=90^\circ-(35^\circ57'+2^\circ24'+34')
$$

$$
=90^\circ-38^\circ55'
$$

$$
\therefore\delta=51^\circ5'\text{N}\tag{12.18}
$$

### 2.1 Theory · 5

<p>I07-T01-B</p>



<h4 id="c2-theory-05-question">Условие</h4>



For an observer at latitude $42.5^\circ$N and longitude $71^\circ$W, estimate the time of sun rise on 21 December if the observer’s civil time is -5 hours from GMT. Ignore refraction of the atmosphere, the size of the solar disc. (I07 - T01 - B)

EA: How much is the error (in minutes) introduced by the atmospheric refraction, size of the solar disc as well as equation of time?

Source: exact IOAA edited compilation, physical native pages 22; printed 8.




<p>Условие: <a href="/collections/ioaa-problems-by-topic/source/711ac8d837c5f8d9a39467d3a734940e193d151edef342c2c232a543d4b48062.pdf#page=22">PDF 22</a></p>

<details id="c2-theory-05-key">

<summary>Печатно решение / форма</summary>



Recall coordinate transformation relations between equatorial and horizontal coordinates.

For the special case of rising or setting of an astronomical object, the relation is reduced to a simplified form

$$
\cos H=-\tan\delta\tan\phi\tag{12.19}
$$


For 21st December, $\delta_\odot=-23.5^\circ$ and question states $\phi_{Observer}=42.5^\circ$. Thus,

$$
H=\cos^{-1}(-\tan(-23.5)\tan42.5)
$$

$$
=\cos^{-1}(0.4348\times0.9163)
$$

$$
=\cos^{-1}(0.3984)
$$

$$
H\approx4^h26^m5^s\tag{12.20}
$$


Thus, equation 12.20 shows that the Sun rises 4 hours 26 minutes before local noon or at 7:34am local time.

Next we notice that the longitude of observer is $\lambda_{Observer}=71^\circ$E. As local time changes by 4 minute for every degree of longitude, his local time must be 4 hours and 44 minutes ahead of GMT. But the problem states that his civil time is 5 hours after GMT.

This means his civil time is 16 minutes behind of his local time.

Applying this correction, the Sunrise time as per the observer’s watch will be,

$$
t=7:34-0:16=7:18am\tag{12.21}
$$


EA: Correction for atmospheric refraction is roughly 34’ and angular radius of solar disk can be upto 16’. As a combined effect of these two factors, the top edge of the Sun becomes visible when the centre of the Sun is about 50’ below horizon.

In the figure, ABC is the horizon line. A is the position where the centre of the Sun will rise. B is the place where top edge of the Sun becomes visible when the centre of the Sun is at S. Thus, $l(BS)=50'$.

It can be seen that

$$
\angle BCS=\angle BSA=\phi\tag{12.22}
$$

$$
\therefore AS=\frac{l(BS)}{\cos\phi}=\frac{50'}{\cos42.5^\circ}\approx67'.8\tag{12.23}
$$


As this is a small length, we approximate the Sun’s speed as 4 minutes per degree. Thus, the Sun will rise about 4.5 minutes earlier due to these two factors. The equation of time shows that the Sun will be about 2 minutes (1.7 minutes to be precise) ahead on 21st December.

Thus total error introduced by all these effects is about 6 minutes.

### 2.1 Theory · 7

<p>I08-T03-B</p>



<h4 id="c2-theory-07-question">Условие</h4>



A full moon occurred on June 19, 2008 at $00^h30^m$ West Indonesian Time (local civil time for western part of Indonesia with reference geographic longitude as $105^\circ$E). Calculate the extreme possible durations of the Moon above the horizon for observers at Bosscha Observatory (longitude: $107^\circ35'\,00''.0$ E, latitude: $6^\circ49'\,00''.0$ S, Elevation: $1300.0\,m$, Time zone = $UT+7^h30^m$). (I08 - T03 - B)

Source: exact IOAA edited compilation, physical native pages 22; printed 8.




<p>Условие: <a href="/collections/ioaa-problems-by-topic/source/711ac8d837c5f8d9a39467d3a734940e193d151edef342c2c232a543d4b48062.pdf#page=22">PDF 22</a></p>

<details id="c2-theory-07-key">

<summary>Печатно решение / форма</summary>



Note that the date of observation is (June 19) very close to summer solstice. Thus,

$\delta\approx+23^\circ.5$, & $\alpha\approx6^h$

At full moon phase, the Moon is nearly diametrically opposite to the Sun along the ecliptic. The diametrically opposite position will be,

$\delta\approx-23^\circ.5$, & $\alpha\approx18^h$

We further note that the Moon can be upto $5^\circ.25$ above or below the ecliptic. Thus, $\delta$ can be between $-18^\circ.25$ and $-28^\circ.75$ depending on the position of node.

$$
\cos(H)=-\tan\delta\tan\phi\tag{12.28}
$$

$$
\therefore H_1=\cos^{-1}(-\tan\delta\tan\phi)\tag{12.29}
$$

$$
=\cos^{-1}(-\tan(-18.5)\tan(-6^\circ49')\tag{12.30}
$$

$$
=92^\circ.3=6^h9^m\tag{12.31}
$$

$$
\therefore H_1=\cos^{-1}(-\tan\delta\tan\phi)\tag{12.32}
$$

$$
=\cos^{-1}(-\tan(-28.75)\tan(-6^\circ49')\tag{12.33}
$$

$$
=93^\circ.8=6^h15^m\tag{12.34}
$$


As a first approximation, the duration will be given by $2H$ i.e. Its extreme values can be from 12 hours 18 minutes and 12 hours 30 minutes. However, we have to include two important corrections to this value.

Firstly, due to the lunar orbital motion, the Moon is moving backwards by slightly less than 53 minutes per day. Thus, in 12.5 hours, it will move backwards by nearly 27 minutes and will be visible for 27 more minutes. Secondly, as the refraction near horizon is nearly half a degree, the moon will be seen longer by 2 minutes each at rising and setting.

Thus, total correction will be for 31 minutes.

Hence, extreme possible values are 12 hours 49 minutes and 13 hours 1 minute.

One may note that some of the data given in this problem like longitude, civil time and elevation is not used in the solution. It is expected that student solving such problems chooses data judiciously and not get confused by excess data.
