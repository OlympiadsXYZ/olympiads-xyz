## Условие

Estimate the orbital speed $V_G$ of Mars during Great oppositions with Earth. The orbital speed of Earth is $V_1=29.8$ km/s. The eccentricities of the orbits of the Earth and Mars are $e_1=0.017$ and $e_2=0.093$ respectively and the mean distance from the Sun to Mars is equal to $a_2=1.524$ a.u.

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

Using the III Kepler law ($T^2/a^3=\mathrm{const}$) one can find that for the circular speed the following relation is valid:

$$
V^2\cdot a=\mathrm{const}
$$

and so:

$$
V_{\mathrm{circ}}\sim a^{-1/2}
$$

It means that the circular speed ($V_2$) for Mars orbit is $1.52^{1/2}$ smaller than one ($V_1$) for Earth orbit.

During the Great oppositions Mars is near the perihelion of its orbit.

Using the II Kepler law one may also write (for the orbits with small eccentricities – see note*):

$$
V_{\mathrm{per}}\cdot R_{\mathrm{per}}=V_{\mathrm{circ}}\cdot R_{\mathrm{circ}}.
$$
$$
V_{\mathrm{per}}\cdot R_{\mathrm{circ}}(1-e)=V_{\mathrm{circ}}\cdot R_{\mathrm{circ}},
$$

where $R_{\mathrm{circ}}$ and $V_{\mathrm{circ}}$ – the mean distance from the Sun to Mars and its mean orbital speed. So

$$
V_G=V_{\mathrm{per}}=V_{\mathrm{circ}}/(1-e)=V_1/\{1.52^{1/2}(1-e)\}\approx26.6\ \mathrm{km/s}.
$$

(*) More accurate using of the II Kepler law here is

$$
V_{\mathrm{per}}\cdot a(1-e)/2=\pi ab/T,
$$

where $a$ and $b$ are the semiaxes of the orbit, $b=a(1-e^2)^{1/2}$.

In this case the answer is

$$
V_G=V_{\mathrm{circ}}\cdot\{(1+e)/(1-e)\}^{1/2}=V_1\cdot\{(1+e)/1.52(1-e)\}^{1/2}\approx26.5\ \mathrm{km/s}.
$$


</Spoiler>
