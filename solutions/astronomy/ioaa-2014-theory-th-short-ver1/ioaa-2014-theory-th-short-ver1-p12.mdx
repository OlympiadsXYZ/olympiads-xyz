## Условие

Inside a photo camera a plane mirror is placed along the optical axis of the lens of the objective (as seen in figure 13). The length of the mirror is half of the focal distance of the lens of the objective. The photo camera is oriented as on the photographic plate situated in the focal plane of the photo camera are captured two images with different illuminations of the Vega star. Find out the difference between the apparent photographical magnitudes of the two images of the Vega stars.

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

***Problem 12. Marking scheme The Vega star in the mirror***

The light beam arriving from  **Vega Star** can be considered paraxial, due to the distance from it to the observer on Earth. The explanation for the existence of two distinct images of the star is that the optical axis of the objective is not parallel with the light beam from the star.

The images on the camera plate are symmetrical placed relative to the principal optical axis.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '83.9%', '--fig-max': '537px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/ioaa-2014-theory-th-short-ver1/p12-sol-fig1.png" alt="Ray diagram of the camera: parallel rays from the left (letters V, E, G, A) strike the lens PQ (centre O, radius r, point C); a grey shaded beam converges from CQ to the image Σ₁ on the photographic plate below the axis; rays through the upper part PC reflect on the plane mirror MN (length f/2, on the dashed optical axis) and converge to the image Σ₂ above the axis; the distances f (lens to plate) and r/2 (N to Σ₁) are marked." width="1679" height="972" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Fig. 12</figcaption>
</figure>

Each of the point images of the Vega Star  $\Sigma_1$  and  $\Sigma_2$  didn’t concentrate the same light fluxes. In the down below figure it can be seen the sections of the lens which correspond to each image. The sector APBC is passed by the light which concentrates in the image $\Sigma_2$  and the light passing the sector ACBQ concentrates into the point image $\Sigma_1$ See the picture in figure 13  .

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '45.9%', '--fig-max': '294px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/ioaa-2014-theory-th-short-ver1/p12-sol-fig2.png" alt="Circular lens of radius r with centre O and vertical dashed diameter PQ; the chord AB crosses PC at C; the upper white segment is S₂ and the grey remainder S₁; dashed radii OA and OB, with the angle 60° at O between OC and OA and the angle 30° at B." width="918" height="927" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Fig.13 b</figcaption>
</figure>

The ratio between the light fluxes concentrated into the two image points will directly depend on the ratio of the two sectors areas.

From the geometry of the figure 2 results :

$$
\mathrm{MN} = \mathrm{OM};\ \mathrm{N}\Sigma_1 = \mathrm{OC} = \frac{r}{2};
$$

$$
\angle\left(\mathrm{CBO}\right) = 30^{\circ};\ \angle\left(\mathrm{BOC}\right) = 60^{\circ};\ \angle\left(\mathrm{AOB}\right) = 120^{\circ};
$$

$$
\frac{S_1}{S_2} = \frac{8\pi + 3\sqrt{3}}{4\pi - 3\sqrt{3}} \approx 4.
$$

Using the Pogson formula :

$$
\log\frac{E_1}{E_2} = \log\frac{\frac{\sigma T_{\mathrm{V}}^4\cdot 4\pi R_{\mathrm{V}}^2}{4\pi d_{\mathrm{PV}}^2}\cdot S_1}{\frac{\sigma T_{\mathrm{V}}^4\cdot 4\pi R_{\mathrm{V}}^2}{4\pi d_{\mathrm{PV}}^2}\cdot S_2} = -0{,}4\left(m_1 - m_2\right);
$$

$$
\log\frac{S_1}{S_2} = -0{,}4\left(m_1 - m_2\right);
$$

$$
m_2 - m_1 = 1{,}5^{\mathrm{m}}.
$$
