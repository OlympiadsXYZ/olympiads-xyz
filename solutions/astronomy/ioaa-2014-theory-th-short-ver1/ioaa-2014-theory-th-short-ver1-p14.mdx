## Условие

You know that  the absolute magnitude of the Moon is $M_L = 0{,}25^{\mathrm{m}}$. Calculate the values of the apparent magnitudes of the Moon corresponding to the following Moon –phases :  full-moon and the first quarter. You know: the Moon – Earth distance - $d_{LP} = 385000\ \mathrm{km}$, the Earth – Sun distance - $d_{PS} = 1\ \mathrm{AU}$, the Moon –Sun distance, $d_{LS} = 1\ \mathrm{AU}$

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- Full moon: $m_M = -12{,}25^{\mathrm{m}}$; first quarter: $m_M = -10{,}5^{\mathrm{m}}$.

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

***Problem 14. Marking scheme Apparent magnitude of the Moon***

1. General analysis of the problem — 6 points
2. The analysis of the 2 particular situations — 4 points

The apparent magnitude of a planet from the Solar System depends on the phase angle $M = M\left(\Psi\right)$.

The apparent magnitude of the body is given by the relation:

$$
m = M + 2{,}5\cdot\log\frac{d_{\mathrm{C,S}}^2\cdot d_{\mathrm{C,O}}^2}{d_0^4\cdot p\left(\Psi\right)},
$$

unde: $d_{B,\mathrm{S}} -$the distance between the body and the Sun; $d_{B,\mathrm{O}} -$distance between the body and observer; $d_0 = 1\ \mathrm{AU};\ \Psi -$the phase angle ; $p\left(\Psi\right) -$the phase function :

$$
p\left(\Psi\right) = \frac{2}{3}\cdot\left[\left(1 - \frac{\Psi}{\pi}\right)\cos\Psi + \frac{1}{\pi}\sin\Psi\right],
$$

$\Psi$ as seen in the figure bellow is given by the cosine law.

$$
\cos\Psi = \frac{d_{\mathbf{B}\mathrm{O}}^2 + d_{B\mathrm{S}}^2 - d_{\mathrm{OS}}^2}{2d_{B\mathrm{O}}\cdot d_{B\mathrm{S}}}.
$$

In particularly for the Moon

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '59.4%', '--fig-max': '380px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/ioaa-2014-theory-th-short-ver1/p14-sol-fig2.png" alt="Sun, Moon and Earth: the Sun at left, the Moon at right as a circle with centre L, points A (top), B (bottom), C, D and B′, radius R, its right half shaded grey; the angle ψ at L between the directions to the Sun and to the Earth, and again between LB and LD; dashed lines d_MS (Sun–Moon), d_ES (Sun–Earth) and d_ME (Moon–Earth); an observer standing on the Earth below." width="1188" height="889" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

$$
\cos\Psi = \frac{d_{ME}^2 + d_{M\mathrm{S}}^2 - d_{E\mathrm{S}}^2}{2d_{ME}\cdot d_{M\mathrm{S}}};
$$

$$
p\left(\Psi\right) = \frac{2}{3}\cdot\left[\left(1 - \frac{\Psi}{\pi}\right)\cos\Psi + \frac{1}{\pi}\sin\Psi\right];
$$

$$
m_M = M_M + 2{,}5\cdot\log\frac{d_{M,\mathrm{S}}^2\cdot d_{ME}^2}{d_0^4\cdot p\left(\Psi\right)}.
$$

Particular cases:

1) Full moon

$$
\Psi = 0;
$$

$$
\cos\Psi = 1;\ \sin\Psi = 0;
$$

$$
p\left(\Psi\right) = \frac{2}{3};
$$

$$
d_{M\mathrm{S}} = 1\ \mathrm{AU};\ d_{ME} = 385000\ \mathrm{km} \approx 0{,}00256\ \mathrm{AU} = 256\cdot 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{SU};\ d_0 = 1\ \mathrm{S}U;
$$

$$
m_M = M_M - 12{,}5^{\mathrm{m}} = 0{,}25^{\mathrm{m}} - 12{,}5^{\mathrm{m}} = -12{,}25^{\mathrm{m}}.
$$

2) First Quarter

$$
\Psi = 90^{\circ};
$$

$$
\cos\Psi = 0;\ \sin\Psi = 1;
$$

$$
p\left(\Psi\right) = \frac{2}{3\pi} \approx 0{,}2;
$$

$$
\frac{d_{M,\mathrm{S}}^2\cdot d_{ME}^2}{d_0^4\cdot p\left(\Psi\right)} = \frac{65536\cdot 10^{-10}}{0{,}2} = 491520\cdot 10^{-10};
$$

$$
m_M = M_M - 10{,}75^{\mathrm{m}} = 0{,}25^{\mathrm{m}} - 10{,}75^{\mathrm{m}} = -10{,}5^{\mathrm{m}}.
$$
