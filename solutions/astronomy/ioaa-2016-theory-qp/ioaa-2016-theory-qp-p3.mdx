## Условие

Cosmological models indicate that radiation energy density, $\rho_{\mathrm{r}}$, in the Universe is proportional to $(1+\mathrm{z})^{4}$, and the matter energy density, $\rho_{\mathrm{m}}$, is proportional to $(1+\mathrm{z})^{3}$, where $\mathrm{z}$ is the redshift. The dimensionless density parameter, $\Omega$, is given as $\Omega = \rho/\rho_{\mathrm{c}}$, where $\rho_{\mathrm{c}}$ is the critical energy density of the Universe. In the present Universe, the density parameters corresponding to radiation and matter, are $\Omega_{\mathrm{r_0}} = 10^{-4}$ and $\Omega_{\mathrm{m_0}} = 0.3$, respectively.

**T3.1** Calculate the redshift, $\mathrm{z_e}$, at which radiation and matter energy densities were equal. **[3 т.]**

**T3.2** Assuming that the radiation from the early Universe has a blackbody spectrum with a temperature of 2.732 K, estimate the temperature, $T_{\mathrm{e}}$, of the radiation at redshift $\mathrm{z_e}$. **[4 т.]**

**T3.3** Estimate the typical photon energy, $E_{\nu}$ (in eV), of the radiation as emitted at redshift $\mathrm{z_e}$. **[3 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**T3.1:**

$$
\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{mo}}/\rho_{\mathrm{c}}}{\rho_{\mathrm{ro}}/\rho_{\mathrm{c}}} = \frac{\Omega_{\mathrm{m}_0}}{\Omega_{\mathrm{r}_0}} = \frac{0.3}{10^{-4}} = 3000
$$

At $z_{\mathrm{e}}$, both matter density and radiation density were equal.

$$
\rho_{\mathrm{r}} = \rho_{\mathrm{m}}
$$

$$
\therefore\ \rho_{\mathrm{r}_0}(1+z_{\mathrm{e}})^4 = \rho_{\mathrm{m}_0}(1+z_{\mathrm{e}})^3
$$

$$
1+z_{\mathrm{e}} = \frac{\rho_{\mathrm{m}_0}}{\rho_{\mathrm{r}_0}} = 3000
$$
**[2.0]**

$$
\therefore\ z_{\mathrm{e}} \simeq 3000
$$
**[1.0]**

**Only $z_{\mathrm{e}} = 2999$ and $z_{\mathrm{e}} = 3000$ are acceptable answers.**

**T3.2:**

As the Universe behaves like an ideal black body, the radiation density will be proportional to the fourth power of the temperature (Stefan's law).

$$
\left(\frac{T_{\mathrm{e}}}{T_0}\right)^4 = \frac{\rho_{\mathrm{re}}}{\rho_{\mathrm{r}_0}}
$$

$$
= \frac{\rho_{\mathrm{r}_0}(1+z_{\mathrm{e}})^4}{\rho_{\mathrm{r}_0}}
$$
**[2.0]**

$$
\left(\frac{T_{\mathrm{e}}}{2.732}\right)^4 = (1+z_{\mathrm{e}})^4
$$
**[1.0]**

$$
\frac{T_{\mathrm{e}}}{2.732} = 1+z_{\mathrm{e}} = 3000
$$

$$
T_{\mathrm{e}} = 3000 \times 2.732
$$

$$
T_{\mathrm{e}} = 8200\ \mathrm{K}
$$
**[1.0]**

**$8100 \leq T_{\mathrm{e}} \leq 8200$ gives 1.0; $8200 < T_{\mathrm{e}} \leq 9000$ gives 0.5; else 0.**

**T3.3:**

Wien's law:

$$
\lambda_{\max} = \frac{0.002898\ \mathrm{m\ K}}{T_e}
$$

$$
= \frac{0.002898}{8200}\ \mathrm{m} = 354\ \mathrm{nm}
$$
**[1.0]**

$$
E_{\nu} = \frac{hc}{\lambda_{\max}}
$$

$$
= \frac{6.62 \times 10^{-34} \times 3 \times 10^8}{354 \times 10^{-9}}\ \mathrm{J} = \frac{5.62 \times 10^{-19}}{1.602 \times 10^{-19}}\ \mathrm{eV}
$$
**[1.0]**

$$
E_{\nu} = 3.5\ \mathrm{eV}
$$
**[1.0]**

Alternative solution:

$$
E_{\nu} = k_{\mathrm{B}}T_{\mathrm{e}}
$$

$$
= 1.38 \times 10^{-23} \times 8200\ \mathrm{J} = \frac{1.13 \times 10^{-19}}{1.602 \times 10^{-19}}\ \mathrm{eV}
$$
**[2.0]**

$$
E_{\nu} = 0.71\ \mathrm{eV}
$$
**[1.0]**

**Use of either Wien's law or $E = k_{\mathrm{B}}T$ gets full credit. $E_{\nu} = 3k_{\mathrm{B}}T/2$ or similar gets no credit. Answers with $E_{\nu} = 3k_{\mathrm{B}}T$ or $E_{\nu} = 2.7k_{\mathrm{B}}T$ also get full credit.**


</Spoiler>
