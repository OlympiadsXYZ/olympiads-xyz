## Условие

In a particular ideal refracting telescope of focal ratio $f$/5, the focal length of the objective lens is 100 cm and that of the eyepiece is 1 cm.

An introduction of a concave lens (Barlow lens) between the objective lens and the prime focus is a common way to increase the magnification without a large increase in the length of the telescope. A Barlow lens of focal length 1 cm is now introduced between the objective and the eyepiece to double the magnification.

A telescope is now constructed with the same objective lens and a CCD detector placed at the prime focus (without any Barlow lens or eyepiece). The size of each pixel of the CCD detector is 10 μm.

**T7.1** What is the angular magnification, $m_0$, of the telescope? What is the length of the telescope, $L_0$, i.e. the distance between its objective and eyepiece? **[4 т.]**

**T7.2** At what distance, $d_{\mathrm{B}}$, from the prime focus must the Barlow lens be kept in order to obtain this desired double magnification? **[6 т.]**

**T7.3** What is the increase, $\Delta L$, in the length of the telescope? **[4 т.]**

**T7.4** What will be the distance in pixels between the centroids of the images of the two stars , $n_{\mathrm{p}}$, on the CCD, if they are 20'' apart on the sky? **[6 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**(T7.1)** What is the angular magnification, $m_0$, of the telescope? What is the length of the telescope, $L_0$, i.e. the distance between its objective and eyepiece? **[4]**

**Solution:**
The magnification will be given by,

$$
m_0 = \frac{f_o}{f_e} \quad \textbf{[1.0]}
$$

$$
= \frac{100}{1} = 100 \quad \textbf{[1.0]}
$$

The magnification is $\boxed{m_0 = 100}$

Length of the telescope will be

$$
L_0 = f_o + f_e \quad \textbf{[1.0]}
$$

$$
= 100 + 1 = 101\ \mathrm{cm} \quad \textbf{[1.0]}
$$

The telescope length will be $\boxed{L_0 = 101\ \mathrm{cm}}$

**Exact answer required for credit.**

**(T7.2)** At what distance, $d_{\mathrm{B}}$, from the prime focus must the Barlow lens be kept in order to obtain this desired double magnification? **[6]**

**Solution:**
We use the following sign convention. Lens is the origin. Direction along the direction of light is taken as positive. The lens formula is $\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{v} - \dfrac{1}{u}$ ($f$ is positive for convex lens and negative for concave lens). Magnification is $m = \dfrac{v}{u}$. Solutions using other sign conventions are acceptable.

Let $v$ be image distance from the Barlow lens.

$$
\frac{1}{f_{\mathrm{B}}} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} \quad \textbf{[1.0]}
$$

Distance of Barlow lens, $d_{\mathrm{B}}$, before the prime focus is same as the object distance, $u$, in this case. **[1.0]**

$$
\frac{1}{f_{\mathrm{B}}} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{d_B}
$$

Also, $m_{\mathrm{B}} = 2 = \dfrac{v}{u} = \dfrac{v}{d_{\mathrm{B}}}$ **[0.5]**

$$
\therefore\ \frac{1}{d_{\mathrm{B}}} = \frac{2}{v} \quad \textbf{[1.0]}
$$

$$
\therefore\ \frac{1}{-1} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{2}{v}
$$

$$
-1 = \frac{-1}{v}
$$

$$
v = 1\ \mathrm{cm}
$$

$$
d_{\mathrm{B}} = \frac{v}{2} = \frac{1\ \mathrm{cm}}{2} = 0.5\ \mathrm{cm} \quad \textbf{[2.5]}
$$

The positive sign for $d_{\mathrm{B}}$ indicates that the Barlow lens was introduced $\boxed{0.5\ \mathrm{cm}}$ before the prime focus.

**(T7.3)** What is the increase, $\Delta L$, in the length of the telescope? **[4]**

**Solution:**
The increase in the length will be,

$$
\Delta L = v - d_{\mathrm{B}} \quad \textbf{[2.0]}
$$

$$
= 1.0 - 0.5 = 0.5\ \mathrm{cm} \quad \textbf{[2.0]}
$$

Thus, the length will be increased by $\boxed{\Delta L = 0.5\ \mathrm{cm}}$

**(T7.4)** What will be the distance in pixels between the centroids of the images of the two stars, $n_{\mathrm{p}}$, on the CCD, if they are $20''$ apart on the sky? **[6]**

**Solution:**
Plate scale at prime focus is given by,

$$
s = \frac{1}{f_o} = \frac{1\ \mathrm{rad}}{1\ \mathrm{m}} = 0.206\,265\ \mathrm{arcsec/\mu m} \quad \textbf{[2.0]}
$$

Since each pixel is $10\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ in size,

$$
s_{\mathrm{p}} = 10 \times 0.206\ \mathrm{arcsec/\mu m} = 2.06\ \mathrm{arcsec/pixel} \quad \textbf{[2.0]}
$$

Two stars will be separated by,

$$
n_{\mathrm{p}} = \frac{20''}{2.06''}\ \mathrm{pixels} \simeq \boxed{10\ \mathrm{pixels}} \quad \textbf{[2.0]}
$$

**Acceptable range: 9.5 to 10.5 pixels.**


</Spoiler>
