## Условие

The first signal of gravitational waves was observed by two advanced LIGO detectors at Hanford and Livingston, USA in September 2015. One of these measurements (strain vs time in seconds) is shown in the accompanying figure. In this problem, we will interpret this signal in terms of a small test mass $m$ orbiting around a large mass $M$ (i.e., $m \ll M$), by considering several models for the nature of the central mass.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '96.1%', '--fig-max': '615px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/ioaa-2016-theory-qp/p11-fig1-v2.png" alt="GW150914 strain versus time from LIGO Louisiana; all axes, merger marker and source attribution." width="1923" height="1282" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

The test mass loses energy due to the emission of gravitational waves. As a result the orbit keeps on shrinking, until the test mass reaches the surface of the object, or in the case of a black hole, the innermost stable circular orbit – ISCO – which is given by $R_{\mathrm{ISCO}} = 3R_{\mathrm{sch}}$, where $R_{\mathrm{sch}}$ is the Schwarzschild radius of the black hole. This is the "epoch of merger". At this point, the amplitude of the gravitational wave is maximum, and so is its frequency, which is always twice the orbital frequency. In this problem, we will only focus on the gravitational waves before the merger, when Kepler's laws are assumed to be valid. After the merger, the form of gravitational waves will drastically change.

**T11.1** Consider the observed gravitational waves shown in the figure above. Estimate the time period, $T_0$, and hence calculate the frequency, $f_0$, of gravitational waves just before the epoch of merger. **[3 т.]**

**T11.2** For any main sequence (MS) star, the radius of the star, $R_{\mathrm{MS}}$, and its mass, $M_{\mathrm{MS}}$, are related by a power law given as,

$$
R_{\mathrm{MS}} \propto (M_{\mathrm{MS}})^{\alpha}
$$

where $\alpha = 0.8$ for $M_{\odot} < M_{\mathrm{MS}}$ and $\alpha = 1.0$ for $0.08M_{\odot} \leq M_{\mathrm{MS}} \leq M_{\odot}$.

If the central object were a main sequence star, write an expression for the maximum frequency of gravitational waves, $f_{\mathrm{MS}}$, in terms of mass of the star in units of solar masses ($M_{\mathrm{MS}}/M_{\odot}$) and $\alpha$. **[10 т.]**

**T11.3** Using the above result, determine the appropriate value of $\alpha$ that will give the maximum possible frequency of gravitational waves, $f_{\mathrm{MS,max}}$ for any main sequence star. Evaluate this frequency. **[9 т.]**

**T11.4** White dwarf (WD) stars have a maximum mass of $1.44\,M_{\odot}$ (known as the Chandrasekhar limit) and obey the mass-radius relation $R \propto M^{-1/3}$. The radius of a solar mass white dwarf is equal to 6000 km. Find the highest frequency of emitted gravitational waves, $f_{\mathrm{WD,max}}$, if the test mass is orbiting a white dwarf. **[8 т.]**

**T11.5** Neutron stars (NS) are a peculiar type of compact objects which have masses between 1 and $3M_{\odot}$ and radii in the range 10 – 15 km. Find the range of frequencies of emitted gravitational waves, $f_{\mathrm{NS,min}}$ and $f_{\mathrm{NS,max}}$, if the test mass is orbiting a neutron star at a distance close to the neutron star radius. **[8 т.]**

**T11.6** If the test mass is orbiting a black hole (BH), write the expression for the frequency of emitted gravitational waves, $f_{\mathrm{BH}}$, in terms of mass of the black hole, $M_{\mathrm{BH}}$, and the solar mass $M_{\odot}$. **[7 т.]**

**T11.7** Based only on the time period (or frequency) of gravitational waves before the epoch of merger, determine whether the central object can be a main sequence star (MS), a white dwarf (WD), a neutron star (NS), or a black hole (BH). Tick the correct option in the Summary Answersheet. Estimate the mass of this object, $M_{\mathrm{obj}}$, in units of $M_{\odot}$. **[5 т.]**

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-1">

### T11.1

**Solution:**

From the graph, just before the peak of emission, the time period of gravitational waves is approximately $(0.007 \pm 0.004)\ \mathrm{s}$.

$$
T_0 \approx 0.007\ \mathrm{s}
$$
**[2.0]**

**Acceptable range:** 0.003 to 0.011 s

That is, the frequency of the gravitational waves is $f_0 \approx 142.86\ \mathrm{Hz}$. **[1.0]**

**Acceptable range:** 333.33 to 90.91 Hz

**Answer given in terms of angular frequency with correct value and units gains full credit.**

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-2">

### T11.2

**Solution:**

Since $m \ll M$ then, by Kepler's third law

$$
f_{\mathrm{orbital}} = \frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{GM}{r^3}}
$$
**[4.0]**

Hence the frequency of the gravitational waves is

$$
f_{\mathrm{grav}} = 2f_{\mathrm{orbital}} = \frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{GM}{r^3}}
$$
**[1.0]**

The frequency will be maximum when $r = R_{\mathrm{MS}}$. **[1.0]**

For main sequence stars,

$$
\frac{R_{\mathrm{MS}}}{R_{\odot}} = \left(\frac{M_{\mathrm{MS}}}{M_{\odot}}\right)^{\alpha}
$$

$$
\therefore\ R_{\mathrm{MS}} = R_{\odot}\left(\frac{M_{\mathrm{MS}}}{M_{\odot}}\right)^{\alpha}
$$
**[1.0]**

$$
\therefore\ f_{\mathrm{MS}} = \frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{GM_{\mathrm{MS}}}{R_{\odot}^3}}\left(\frac{M_{\odot}}{M_{\mathrm{MS}}}\right)^{3\alpha/2}
$$

$$
= \frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{GM_{\odot}}{R_{\odot}^3}}\left(\frac{M_{\odot}}{M_{\mathrm{MS}}}\right)^{(3\alpha-1)/2}
$$

$$
f_{\mathrm{MS}} = \frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{GM_{\odot}}{R_{\odot}^3}}\left(\frac{M_{\mathrm{MS}}}{M_{\odot}}\right)^{(1-3\alpha)/2}
$$
**[3.0]**

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-3">

### T11.3

**Solution:**

For possible values of $\alpha$ given in the question, the exponent $\frac{1-3\alpha}{2}$ is negative. Thus, if $M_{\mathrm{MS}} > M_{\odot}$, the frequency will be smaller. Thus, for highest possible frequency coming from a main sequence star, you should take lowest possible mass i.e. $\alpha = 1.0$ **[4.0]**

$$
f_{\mathrm{MS,max}} = \frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{GM_{\odot}}{R_{\odot}^3}}\left(\frac{M_{\mathrm{MS}}}{M_{\odot}}\right)^{\left(\frac{1-3\times 1}{2}\right)}
$$

$$
= \frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{GM_{\odot}}{R_{\odot}^3}} \times \frac{M_{\odot}}{M_{\mathrm{MS}}}
$$
**[2.0]**

The frequency of gravitational waves will be given by,

$$
f_{\mathrm{MS,max}} = \frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{6.674 \times 10^{-11} \times 1.989 \times 10^{30}}{(6.955 \times 10^8)^3}} \times \frac{1}{0.08}
$$

$$
f_{\mathrm{MS,max}} = 2.5\ \mathrm{mHz}
$$
**[3.0]**

**Answer given in terms of angular frequency with correct value and units gains full credit.**

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-4">

### T11.4

**Solution:**

The maximum frequency would be when $r = R_{\mathrm{WD}}$. **[1.0]**

We use the notation $R_{WD_{\odot}}$ for the radius of a solar mass white dwarf. Then for white dwarfs

$$
R_{WD}^3 = R_{WD_{\odot}}^3\,\frac{M_{WD}}{M_{\odot}}
$$
**[1.0]**

$$
f_{\mathrm{WD}} = \frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{GM_{\mathrm{WD}}}{R_{WD}^3}}
$$

$$
= \frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{GM_{\odot}}{R_{WD_{\odot}}^3}}\,\frac{M_{WD}}{M_{\odot}}
$$
**[2.0]**

For maximum frequency, we have to take highest white dwarf mass. **[2.0]**

$$
f_{\mathrm{WD,max}} = 2.600 \times 10^{-6} \times \sqrt{\frac{1.989 \times 10^{30}}{(6000 \times 10^3)^3}} \times 1.44
$$

$$
= 2.600 \times 10^{-6} \times 95.96 \times 10^3 \times 1.44
$$

$$
f_{\mathrm{WD,max}} = 0.359\ \mathrm{Hz}
$$
**[2.0]**

**Answer given in terms of angular frequency with correct value and units gains full credit.**

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-5">

### T11.5

**Solution:**

$$
f_{grav} = \frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{GM_{\mathrm{NS}}}{R_{\mathrm{NS}}^3}}
$$

The lowest possible frequency is when $M_{\mathrm{NS}}$ is lowest and $R_{\mathrm{NS}}$ is the highest. **[2.0]**

For $M_{\mathrm{NS}} = M_{\odot}$ and $R = 15\ \mathrm{km}$, we get

$$
f_{\mathrm{NS,min}} = 1.996\ \mathrm{kHz}
$$
**[2.0]**

Similarly, the largest possible frequency is when $M_{\mathrm{NS}}$ is the largest and $R_{\mathrm{NS}}$ is the smallest. **[2.0]**

For $M_{\mathrm{NS}} = 3M_{\odot}$ and $R = 10\ \mathrm{km}$, we get

$$
f_{\mathrm{NS,max}} = 6.352\ \mathrm{kHz}
$$
**[2.0]**

**Answer given in terms of angular frequency with correct value and units gains full credit.**

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-6">

### T11.6

**Solution:**

For black holes, we have to consider $R_{ISCO}$. **[2.0]**

Hence the equation will be,

$$
f_{BH} = \frac{1}{\pi} \times \sqrt{\frac{GM_{\odot}}{27R_{sch-\odot}^3}} \times \frac{M_{\odot}}{M_{BH}}
$$
**[2.0]**

$$
f_{BH} = 4.396\ \mathrm{kHz} \times \frac{M_{\odot}}{M_{BH}}
$$
**[3.0]**

**Answer given in terms of angular frequency with correct value and units gains full credit.**

</ProblemSection>

M_{\mathrm{obj}} = \frac{4396}{142.86}\,M_{\odot} \approx 31\,M_{\odot}
