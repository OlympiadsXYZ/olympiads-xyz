## Условие

India's Mars Orbiter Mission (MOM) was launched using the Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle (PSLV) on 5 November 2013. The dry mass of MOM (body + instruments) was 500 kg and it carried fuel of mass 852 kg. It was initially placed in an elliptical orbit around the Earth with perigee at a height of 264.1 km and apogee at a height of 23903.6 km, above the surface of the Earth. After raising the orbit six times, MOM was transferred to a trans-Mars injection orbit (Hohmann orbit).

The first such orbit-raising was performed by firing the engines for a very short time near the perigee. The engines were fired to change the orbit without changing the plane of the orbit and without changing its perigee. This gave a net impulse of $1.73 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{kg\ m\ s^{-1}}$ to the satellite. Ignore the change in mass due to burning of fuel.

**T9.1** What is the height of the new apogee, $h_{\mathrm{a}}$ above the surface of the Earth, after this engine burn? **[14 т.]**

**T9.2** Find the eccentricity ($e$) of the new orbit after the burn and the new orbital period ($P$) of MOM in hours. **[6 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**(T9.1) What is the height of the new apogee, $h_{\mathrm{a}}$, above the surface of the Earth, after this engine burn?**

Solution:

Let apogee and perigee distances be $r_{\mathrm{a}}$ and $r_{\mathrm{p}}$ respectively.

$$r_{\mathrm{p}} = R_{\oplus} + h_{\mathrm{p}}^{i} = (6371 + 264)\ \mathrm{km} = 6635\ \mathrm{km}$$ **[1.0]**

$$r_{\mathrm{a}} = R_{\oplus} + h_{\mathrm{a}}^{i} = (6371 + 23904)\ \mathrm{km} = 30275\ \mathrm{km}$$ **[1.0]**

Conservation of energy and angular momentum gives

$$E = -\frac{GmM_{\oplus}}{r_{\mathrm{p}} + r_{\mathrm{a}}}$$

Total energy at perigee

$$\frac{1}{2}m{v_{\mathrm{p}}}^2 - \frac{GmM_{\oplus}}{r_{\mathrm{p}}} = E = -\frac{GmM_{\oplus}}{r_{\mathrm{p}} + r_{\mathrm{a}}}$$

$$\frac{1}{2}{v_{\mathrm{p}}}^2 = \frac{GM_{\oplus}}{r_{\mathrm{p}}}\left(1 - \frac{r_{\mathrm{p}}}{r_{\mathrm{p}} + r_{\mathrm{a}}}\right)$$

$$\therefore\ v_{\mathrm{p}} = \sqrt{2\frac{GM_{\oplus}\ r_{\mathrm{a}}}{r_{\mathrm{a}} + r_{\mathrm{p}}}\ r_{\mathrm{p}}}$$ **[2.0]**

$$= \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 6.674 \times 10^{-11} \times 5.972 \times 10^{24} \times 3.0275 \times 10^7}{6.635 \times 10^6 \times (3.0275 + 6.635 \times 10^6)}}$$

$$= 9.929\ \mathrm{km\ s^{-1}}$$ **[1.0]**

As the engine burn is just 41.6 s, we assume that the entire impulse is applied instantaneously at perigee. The impulse is $J = 1.73 \times 10^5\ \mathrm{kg\ m\ s^{-1}}$. Note that the total mass of MOM must include the fuel, so we have to use $m = 500 + 852 = 1352\ \mathrm{kg}$. **[1.0]**

Change in velocity due to impulse at perigee is

$$\Delta v = \frac{J}{m} = \frac{1.73 \times 10^5}{1352} = 128.0\ \mathrm{m\ s^{-1}}$$ **[1.0]**

The new velocity will be given by (we use $'$ symbol to denote quantities after the first orbit-raising maneuvre)

$$v_{\mathrm{p}}' = v_{\mathrm{p}} + \Delta v = 10.06\ \mathrm{km\ s^{-1}}$$ **[1.0]**

The perigee remains unchanged. So we get $r_{\mathrm{p}}' = r_{\mathrm{p}}$.

Since the satellite is moving faster, the new apogee will be higher.

$$v_{\mathrm{p}}' = \sqrt{2GM_{\oplus}\frac{r_{\mathrm{a}}'}{r_{\mathrm{p}}'(r_{\mathrm{a}}' + r_{\mathrm{p}}')}}$$

$$\therefore\ 1 + \frac{r_{\mathrm{p}}'}{r_{\mathrm{a}}'} = \frac{2GM_{\oplus}}{(v_{\mathrm{p}}')^2 \times r_{\mathrm{p}}'}$$ **[2.0]**

$$= \frac{2 \times 6.674 \times 10^{-11} \times 5.972 \times 10^{24}}{(10.06 \times 10^3)^2 \times 6.635 \times 10^6} = 1.188$$

$$r_{\mathrm{a}}' = \frac{6635}{0.188} = 35\,380\ \mathrm{km}$$ **[2.0]**

$$h_{\mathrm{a}} = 35380 - 6371 = \boxed{29\,009\ \mathrm{km}}$$ **[1.0]**

**Acceptable range:** ±150 km

**(T9.2) Find eccentricity ($e$) of the new orbit after the burn and new orbital period ($P$) of MOM in hours.**

Solution:

As seen above,

$$\frac{r_{\mathrm{p}}'}{r_{\mathrm{a}}'} = 0.188 = \frac{1 - e}{1 + e}$$ **[1.0]**

$$\therefore\ e = \frac{1 - 0.188}{1 + 0.188} = \boxed{0.683}$$ **[1.0]**

**Acceptable range:** ±0.002

The new orbital semi-major axis and orbital period will be,

$$a' = \frac{r_{\mathrm{a}}' + r_{\mathrm{p}}'}{2}$$ **[1.0]**

$$= \frac{35380 + 6635}{2} = 20\,933\ \mathrm{km}$$ **[1.0]**

$$P = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{a'^3}{GM_{\oplus}}}$$ **[1.0]**

$$= 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{(2.0933 \times 10^7)^3}{6.674 \times 10^{-11} \times 5.972 \times 10^{24}}} = \boxed{30\,136\ \mathrm{s} = 8.37\ \mathrm{h}}$$ **[1.0]**

**Acceptable range:** ±0.1 h


</Spoiler>
