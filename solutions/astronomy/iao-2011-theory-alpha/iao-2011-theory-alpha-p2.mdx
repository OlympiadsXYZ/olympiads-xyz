## Условие

Classical devices "planetaria" are arranged so that each group of stars is projected on a dome by a small optical system. Foils with hole-stars of the corresponding sizes are often used as these "slides" of constellations which are projected on the dome, so that most of the light is blocked by the foil (the black sky appears), and only light through the holes are transmitted (so stars appear). For example, images of $0^{\mathrm{m}}$ stars on the foil have the size $l_0 = 0.1\ \mathrm{mm}$, and stars up to $6^{\mathrm{m}}$ are demonstrated, the focal length of the projecting system is $f = 25\ \mathrm{cm}$, and the device has 16 separate projecting systems for every hemisphere. The dome of the planetarium of the observatory “Bobek” has a diameter of $2R = 10\ \mathrm{m}$.

Let's suppose that all slides have been removed for replacement by more perfect ones, and the whole light began to be projected on the dome. What would the total stellar magnitude of the illuminated dome be (the artificial gray sky)? Would it be possible to read a newspaper in such an illuminance?

The answer has to include a list of the necessary parameters with formulae and numerical values.
