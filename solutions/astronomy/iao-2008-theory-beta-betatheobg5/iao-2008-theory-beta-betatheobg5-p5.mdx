## Условие

Теорията за разширяващата се Вселена понастоящем е най-разпространената космологическа теория. Без каквито и да е съмнения към тази теория се придържат над 85% от астрофизиците. Съществуват обаче и алтернативни теории. В една такава теория се предполага, че Вселената е стационарна, а космологичното червено отместване се дължи не на ефекта на Доплер, а на „стареенето“ на фотоните, т.е. енергията на всеки фотон намалява с времето по следния закон: $E = E_0 \cdot 2^{-t/T_0}$, където $E_0$ е първоначалната енергия на фотона (в момента на неговата поява), $t$ е времето, изминало от момента на неговата поява, $T_0$ е т. нар. период на полуразпад на фотона, аналогичен на времето за полуразпадане в ядрената физика. Оценете на колко е равен периодът на полуразпад на фотона $T_0$ (в години) в модела на „стареенето“ на фотоните, който би съответствал на наблюдателните астрономически данни.

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- Official key: order 10¹⁰ years; classical direct estimate 14 × 10⁹ years, relativistic direct estimate 8.4 × 10⁹ years, derivative method T₀ = ln 2/H ≈ 9.7 × 10⁹ years.

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

Официални скици на решенията, на оригиналния английски език.

<ProblemSection id="solution-classical">

### Simplified direct solution — classical Doppler effect

The energy of every photon becomes smaller according to the law $E=E_0\cdot2^{-t/T_0}$, which means that after a time $t=T_0$ the energy becomes smaller by a factor 2. $E_1=E_0/2$, $h\nu_1=h\nu_0/2$, $hc/\lambda_1=(hc/\lambda_0)/2$, so $\lambda_1=2\lambda_0$. The theory of an expanding Universe explains it by terms of the Doppler effect due to that the source of the photon is receding from us with the velocity $v$.

There are two ways of solution presented later: in the classical theory and in the special theory of relativity. The solution according to the special theory of relativity is better but not all students know the formula of this theory (*1 pt difference recommended in the evaluation for Group β, no difference for Group α*).

According to the classical Doppler effect,

$$
\lambda_1=\lambda_0(1+v/c),
$$

so

$$
2\lambda_0=\lambda_0(1+v/c),\qquad v/c=1,\qquad v=c.
$$

In other words the red shift

$$
z=1.
$$

According to the Hubble law

$$
v=H\cdot R,\qquad R=v/H.
$$

Since $v=c$,

$$
R=c/H.
$$

The time passed since the emission of the photon is $t=R/c$, so

$$
t=R/c=c/H/c=1/H.
$$

So

$$
T_0=t=(1/70)\cdot\mathrm{s\cdot Mpc/km}=(1/70)\cdot\mathrm s\cdot3.26\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{years}\cdot3\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{km/s/km}\approx14\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{years}.
$$

In the theory of an expanding Universe this period is equal to cosmological age of the Universe.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-relativistic">

### Simplified direct solution — special relativity

According to the Doppler effect in special theory of relativity the red shift is also $z=1$ but

$$
\lambda_2=\lambda_0(1+v/c)/(1-(v/c)^2)^{1/2}.
$$

So

$$
2\lambda_0=\lambda_0(1+v/c)/(1-(v/c)^2)^{1/2},
$$
$$
2(1-(v/c)^2)^{1/2}=(1+v/c),
$$
$$
4(1-(v/c)^2)=(1+v/c)^2,
$$
$$
4(1-v/c)(1+v/c)=(1+v/c)^2,
$$
$$
4(1-v/c)=(1+v/c),
$$
$$
v/c=0.6.
$$

According to the Hubble law

$$
v=H\cdot R,\qquad R=v/H.
$$

Since $v=0.6c$,

$$
R=0.6c/H.
$$

The time passed since the emission of the photon is $t=R/c$, so

$$
t=R/c=0.6c/H/c=0.6/H.
$$

So

$$
T_0=t=0.6(1/70)\cdot\mathrm{s\cdot Mpc/km}=0.6(1/70)\cdot\mathrm s\cdot3.26\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{years}\cdot3\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{km/s/km}\approx8.4\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{years}.
$$

In the theory of an expanding Universe this time is roughly the cosmological age of the Universe.

Answer: in both cases (the classical theory or the special theory of relativity) the parameter of the so-called half-decay period of a photon is about $10^{10}$ (10 billion) years that is of the order of the cosmological age of the Universe according to the theory of an expanding Universe.



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-derivatives">

### Solution with derivatives and jury notes

This method is more precise than the “direct solution” but the math apparatus is not possible for most students.

For small values, if $\Delta\lambda\ll\lambda_0$ using derivatives of parts of equation ($E=E_0\cdot2^{-t/T_0}$) one can find:

$$
dE=d(E_0\cdot2^{-t/T_0})=dt\cdot(-\ln2/T_0)\cdot(E_0\cdot2^{-t/T_0})=dt\cdot(-\ln2/T_0)\cdot E,
$$
$$
dE/E=-\ln2\cdot dt/T_0.
$$

Using a small time $t$ in comparison with $T_0$ we may write

$$
\Delta E/E=-\ln2\cdot t/T_0.
$$

Since

$$
\Delta E/E=-\Delta\lambda/\lambda=-v/c,
$$
$$
-v/c=-\ln2\cdot t/T_0.
$$

According to the Hubble law

$$
v=H\cdot R,
$$

so

$$
H\cdot R/c=\ln2\cdot t/T_0.
$$

Since the time passed since the emission of the photon is $t=R/c$,

finally we have

$$
T_0=\ln2/H.
$$

So

$$
T_0=\ln2\cdot(1/70)\cdot\mathrm{s\cdot Mpc/km}=0.69\cdot(1/70)\cdot\mathrm s\cdot3.26\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{years}\cdot3\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{km/s/km}=9.7\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{years}\approx10\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{years}.
$$

In the theory of an expanding Universe this period is about cosmological age of the Universe.

Answer: the parameter of the so-called half-decay period of a photon is about $10^{10}$ (10 billion) years that is of order of cosmological age of the Universe according to the theory of expanding Universe.

Note: students of group β should take the Hubble constant $H_0$ themselves. It may be in the range from 50 till 100 km/s/Mpc. The answers for these values will appear $\sqrt2$ larger or $\sqrt2$ smaller.

Note: the results are depend on the objects (or times) that we use as references (like for the nearest universe (solution with derivatives) or objects with the red shift 1). Nevertheless it does mean that “the situation is impossible”. It means only that at least one of the formulae (formula of Hubble $v=HR$ or formula of aging of photons with a “half-decay” $E=E_0\cdot2^{-t/T_0}$) is not coincide reality.

</ProblemSection>
