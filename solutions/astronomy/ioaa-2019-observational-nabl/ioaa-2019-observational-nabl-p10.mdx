## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

Planetarium round — problems, solutions, marking

<ProblemSection id="solution-answer-3-1">

### 3.1.

Autumn (fall). The Moon, Earth and Sun are on the same line. From Earth the Sun is in Virgo, next to the autumnal equinox point. **Marking:** Autumn: 2 points. **Maximum: 2 points.**

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-answer-3-2">

### 3.2.

598.8 (approximately 599) million km. Juno–Sun–Earth–Moon are nearly on the same line, so the distance is 3 AU + 1 AU + d(Earth–Moon) = (4 × 149.6) + 0.4 million km = 598.8 million km. **Marking:** within ±1 million km: 2 points; within ±2 million km: 1 point. **Maximum: 2 points.** The solution question asks for rounding to 0.1 million km; the question edition above asks for the nearest integer.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-answer-3-3">

### 3.3.

**Solution 1:** Sunset is about 3–4 terrestrial mean solar days later. The lunar day equals the synodic month: 29.53 days (708.72 hours). The Sun moves 360°/29.53 = 12.2° per day from East to West. It is 40° above the horizon and we are exactly on the equator, so setting takes 40°/12.2° = 3.28 days = 78.7 hours. The solar disc adds half an hour until complete sunset. **Marking:** 3–4 days: 4 points; 2 or 5 days: 2 points. **Maximum: 4 points.**

**Solution 2:** Earth never sets. The Earth–Moon system has synchronous rotation. The observing site is on the near side, outside the libration zone; Earth is continuously observable. **Marking:** 2 points. **Maximum: 2 points.**

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-answer-3-4">

### 3.4.

**Solution 1:** Latitude 0°, longitude 50° E. At the origin of selenographic coordinates the solar eclipse by Earth would be at the zenith. Here it is almost exactly West at altitude 40°, so we are on the lunar equator about 50° East of the origin.

**Marking 1:** Latitude within ±2° (S 2°–N 2°): 3 points; within ±5° (S 3–5° or N 3–5°): 2 points. Longitude within ±2° (48–52°): 3 points; within ±5° (45–47° or 53–55°): 2 points. **Maximum: 6 points.**

**Solution 2:** Mare Fecunditatis, Sea of Fecundity, or Sea of Fertility. **Marking 2:** any of these names: 6 points. **Maximum: 6 points.**

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-answer-3-5">

### 3.5.

About 800 km. This is a tribute to the Apollo-11 astronauts on the 50th anniversary of the first lunar landing, a few days before IOAA2019. Disregard the latitudinal difference (less than 1°), and consider both sites on the equator, a great circle. Its circumference is D_Moon × π = 3474 km × 3.14 = 10914 km, corresponding to 360°. The longitudinal difference is 50° − 23.473° = 26.527°, giving an arc length of 803.7 km. The solution prints 23.473° here; the question prints 23.4333°.

**Marking:** ±100 km (700–900): 6 points; ±200 km (600–699 or 901–1000): 5 points; ±300 km (500–599 or 1001–1100): 4 points; ±400 km (400–499 or 1101–1200): 3 points. **Maximum: 6 points.**
