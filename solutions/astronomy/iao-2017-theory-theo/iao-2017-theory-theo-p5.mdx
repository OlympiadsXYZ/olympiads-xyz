## Условие

There is a system, consisting of a plane mirror (100% reflection) and a plane glass plate that transmits K% of the light and reflects the other (100-K)% (this characteristics is the same for the light coming from both the directions). The mirror and plate are parallel and infinite on their right side. An observer is placed at the point O (marked by *) and sees a star with a magnitude $2^{\mathrm m}$ within a cone around direction «A». What magnitude(s) star(s) (if any) would the observer see in a cone near direction «B» that is symmetrical to «A»? Calculate numerical value for K = 50% and K = 5% for each such a star (if any visible) or explain why no stars are visible.

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

The observer sees the combined light from parallel rays A1, A2, A3, A4,.. in the direction B. Every beam AN once passed through the plate (losing K%), then successively reflected from the mirror (without losing intensity) and from plates (each time losing (100-K)% of the light), with N reflections from mirrors and N-1 reflections from the plate.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '69.7%', '--fig-max': '446px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/iao-2017-theory-theo/mirror-solution.png" alt="Official reflected-ray diagram" width="1394" height="486" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


As the star is at a very large distance, all the rays AN are coming from the mentioned star $2^{\mathrm m}$. Therefore, the observer sees in the direction “B” a single star, the intensity of which we need to calculate as the sum of the series:
$$
P_0k+P_0(1-k)k+P_0(1-k)^2k+P_0(1-k)^3k+P_0(1-k)^4k+P_0(1-k)^5k+\ldots
$$
where $P_0$ is the flow of light from the star $2^{\mathrm m}$, and k is the fraction of the transmitted light from the plate (i.e., k = K/100).

The sum of the series $1+(1-k)+(1-k)^2+(1-k)^3+(1-k)^4+(1-k)^5+\ldots$ is equal to 1/k. Accordingly, the sum of our series is
$$
P_0k(1+(1-k)+(1-k)^2+(1-k)^3+(1-k)^4+(1-k)^5+\ldots)=P_0k\,1/k=P_0.
$$
Paradoxically, the fact! Regardless of the value of K the observer would see the only one star in the direction “B”, and its magnitude is $2^{\mathrm m}$.

So the numerical answer in the both cases: $2^{\mathrm m}$.
