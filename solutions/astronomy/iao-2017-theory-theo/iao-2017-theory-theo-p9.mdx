The low-resolution spectrum of a galaxy: NGC 7479 ($\alpha=23^{\mathrm h}04^{\mathrm m}57^{\mathrm s}$, $\delta=+12^\circ19'22''$) was observed by the 2.16 m telescope of Xinglong Station near Beijing, National Astronomical Observatories of China, on September 14, 2009. The red part of this spectrum is shown in Fig.β5. (see separate sheet). The emission lines in the part of the spectrum are (from left to right): [NII]a, Hα, [NII]b.

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-1">

### 5.1. Method of solution 1, without explanations

$$
S=\alpha=23^{\mathrm h}04^{\mathrm m}57^{\mathrm s}=346.238^\circ=S_0+(T_c-8^{\mathrm h})\times(1+1/365.2422)+117.575^\circ,
$$
$$
S_0\approx-8^{\mathrm d}=-8^{\mathrm d}\times(360^\circ/365.2422^{\mathrm d})=-7.9^\circ
$$
$$
\Rightarrow T_c=-123.5^\circ+120^\circ=-3.5^\circ=-0.23^{\mathrm h}\ \to\ 23.77^{\mathrm h}=23^{\mathrm h}46^{\mathrm m}.
$$
Answer: 23:46.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-2">

### 5.1. Method of solution 2, with explanations

The upper culmination of a celestial body occurs when its sidereal time equals to its right ascension. It is ≈ 23ʰ05ᵐ for NGC 7479. Sidereal time is equal to mean solar time at the date of autumnal equinox, and then begins to outstrip it with speed 1ᵈ/365.2422 per day (about 3ᵐ56ˢ per day).

*Note. Strictly speaking, the sidereal time is equal to mean solar time about two days earlier, but we are not going to account for this subtle effect.*

8 days to be passed till September 22/23 (autumnal equinox) since September 14/15 (the observation was made). The difference between mean solar time and sidereal time at the night of observations is:
$$
24^{\mathrm h}00^{\mathrm m}\times-8^{\mathrm d}/365.2422^{\mathrm d}=-31.5^{\mathrm m}
$$
So on September 14/15 sidereal time 23ʰ05ᵐ corresponds to
$$
23^{\mathrm h}05^{\mathrm m}-(-31.5^{\mathrm m})=23^{\mathrm h}36.5^{\mathrm m}\quad\text{of mean solar time.}
$$
Xinglong Station is located in 2.425° West from the meridian of 120° (base meridian for Beijing time UT+08), so the mean solar time here behind the Beijing time by $2.425^\circ\times4^{\mathrm{m}/\circ}=9.7^{\mathrm m}\approx10^{\mathrm m}$. So to get answer according to time UT+08 we have to add these 9.7ᵐ ≈ 10ᵐ, and we have
$$
23^{\mathrm h}36.5^{\mathrm m}+0^{\mathrm h}09.7^{\mathrm m}=23^{\mathrm h}46.2^{\mathrm m}\approx23^{\mathrm h}46^{\mathrm m}\quad\text{Beijing time.}
$$
Answer: 23:46.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-3">

### 5.2.

Maximum of the Hα emission line is observed at λobs = 6614 Å, while its laboratory wavelength is known as λ₀ = 6563 Å. So the redshift of NGC 7479 is
$$
z=(\lambda_\mathrm{obs}-\lambda_0)/\lambda_0=(6614\ \text{Å}-6563\ \text{Å})/6563\ \text{Å}=51\ \text{Å}/6563\ \text{Å}\approx0.0078.
$$
*Note. Real value from literature is z = 0.007935 ± 0.000017.*

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-4">

### 5.3.

$$
D=v/H_0=zc/H_0=0.0078\times300000\ \mathrm{km/s}/68\ \mathrm{Mpc/(km/s)}\approx34\ \mathrm{Mpc}.
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-5">

### 5.4.

Measured from the spectrum, FWHM(Hα) = 10 Å, so in units of km/s,
$$
\mathrm{FWHM}(\mathrm H\alpha)=10\ \text{Å}/6563\ \text{Å}\times300000\ \mathrm{km/s}\approx460\ \mathrm{km/s}.
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-6">

### 5.5.

Type = Seyfert-II.

</ProblemSection>
