<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-1">

### 5.1. Method of solution 1, without explanations

$$
S=\alpha=13^{\mathrm h}29^{\mathrm m}56^{\mathrm s}=202.483^\circ=S_0+(T_c-8^{\mathrm h})\times(1+1/365.2422)+122.05^\circ,
$$
$$
S_0(^{\mathrm d})\approx93^{\mathrm d},\quad S_0(^\circ)=93^{\mathrm d}\times(360^\circ/365.2422^{\mathrm d})=91.67^\circ
$$
$$
\Rightarrow T_c=-11.2^\circ+120^\circ=108.8^\circ=7.25^{\mathrm h}=07^{\mathrm h}15^{\mathrm m}.
$$
Answer: 07:15.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-2">

### 5.1. Method of solution 2, with explanations

The upper culmination of a celestial body occurs when its sidereal time equals to its right ascension. It is ≈ 13ʰ30ᵐ for NGC 5194. Sidereal time is equal to mean solar time at the date of autumnal equinox, and then begins to outstrip it with speed 1ᵈ/365.2422 per day (about 3ᵐ56ˢ per day).

*Note. Strictly speaking, the sidereal time is equal to mean solar time about two days earlier, but we are not going to account for this subtle effect.*

93 days passed since September 22/23 (autumnal equinox) till December 24/25 (the observation was made). The difference between mean solar time and sidereal time at the night of observations is:
$$
24^{\mathrm h}00^{\mathrm m}\times93^{\mathrm d}/365.2422^{\mathrm d}=6^{\mathrm h}07^{\mathrm m}
$$
So on December 24/25 sidereal time 13ʰ30ᵐ corresponds to
$$
13^{\mathrm h}30^{\mathrm m}-6^{\mathrm h}07^{\mathrm m}=7^{\mathrm h}23^{\mathrm m}\quad\text{of mean solar time.}
$$
Weihai is located in 2.05° East from the meridian of 120° (base meridian for Beijing time UT+08), so the mean solar time here ahead the Beijing time by $2.05^\circ\times4^{\mathrm{m}/\circ}=8.2^{\mathrm m}\approx8^{\mathrm m}$. So to get answer according to time UT+08 we have to subtract these 8ᵐ, and we have
$$
7^{\mathrm h}23^{\mathrm m}-0^{\mathrm h}08^{\mathrm m}=7^{\mathrm h}15^{\mathrm m}\quad\text{Beijing time.}
$$
Answer: 07:15.

</ProblemSection>
