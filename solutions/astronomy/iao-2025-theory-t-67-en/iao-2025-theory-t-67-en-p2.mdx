## Условие

The wavelengths $\lambda$ of the spectral lines of hydrogen obey the formula:

$$
\frac{1}{\lambda} = R\left(\frac{1}{m^2} - \frac{1}{n^2}\right),
$$

where $R = const$, $m$ and $n$ are natural numbers, and $n > m$. Lines with the same $m$ are combined in series. The most famous line is Hα, the first line (i.e., the line with the longest wavelength) in the Balmer series ($m=2$).

Research of a distant galaxy showed us that the Hα line in its spectrum is observed at a wavelength of about the first line of the Brackett series ($m=4$). Roughly (with an accuracy of 10%) estimate:

**7.1.** The receding velocity of the galaxy. **[5 т.]**

**7.2.** The distance to the galaxy. **[3 т.]**

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-redshift">

### Redshift and approximate answers

From the formula

$$
\frac1\lambda=R\left(\frac1{m^2}-\frac1{n^2}\right)
$$

it is easy to understand that the first line (that is, the line with the longest wavelength) in each series is the line for which $n=m+1$. Therefore, for the ratio between the inverse wavelengths for Hα and the first line of the Brackett series, we can write:

$$
\frac1{\lambda_\alpha}\bigg/\frac1{\lambda_B}=\left(\frac1{2^2}-\frac1{3^2}\right)\bigg/\left(\frac1{4^2}-\frac1{5^2}\right),
$$

$$
\lambda_B/\lambda_\alpha=\left(\frac{9-4}{36}\right)\bigg/\left(\frac{25-16}{400}\right)=\frac{500}{81}.
$$

The wavelength $\lambda_B$ itself may not even be calculated, as it is possible not to know the wavelength $\lambda_\alpha$. The redshift for this galaxy is

$$
z=\left(\frac\lambda{\lambda_0}-1\right)=\frac{419}{81}\approx5.17.
$$

If we use the Doppler formula from classical mechanics ($v/c=(\lambda-\lambda_0)/\lambda_0$), we get the galaxy receding with a velocity of $z\cdot c=5.17c$, greater than the speed of light. Therefore, since the redshift is large, we need to use the formulas of special relativity for an accurate answer. But to solve the problem as formulated (with an accuracy of 10%), it is not necessary to know these formulas, it is enough to realize that the relative velocity of the galaxy is close to the speed of light, i.e. it is moving away from us at a speed of about **300000 km/s** (answer to 7.1.), and the distance to it can be found from the Hubble formula $v=H\cdot R$. It is about

$$
R=c/H=\frac{300000\,\mathrm{km/s}}{68\,\mathrm{km/s/Mpc}}\approx4400\,\mathrm{Mpc}\approx4.4\,\mathrm{Gpc}\quad\text{(answer to 7.2.)}.
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-exact">

### Note: exact solution as supplied

The exact solution (not required by the condition of the problem):

Redshift

$$
z=(\lambda-\lambda_0)/\lambda_0=(40525\,\text{Å}-6563\,\text{Å})/6563\,\text{Å}=5.17.
$$

$$
z+1=(1+v/c)/(1-v^2/c^2)^{1/2},
$$

$$
v/c=\frac{(z+1)^2-1}{(z+1)^2+1}.
$$

Then for $z=5.17$ obtain

$$
v=0.949c\approx285000\,\mathrm{km/s}\quad\text{(answer to 7.1.)}.
$$

According to the Hubble formula ($v=H\cdot R$), we obtain

$$
R=v/H=\frac{285000\,\mathrm{km/s}}{68\,\mathrm{km/s/Mpc}}\approx4190\,\mathrm{Mpc}\approx4.2\,\mathrm{Gpc}\quad\text{(answer to 7.2.)}.
$$

*Transcription note: “exact solution” is the supplied sketch's label. Its special-relativistic velocity and linear Hubble-law distance are reproduced as the official approach, not independently replaced with a cosmological distance model.*

</ProblemSection>
