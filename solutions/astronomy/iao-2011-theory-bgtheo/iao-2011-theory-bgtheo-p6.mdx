<ProblemSection id="solution-solar-mass-loss">

### Annual fractional mass loss

$$
E=4\pi R^2\cdot A\cdot\tau,\qquad E=mc^2,\qquad X=(m/M)\cdot100\%.
$$

$R$ — Earth–Sun distance, $R=149600000000\,\mathrm m$.

$A$ — Solar constant, $A=1367\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$.

$\tau=1\text{ year}=3.16\cdot10^7\,\mathrm s$.

$c$ — speed of light, $c=299800000\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$M$ — mass of the Sun, $M=1.989\cdot10^{-30}\,\mathrm{kg}$.

$$
X=(4\pi R^2\cdot A\cdot\tau/c^2/M)\cdot100\%.
$$

$$
X=(4\pi A\tau(R/c)^2/M)\cdot100\%.
$$

$$
X=(0.068\cdot10^{-12})\cdot100\%=6.8\cdot10^{-12}\%.
$$

*Transcription note: the source prints $10^{-30}$ in the solar mass, whereas its included question-data table gives $1989000\times10^{24}\,\mathrm{kg}$. The source's exponent and final answer are retained as printed.*

</ProblemSection>
