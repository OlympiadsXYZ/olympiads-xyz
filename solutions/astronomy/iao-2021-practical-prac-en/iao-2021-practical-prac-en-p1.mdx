Assuming that the orbits of Earth and Mars are circular, picture 12.a shows such orbits to scale; positions of the Sun and of the Earth and Mars when Mars is at opposition are also represented.

In table 12.b you will report distance from Earth and angular data for Mars:

- Column 1: day of observation (in the interval ±105 days from time of opposition $t_0$).
- Column 2: distance to Mars from Earth, to be given in km.
- Column 3: position angle $\alpha$ of Mars, to be given in degrees.
- Column 4: day/days in which Mars is in quadrature with the Sun as seen from Earth (to be indicated by the symbol Q) or it appears “stationing” (to be indicated by the symbol S).

<ProblemSection id="positions">

### 12.1

**12.1** Taking into account the sidereal periods of the Earth and Mars with a good approximation and starting from the initial configuration where Mars is in opposition (i.e. along the up-down direction the orbit representation), compute the positions of the two planets every 15 days for a time interval of ±105 days and draw them on picture 12.a.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="measurements">

### 12.2–12.4

In picture 12.a draw the lines connecting the positions of Earth and Mars at the same epoch and, for each epoch, measure:

**12.2** the Earth-Mars distance $D$ and report it, in km, in column 2 of table 12.b,

**12.3** the position angle $\alpha$ of Mars as seen from the Earth with reference to the up-down direction (i.e. line connection Earth and Mars when the Mars is at opposition) and report it, in degrees, in column 3 of table 12.b.

**12.4** Infer in which of the positions of Earth and Mars at the same epoch you have drawn Mars would be in quadrature with the Sun and mark them (one, two or more) with the symbol Q in column 4 of table 12.b.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="retrograde">

### 12.5–12.6

In picture 12.c report the distance (in au, Y axis) Mars-Earth $D$ as a function of the position angle $\alpha$ (in degrees, X axis).

**12.5** Infer the days (one, two or more with the precision allowed by the data) when Mars appears stationing in the sky during the considered time interval (±105 days) and mark them with the symbol S in column 4 of table 12.b.

**12.6** Provide an estimation of the duration of the apparent retrograde motion of Mars $T(\mathrm{retrograde})$ and write it, in days, in the box at the bottom of picture 12.c.

</ProblemSection>

### 12.2–12.4

**12.2** By measuring in the graph the Earth-Mars distance $D$, it then can be converted to km considering that the Sun-Eart distance is equal to 1 AU

**12.3** Once the position of the two planets every 15 days are known, the angle $\alpha$ of Mars as seen from the Earth with reference to the up-down direction can be measured (for clarity only the value for $t_0-105^{\mathrm d}$ is shown in picture 12.a)

**12.4** Looking at the positions of Earth and Mars at the same epoch, Mars would be in quadrature (or nearby) for $t_0-105^{\mathrm d}$ and for $t_0+105^{\mathrm d}$

</ProblemSection>
