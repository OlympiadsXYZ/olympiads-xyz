## Условие

The astronomical refraction correction, $\rho_{\mathrm{r}}$, has the minimum value, $\rho_{\mathrm{r,min}} = 0$, when the star is at the zenith of the Earth's surface observer $\left(z = 0; h = 90^{0}\right)$, and has the maximum value, $\rho_{\mathrm{r,max}}$, when the star, whose light passes through the Earth's atmosphere, in order to reach the Earth's surface observer, is at the horizon $\left(z = 90^{0}; h = 0\right)$, that is, when the star rises or sets.

It is known, that for an observer, atmospheric refraction raises the Sun in the moments of sunrise and sunset, that is, when it is below the horizon, approximately at its edge, by an apparent disk.

It is given: the radius of the Sun, $R_S = 6.96 \cdot 10^{5}\ km$ the distance between the centre of the Earth and the centre of the Sun, $d = 15 \cdot 10^{7}\ km$.

The value of $\rho_{r,max}$ ,is:

a) $\rho_{r,max} \approx 23.7'$

b) $\rho_{r,max} \approx 43.7'$

c) $\rho_{r,max} \approx 53.7'$

d) $\rho_{r,max} \approx 33.7'$

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- d)

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

Atmospheric refraction raises the Sun above its horizon, at its limit, at sunrise and sunset, approximately, with an apparent disk, as shown in the drawing in Figure 1.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '83.3%', '--fig-max': '534px'}}>

<figcaption>Fig. 1</figcaption>
</figure>


Therefore, in reality, the rising of the upper arc of the Sun's disk begins after the moment when we already see the entire solar disk above the horizon. We see the sunrise and sunset, later than they actually occur, which makes the day a little longer.

I mean, I see the disk of the whole Sun above the horizon, at its limit, but the Sun has not yet risen, it is still below the horizon !

I mean, the sun went down, it went down below the horizon, at the edge of it, but I still see the whole disk of it above the horizon !

Under these conditions, the astronomical refraction has the maximum value :

$$
\rho_{\mathrm{r,max}}=\theta_S;
$$

$$
\sin\frac{\theta_S}2=\frac{R_S}d\approx\frac{\theta_S}2;
$$

$$
\theta_S=\frac{2R_S}d=\frac{2\cdot6.96\cdot10^5\ \mathrm{km}}{15\cdot10^7\ \mathrm{km}}=0.0098\ \text{radian};
$$

$$
1\ \mathrm{rad}=\frac{180\cdot60'}{3.14};
$$

$$
\theta_S=0.0098\cdot\frac{180\cdot60'}{3.14}\approx33.7'=\rho_{\mathrm{r,max}},
$$

which proves that the value of the maximum astronomical refraction correction, $\rho_{\mathrm{r,max}}$, is approximately equal to the apparent angular diameter of the Sun, $\theta_S$.


</Spoiler>
