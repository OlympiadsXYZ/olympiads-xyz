## Условие

A radiotelescope RT-22 (CrAO) has a mirror dish with a diameter of 22 m and focal length $F = 9.5$ m. They are going to observe the Sun in the first sidelobe of the radiation pattern of the telescope (see Figure 1 on page 1) at a frequency of 420 MHz. A receiver is located at the primary (main) focus of the mirror. Consider the mirror to be spherical. Determine the distance between the center of the mirror and the center of the receiver’s shadow on the dish.

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '100%', '--fig-max': '652px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/owao-2024-theory-t/p9-sol-fig1.png" alt="The same antenna radiation-pattern drawing as Figure 1 of the problems: dish antenna with main lobe, first and second sidelobes, labelled 'Main lobe', 'First sidelobe', 'Second sidelobe' and 'Nulls'." width="2037" height="990" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Figure 7: Radiation pattern lobes (beams) of an antenna</figcaption>
</figure>

An image in the focal plane of the dish is determined by the diffraction pattern on a circular aperture (Figure 8). First, we estimate the angular distance of the peak of the first sidelobe relative to the axis of symmetry of the system. Note that to do this, we need to determine the position of the first maximum of the diffraction pattern outside the Airy disc. Recall that the angular radius of the Airy disc is $1.22\lambda/D$, where $D$ is a diameter of the dish and $\lambda$ is the wavelength of light. Further, the distances between the minima are about $\lambda/D$, so the angular distance between the peak of the main lobe (the center of the image) and the peak of the first sidelobe is about $1.22\lambda/D + 0.5\lambda/D \approx 1.7\lambda/D$.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '42.8%', '--fig-max': '274px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/owao-2024-theory-t/p9-sol-fig2.png" alt="Grey-scale image of an Airy diffraction pattern: a bright central disc surrounded by alternating dark and faint bright rings." width="857" height="871" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Figure 8: Airy pattern</figcaption>
</figure>

To estimate the angular distance, we calculate the wavelength at which the observations are carried out:

$$\lambda = \frac{c}{\nu} = \frac{3.00 \cdot 10^8\ \mathrm{m/s}}{420 \cdot 10^6\ \mathrm{Hz}} = 0.714\ \mathrm{m}.$$

Thus,

$$\theta \approx 1.7 \cdot \frac{\lambda}{D} = 1.7 \times \frac{0.714\ \mathrm{m}}{22\ \mathrm{m}} \approx 0.055\ \mathrm{rad} \approx 3.2^{\circ}.$$

Note that the angular diameter of the Sun ($\approx 0.5^{\circ}$) is much smaller than the resolution of the telescope ($\lambda/D \approx 1.9^{\circ}$), so we may consider the Sun to be a point source.

Such an angular distance corresponds to a linear distance of

$$l = F\tan\theta \approx 9.5\ \mathrm{m} \times 0.055\ \mathrm{rad} \approx 0.5\ \mathrm{m}.$$

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '65.2%', '--fig-max': '418px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/owao-2024-theory-t/p9-sol-fig3.png" alt="Cross-section of the spherical mirror (arc at the left) with its focus F: a horizontal line from the 'Mirror’s center' through F to the arrow 'Main lobe', and a line from the 'Receiver’s shadow' point on the mirror through F at angle θ to the arrow '⊙ — First sidelobe'." width="1305" height="689" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

Note that the sphericity of the mirror can be ignored. Possible mirror aberrations are proportional to the third power of the angle between the beam and the main optical axis. Taking into account such effects will affect only the fourth digit in the answer. The accuracy of the data given in the problem does not allow one to discuss such effects. The difference in the distance along the surface of the mirror dish and along the chord can also be neglected due to the smallness of the angle obtained.

**Marking Scheme:**

1. Wavelength $\lambda$ — **2 pt.**
2. Resolution estimation using the formula $\propto \lambda/D$ — **3 pt.**
3. Coefficient in the formula for the angular distance with justification — **6 pt.**
4. Linear distance based on angular distance — **4 pt.**
5. Rationale for not taking into account the sphericity of the mirror — **5 pt.**
6. *Bonus criterion.* The final answer MUST NOT contain more than two significant digits. For each extra digit the penalty is $-2$ points.

   *1 pt. is awarded in total for the problem in case of some sane attempt to solve it.*
