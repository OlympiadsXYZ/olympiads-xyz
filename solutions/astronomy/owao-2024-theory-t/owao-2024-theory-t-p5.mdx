## Условие

Let’s consider stars for which it is true at the same time: (i) in their diurnal motion they cross the horizon, and (ii) their upper culmination occurs to the north of the zenith.

**a)** Express the fraction of such stars as a function of latitude $\varphi$. Assume that stars are distributed uniformly over the celestial sphere.

**b)** Determine what is the maximum possible fraction of such stars and at what latitude this maximum is achieved.

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a)** $\eta(\varphi) = \begin{cases} \cos\varphi, & \varphi \leqslant -45^{\circ};\\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sin(45^{\circ} - \varphi), & -45^{\circ} \leqslant \varphi \leqslant 45^{\circ};\\ 0, & \varphi \geqslant 45^{\circ}. \end{cases}$
- **b)** 0.71

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

a) Let us determine the location of suitable stars on the diagram $(\varphi;\ \delta)$. The upper culmination of the star occurs to the north of the zenith if $\delta > \varphi$.

A star does not cross the horizon in two cases: either it is circumpolar or it is non-rising. A star is circumpolar if its lower culmination is above the horizon: $h_{\min} = |\varphi + \delta| - 90^{\circ} \geqslant 0^{\circ} \Longrightarrow |\varphi + \delta| \geqslant 90^{\circ}$. A star is non-rising if its upper culmination is below the horizon: $h_{\max} = 90^{\circ} - |\varphi - \delta| \leqslant 0^{\circ} \Longrightarrow |\varphi - \delta| \geqslant 90^{\circ}$. Considering $\delta > \varphi$, we find 4 different cases:

$$\begin{array}{llcr} \text{Case 1:} & \varphi \leqslant -45^{\circ} & \Longrightarrow & -(90^{\circ} + \varphi) \leqslant \delta \leqslant 90^{\circ} + \varphi;\\ \text{Case 2:} & -45^{\circ} \leqslant \varphi \leqslant 0^{\circ} & \Longrightarrow & \varphi \leqslant \delta \leqslant 90^{\circ} + \varphi;\\ \text{Case 3:} & 0^{\circ} \leqslant \varphi \leqslant 45^{\circ} & \Longrightarrow & \varphi \leqslant \delta \leqslant 90^{\circ} - \varphi;\\ \text{Case 4:} & \varphi \geqslant 45^{\circ} & \Longrightarrow & \delta \in \varnothing. \end{array}$$

The fraction of the area of a sphere enclosed between small circles with declinations from $\delta_{\min}$ to $\delta_{\max}$ can be calculated using the spherical segment area formula $S_{\delta \geqslant \delta_0} = 2\pi R^2(1 - \sin\delta_0)$:

$$\eta = \frac{2\pi R^2(1 - \sin\delta_{\min}) - 2\pi R^2(1 - \sin\delta_{\max})}{4\pi R^2} = \frac{\sin\delta_{\max} - \sin\delta_{\min}}{2}\,.$$

Next, we obtain the fractions for the each case stated above:

$$\begin{array}{ll} \text{Case 1:} & \eta_1 = \dfrac{\sin(90^{\circ} + \varphi) - \sin(-90^{\circ} - \varphi)}{2} = \cos\varphi;\\[2ex] \text{Case 2:} & \eta_2 = \dfrac{\sin(90^{\circ} + \varphi) - \sin\varphi}{2} = \dfrac{\cos\varphi - \sin\varphi}{2} = \dfrac{\sin(45^{\circ} - \varphi)}{\sqrt{2}}\,;\\[2ex] \text{Case 3:} & \eta_3 = \dfrac{\sin(90^{\circ} - \varphi) - \sin\varphi}{2} = \dfrac{\cos\varphi - \sin\varphi}{2} = \dfrac{\sin(45^{\circ} - \varphi)}{\sqrt{2}}\,. \end{array}$$

Finally,

<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/owao-2024-theory-t/p5-sol-fig2.png" alt="Graph of the fraction of stars η (vertical axis, 0 to 0.8) against latitude φ in degrees (horizontal axis, −90 to 90): the curve rises linearly-looking from 0 at −90° to about 0.71 at −45° (marked by a vertical dashed line), then falls to 0 at 45° and stays 0 up to 90°." width="1062" height="716" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

$$\eta(\varphi) = \begin{cases} \cos\varphi, & \varphi \leqslant -45^{\circ};\\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sin(45^{\circ} - \varphi), & -45^{\circ} \leqslant \varphi \leqslant 45^{\circ};\\ 0, & \varphi \geqslant 45^{\circ}. \end{cases}$$

b) Obviously, the maximum of $\eta$ is achieved at $\varphi = -45^{\circ}$ and equals

$$\cos(-45^{\circ}) = \frac{\sin(45^{\circ} - (-45^{\circ}))}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.71.$$
