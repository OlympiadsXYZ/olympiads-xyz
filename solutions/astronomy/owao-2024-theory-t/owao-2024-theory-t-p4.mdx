## Условие

*When the sun rises in the west and sets in the east, when the seas go dry and mountains blow in the wind like leaves... (George R. R. Martin)*

**a)** What should be the minimum eccentricity of the Earth’s orbit so that the Sun sometimes rises in the west? Consider the semi-major axis of the Earth’s orbit and the sidereal day to remain the same as today. Neglect the obliquity of the ecliptic.

**b)** Estimate the maximum surface temperature of the Earth when the Sun rises in the west. Consider the spherical albedo of the Earth to be equal to 0.3.

**c)** Can the Earth have an atmosphere under such conditions? Prove your answer with calculations.

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a)** 0.975
- **b)** 1600 K
- **c)** Yes: the RMS velocity of an air molecule (≈ 1.2 km/s ≈ 0.1 v_II) stays below ~20 % of the escape velocity, so the Earth retains its atmosphere (but the rocks begin to melt).

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

a) The Sun will move in the opposite direction along the celestial sphere if the angular velocity of the planet’s orbital motion around the Sun is greater than the angular velocity of the planet’s rotation.

The maximal angular orbital velocity is reached at the point of perihelion:

$$\omega_{\mathrm{p}} = \frac{v_{\mathrm{p}}}{r_{\mathrm{p}}} = \sqrt{\frac{G\mathfrak{M}_\odot}{a_\oplus} \cdot \frac{1 + e}{1 - e}} \cdot \frac{1}{a_\oplus(1 - e)} = \sqrt{\frac{G\mathfrak{M}_\odot}{a_\oplus^3} \cdot \frac{1 + e}{(1 - e)^3}} = \omega_\oplus^{\mathrm{orb}}\sqrt{\frac{1 + e}{(1 - e)^3}}\,.$$

Here $\omega_\oplus^{\mathrm{orb}} = 360^{\circ}/T_\oplus = 360^{\circ}/365.26^{\mathrm{d}} = 0.986^{\circ}/\mathrm{d}$.

We equate the obtained value to the angular velocity of the Earth’s rotation around its axis $\omega_\oplus^{\mathrm{rot}} = 360^{\circ}/\tau_\oplus = 360^{\circ}/23^{\mathrm{h}}\,56^{\mathrm{m}}\,04^{\mathrm{s}} \approx 361^{\circ}/\mathrm{d}$:

$$361^{\circ} = 0.986^{\circ} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{1 + e}{(1 - e)^3}} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad e = \left(\frac{361}{0.986}\right)^2 \cdot (1 - e)^3 - 1 \qquad (0 \leqslant e < 1).$$

We see an increasing function on the left side of the equation and a decreasing one on the right side. So the equation has only one root. The root can be easily found by brute-force, or one could use the iterative method after rewriting the equation in the form

$$e_{n+1} = 1 - \sqrt[3]{(1 + e_n) \cdot \left(\frac{0.986}{361}\right)^2},$$

in which it converges to $e \approx 0.975$ really quickly from any initial seed value.

b) At perihelion, the distance between the Earth and the Sun is equal to

$$r_p = a_\oplus \cdot (1 - e) = 0.025\ \mathrm{au} = 3.74 \cdot 10^6\ \mathrm{km} \gg R_\odot.$$

We can write the heat balance equation for the Earth at perihelion:

$$\frac{L_\odot}{4\pi r_p^2} \cdot \pi R_\oplus^2 \cdot (1 - A) = 4\pi R_\oplus^2\sigma\Theta^4$$

$$\Longrightarrow\quad \Theta = \sqrt[4]{\frac{L_\odot(1 - A)}{16\pi r_p^2\sigma}} = \sqrt[4]{\frac{3.828 \cdot 10^{26}\ \mathrm{W} \times (1 - 0.3)}{16\pi \times 3.74 \cdot 10^9\ \mathrm{m} \times 5.67 \cdot 10^{-8}\ \mathrm{W/(m^2 \cdot K^4)}}} \approx 1.6 \cdot 10^3\ \mathrm{K}.$$

Here we consider that the Earth radiates as a black body (mainly in the infrared part of the spectrum), while in the optical spectral range it reflects $A = 0.3$ of the incoming energy flux.

c) The root mean square (RMS) velocity of an “average” air molecule (its molar mass is $\mu = 0.029\ \mathrm{kg/mol}$) at this temperature is

$$v = \sqrt{\frac{3\mathfrak{R}\Theta}{\mu}} = \sqrt{\frac{3 \times 8.314\ \mathrm{J/(mol \cdot K)} \times 1.6 \cdot 10^3\ \mathrm{K}}{0.029\ \mathrm{kg/mol}}} \approx 1.2\ \mathrm{km/s} \approx 0.1\,v_{\mathrm{II}}\,,$$

where $v_{\mathrm{II}} = 11.2\ \mathrm{km/s}$ is the escape velocity for the Earth’s surface.

The atmosphere is considered stable if the RMS velocity does not exceed $\sim 20$ % of the escape velocity. Thus, the Earth will retain its atmosphere, but the rocks will begin to melt.
