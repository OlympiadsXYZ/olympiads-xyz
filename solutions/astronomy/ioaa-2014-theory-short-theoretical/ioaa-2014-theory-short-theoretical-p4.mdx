## Условие

An astronaut, with mass $M=100\ \mathrm{kg}$, gets out of the space ship for a repairing mission. He has to repair a satellite at rest relative to the space ship, at about $d=90\ \mathrm{m}$ away from it. After he finishes his job, he realizes that the systems designed to assure his come-back to shuttle are broken. He also observes that he has air only for 3 minutes. He also notices that he possessed a sealed cylindrical can (base section $S=30\ \mathrm{cm^2}$) firmly attached to his/her glove, with $m=200\ \mathrm{g}$ of ice inside. The can is not completely filled with ice.

Determine if the astronaut is able to return safely to the shuttle, before his air reserve is empty, if he manages to open the can in correct direction. Briefly explain your calculations. Note that he cannot throw away anything of its equipment, or touch the satellite.

*You may use the following data:* $T=272\ \mathrm{K}$ / the temperature of the ice in the can, $p_s=550\ \mathrm{Pa}$ - the pressure of the saturated water vapors at the temperature $T=272\ \mathrm{K}$; $R=8300\ \mathrm{J/(kmol\cdot K)}$ - the universal gas constant; $\mu=18\ \mathrm{kg/kmol}$ - the molar mass of the water.

Detailed solution

<ProblemSection id="solution-theoretical-considerations">

### Theoretical considerations:

$$
\Omega=\frac{\Delta N}{\Delta t}=\frac{N/6}{\Delta t}=\frac{N/6}{a/\bar v}=\frac16\frac{N\bar v}{a},
$$

$$
\bar v=\sqrt{\frac{3RT}{\mu}},\quad N=n\cdot a^3,
$$

$$
\Omega=\frac16\frac{n\cdot a^3\bar v}{a}=\frac16n\cdot a^2\cdot\bar v;
$$

$$
a^2=S;\quad\Omega=\frac16n\cdot S\cdot\bar v;
$$

$$
\Phi=\frac{\Delta m}{\Delta t},\quad\Phi=\frac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}=\frac{m_0\Delta N}{\Delta t},
$$

$$
\Phi=m_0\Omega=\frac16m_0nS\bar v;
$$

$$
m_0n=m_0\frac{N}{a^3}=\frac{m_0N}{a^3}=\frac mV=\rho,\quad\rho=\frac{\mu p}{RT},
$$

$$
\Phi=\frac16\rho S\bar v=\frac16\frac{\mu p}{RT}S\sqrt{\frac{3RT}{\mu}}=\frac16pS\sqrt{\frac{3\mu}{RT}}.
$$

**3p**

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-b">

### B. Reasoning

Because the cylindrical can is not full of ice, in the empty part of it there are saturated vapors, i.e the mass flux of the molecule which  sublimate is equal with mass flux of gas which transform into ice.  Thus the pressure in the can is the saturated vapor pressure  $p_s$  and it has the corresponding maximum density  $\rho_s$  See figure 6.2

$$
\Phi_1=\Phi_2.
$$

**2p**

$$
\Phi_1 = \Phi_{sublimation} = \frac{1}{6}\cdot\rho_s\cdot S\cdot\bar{v} = \frac{1}{6}\cdot p_s\cdot S\cdot\sqrt{\frac{3\mu}{RT}};
$$

$$
\Phi_2 = \Phi_{solidification} = \frac{1}{6}\cdot\rho_s\cdot S\cdot\bar{v} = \frac{1}{6}\cdot p_s\cdot S\cdot\sqrt{\frac{3\mu}{RT}};
$$



After the can was opened, there will be no molecules which desublimate thus the mass flux of the molecules which gather the ice become null. So the pressure becomes $\left(p_s/2\right)$. **2p**



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-c">

### C. Calculations according to the reasoning, and/or as support for reasoning

$$
F = \frac{p_s}{2}\cdot S,
$$

$$
a = \frac{F}{M} = \frac{p_s\cdot S}{2M} = \frac{550\ \mathrm{Nm^{-2}}\cdot 30\cdot 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{m^2}}{2\cdot 10^2\ \mathrm{kg}} = 0{,}00825\ \mathrm{ms^{-2}}.
$$

**1p**

The total time of the acceleration movement will be the total time of ice sublimation:

$$
\tau = \frac{m}{\Phi_1} = \frac{m}{\frac{1}{6}\cdot p_s\cdot S\cdot\sqrt{\frac{3\mu}{RT}}} = \frac{6m}{p_s\cdot S}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{RT}{3\mu}} \approx 150\ \mathrm{s}.
$$

**1p**

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-d">

### D. Correct result (1 т.)

The  travel distance in this time will be :

$$
L = \frac{a\tau^2}{2} = \frac{0{,}00825\ \mathrm{ms^{-2}}\cdot 225\cdot 10^2\ \mathrm{s^2}}{2} \approx 93\ \mathrm{m},
$$

The astronaut could arrive safely in at the shuttle if he didn’t lose to much time by solving the problem.

</ProblemSection>
