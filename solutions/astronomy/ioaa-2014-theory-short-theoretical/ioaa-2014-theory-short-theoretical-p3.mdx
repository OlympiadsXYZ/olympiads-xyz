## Условие

During studies concerning cosmic radiation, a neutral unstable particle – the $\pi^0$ meson was identified. The rest-mass of meson $\pi^0$ is much larger than the rest-mass of the electron. The studies reveal that during its flight, the meson $\pi^0$ disintegrates into 2 photons. In a particular case, one of the created photons has the maximum possible energy $E_{\max}$ and, consequently, the other one has the minimum possible energy $E_{\min}$.

Find an expression for the initial velocity of the meson $\pi^0$, as a function of $E_{\max}$ and $E_{\min}$. You may use as known c - the speed of light and the relation between the energy and momentum of any relativistic particles

$$
E^2=p^2c^2+m_0^2c^4.
$$

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- $v=c(E_{\max}-E_{\min})/(E_{\max}+E_{\min})$.

  Result as printed in the supplied final official key.

</Spoiler>

Detailed solution

<ProblemSection id="solution-a">

### (A) 5 points

In the disintegration process the laws of energy conservation and the law of the conservation of momentum are both obeyed.

In the general case the law of conservation of the momentum is represented in the down below figure.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '68.2%', '--fig-max': '437px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/ioaa-2014-theory-short-theoretical/p3-sol-fig1-v3.png" alt="Momentum of the neutral pion and two photon momenta with angles theta1 and theta2." width="1365" height="457" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

the total initial energy of the $\pi^0$ meson is

$$
E^2=p^2c^2+m_0^2c^4
$$

**1p**

And its kinetic energy is

$$
E_c=E-m_0c^2
$$

The expressions of the 2 conservation laws written after the disintegration are:

$$
\vec p=\vec p_1+\vec p_2;
$$

**1p**

$$
E_c+m_0c^2=E_1+E_2,
$$

**1p**

The energy of the photon 1 can be calculated using the notations in the figure

$$
\begin{cases}p_1\sin\theta_1=p_2\sin\theta_2\\p=p_1\cos\theta_1+p_2\cos\theta_2\end{cases};
$$

$$
\frac{E_1}{c}\sin\theta_1=\frac{E_2}{c}\sin\theta_2;
$$

**1p**

$$
p^2c^2=E^2-m_0^2c^4;\quad E=E_1+E_2;
$$

$$
E_1=\frac{m_0^2c^4}{2}\frac1{E_c+m_0c^2-\cos\theta_1\sqrt{E_c(E_c+m_0c^2)}}.
$$

Similar the second photon energy is:

$$
E_2=\frac{m_0^2c^4}{2}\frac1{E_c+m_0c^2-\cos\theta_2\sqrt{E_c(E_c+m_0c^2)}}
$$

**1p**



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-b">

### (B) 4 points

If one of the photon has the maximum possible energy $E_{\max}$ and consequently the other photon has the minimum possible energy $E_{\min}$ the law of momentum conservation is sketched: **2p**

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '54.8%', '--fig-max': '351px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/ioaa-2014-theory-short-theoretical/p3-sol-fig2-v3.png" alt="Collinear photon emission at the maximum and minimum photon energies." width="1097" height="211" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

Thus the relations become very simple:

$$
mv=\frac{m_0v}{\sqrt{1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}}}=\frac{E_{\max}-E_{\min}}c
$$

**1p**

$$
mc^2=\frac{m_0c^2}{\sqrt{1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}}}=E_{\max}+E_{\min}
$$

**1p**

$$
v=c\frac{E_{\max}-E_{\min}}{E_{\max}+E_{\min}}.
$$

**1p**

</ProblemSection>
