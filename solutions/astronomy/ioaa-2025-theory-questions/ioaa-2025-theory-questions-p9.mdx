## Условие

A telescope with an achromatic convex objective lens of diameter $D=15\,\mathrm{cm}$ and focal length $f=200\,\mathrm{cm}$ is pointed to a star at the zenith.

**(T09.1)** Find the diameter (in m), $d_{\mathrm{image}}$, of the image of a point source as produced by the objective lens at its focal plane for green light ($\lambda=550\,\mathrm{nm}$), considering only the effects of diffraction. **[1 т.]**

The image of an astronomical source is also affected by the so-called “atmospheric seeing”.

The boundaries between the layers in the atmosphere as well as the refractive indices of the layers change continuously due to turbulence, temperature variation and other factors. This leads to tiny changes in the position of the image in the focal plane of the telescope, known as the “twinkling effect”. For rest of the problem, apart from the diffraction limited finite size of the image of the star discussed above, no interference effects will be considered.

The left panel of the figure below shows a vertical cross-section of the atmosphere with multiple layers of different refractive indices ($n_1,n_2,n_3,\ldots$). The right panel shows the zoomed in view of a thin vertical segment of the atmosphere and the boundary between the two lowest atmospheric layers of refractive indices $n_1$ and $n_2$ ($n_1>n_2$). We consider only these two layers and their boundary for this problem. The diagrams are not to scale.

### (T09.1)

Angular radius of the diffraction disk is given by $1.22\lambda/D$. The radius of the image is therefore,

$$
d_\mathrm{image}=f\left(\frac{1.22\lambda}D\right)\qquad\text{(0.5)},
$$
$$
\boxed{d_\mathrm{image}=8.95\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}}\qquad\text{(0.5)}.
$$

We accept $f(\lambda/D)=7.33\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}$ (i.e., without the factor of 1.22 in the formula). No other values accepted.

### (T09.2b)

Using Snell’s Law,

$$
n_2\sin\theta=n_1\sin(\theta-\alpha),
$$
$$
\frac{n_2}{n_1}\sin\theta=\sin\theta\cos\alpha-\cos\theta\sin\alpha\qquad\text{(1.0)}.
$$

Using small angle approximation ($\sin\alpha\approx\alpha$ and $\cos\alpha\approx1$)

$$
\frac{n_2}{n_1}\sin\theta=\sin\theta-\alpha\cos\theta,
$$
$$
\boxed{\alpha=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}\left(1-\frac{n_2}{n_1}\right)}\qquad\text{(1.0)}.
$$

### (T09.2c)

The shift in position of image due to change in $\theta$ is:

$$
\Delta x_\theta=f\alpha=f\Delta\tan\theta\left(1-\frac{n_2}{n_1}\right)\qquad\text{(1.0)}
$$
$$
=f\left(1-\frac{n_2}{n_1}\right)\big(\tan(\theta+\Delta\theta)-\tan\theta\big)
$$
$$
=f\left(1-\frac{n_2}{n_1}\right)\sec^2\theta\Delta\theta\qquad\text{(1.0)}
$$
$$
=f\times(6.98\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{rad}),
$$
$$
\boxed{\Delta x_\theta=1.40\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m}}\qquad\text{(1.0)}.
$$

Wrong sign will have penalty of 1.0 mark.

### (T09.2d)

Shift in the position of image due to the change in $n_2$ is:

$$
\Delta x_n=f\tan\theta\left(-\frac{n_2+\Delta n_2}{n_1}+\frac{n_2}{n_1}\right)
$$
$$
=-f\tan\theta\left(\frac{0.0001}{100}\times\frac{n_2}{n_1}\right)\qquad\text{(2.0)}
$$
$$
=-f\times(5.77\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{rad}),
$$
$$
\boxed{\Delta x_n=-1.15\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}}\qquad\text{(1.0)}.
$$

Wrong sign will have penalty of 1.0 mark.

### (T09.3)

Blue light has larger refractive index than the red light, hence it will bend more. So incoming light will vary in colour from red to blue towards right. So, the correct answer will be **F** (2.0).

Note: convex lens inverts image, but does not affect refractive index dependent bending. Options D or E get 1.0 mark.

### (T09.4b)

Each set of planes, with specific tilt, will lead to one set of stripes of parallel rays. From solution of part (T09.2b),

$$
\alpha=\tan\theta\frac{n_1-n_2}{n_1}.
$$

Two sets of planes with relative angle of $2\theta$ will create angular separation of $2\alpha$ between the stripes (1.0). At a distance of $H$ this will create a horizontal separation of $\approx2\alpha H$. For $H=1\,\mathrm{km}$, $\theta=10^\circ$, the horizontal shift will be

$$
\Delta x\simeq2\alpha H=2\tan\theta\frac{n_1-n_2}{n_1}H=2\times1.76\times10^{-6}\times1\times10^5\,\mathrm{cm}=0.35\,\mathrm{cm}\qquad\text{(1.0)}.
$$

Just below the point where two planes make upward bend, a patch of this width 0.35 cm will be centred where no light from the star will reach. Hence, $\boxed{W_X=0.35\,\mathrm{cm}}$ (1.0).

### (T09.4c)

From the figure one can see that two adjacent planes (with downward bend), will lead to overlapping stripes of rays producing two images. Therefore, the region in which only single image is formed will be 10.0 cm (including the region X). Hence, $\boxed{D_\mathrm{max}=10.0\,\mathrm{cm}}$ (4.0).

$(10\,\mathrm{cm}-2W_X)$ will get 1.0 mark. $(10\,\mathrm{cm}-W_X)$ will get 2.0 marks.

### (T09.5)

For $D\gg d$, several stripes will fall on the lens, leading to multiple images. Here, four images will form, two each from the set of planes with zigzag shapes along $x$-axis and $y$-axis respectively.

First consider the zigzag shape of the boundary along the x axis. From solution of (T09.4), angle between these two sets of rays will be $3.52\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{rad}$. With $f=2\,\mathrm{m}$, the separation between the two images will be $=7.0\,\mu\mathrm{m}$.

For $D=100\,\mathrm{cm}$, the diffraction peak size $=1.34\,\mu\mathrm{m}$. So the images formed by the two sets of planes (with tilts of $\pm10^\circ$ from horizontal for each set) will form two spots, clearly separated along the $x$-axis. Similarly for the $y$-axis.

### (T09.6)

A single image is formed when only one stripe of rays is allowed to fall on the objective. This is only possible when the objective is placed suitably below the zigzag pattern.

The diffraction-limited image size for a $D=8\,\mathrm{cm}$ lens is $8.4\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}$ (1.0).

The angle of the atmospheric layer changes from $+10^\circ$ to $-10^\circ$.

$\implies$ Change in angle of rays is $3.52\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{rad}$.

$\implies$ Shift in image position $=3.52\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ (1.0).

Now, shift in the image position allowed is 1% of the image size. $\therefore$ Allowed shift $=0.084\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ (1.0).

Longest exposure time $=$ Time taken by the image to shift by $0.084\,\mu\mathrm{m}$.

Image shifts by $3.52\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ in 1 s $\implies$ $0.084\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ shift occurs in 0.024 s.

$$
\boxed{t_\mathrm{max}=0.024\,\mathrm{s}}\qquad\text{(2.0)}.
$$

Its answer sheet labels a temperature field with “s”. These native forms are preserved, including every original blank answer page.
