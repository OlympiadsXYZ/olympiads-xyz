## Условие

In a binary system involving a compact star, where the companion star does not overflow its Roche lobe, the primary source of accretion for the compact star is the stellar wind from the companion star. This wind-fed accretion is especially significant in systems that include an early-type star (such as an O or B star, indicated henceforth as an OB star), alongside a compact object like a neutron star (NS) in a close orbit.

Consider such a NS-OB star binary system where a neutron star of mass $M_{\mathrm{NS}}=2.0\,M_\odot$ and radius $R_{\mathrm{NS}}=11\,\mathrm{km}$ is orbiting in a circular orbit of radius $a$ around the centre of the OB star with velocity $v_{\mathrm{orb}}=1.5\times10^5\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ (see figure below). Throughout this problem the mass loss from the OB star is assumed to be spherically symmetric and its rate is $\dot M_{\mathrm{OB}}=1.0\times10^{-4}\,M_\odot\,\mathrm{yr^{-1}}$.

**(T07.1)** The accretion radius, $R_{\mathrm{acc}}$, is defined as the maximum distance from the NS at which the stellar wind can be captured by the NS. If the stellar wind speed at the orbital distance of the NS is $v_{\mathrm w}=3.0\times10^6\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, find $R_{\mathrm{acc}}$ for the above system in km using standard escape velocity calculations. **[3 т.]**

**(T07.2)** Assuming that all captured material is accreted by the NS, estimate the mass accretion rate, $\dot M_{\mathrm{acc}}$, from the stellar wind onto the NS in units of $M_\odot\,\mathrm{yr^{-1}}$ if $a=0.5\,\mathrm{au}$. Neglect the effects of radiation pressure and finite cooling time of the accreting gas. **[3 т.]**

**(T07.3)** Now consider the situation where the stellar wind speed at the orbital distance $a$ (near the NS) becomes comparable with the orbital speed of the NS. The mass accretion rate from the stellar wind onto the NS in this case would be given by an expression of the form $\dot M_{\mathrm{acc}}=\dot M_{\mathrm{OB}}f(\tan\beta,q)$, where $q=M_{\mathrm{NS}}/M_{\mathrm{OB}}$ is the mass ratio of the binary and $\beta$ is the angle in the frame of the NS between the wind velocity direction and radial direction away from the OB star. Obtain the expression for $f(\tan\beta,q)$ assuming $M_{\mathrm{OB}}\gg M_{\mathrm{NS}}$. **[6 т.]**

**(T07.4)** Consider that the fully ionized material accretes radially and is hindered by the strong magnetic field $\vec B$ of the NS. The pressure exerted by the magnetic field is given by $\dfrac{B^2}{2\mu_0}$. We shall assume that the NS has a dipole magnetic field whose magnitude in the equatorial plane varies with the distance $r$ from the NS for $r\gg R_{\mathrm{NS}}$ as

$$
B(r)=B_0\left(\frac{R_{\mathrm{NS}}}{r}\right)^3
$$

where $B_0$ is the magnetic field at the equator of the NS. Assume that the axis of the magnetic dipole aligns with the rotation axis of the NS.

**(T07.4a)** Obtain the magnetic pressure, $P_{\mathrm{eq,mag}}$, in the equatorial plane in terms of $B_0$, $R_{\mathrm{NS}}$, $r$, and other suitable constants. **[1 т.]**

**(T07.4b)** The maximum distance where the accretion flow is stopped by the magnetic field at the equatorial plane is called the magnetospheric radius $R_m$. This flow of matter will exert a pressure due to the relative motion between incoming stellar wind and the NS. Obtain an approximate expression for the critical magnetic field $B_{0,c}$ for which $R_m$ coincides with $R_{\mathrm{acc}}$ and calculate its value in Tesla. Magnetic effects are neglected for $r>R_m$ and consider $v_{\mathrm w}\gg v_{\mathrm{orb}}$. **[7 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

### (T07.1)

The accretion radius can be estimated by equating the gravitational potential energy of the stellar wind to its kinetic energy

$$
\frac12\rho_wv_\mathrm{rel}^2=\frac{GM_\mathrm{NS}\rho_w}{R_\mathrm{acc}}\qquad\text{(0.5)},
$$
$$
\implies R_\mathrm{acc}=\frac{2GM_\mathrm{NS}}{v_\mathrm{rel}^2}\qquad\text{(0.5)},
$$

where, $\rho_w$ is the wind density and $v_\mathrm{rel}$ is the relative velocity of the wind. For the case considered above,

$$
v_\mathrm{rel}^2=v_w^2+v_\mathrm{orb}^2.
$$

Here, $v_w\gg v_\mathrm{orb}$, therefore, $v_\mathrm{rel}^2\approx v_w^2=9\times10^{12}\,\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}$ (1.0). which gives,

$$
R_\mathrm{acc}=\frac{2\times6.674\times10^{-11}\times2.0\times1.988\times10^{30}}{9\times10^{12}}\,\mathrm{m},
$$
$$
\boxed{R_\mathrm{acc}=5.9\times10^4\,\mathrm{km}}\qquad\text{(1.0)}.
$$

### (T07.2)

In the case of spherically symmetric mass loss from the companion and $v_w\gg v_\mathrm{orb}$, the companion mass loss is

$$
\dot M_\mathrm{OB}=4\pi a^2\rho_wv_w\qquad\text{(1.0)},
$$

where $a=0.5\,\mathrm{au}=7.480\times10^{10}\,\mathrm{m}$ is the orbital separation. Mass accretion rate on the compact object is

$$
\dot M_\mathrm{acc}\approx\pi R_\mathrm{acc}^2\rho_wv_\mathrm{rel}\qquad\text{(1.0)}.
$$

Also here, since $v_w\gg v_\mathrm{orb}$, $v_\mathrm{rel}\approx v_w$. This implies,

$$
\dot M_\mathrm{acc}\approx\left(\frac{\dot M_\mathrm{OB}}4\right)\left(\frac{R_\mathrm{acc}}a\right)^2\approx9.74\times10^{11}\,\mathrm{kg\,s^{-1}},
$$
$$
\implies\boxed{\dot M_\mathrm{acc}\approx1.6\times10^{-11}M_\odot\,\mathrm{yr^{-1}}}\qquad\text{(1.0)}.
$$

If the student does not use the approximation for speed, then he will get $9.75\times10^{11}\,\mathrm{kg\,s^{-1}}$, but the final answer is same for required significant digits. No marks should be deducted if this is the case.

### (T07.3)

The orbital speed of the NS in the frame corresponding to the center of OB star (assuming the star is stationary) is given by

$$
v_\mathrm{orb}^2=\frac{GM_\mathrm{OB}}a\qquad\text{(1.0)}.
$$

Using vector algebra we get $\tan\beta=v_\mathrm{orb}/v_w$ (1.0). Now, the ratio of the mass accretion rate to that of mass loss rate from the star

$$
\frac{\dot M_\mathrm{acc}}{\dot M_\mathrm{OB}}\sim\frac{\pi R_\mathrm{acc}^2\rho_wv_\mathrm{rel}}{4\pi a^2\rho_wv_w}
$$
$$
=\frac14\left(\frac{R_\mathrm{acc}}a\right)^2\sqrt{\frac{v_\mathrm{orb}^2+v_w^2}{v_w^2}}=\frac14\left(\frac{R_\mathrm{acc}}a\right)^2\sqrt{1+\tan^2\beta}\qquad\text{(1.0)}.
$$

To find the expression of the ratio of the two radii, $R_\mathrm{acc}$ and orbital separation $a$,

$$
\frac{R_\mathrm{acc}}a=\frac{2GM_\mathrm{NS}}{v_\mathrm{rel}^2}\frac1a=\frac{2GM_\mathrm{OB}q}{v_\mathrm{rel}^2}\frac1a=\frac{2v_\mathrm{orb}^2}{v_\mathrm{rel}^2}q\qquad\text{(1.0)}
$$
$$
=\left(\frac{2\tan^2\beta}{1+\tan^2\beta}\right)q\qquad\text{(1.0)}.
$$

On substituting and simplifying, we can express,

$$
\implies\boxed{f(\tan\beta,q)=q^2\left(\frac{\tan^4\beta}{(1+\tan^2\beta)^{3/2}}\right)}\qquad\text{(1.0)}.
$$

### (T07.4a)

The magnetic pressure $P_\mathrm{mag}=B^2/2\mu_0$ increases rapidly towards the NS magnetic poles.

$$
\implies\boxed{P_{\mathrm{eq,mag}}=\frac{B_0^2R_\mathrm{NS}^6}{2\mu_0r^6}}\qquad\text{(1.0)}.
$$

### (T07.4b)

The critical magnetic field $B_{0,c}$ can be obtained by equating pressure due to incoming wind with the $P_{\mathrm{eq,mag}}$ at $r=R_\mathrm{acc}$ (1.0). The pressure due to incoming wind at $r=R_\mathrm{acc}$ is given by

$$
\rho_wv_\mathrm{rel}^2\sim\rho_wv_w^2\qquad\text{(1.0)}.
$$

If the student writes the pressure due to incoming wind as dynamic pressure, i.e., $\frac12\rho_wv_\mathrm{rel}^2\sim\frac12\rho_wv_w^2$, 0.5 credit will be given.

From part (T07.2), one can obtain the expression for $\rho_w$ as follows:

$$
\rho_w=\frac{\dot M_\mathrm{acc}}{\pi R_\mathrm{acc}^2v_w}=\frac{\dot M_\mathrm{OB}}{4\pi a^2v_w}\qquad\text{(1.0)}.
$$

If the student writes the density of wind as $\dot M_\mathrm{acc}/(4\pi R_\mathrm{acc}^2v_w)$, 0.5 credit will be given.

Now equating the pressures due to wind and magnetic field, we have:

$$
\frac{B_{0,c}^2R_\mathrm{NS}^6}{2\mu_0R_\mathrm{acc}^6}=\frac{\dot M_\mathrm{acc}}{\pi R_\mathrm{acc}^2}\left(\frac{2GM_\mathrm{NS}}{R_\mathrm{acc}}\right)^{1/2}\qquad\text{(1.0)},
$$

which gives the value of the critical magnetic field as

$$
\implies\boxed{B_{0,c}=\left(\frac{2\mu_0\dot M_\mathrm{acc}\sqrt{2GM_\mathrm{NS}}}{\pi R_\mathrm{NS}^6}\right)^{1/2}R_\mathrm{acc}^{7/4}}\qquad\text{(1.0)}.
$$

No credit will be given if the expression has wrong exponent, or is missing a constant quantity.

Now putting the values we get $\implies\boxed{B_{0,c}\approx4.0\times10^9\,\mathrm{T}}$ (2.0).

### General Marking Principles

- STRICT adherence to given marking scheme.
- No double penalty – deduction only at the original place of error; no further penalty in consequent steps, if they are otherwise correct – if final answer is physically unviable or dimensionally incorrect, deduct for final answer also.
