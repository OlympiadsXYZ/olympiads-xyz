*ΩWAO 2023 ◇ Theoretical Round ◇ Problems*

## Условие

At the 50$^{\mathrm{th}}$ parallel ($50^{\circ}$ N), some star rises just before Antares ($\alpha_A = 16^{\mathrm{h}}\,29^{\mathrm{m}};\ \delta_A = -26^{\circ}\,26'$) disappears behind the horizon, and it sets just when Sirius ($\alpha_S = 6^{\mathrm{h}}\,45^{\mathrm{m}};\ \delta_S = -16^{\circ}\,43'$) appears. The star is brighter than $+1.5^{\mathrm{m}}$. Estimate the equatorial coordinates of the star. What is the name of this star? Consider the Earth to be perfectly spherical with no atmosphere.

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- $\alpha_{\star} \approx 23^{\mathrm{h}}$, $\delta_{\star} \approx -30^{\circ}$; the star is Fomalhaut ($\alpha$ Piscis Austrini).

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

First we determine hour angle $t_A$ of Antares setting. Using spherical law of cosines for the spherical triangle with vertices in the north celestial pole, zenith and Antares — navigational triangle, we have **[2 pt]**

$$\cos z_A = \sin\varphi\sin\delta_A + \cos\varphi\cos\delta_A\cos t_A. \qquad (2)$$

We assume $z_A = 90^{\circ}$ because we neglect the refraction. Therefore, **[1 pt]**

$$\cos t_A = -\tan\varphi\tan\delta_A = 0.592 \quad\Rightarrow\quad t_A = 53.7^{\circ}.$$

Antares setting sidereal time is **[1 pt]**

$$s_1 = t_A + \alpha_A = \frac{53.7^{\circ}}{15^{\circ}/\mathrm{h}} + 16^{\mathrm{h}}\,29^{\mathrm{m}} = 20.06^{\mathrm{h}}.$$

Similarly, we estimate hour angle $t_S$ and sidereal time $s_2$ at the time of Sirius rising above the horizon **[1 pt]:**

$$\cos t_S = -\tan\varphi\tan\delta_S = 0.359 \quad\Rightarrow\quad t_S = -69.0^{\circ},$$

$$s_2 = \frac{-69^{\circ}}{15^{\circ}/\mathrm{h}} + 6^{\mathrm{h}}\,45^{\mathrm{m}} = 2.15^{\mathrm{h}}.$$

The sidereal time of the star’s upper culmination is equal to its right ascension **[2 pt]:**

$$\alpha_{\star} = s_c = \frac{s_1 + s_2}{2} \approx 23^{\mathrm{h}}.$$

The hour angle of the star setting is

$$t_{\star} = s_c - s_1 = 23.1^{\mathrm{h}} - 20.06^{\mathrm{h}} = 3.04^{\mathrm{h}}.$$

Therefore, we may estimate the declination **[2 pt]** by the formula (2):

$$\cos t_{\star} = -\tan\varphi\tan\delta_{\star} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \tan\delta_{\star} = -\frac{\cos t_{\star}}{\tan\varphi} = -0.587 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \delta_{\star} \approx -30^{\circ}.$$

These coordinates are quite similar to the coordinates of Fomalhaut ($\alpha$ Piscis Austrinus; $\alpha = 22^{\mathrm{h}}\,57^{\mathrm{m}};\ \delta = -29^{\circ}\,37'$) **[1 pt].**


</Spoiler>
