*ΩWAO 2023 ◇ Theoretical Round ◇ Problems*

## Условие

A planet orbits a main sequence star with apparent bolometric magnitude $m = +7^{\mathrm{m}}$ in a circular orbit with period $T = 570$ years. The star is 385 light years from the Sun.

**a)** Estimate the maximum angular distance between the planet and the star for an observer on the Earth.

**b)** Compare the obtained value with the angular resolution of the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) at wavelength $\lambda = 3.8$ µm.

The effective diameter of JWST is $D = 6.5$ m. Neglect the interstellar extinction.

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a)** 0.7 arcsec
- **b)** The JWST resolution is $\beta \approx 6 \cdot 10^{-7}$ rad $\approx 0.1''$; the maximum planet–star separation exceeds it.

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

a) In order to determine the maximum angular distance between a star and a planet, we need to know the radius of the planet’s orbit, and for this we need to estimate the mass of the star. Recalling$^{3}$ that 1 pc = 3.26 light years, we estimate the luminosity based on the apparent magnitude and distance, namely

$$
M = m + 5 - 5\lg r = 7 + 5 - 5\lg\frac{385}{3.26} = 1.6^{\mathrm{m}},
$$

$$
M - M_{\odot} = -2.5\lg\frac{L}{L_{\odot}} \quad\Rightarrow\quad L = 10^{0.4(M_{\odot} - M)}L_{\odot} = 10^{0.4(4.7 - 1.6)}L_{\odot} = 17L_{\odot}.
$$

According to the mass–luminosity relation for main sequence stars, for such luminosities there is a power law relationship between mass $\mathfrak{M}$ and luminosity $L$:

$$
L \propto \mathfrak{M}^4.
$$

Therefore, the mass of the star is

$$
\mathfrak{M} = \left(\frac{L}{L_{\odot}}\right)^{1/4}\mathfrak{M}_{\odot} \approx 2\mathfrak{M}_{\odot}.
$$

Next, we estimate the radius of the orbit:

$$
\frac{T^2}{a^3} = \frac{4\pi^2}{G\mathfrak{M}} \quad\Rightarrow\quad a = \sqrt[3]{\frac{G\mathfrak{M}T^2}{4\pi^2}};
$$

$$
a = \sqrt[3]{\frac{6.67 \cdot 10^{-11} \times (2 \times 2.0 \cdot 10^{30}) \times (570 \times 365.25 \cdot 24 \cdot 60 \cdot 60)^2}{4\pi^2}} = 1.3 \cdot 10^{13}\ \mathrm{m} = 87\ \mathrm{au}.
$$

The maximum angular distance is

$$
\alpha = \frac{a}{r} = \frac{87\ \mathrm{au}}{\frac{385}{3.26} \cdot 206265\ \mathrm{au}} \approx 4 \cdot 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{rad} \approx 0.7''.
$$

b) Next, we estimate the angular resolution of JWST:

$$
\beta \sim \frac{\lambda}{D} = \frac{3.8 \cdot 10^{-6}}{6.5} \approx 6 \cdot 10^{-7}\ \mathrm{rad} \approx 0.1''.
$$

It can be noticed that the maximum angular distance between the planet and the star exceeds the angular resolution of the telescope.

*The problem discusses the planetary system* ***HIP 65426,*** *the planet was observed directly (Carter et al., 2023, arXiv:2208.14990).*
