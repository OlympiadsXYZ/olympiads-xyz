## Условие

VOIDED Austin is now observing LARES, but in this problem he is allowed to observe LARES at any declination. Assume that LARES is a uniform spherical ball, and for the purposes of this problem, assume that LARES is a perfect blackbody; i.e. THA-18N has an albedo of 0. What is the brightest apparent magnitude Austin can observe LARES? You can use the fact that the Sun’s apparent magnitude is $-26.74$.

(a) 6.78

(b) 7.77

(c) 8.76

(d) 9.75

(e) 10.74

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**Solution:**

The shortest distance Austin can be from LARES is simply its orbiting distance (when LARES is directly overhead Austin):

$$
d = 1450\ \mathrm{km}.
$$

Let the radius of LARES be $r$. Power LARES receives from the Sun:

$$
P = \frac{L_{\odot}}{4\pi(1\mathrm{AU})^2}\pi r^2
$$

Flux LARES emits to Boston:

$$
F = \frac{P}{4\pi d^2} = \frac{L_{\odot}}{16\pi}\left(\frac{r}{1\mathrm{AU} \cdot d}\right)^2
$$

Magnitude-Flux relation with the Sun:

$$
m_{\odot} - m = -2.5\log\left(\frac{F_{\odot}}{F}\right) = -2.5\log\left(\frac{\frac{L_{\odot}}{4\pi(1\mathrm{AU})^2}}{\frac{L_{\odot}}{16\pi}\left(\frac{r}{1\mathrm{AU} \cdot d}\right)^2}\right) = -2.5\log\left(\frac{4d^2}{r^2}\right) \Longrightarrow
$$

$$
m = 7.77
$$

**Answer: B**


</Spoiler>
