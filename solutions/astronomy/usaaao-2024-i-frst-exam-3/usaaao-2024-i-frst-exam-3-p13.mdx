## Условие

Samvit observes a binary star system of masses $M_1$ and $M_2$. Unfortunately, the star with mass $M_2$ is too dim for him to observe it, leading to the following snapshot below.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '43%', '--fig-max': '275px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usaaao-2024-i-frst-exam-3/p13-fig1.png" alt="Coordinate axes with arrows in both directions and an ellipse centred at the origin crossing the axes at (-a,0), (a,0), (0,b) and (0,-b); a grey dot labelled M1 sits at the top of the ellipse at (0,b)." width="860" height="582" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

What could Samvit hypothesize to be the position and mass of the other star at this instant that would be consistent with the laws of physics and the orbit snapshot that he sees? To be clear, he has *no knowledge* of the value of $M_2$ or the period of the binary system.

(a) At $(\sqrt{a^2 - b^2}, 0)$ with mass $M_1 + M_2$

(b) At $(0, -b)$ with mass $M_1$

(c) At $(\sqrt{a^2 - b^2}, 0)$ with mass $\frac{M_1 M_2}{M_1 + M_2}$

(d) At $(-2\sqrt{a^2 - b^2}, -b)$ with mass $M_1$

(e) At $(\frac{M_2}{M_1}\sqrt{a^2 - b^2}, 0)$ with mass $M_2$

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- D

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**Solution:** The trick to this question is that the center of mass of the system has to lie at the focus of the ellipse, and this is the only thing that matters if you have no knowledge of the other parameters in the system. In fact, if you had the period of the binary system, you could fully determine the value of $M_2$ in terms of $M_1$ and the other parameters of the problem. So, we can simply go through the choices and see which choice places the center of mass of the system at one of the foci, $(\pm\sqrt{a^2 - b^2}, 0)$, and choice **(d)** does.

**Answer: D**


</Spoiler>
