## Условие

Circumpolar stars are stars that never set below the horizon. Consider such a star with declination $\delta$, as seen by a northern observer at latitude $\Phi$.

**(a)** Draw a figure that illustrates the motion of such a star in the sky. Label all relevant angles. [3] **[3 т.]**

**(b)** Find the condition $\delta$ and $\Phi$ must fulfill for the star to be circumpolar. [3] **[3 т.]**

**(c)** Write an expression (in terms of $\delta$ and $\Phi$) for the maximum azimuth of a circumpolar star that cannot reach 180 degrees. (The azimuth of a star is its angle from north) [6] **[6 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **(b)** $\phi + \delta > 90^{\circ}$
- **(c)** $\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{\cos\delta}{\cos\phi}\right)$

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

a)

Correct shape [1]

Correct direction [1]

Correct angles [1]

b)

- $(90^{\circ} - \phi) + (90^{\circ} - \delta) < 90^{\circ}$ [2]
- $\phi + \delta > 90^{\circ}$ [1]

Full points were awarded to correct answers without any working shown.

c)

For $(90^{\circ} - \delta) > (90^{\circ} - \phi)$, or $\phi > \delta$:

The maximum azimuth is 180°.

(According to the question, this is not the case)

For $(90^{\circ} - \delta) < (90^{\circ} - \phi)$, or $\phi < \delta$:

Let P be the point of greatest azimuth.

Angle ZPN = 90° [2]

Applying the spherical sine rule on ZPN:

$$
\frac{\sin\theta}{\sin(90^{\circ} - \delta)} = \frac{\sin 90^{\circ}}{\sin(90^{\circ} - \phi)}
$$
[3]

$$
\sin\theta = \frac{\cos\delta}{\cos\phi}
$$

The maximum azimuth = $\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{\cos\delta}{\cos\phi}\right)$. [1]
