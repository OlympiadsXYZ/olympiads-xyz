## Условие



**5.1.** Calculate the most favorable time for observations of Canopus in Dhaka over the next 24 hours. Bangladesh time should be used. **[3,5 т.]**

**5.2.** Suppose that due to smog, Canopus will be visible in Dhaka only at the most favorable time for its observation, and at the limit of visibility to the naked eye. What magnitude will Sirius be visible at this time? **[4,5 т.]**

### 5.1.

Canopus is visible in Dhaka, but not high above the horizon. Therefore, the best conditions for the observation should be at the time of its upper culmination. The upper culmination of a celestial body occurs when its sidereal time equals to its right ascension. It is $6^{\mathrm h}24^{\mathrm m}$ for Canopus. The sidereal time is equal to the mean solar time at the date of autumnal equinox, and then begins to outstrip it with speed $1^{\mathrm d}/365.2422$ per day (about $3^{\mathrm m}56^{\mathrm s}$ per day). 47–48 days passed since September 22 (autumnal equinox) till November 8–9 (the next 24 hours), let us use 47.5 days difference. The difference between the mean solar time and the sidereal time is now:

$$
24^{\mathrm h}00^{\mathrm m}\times47.5/365.2422=3^{\mathrm h}07^{\mathrm m}.
$$

Thus, now sidereal time $6^{\mathrm h}24^{\mathrm m}$ corresponds to

$$
6^{\mathrm h}24^{\mathrm m}-3^{\mathrm h}07^{\mathrm m}=3^{\mathrm h}17^{\mathrm m}\quad\text{mean solar time}.
$$

Dhaka is located about $0.5^\circ$ East from the $90^\circ$ meridian, so the mean solar time here is $0.5^\circ\times4^{\mathrm m}/{}^\circ=2^{\mathrm m}$ ahead of the local time UT+6 used in Bangladesh. So to get the answer for UT+6, you need to subtract these $2^{\mathrm m}$, and we get

$$
3^{\mathrm h}17^{\mathrm m}-0^{\mathrm h}02^{\mathrm m}=3^{\mathrm h}15^{\mathrm m}\quad\text{time of Bangladesh}.
$$

Thus, there will be favorable conditions for observing Canopus tonight. Don't miss the chance!

</ProblemSection>

### 5.2.

During the upper culmination, Canopus is at an altitude

$$
h_c=90^\circ+\delta_c-\varphi=90^\circ-52^\circ42'-23^\circ48'=13^\circ30'.
$$

At this time, Sirius is also located near the upper culmination (from the difference in right ascensions, it can be seen that its culmination will be only 21 minutes later), so its altitude is approximately equal to

$$
h_s\approx90^\circ+\delta_s-\varphi=90^\circ-16^\circ43'-23^\circ48'=49^\circ29'.
$$

If the light of celestial bodies from the zenith passes through a layer $d_0$ of polluted air, then the layer from the stars at an altitude of $h$: $d_1=d_0/\sin h$.

For Canopus, the layer is

$$
d_c=d_0/\sin13^\circ30'=4.28h_0,
$$

and for Sirius the layer is

$$
d_s=d_0/\sin49^\circ29'=1.32h_0.
$$

Under the most favorable conditions, a layer of one atmosphere weakens light by $0.23^{\mathrm m}$, thus, the exoatmospheric magnitude of Canopus is

$$
-0.72^{\mathrm m}-0.23^{\mathrm m}=-0.95^{\mathrm m}.
$$

We will assume that the limit of visibility by the naked eye is $6^{\mathrm m}$. Thus, the layer $d_c=4.28h_0$ weakens the light by $6^{\mathrm m}-(-0.95^{\mathrm m})=6.95^{\mathrm m}$. Accordingly, the layer $d_0$ weakens the light by $6.95^{\mathrm m}/4.28=1.62^{\mathrm m}$, and a layer $d_s=1.32h_0$ weakens the light by $1.62^{\mathrm m}\cdot1.32=2.13^{\mathrm m}$.

The exoatmospheric magnitude of Sirius is

$$
-1.46^{\mathrm m}-0.23^{\mathrm m}=-1.69^{\mathrm m}.
$$

Thus, in the conditions of smog of the considered level, the apparent magnitude of Sirius will be

$$
-1.69^{\mathrm m}+2.13^{\mathrm m}=0.44^{\mathrm m}.
$$

*Transcription note: the sketch changes the atmospheric layer notation from $d_0$ to $h_0$ in several expressions; this is retained.*
