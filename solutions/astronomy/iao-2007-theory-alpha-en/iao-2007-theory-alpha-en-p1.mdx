## Условие

The photo of the Earth and the Moon is taken from an artificial satellite of the Earth. Estimate the stellar magnitude of the Moon for an observer on this satellite.

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

Let us find the distance from the satellite to the Moon.

Let $\rho$, $R$ and $L$ are angular radii as seen from the satellite, (radii and distances to the Earth (E) and Moon (M)). From the equations

$$
R_E=L_E\sin\rho_E\approx L_E\rho_E,
$$

$$
R_M=L_M\sin\rho_M\approx L_M\rho_M,
$$

one can find

$$
L_M/L_E=(R_M/R_E)(\rho_E/\rho_M)
$$

or

$$
L_E/L_M=(R_E/R_M)(\rho_M/\rho_E).
$$

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '45.2%', '--fig-max': '289px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/iao-2007-theory-alpha-en/p1-earth-moon-key.png" alt="p1 earth moon key" width="904" height="1047" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


The close positions of the Earth and the Moon on the picture shows us that the satellite, the Earth and the Moon are approximately on one line and therefore we can approximately say

$$
L_M=L_{EM}+L_E,
$$

or

$$
L_E=L_M-L_{EM},
$$

where $L_{EM}$ is the distance from the Earth to the Moon.

$$
L_M(R_E/R_M)(\rho_M/\rho_E)=L_M-L_{EM};
$$

$$
L_M=L_{EM}/(1-(R_E/R_M)(\rho_M/\rho_E)).
$$

From the picture we see that for the observer on the satellite the phase of the Moon is full moon. If $m_0$ is the magnitude of the full moon for the observer on the Earth (the distance to the Moon is $L_{EM}$), the magnitude for the observer on the satellite is

$$
m_1=m_0+5\lg(L_M/L_{EM})=m_0-5\lg(1-(R_E/R_M)(\rho_M/\rho_E)).
$$

We may measure values $\rho_E$ and $\rho_M$ on the picture and take values $R_M$, $R_E$ and $m_0$ (and, by the way, $L_{EM}$) from the table of the Solar System,

$$
\rho_E/\rho_M\approx80/13\approx6.15,\quad L_{EM}=384\,400\ \mathrm{km},
$$

$$
R_E/R_M=D_E/D_M=12756\ \mathrm{km}/3475\ \mathrm{km}=3.67,\quad m_0=-12.7^{\mathrm m}.
$$

$$
m_1=-12.7^{\mathrm m}-5^{\mathrm m}\lg(1-3.67\cdot0.16)=-12.7^{\mathrm m}-5^{\mathrm m}\lg(0.41)\approx-12.7^{\mathrm m}+1.9^{\mathrm m}\approx-10.8^{\mathrm m}.
$$

*Note 1 for Jury members. Due to difference in printers the scales of the pictures were different in different copies of the pictures. Therefore the values of the lengths may be different from those written here. But the ratios, which are necessary for the solutions, of the lengths should be about the same. Nevertheless the problem is estimational. Measuring of $\rho_E/\rho_M$ and correct calculations have to result to answers from $-10.6^{\mathrm m}$ to $-11.0^{\mathrm m}$.*

*Note 2 for Jury members. By the way, the short approximate solution that also should be regarded as a full solution (the so called solution in integer values):*

After using the formula $L_M=L_{EM}/(1-(R_E/R_M)(\rho_M/\rho_E))$ (or an analogous formula) and calculations we may find that $L_M\approx2.5L_{EM}$, enlarging distance every 2.5 times (more exactly 2.512) enlarges the magnitude on $2^{\mathrm m}$, so

$$
m_1=-12.7^{\mathrm m}+2^{\mathrm m}\approx-10.7^{\mathrm m}.
$$
