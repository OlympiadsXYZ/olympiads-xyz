## Условие

For the reconstruction of historical events, dates and places of the past solar eclipses are often calculated. However, the rotation of the Earth continually slows down and solar eclipses take place not there, where they should be in agreement with calculations on a model with constant rotation of the Earth with the present angular rate. In what century did such an eclipse take place in Paris ($\lambda = 0^{\circ}$, $\varphi = 45^{\circ}$) instead of Crimea ($\lambda = 34^{\circ}$, $\varphi = 45^{\circ}$)? The length of a day increases by 0.0016 seconds per 100 years due to that rotation of the Earth slows down.

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- In the 32-th century B.C. (in the fourth century)

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

The mechanical analogy for the process is a race of two trains. One of them moves with a constant velocity (moving without deceleration, hypothetical Earth), and the second one – with the constant acceleration "a" (as the real Earth). If the trains came to one point with identical speeds, and the acceleration of the second train is equal "a", in the past (time t ago) the distance between them was $S = at^2/2$. The analog of S in our case is $\Delta\lambda = 360^{\circ} - 34^{\circ} = 326^{\circ}$ or $34/360 = 0.906$ revolutions of the planet. (In the opposite case $\Delta\lambda = 34^{\circ}$ or $34/360 = 0.094$ revolutions of the planet.) If S will be used in connection with revolutions of the planet (i.e. in days), and time is given in years, $a = 0.0016\ \mathrm{sec}/(\mathrm{day}\cdot100\ \mathrm{years})$ should be in units day/yrs$^2$. Evidently for this purpose it is required to divide it by the number of seconds in a day and to multiply with the number of days in one year:
$$
a = 0.0016\ \mathrm{sec}/(1\ \mathrm{day}\cdot100\ \mathrm{yrs}) = (1.6\cdot10^{-3}\cdot365.24)/(100\cdot86400)\ \mathrm{day/yrs^2} = 6.76\cdot10^{-8}\ \mathrm{day/yrs^2}.
$$
Now we easily find the time, for which the difference 0.906 revolutions will be accumulated:
$$
t = (2S/a)^{1/2} = (0.189\ \mathrm{day}/6.76\cdot10^{-8}\ \mathrm{day/yrs^2})^{1/2} \approx 5180\ \mathrm{years},
$$
the 32-th century B.C.

(For the case $\Delta\lambda = 34^{\circ}$ $t = (2S/a)^{1/2} = (0.189\ \mathrm{day}/6.76\cdot10^{-8}\ \mathrm{day/yrs^2})^{1/2} \approx 1670$ years, the fourth century.)

Answer: in the 32-th century B.C. (in the fourth century).

*Another way of middle part of the solution.* If S will be used in degrees (i.e. in units of 1/360 revolution of the planet = 1/360 day), and time is given in years, $a = 0.0016\ \mathrm{sec}/(\mathrm{day}\cdot100\ \mathrm{years})$ should be in units degree/yrs$^2$. Evidently for this purpose it is required to divide it by the number of seconds for one degree of the Earth rotation and to multiply with the number of days in one year:
$$
a = 0.0016\ \mathrm{sec}/(1\ \mathrm{day}\cdot100\ \mathrm{yrs}) = (1.6\cdot10^{-3}\cdot365.24)/(100\cdot240)\ {}^{\circ}/\mathrm{yrs^2} = 2.43\cdot10^{-5}\ {}^{\circ}/\mathrm{yrs^2}.
$$
Now we easily find the time, for which the difference 326° will be accumulated:
$$
t = (2S/a)^{1/2} = (652^{\circ}/2.43\cdot10^{-5}\ {}^{\circ}/\mathrm{yrs^2})^{1/2} \approx 5180\ \mathrm{years}.
$$
(For the case $\Delta\lambda = 34^{\circ}$ $t = (2S/a)^{1/2} = (68^{\circ}/2.43\cdot10^{-5}\ {}^{\circ}/\mathrm{yrs^2})^{1/2} \approx 1670$ years.)


</Spoiler>
