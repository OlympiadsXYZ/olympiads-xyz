## Условие

A rocket is launched from the North Pole such that it lands on the Equator.

**(a)** At what angle should the rocket be launched such that the required launch velocity is a minimum?  
*You may use the following ellipse property: The normal bisects the angle between the lines to the foci. Similarly, the smaller angles between the tangent and each line to the foci are equal. You are also encouraged to use any other relevant properties of an ellipse.* **[12 т.]**

**(b)** What is this minimal launch velocity (in m/s)? Determine the maximum height (in meters) of the rocket as well. **[6 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **(a)** 22.5° to the horizon (67.5° to the vertical)
- **(b)** v = 7.20 × 10³ m/s; h = 3.57 × 10⁶ m

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**1a**

Suppose the semi-major axis of the orbit is $a$.

$$
E = -\frac{GMm}{2a}, U = -\frac{GMm}{R}
$$

**M1**

The required kinetic energy for launch is then $\frac{1}{2}mv^2 = E - U = GMm\left(\frac{1}{R} - \frac{1}{2a}\right)$, giving $v = \sqrt{GM\left(\frac{2}{R} - \frac{1}{a}\right)}$ **M2**

From the above expression for $v$, we observe that the launch velocity is minimized by **minimizing $a$**, which is where the ellipse property provided will be useful. **M3**

In the diagram above, observe that $2a - R$ is minimized by setting it to be the perpendicular distance (i.e. $\beta = 90^{\circ}$). **M2**

Then, $\gamma = 45^{\circ}$ and $2\alpha = 135^{\circ}$, giving $\alpha = 67.5^{\circ}$. Observe that $\alpha = \delta$, the angle between the launch and the vertical axis. With respect to the horizon, the angle would be $90^{\circ} - 67.5^{\circ} = 22.5^{\circ}$. **M3** **A1**

**1b**

$$
2a - R = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}R \to a = \frac{R}{2}\left(1 + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right) = 5.438 \times 10^6m
$$

**M1**

$$
v = \sqrt{GM\left(\frac{2}{R} - \frac{1}{a}\right)} = \sqrt{GM\left(\frac{2}{R} - \frac{2}{R\left(1 + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)}\right)}
$$

**M1**

$$
= \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}\frac{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}}{1 + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}}}
$$

$$
= 7.20 \times 10^3m/s
$$

**A1**

Note that this amount of symbolic working is not necessary, and participants are expected to calculate numerical values earlier on instead for efficiency

$$
h = a(1 + e) - R = a + c - R
$$

$$
= a + \sqrt{R^2 - (2a - R)^2} - R
$$

**M2**

$$
= \frac{R}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} - 1\right) + \sqrt{4aR - 4a^2}
$$

**A1**

$$
= 3.57 \times 10^6m
$$
