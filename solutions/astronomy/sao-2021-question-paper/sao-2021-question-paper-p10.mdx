## Условие

The spectral radiance of a body for a wavelength $\lambda$ at temperature $T$ is given by

$$
B(\lambda, T) = \frac{2hc^2}{\lambda^5}\frac{1}{e^{\frac{hc}{\lambda k_B T}} - 1}
$$

**(a)** For wavelengths such that $\frac{hc}{\lambda k_B T} \ll 1$, obtain an approximation for this formula.

*Hint: You are given that $e^x \approx 1 + x$ for small $x$* **[2 т.]**

**(b)** Rigel is a blue supergiant with a surface temperature of 12100K, and $B - V$ colour index of -0.03. Determine its peak emission wavelength. **[2 т.]**

**(c)** Given that the colour filter B corresponds to the wavelength 440nm and the colour filter V corresponds to 550nm, obtain a theoretical estimate for the $B - V$ index. How does it compare with the true $B - V$ index? **[5 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **(a)** $B \approx \frac{2\lambda c k_B T}{\lambda^4}$
- **(b)** 240 nm
- **(c)** -0.567

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**4a**

Using the given approximation, we get

$$
B \approx \frac{2hc^2}{\lambda^5}\frac{1}{1+\frac{hc}{\lambda k_B T}-1}
$$

**M1**

$$
= \frac{2hc^2}{\lambda^5}\frac{1}{hc/\lambda k_B T}
$$

$$
= \frac{2\lambda c k_B T}{\lambda^4}
$$

**A1**

**4b**

Using Wien’s law, $\lambda = \frac{2.898\times10^{-3}}{12100} = 239.5\mathrm{nm}\ \mathit{or}\ 240\mathrm{nm}$ **M1 A1**

(depending on whether 12100 is interpreted as 3s.f. or 5s.f.)

**4c**

The approximation in (a) is not valid here, because $\frac{hc}{\lambda k_B T} \approx 5$. It’s important to use the full planck formula – participants should not be thrown off by the approximation in (a). This is the infamous *Ultraviolet Catastrophe*. **M2 (choice of eqn) [do not penalize again after]**

$$
B_{440nm} = \frac{2hc^2}{\lambda^5}\left(\frac{1}{e^{\frac{hc}{\lambda k_B T}} - 1}\right) = 5.194 \times 10^{14}
$$

$$
B_{550nm} = \frac{2hc^2}{\lambda^5}\left(\frac{1}{e^{\frac{hc}{\lambda k_B T}} - 1}\right) = 3.080 \times 10^{14}
$$

**M1**

$$
B - V = m_B - m_V = -2.5\log\frac{B_{440}}{B_{550}} = 2.5\log\frac{5.194}{3.080} = -0.567\ (3.\mathrm{s.f.})
$$

**M1 A1**

There is still some deviation from the theoretical value -- stars are not perfect blackbodies.


</Spoiler>
