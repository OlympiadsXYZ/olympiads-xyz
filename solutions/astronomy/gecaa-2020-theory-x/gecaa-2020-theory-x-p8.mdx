## Условие

In the following problem, the fluid mechanics of Jupiter’s Great Red Spot (GRS) is studied based on the velocity field data. The diagram on the last page shows a map of relative velocity for GRS and the surrounding region. The arrows are oriented and scaled as per the directions and magnitudes of winds at different points.

Due to the combined effects of gravity and rotation, Jupiter is slightly flattened at its poles. The equation of a spheroid approximating for the shape of Jupiter can be stated as:

$$
\frac{x^2+y^2}{R_e^2}+\frac{z^2}{R_p^2}=1,
$$

where $R_e=7.15\times10^7\ \mathrm m$ is the equatorial radius of Jupiter, and $R_p=6.69\times10^7\ \mathrm m$ the polar radius. The radii of curvature of this spheroid in any direction can be calculated by the following equations ($\varepsilon=R_e/R_p$):

$$
r(\varphi)=R_e\left(1+\varepsilon^{-2}\tan^2\varphi\right)^{-1/2},
$$

$$
R(\varphi)=R_e\varepsilon^{-2}\left(\frac{r(\varphi)}{R_e\cos\varphi}\right)^3.
$$

where $r(\varphi)$ and $R(\varphi)$ are the zonal (aka in the zone of a particular latitude) and meridional (aka longitudinal) radii of curvature, respectively, as a function of planetographic latitude $\varphi$. The sidereal rotation period of Jupiter is $P=3.57\times10^4\ \mathrm s$.

**a)** Calculate the zonal and meridional radii values ($\bar r$ and $\bar R$ respectively) at the location of the centre of the GRS. Write numeric answer in km. **[4 т.]**

**b)** Estimate the eccentricity of the GRS. **[5 т.]**

**c)** ‘Vorticity’ at any point is a measure of local spinning of the fluid as measured by an observer situated in the reference frame of the fluid. Mathematically, it is calculated as ‘curl’ (vector derivative product) of the velocity field. In this case, the average relative vorticity may be estimated by the equation:

$$
\xi=\frac{V_wL_{\mathrm{GRS}}}{A_{\mathrm{GRS}}},
$$

where $V_w$ is the maximum value of winds as per the velocity field, $L_{\mathrm{GRS}}$ is the length of the circumference of the GRS and $A_{\mathrm{GRS}}$ is the area of the GRS. Estimate average relative vorticity of the GRS. Give the answer in SI units.

Hint: The circumference of an ellipse is well approximated by an average of circumferences of the corresponding auxiliary and minor circles. **[6 т.]**

**d)** Find the absolute vorticity $\xi_a=\xi+f$ by adding the Coriolis parameter

$$
f=2\Omega\sin\varphi,
$$

where $\Omega$ is the angular velocity of the Jupiter (due to axial rotation) and $\varphi$ is the appropriate latitude. Give the answer in SI units. **[2 т.]**

**e)** If the absolute vorticity has the same sign as the latitude, we call the storm a ‘cyclonic storm’. If they have opposite signs, the system is ‘anticyclonic’. Is the GRS cyclonic or anticyclonic? **[1 т.]**

**f)** Imagine that the GRS moves to another latitude $\varphi_1$, where the absolute vorticity changes the sign (changes from anti-cyclonic to cyclonic or vice versa). Assuming minimum possible displacement of the GRS, at what value of $\varphi_1$ do we expect this change? Give the answer in degrees, rounded to nearest integer. In your analysis, assume that the GRS at the new location would occupy the same angular span in latitude, as well as have the same wind velocities and eccentricity as the original. **[12 т.]**

## Отговори

- **a)** $\bar r=6.77\times10^7\ \mathrm m$, $\bar R=6.40\times10^7\ \mathrm m$ (key’s printed units).
- **b)** $e_{\mathrm{GRS}}\approx0.87$.
- **c)** $\xi\approx3.7\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$.
- **d)** $\xi_a=-8.3\times10^{-5}$ (as printed).
- **e)** Cyclonic.
- **f)** $\varphi_1=-6.2^\circ\approx-6^\circ$.

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**(a)** From the figure, the centre of GRS lies at $\varphi=-20.0^\circ$. **[1.0]**

$$
\epsilon=\frac{R_e}{R_p}=\frac{7.15}{6.69}=1.069.\qquad[1.0]
$$

$$
\bar r=r(-20^\circ)=R_e(1+\epsilon^{-2}\tan^2\varphi)^{-1/2}=7.15\times10^7\times\left(1+(1.0688)^{-2}\tan^2(-20^\circ)\right)^{-1/2},
$$

$$
\bar r=6.77\times10^7\ \mathrm m.\qquad[1.0]
$$

$$
\bar R=R(-20^\circ)=R_e\epsilon^{-2}\left(\frac{r(\varphi)}{R_e\cos\varphi}\right)^3=7.15\times10^7\times(1.0688)^{-2}\left(\frac{r(-20^\circ)}{7.15\times10^7\times\cos(-20^\circ)}\right)^3,
$$

$$
\bar R=6.40\times10^7\ \mathrm m.\qquad[1.0]
$$

**(b)** We use the figure to estimate size major and minor axis of the GRS and hence estimate the eccentricity.

$$
a=\bar r\theta_{\mathrm{long}}=6.77\times10^7\times\frac{8.2^\circ\times\pi}{180^\circ},\qquad a=9.69\times10^6\ \mathrm m.\qquad[2.0]
$$

$$
b=\bar R\theta_{\mathrm{lat}}=6.40\times10^7\times\frac{4.3^\circ\times\pi}{180^\circ},\qquad b=4.80\times10^6\ \mathrm m.\qquad[2.0]
$$

$$
e_{\mathrm{GRS}}=\sqrt{1-\left(\frac ba\right)^2}=\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{4.8}{9.7}\right)^2},\qquad e_{\mathrm{GRS}}\approx0.87.\qquad[1.0]
$$

**(c)** The maximum value of wind velocity ($V_w$) is about 120 m/s. **[1.0]**

$$
A_{\mathrm{GRS}}=\pi ab.\qquad[1.0]
$$

$$
L_{\mathrm{GRS}}\approx\frac{2\pi a+2\pi b}2=\pi a(1+\sqrt{1-e^2}).\qquad[2.0]
$$

$$
\therefore\xi=\frac{V_wL_{\mathrm{GRS}}}{A_{\mathrm{GRS}}}=\frac{V_w\pi a(1+\sqrt{1-e^2})}{\pi ab}=\frac{120\times(1+\sqrt{1-0.87^2})}{4.8\times10^6},
$$

$$
\xi\approx3.7\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}.\qquad[2.0]
$$

**(d)** The absolute vorticity will be given by

$$
\xi_a=\xi+2\Omega\sin\varphi=\xi+\frac{2\times2\times\pi\sin(-20^\circ)}P=3.7\times10^{-5}+\frac{4\pi\sin(-20^\circ)}{3.57\times10^4},
$$

$$
=3.7\times10^{-5}-1.2\times10^{-4},\qquad\xi_a=-8.3\times10^{-5}.\qquad[2.0]
$$

**(e)** The GRS is a cyclonic system. **[1.0]**

**(f)** For this, we have to write $\xi_a$ as a function of $\varphi$ and then find root of the function closest to the current latitude.

$$
\xi_a=0=\xi+2\Omega\sin\varphi.\qquad[1.0]
$$

$$
0=\frac{V_w(1+\sqrt{1-e^2})}b+2\Omega\sin\varphi_1,
$$

$$
0=\frac{V_w(1+\sqrt{1-e^2})}{\bar R_1\theta_{\mathrm{lat}}}+2\Omega\sin\varphi_1,
$$

$$
0=\frac{V_w\epsilon^2R_e^2(1+\sqrt{1-e^2})\cos^3\varphi}{\theta_{\mathrm{lat}}r^3(\varphi_1)}+2\Omega\sin\varphi_1,
$$

$$
0=\frac{V_w\epsilon^2R_e^2(1+\sqrt{1-e^2})\cos^3\varphi_1}{\theta_{\mathrm{lat}}\left(R_e(1+\epsilon^{-2}\tan^2\varphi_1)^{-1/2}\right)^3}+2\Omega\sin\varphi_1.\qquad[3.0]
$$

$$
0=\left(\frac{V_w\epsilon^2(1+\sqrt{1-e^2})}{\theta_{\mathrm{lat}}R_e}\right)\left(\cos\varphi_1\sqrt{1+\epsilon^{-2}\tan^2\varphi_1}\right)^3+2\Omega\sin\varphi_1,
$$

$$
-3.520\times10^{-4}\sin\varphi_1=3.81\times10^{-5}\times\left(1-(1-\epsilon^{-2})\sin^2\varphi_1\right)^{3/2},
$$

$$
35.2^2\sin^2\varphi_1=3.81^2\times\left(1-(1-\epsilon^{-2})\sin^2\varphi_1\right)^3.
$$

Let us define $\sin^2\varphi_1=x$.

$$
\therefore x=0.01172(1-0.1245x)^3.\qquad[4.0]
$$

Iterating over $x$, with starting value as $x_0=\sin^2(-20^\circ)=0.11698$,

| $x_0$ | $x_1$ |
| --- | --- |
| 0.11698 | 0.01121 |
| 0.01121 | 0.01167 |
| 0.01167 | 0.01167 |

**[2.0]** Thus,

$$
\varphi_1=-\sin^{-1}(\sqrt{0.01167}),\qquad\varphi_1=-6.2^\circ\approx-6^\circ.\qquad[2.0]
$$

An alternative solution is to try to find this using the plotting method, but one needs to make sure the result is obtained to the equivalent precision.
