## Условие

An astrophotographer, based on the Equator, uses a good digital camera on a tripod, but with no tracking. The camera has a telescopic lens with focal length of 150 mm and aperture (objective diameter) of 84 mm. The sensor has an effective light collecting diameter of 22.5 mm. The photographic target is a star field at the observer’s Zenith.

**a)** Calculate the field of view (the angular width of the image) which can be captured on the sensor using this equipment. Give the answer in degrees. **[2 т.]**

**b)** The pixels in the camera’s sensor are separated by a distance of $3.23\ \mu\mathrm m$. What is the maximum possible exposure time for a single frame, so that no star trails appear on the exposed image? Give the answer in seconds. **[5 т.]**

**c)** For a better-quality image of the star field, the photographer decides to take multiple shots at the exposure time calculated in b) and then to stack them together. The total time for all these shots is 30 seconds (ignore any time taken to write data to the memory card). What fraction of the total field of view is always visible throughout the shots? Give the answer as a number between 0 and 1. **[3 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a)** $\alpha\approx8.58^\circ$.
- **b)** $t_1\approx0.3\ \mathrm s$.
- **c)** 98.5% (the official key’s printed form).

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**(a)** By simple right angle triangle,

$$
\mathrm{FOV}=2\times\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\text{sensor width}}{2\times\text{Focal Length}}\right)=2\times\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{22.5}{2\times150}\right),
$$

$$
\alpha\approx8.58^\circ.\qquad[2.0]
$$

**(b)** Number of pixels on the sensor will be given by,

$$
N=\frac{\text{Sensor width}}{\text{pixel width}}=\frac{22.5}{3.23\times10^{-3}}=6965.94.\qquad[1.0]
$$

As the number of pixels have to be integer, we take $N=6966$. **[1.0]** Angular coverage of each pixel will be,

$$
\text{Pixel view}=\frac{8.578^\circ}{6966}=0.00123^\circ/\text{pixel}\approx4.43''/\text{pixel}.\qquad[1.0]
$$

As the stars complete one full circle in 23.9344 hours, **[1.0]**

**Marking note:** student loses this one mark if $15^\circ$ per hour is used.

$$
t_1=1.23\times10^{-3}\frac{23.9344^{\mathrm h}}{360}=0.294\ \mathrm s\approx0.3\ \mathrm s.\qquad[1.0]
$$

(in reality this is probably a factor of 10 smaller than the eye would detect)

**(c)** In 30 seconds, the sky will move by,

$$
\Delta\alpha=\frac{360^\circ}{23.9344\times120}=0.125342^\circ.\qquad[1.0]
$$

$$
\therefore\frac{\alpha-\Delta\alpha}{\alpha}=\frac{8.578-0.125342}{8.578}=98.5\%.\qquad[2.0]
$$

(or $8.453^\circ$ is total field of view in high resolution images from the stack)

**Marking note:** Only penalise $15^\circ/\mathrm{hr}$ once in the whole solution.

> **Source note:** The English key’s reprinted part (c) asks “What proportion of the total field of view is possible at the higher signal to noise ratio?”, while the primary bilingual question asks the fraction always visible and specifies a number between 0 and 1. The key prints 98.5%. Its exposure-time line prints hours in the numerator and seconds in the result without an explicit hour-to-second conversion. Both printed forms and both marking notes are retained.


</Spoiler>
