## Условие

Consider a satellite observing X-ray sources, while orbiting the Earth in the equatorial plane with orbital radius $r$, and orbital time period $P$. Assume that this satellite is pointed in one fixed direction in space for a given length of time. Take the radius of the Earth as $R$.

When the satellite moves ‘behind’ the Earth in its orbit, the X-ray source is ‘occulted’ and the measured X-ray flux from the source drops to zero. However, due to Earth’s atmosphere, this drop is gradual. If the line of sight to the source passes through the atmosphere, the extent of the attenuation depends on the air-mass (i.e. length of air column) along the line of sight.

**a)** Let us assume that the satellite is pointing towards a fixed source at $0^\circ$ declination. We consider that the source is occulted when 50% of the light coming from the source gets attenuated due to the atmosphere. Let us say that this happens when the minimum height of the line of sight from the surface of the Earth is $h$. If $\theta_0$ is the angle between the direction of the source and the direction of the Earth, as measured from the spacecraft, find an expression for $\theta_0$. **[1 т.]**

**b)** The time duration $\Delta t$ between the source getting attenuated from 90% of the pre-occultation flux to 10% is defined as the ‘occultation time’ for the source. Assume the flux attenuates to 90% when the minimum height of the line of sight $(h+0.5\Delta h)$ and similarly the flux attenuates to 10% at $(h-0.5\Delta h)$, where $\Delta h\ll R$. Find the expression for $\Delta t$ in terms of $r$, $P$, $\Delta h$ and $\theta_0$. **[4 т.]**

**c)** If the satellite was pointing towards a source at an arbitrary declination $\beta$ instead (where $\beta$ is not too large), what will be the expression for $\Delta t$? **[15 т.]**

Note: If the satellite was not in the equatorial plane, then the problem could have been simply rephrased by assuming the satellite’s orbital plane to be the equatorial plane. In that case, $\beta$ becomes ‘relative declination’.

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a)** $\theta_0=\sin^{-1}((R+h)/r)$.
- **b)** $\Delta t=P\Delta h/(2\pi r\cos\theta_0)$.
- **c)** $\Delta t=\dfrac{P\Delta h\tan\theta}{2\pi r}\dfrac{\sin\theta_0}{\sin^2\theta_0-\tan^2\beta}$.

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**(a)** The solution for first part is obvious from the figure below.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '49.6%', '--fig-max': '318px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/gecaa-2020-theory-x/p6-sol-fig1.png" alt="Entire native satellite/source/Earth occultation diagram, Earth and full atmospheric circles, tangent source arrow, r/R+h/theta0 labels and both radius lines." width="993" height="573" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


$$
\sin\theta_0=\left(\frac{R+h}r\right),\qquad\therefore\theta_0=\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{R+h}r\right).\qquad[1.0]
$$

**(b)** Let us say the satelite has to move in the orbit by $\Delta\theta/2$ for $0.5\Delta h/2$ change in height.

$$
\sin\left(\theta_0\pm\frac{\Delta\theta}2\right)=\left(\frac{R+h\pm0.5\Delta h}r\right).\qquad[1.0]
$$

$$
\sin\left(\theta_0+\frac{\Delta\theta}2\right)-\sin\left(\theta_0-\frac{\Delta\theta}2\right)=\left(\frac{\Delta h}r\right),
$$

$$
\therefore2\cos\theta_0\sin\left(\frac{\Delta\theta}2\right)=\left(\frac{\Delta h}r\right),
$$

$$
2\times\frac{\Delta\theta}2\cos\theta_0=\left(\frac{\Delta h}r\right).\qquad[1.0]
$$

$$
\Delta\theta=\left(\frac{\Delta h}{r\cos\theta_0}\right)=\frac{2\pi\Delta t}P.\qquad[1.0]
$$

$$
\therefore\Delta t=\left(\frac{P\Delta h}{2\pi r\cos\theta_0}\right).\qquad[1.0]
$$

**(c)** Let $\theta$ be the new angle for 50% attenuation. In the diagram below, The line of sight from the satelite (S) to the source is at minimum distance from the centre of the earth (C) at point A. The points S, C and B are in the equatorial plane with line BA normal to the equatorial plane. One also realises that the line BS is normal to the plane defined by A, B and C. **[3.0]**

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '61.5%', '--fig-max': '394px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/gecaa-2020-theory-x/p6-sol-fig2.png" alt="Entire spatial occultation triangle with S satellite,C Earth Centre,A,B,all line segments/radii, complete source arrow,a/b/c/r/R+h/theta/beta labels." width="1231" height="976" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


$$
a=r\cos\theta,\qquad c=r\sin\theta,\qquad b=a\tan\beta,
$$

$$
(R+h)^2=b^2+c^2=(r\cos\theta\tan\beta)^2+(r\sin\theta)^2.\qquad[3.0]
$$

$$
\therefore\frac{(R+h)^2}{r^2}=\cos^2\theta\tan^2\beta+\sin^2\theta=(1-\sin^2\theta)\tan^2\beta+\sin^2\theta=\tan^2\beta+\sin^2\theta(1-\tan^2\beta),
$$

$$
\therefore\sin^2\theta=\left(\frac{(R+h)^2}{r^2}-\tan^2\beta\right)\left(\frac1{1-\tan^2\beta}\right).\qquad[1.0]
$$

Let us say $\tan^2\beta=k$.

$$
\therefore\sin\theta=\sqrt{\left(\frac{(R+h)^2}{r^2}-k\right)\left(\frac1{1-k}\right)}.
$$

Using similar analysis as the previous part,

$$
\Delta\theta\cos\theta=\sqrt{\frac1{1-k}}\left[\sqrt{\frac{(R+h+\frac{\Delta h}2)^2}{r^2}-k}-\sqrt{\frac{(R+h-\frac{\Delta h}2)^2}{r^2}-k}\right].\qquad[2.0]
$$

$$
=\sqrt{\frac1{1-k}}\left(\frac{\Delta h(R+h)}{r^2}\right)\left(\frac{(R+h)^2}{r^2}-k\right)^{-0.5}.\qquad[2.0]
$$

$$
=\sqrt{\frac1{1-k}\left(\frac{(R+h)^2}{r^2}-k\right)}\left(\frac{\Delta h}r\times\frac{R+h}r\right)\left(\left(\frac{R+h}r\right)^2-k\right)^{-1}.\qquad[2.0]
$$

$$
=\sin\theta\left(\frac{\Delta h\sin\theta_0}r\right)(\sin^2\theta_0-\tan^2\beta)^{-1},
$$

$$
\therefore\Delta t=\left(\frac{P\Delta h\tan\theta}{2\pi r}\right)\left(\frac{\sin\theta_0}{\sin^2\theta_0-\tan^2\beta}\right).\qquad[1.0]
$$

> **Source note:** The key’s opening prose in (b) prints $0.5\Delta h/2$, while the immediately following formula uses $0.5\Delta h$. The extra divisor in the printed prose is retained; the derivation is transcribed as printed.
