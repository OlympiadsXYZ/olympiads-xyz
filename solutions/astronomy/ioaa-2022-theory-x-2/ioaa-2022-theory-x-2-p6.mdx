*International Olympiad on Astronomy and Astrophysics 2022 Georgia — Theoretical Competition · 16 август 2022 г. · 20 т.*

## Условие

We observe a binary system, at a distance $d = 89\,\mathrm{pc}$, with a circular, edge-on orbit around the common center of mass and period of 100 days. Our space-based telescope is of diameter $D = 10\,\mathrm{m}$ and operates at a wavelength $\lambda = 364\,\mathrm{nm}$. We notice that for a total of 38 days during each full period, the two stars cannot be resolved by this telescope as separate objects. The wavelength of peak emission for star $A$ is $\lambda_A = 500\,\mathrm{nm}$ and that for star $B$ is $\lambda_B = 600\,\mathrm{nm}$.

**(a)** What are the temperatures of the stars $A$ and $B$, ($T_A$, $T_B$) **[2 т.]**

**(b)** Calculate the distance $l$ between the stars. **[6 т.]**

**(c)** Calculate the sum of the masses of the stars ($M_T$). **[2 т.]**

Combined photometry of the system:

| | Configuration | $U_0$ | $(U - B)$ | $(B - V)$ | $BC$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stars next to each other | 6.39 | 0.2 | 0.1 | 0.1 |
| 2 | B transiting in front of A | 6.86 | 0.25 | 0.12 | 0.175 |

The interstellar extinction per kpc of $U$ is $a_U = 1.4\,\mathrm{mag/kpc}$. The ratio of the densities of the stars $\rho_A/\rho_B = 0.7$

note: For Bolometric correction we use convention:

$$
BC = m_{bol} - m_V
$$

**(d)** Calculate the masses of both the stars ($M_A$, $M_B$). **[10 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **(a)** $T_A = 5978\,\mathrm{K}$, $T_B = 4830\,\mathrm{K}$
- **(b)** 1.45 au
- **(c)** 40.7 M<sub>sun</sub>
- **(d)** $M_A = 29.3M_{\odot}$, $M_B = 11.4M_{\odot}$

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

(a) From the Wien’s displacement law the temperature of a black body is related to a peak wavelength of emission by the relation:

$$
\begin{aligned} \lambda_{max} T &= b && \mathbf{1.0} \\ T_A &= 5978K && \mathbf{0.5} \\ T_B &= 4830K && \mathbf{0.5} \end{aligned}
$$

(b) Due to the diffraction minimal angular separation between two celestial objects to distinguish them:

$$
\delta = 1.22\frac{\lambda}{D} = 4.44 \times 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{rad} = 9.16\,\mathrm{mas} \qquad \mathbf{1.0}
$$

Now let us derive the time dependence of the angular separation between two stars as seen from the earth

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '85.1%', '--fig-max': '545px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/ioaa-2022-theory-x-2/p6-sol-fig1.png" alt="Two concentric circles (orbits) around the centre of mass C; star B on the inner circle and star A on the outer circle joined by a line of length l through C; the angle θ is measured from a dashed vertical line through C; horizontal rays from B and A go right to the label Observer, and their separation is marked s." width="1703" height="927" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

As it is seen from the figure, distance $s = l\cos\theta$, where $l$ is the distance between stars and $\theta$ is the angle of rotation of the stars about their center of mass after the stars are seen with maximum angular separation. The angular separation seen by an observer on the earth:

$$
\gamma = \frac{s}{d} = \frac{l}{d}\cos\theta \qquad \mathbf{1.0}
$$

The 38 days, during which objects are seen as a one object the system rotates by the angle $\beta = 2\pi\frac{38}{100}$. **1.0**

Thus, the corresponding starting angle $\theta'$ at which stars are no longer distinguishable:

$$
\theta' = \frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{\beta}{4} = \frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{19\pi}{100} = \frac{31\pi}{100} \qquad \mathbf{1.5}
$$

and this is the moment, when stars stop to be distinguishable. Thus, corresponding angular separation on the sky is $\delta$:

$$
\delta = \frac{l}{d}\cos\theta'
$$

From which one obtains

$$
l = \frac{\delta d}{\cos\theta'} = 1.45\,\mathrm{au} \qquad \mathbf{1.5}
$$

(c) From the Kepler’s 3rd law:

$$
\begin{aligned} T^2 &= \frac{4\pi^2 l^3}{G(M_A + M_B)} \\ M_A + M_B &= \frac{4\pi^2 l^3}{GT^2} \\ &= 40.7M_{\odot} && \mathbf{2.0} \end{aligned}
$$

(d) From given data for case 1:

$$
\begin{aligned} (U - V)_1 &= (U - B)_1 + (B - V)_1 = 0.3 \\ U_1 &= U_{0_1} + a_U d = 6.51 \\ V_1 &= U_1 - (U - V)_1 = 6.21 \\ m_{Bol_1} &= V_1 + BC_1 = 6.31 && \mathbf{1.5} \end{aligned}
$$

Similarly for second configuration:

$$
\begin{aligned} (U - V)_2 &= (U - B)_2 + (B - V)_2 = 0.37 \\ U_2 &= U_{0_2} + a_U d = 6.98 \\ V_2 &= U_2 - (U - V)_2 = 6.61 \\ m_{Bol_2} &= V_2 + BC_2 = 6.79 && \mathbf{1.0} \end{aligned}
$$

The difference in bolometric magnitudes:

$$
\begin{aligned} m_{bol_2} - m_{bol_1} &= -2.5\log\left(\frac{L_2}{L_1}\right) \\ &= -2.5\log\left(\frac{\pi(R_A^2 - R_B^2)\sigma T_A^4 + \pi R_B^2 \sigma T_B^4}{\pi R_A^2 \sigma T_A^4 + \pi R_B^2 \sigma T_B^4}\right) && \mathbf{1.5} \\ \therefore 6.79 - 6.31 &= -2.5\log\left(\frac{(R_A^2 - R_B^2)T_A^4 + R_B^2 T_B^4}{R_A^2 T_A^4 + R_B^2 T_B^4}\right) \\ &= -2.5\log\left(\frac{(R_A^2/R_B^2 - 1)T_A^4 + T_B^4}{T_A^4 R_A^2/R_B^2 + T_B^4}\right) \\ \therefore 0.48 &= -2.5\log\left[\frac{\left(\frac{R_A^2}{R_B^2} - 1\right) + \frac{T_B^4}{T_A^4}}{\frac{T_B^4}{T_A^4} + \frac{R_A^2}{R_B^2}}\right] && \mathbf{1.0} \\ \therefore 10^{\frac{-0.48}{2.5}} &= \frac{\frac{R_A^2}{R_B^2} - 1 + \frac{T_B^4}{T_A^4}}{\frac{T_B^4}{T_A^4} + \frac{R_A^2}{R_B^2}} = 10^{-0.19} = 0.65 && \mathbf{1.0} \\ \frac{R_A^2}{R_B^2} - 1 + \frac{T_B^4}{T_A^4} &= 0.65\frac{T_B^4}{T_A^4} + 0.65\frac{R_A^2}{R_B^2} \\ 0.35\frac{R_A^2}{R_B^2} &= 1 - 0.35\frac{T_B^4}{T_A^4} \\ &= 1 - 0.35 \times 0.482 = 0.83 \\ \therefore \frac{R_A}{R_B} &= \sqrt{\frac{0.83}{0.35}} = 1.54 && \mathbf{1.0} \\ \frac{M_A}{M_B} &= \frac{\rho_A R_A^3}{\rho_B R_B^3} = 1.54^3 \cdot 0.7 = 2.56 && \mathbf{1.0} \\ \text{But, } M_A + M_B &= 40.7M_{\odot} && \mathbf{1.0} \\ \therefore M_B &= \frac{40.7M_{\odot}}{3.56} = 11.4M_{\odot} && \mathbf{1.0} \\ M_B &= 29.3M_{\odot} \end{aligned}
$$


</Spoiler>
