*Data Analysis Examination · 50 т.*

## Условие

Through systematic searches during the past decades, astronomers have found a large number of millisecond pulsars (spin period $< 10\ \mathrm{ms}$). Majority of these pulsars are found in binaries, with nearly circular orbits.

For a pulsar in a binary orbit, the measured pulsar spin period ($P$) and the measured line-of-sight acceleration ($a$) both vary systematically due to orbital motion. For circular orbits, this variation can be described mathematically in terms of orbital phase $\phi$ ($0 \le \phi \le 2\pi$) as,

$$
P(\phi) = P_0 + P_{\mathrm{t}} \cos\phi \qquad \text{where } P_t = \frac{2\pi P_0 r}{c P_{\mathrm{B}}}
$$

$$
a(\phi) = -a_{\mathrm{t}} \sin\phi \qquad \text{where } a_{\mathrm{t}} = \frac{4\pi^2 r}{P_{\mathrm{B}}^2}
$$

where $P_{\mathrm{B}}$ is the orbital period of the binary, $P_0$ is the intrinsic spin period of the pulsar and $r$ is the radius of the orbit.

The following table gives one such set of measurements of $P$ and $a$ at different heliocentric epochs, $T$, expressed in truncated Modified Julian Days (tMJD), i.e. number of days since MJD = 2,440,000.

| No. | T (tMJD) | P (μs) | a (m s$^{-2}$) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5740.654 | 7587.8889 | - 0.92 ± 0.08 |
| 2 | 5740.703 | 7587.8334 | - 0.24 ± 0.08 |
| 3 | 5746.100 | 7588.4100 | - 1.68 ± 0.04 |
| 4 | 5746.675 | 7588.5810 | + 1.67 ± 0.06 |
| 5 | 5981.811 | 7587.8836 | + 0.72 ± 0.06 |

| 7 | 6005.893 | 7589.1029 | + 0.52 ± 0.08 |
| 8 | 6040.857 | 7589.1350 | + 0.00 ± 0.04 |
| 9 | 6335.904 | 7589.1358 | + 0.00 ± 0.02 |

By plotting $a(\phi)$ as a function of $P(\phi)$, we can obtain a parametric curve. As evident from the relations above, this curve in the period-acceleration plane is an ellipse.

In this problem, we estimate the intrinsic spin period, $P_0$, the orbital period, $P_{\mathrm{B}}$, and the orbital radius, $r$, by an analysis of this data set, assuming a circular orbit.

**(D1.1)** Plot the data, including error bars, in the period-acceleration plane (mark your graph as “D1.1”). **[7 т.]**

**(D1.2)** Draw an ellipse that appears to be a best fit to the data (on the same graph “D1.1”). **[2 т.]**

**(D1.3)** From the plot, estimate $P_0$, $P_{\mathrm{t}}$ and $a_{\mathrm{t}}$, including error margins. **[7 т.]**

**(D1.4)** Write expressions for $P_{\mathrm{B}}$ and $r$ in terms of $P_0$, $P_{\mathrm{t}}$, $a_{\mathrm{t}}$. **[4 т.]**

**(D1.5)** Calculate approximate value of $P_{\mathrm{B}}$ and $r$ based on your estimations made in (D1.3), including error margins. **[6 т.]**

**(D1.6)** Calculate orbital phase, $\phi$, corresponding to the epochs of the following five observations in the above table: data rows 1, 4, 6, 8, 9. **[4 т.]**

**(D1.7)** Refine the estimate of the orbital period, $P_{\mathrm{B}}$, using the results in part (D1.6) in the following way:

**(D1.7a)** First determine the initial epoch, $T_0$, which corresponds to the nearest epoch of zero orbital phase before the first observation. **[2 т.]**

**(D1.7b)** The expected time, $T_{\mathrm{calc}}$, of the estimated orbital phase angle of each observation is given by,

$$
T_{\mathrm{calc}} = T_0 + \left(n + \frac{\phi}{360^{\circ}}\right) P_{\mathrm{B}},
$$

where $n$ is the number of full cycle of orbital phases that may have elapsed between $T_0$ and $T$ (or $T_{\mathrm{calc}}$). Estimate $n$ and $T_{\mathrm{calc}}$ for each of the five observations in part (D1.6). Note down difference $T_{\mathrm{O-C}}$ between observed $T$ and $T_{\mathrm{calc}}$. Enter these calculations in the table given in the Summary Answersheet. **[7 т.]**

**(D1.7c)** Plot $T_{\mathrm{O-C}}$ against $n$ (mark your graph as “D1.7”). **[4 т.]**

**(D1.7d)** Determine the refined values of the initial epoch, $T_{0,\mathrm{r}}$, and the orbital period, $P_{\mathrm{B,r}}$. **[7 т.]**

**(D1.4)**

We can easily recover the orbital period ($P_{\mathrm{B}}$) and the radius of the orbit ($r$) in a circular orbit:

$$
a_{\mathrm{t}} = \frac{4\pi^2}{P_{\mathrm{B}}^2} r
$$

$$
\therefore r = \frac{P_{\mathrm{B}}^2 a_{\mathrm{t}}}{4\pi^2}
$$

**[1.0]**

$$
P_{\mathrm{t}} = \frac{2\pi P_0}{P_{\mathrm{B}}} \times \frac{r}{c} = \frac{2\pi P_0}{P_{\mathrm{B}} c} \times \frac{P_{\mathrm{B}}^2 a_{\mathrm{t}}}{4\pi^2} = \frac{P_0 P_{\mathrm{B}} a_{\mathrm{t}}}{2\pi c}
$$

$$
\therefore \boxed{P_{\mathrm{B}} = \frac{P_{\mathrm{t}}}{P_0}\frac{2\pi c}{a_{\mathrm{t}}}}
$$

**[1.0]**

$$
r = \frac{a_{\mathrm{t}}}{4\pi^2}\left(\frac{P_{\mathrm{t}}}{P_0}\frac{2\pi c}{a_{\mathrm{t}}}\right)^2
$$

**[1.0]**

$$
\therefore \boxed{r = \left(\frac{P_{\mathrm{t}}}{P_0}\right)^2 \frac{c^2}{a_{\mathrm{t}}}}
$$

**[1.0]**

**Alternative algebraic routes accepted. Each of $P_{\mathrm{B}}$ and $r$ carry 2.0 marks.**

**(D1.5)**

$$
P_{\mathrm{B}} = \frac{P_{\mathrm{t}}}{P_0}\frac{2\pi c}{a_{\mathrm{t}}} = \frac{0.67}{7588.48} \times \frac{2\pi \times 2.998 \times 10^8}{1.71}\,\mathrm{s} = 96\,260\ \mathrm{s} = 1.125\,70\ \mathrm{d}
$$

**[1.0]**

$$
\Delta P_{\mathrm{B}} = P_{\mathrm{B}}\sqrt{\left(\frac{\Delta P_{\mathrm{t}}}{P_{\mathrm{t}}}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\Delta P_0}{P_0}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\Delta a_{\mathrm{t}}}{a_{\mathrm{t}}}\right)^2}
$$

**[1.0]**

$$
= 1.12570 \times \sqrt{\left(\frac{0.02}{0.67}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{0.02}{7588.48}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{0.06}{1.71}\right)^2}\,\mathrm{d} = 1.12570 \times 0.0461 \simeq 0.052\ \mathrm{d}
$$

$$
\therefore \boxed{P_{\mathrm{B}} = (1.13 \pm 0.05)\ \mathrm{d}}
$$

**[1.0]**

$$
r = \left(\frac{P_{\mathrm{t}}}{P_0}\right)^2 \frac{c^2}{a_{\mathrm{t}}} = \left(\frac{0.67}{7588.48}\right)^2 \times \frac{\left(2.998 \times 10^8\right)^2}{1.71}\,\mathrm{m}
$$

$$
\therefore r = 4.097\,39 \times 10^8\ \mathrm{m} = 2.738\,90 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{AU}
$$

**[1.0]**

$$
\Delta r = r\sqrt{\left(\frac{2\Delta P_{\mathrm{t}}}{P_{\mathrm{t}}}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{2\Delta P_0}{P_0}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\Delta a_{\mathrm{t}}}{a_{\mathrm{t}}}\right)^2}
$$

**[1.0]**

$$
= 2.738\,90 \times 10^{-3} \times \sqrt{\left(\frac{2 \times 0.02}{0.67}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{2 \times 0.02}{7588.48}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{0.06}{1.71}\right)^2}\,\mathrm{AU} = 2.738\,90 \times 10^{-3} \times 0.069\,25\ \mathrm{AU} \simeq 0.19 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{AU}
$$

$$
\boxed{r = (2.74 \pm 0.19) \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{AU}}
$$

**[1.0]**

Errors in $P_{\mathrm{B}}$ and $r$ can be also estimated as maximum possible (worst case) error. In such case, errors would be about 1.5 times the standard error calculated above ($\delta P_{\mathrm{B}} = 0.07, \delta r = 0.25$).

**(D1.6)**

Using these newly determined orbital parameters, we can calculate the angular orbital phase for each data point, i.e., for each pair of acceleration and period measured ($P$, $a$).

$$
\phi = \tan^{-1}\left(-\frac{a}{a_{\mathrm{t}}}\frac{P_{\mathrm{t}}}{P - P_0}\right)
$$

Care has to be taken to choose the value of the phase from among $\phi, \pi \pm \phi, 2\pi - \phi$, depending on the sign of $\cos\phi$ and $\sin\phi$.
