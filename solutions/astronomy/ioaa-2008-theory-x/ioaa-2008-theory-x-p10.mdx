## Условие

The coordinates of the components of Visual Binary Star $\mu$ Sco on August 22, 2008 are given in the table below

| | $\alpha$ (RA) | $\delta$ (Dec) |
|---|---|---|
| $\mu$ Sco 1 (primary) | $20^{\mathrm{h}}\ 17^{\mathrm{m}}\ 38^{\mathrm{s}}.90$ | -12˚ 30' 30" |
| $\mu$ Sco 2 (secondary) | $20^{\mathrm{h}}\ 18^{\mathrm{m}}\ 03^{\mathrm{s}}.30$ | -12˚ 32' 41" |

The stars are observed using Zeiss refractor telescope at the Bosscha Observatory with aperture and focal length are 600 mm and 10 780 mm, respectively. The telescope is equipped with 765 × 510 pixels CCD camera. The pixel size of the chip is 9 µm × 9 µm.

**a.** Can both components of the binary be inside the frame? (“YES” or “NO”, show it in your computation!)

**b.** What is the position angle of the secondary star, with respect to the North?

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

a. Chip size: $(9\times765)\ \mu\mathrm m\times(9\times510)\ \mu\mathrm m=6885\ \mu\mathrm m\times4590\ \mu\mathrm m\approx6.9\text{ mm}\times4.6\text{ mm}$ **(10%)**

For Zeiss telescope: FL = 10780 mm

Pixel size (arc sec) = 206265 × pixel size (μm)/(1000 × focal length (mm))″ **(10%)**

$$
=206265\times9/(1000\times10780)=0.1722''\qquad(10\%)
$$

FOV CCD chip $=(765\times510)\times0.1722=131.7330\times87.8220''=2.1956\times1.4637'$ **(10%)**

For $\mu$ Sco 1: $\alpha_1=20^{\mathrm h}17^{\mathrm m}38^{\mathrm s}.90=20.2941^{\mathrm h}=304.4121^\circ$

$$
\delta_1=-12^\circ30'30''=-12.5083^\circ
$$

For $\mu$ Sco 2: $\alpha_2=20^{\mathrm h}18^{\mathrm m}03^{\mathrm s}.30=20.3009^{\mathrm h}=304.5138^\circ$

$$
\delta_2=-12^\circ32'41''=-12.5447^\circ
$$

Let the angular separation between the stars be $\gamma$

Use spherical trigonometry:

$$
\cos\gamma=\cos(90^\circ-\delta_1)\cos(90^\circ-\delta_2)+\sin(90^\circ-\delta_1)\sin(90^\circ-\delta_2)\cos(\alpha_2-\alpha_1)\qquad(10\%)
$$

$$
\cos\gamma=\cos(90^\circ-(-12.5083))\cos(90^\circ-(-12.5447))+\sin(90^\circ-(-12.5083))\sin(90^\circ-(-12.5447))\cos(304.5138^\circ-304.4121^\circ)
$$

$$
\cos\gamma=\cos(102.5083^\circ)\cos(102.5447^\circ)+\sin(102.5083^\circ)\sin(102.5447^\circ)\cos(0.1017^\circ)=0.999998298\qquad(10\%)
$$

$$
\gamma=0.1057^\circ=6.3424'
$$

NO, because $\gamma>\sqrt{2.1956^2+1.4637^2}$ (diagonal of FOV Chip). **(10%)**

b. Position angle ($S_1$):

$$
\frac{\sin(\alpha_2-\alpha_1)}{\sin\gamma}=\frac{\sin S_1}{\sin(90^\circ-\delta_2)}
$$

$$
\sin S_1=\frac{\sin(0^\circ6'6'')\sin(102^\circ32'41'')}{\sin(6'.1020)}=0.9388
$$

$$
S_1=69.8535^\circ\text{ or }110.1466^\circ\qquad(20\%)
$$

From the figure above, we found that the correct position angle is $110.15^\circ$ **(10%)**

($\pm6^\circ$ is full mark)
