2I/Borisov is the first observed interstellar comet. It was discovered on August 30 this year by the Crimean astronomer G. V. Borisov with a 65-cm telescope of his own development. The point on celestial sphere from where the comet moved before entering the Solar System is located near the star Ruchbah in the constellation Cassiopeia. On December 7, the comet will pass the perihelion of its orbit at a speed of 43 km/s relative to the Sun, being 2.01 au from it.

### 5.1.

Will it be possible to observe the star Ruchbah from Piatra Neamt tonight?

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="part-2">

### 5.2.

Estimate how long ago the comet 2I/Borisov passed near the star Ruchbah.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="part-3">

### 5.3.

Calculate whether our Sun is visible with the naked eye from the vicinity of the Ruchbah star (write the answer in English, “Yes” or “No”).

</ProblemSection>

*By the way. The comet was discovered at the observational round place of the IV and VI IAO (1999, 2001), and G. V. Borisov was a member of the Organizing Committee of these IAOs.*

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-1">

### 5.1.

Sure, we can. The entire constellation of Cassiopeia is circumpolar in Piatra Neamt. To confirm, you can find the height of Ruchbah at the lower culmination:

$$
h=\varphi-90°+\delta=17°10'.
$$

Thus, the star is always above the horizon, it can be observed at any clearly night.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-2">

### 5.2.

According to the law of conservation of energy, the perihelion speed of the comet and its speed at infinity are related by the equation:

$$
mV_P^2/2-GMm/R_P=mV_\infty^2/2,
$$
$$
V_\infty^2=V_P^2-2GM/R_P,
$$
$$
V_\infty=(V_P^2-2GM/R_P)^{1/2}=(43000^2-2\cdot6.67\cdot10^{-11}\cdot2\cdot10^{30}/(2.01\cdot1.5\cdot10^{11}))^{1/2}\approx31\ \mathrm{km/s}.
$$

The comet makes almost its entire journey away from the stars. Therefore, most of the way (namely, relatively speaking, outside the Solar System) it traveled from the vicinity of the Ruchbah star to us with a speed $V_\infty\approx31\ \mathrm{km/s}$ relative to the Earth.

Let us find the distance to Ruchbah.

$$
D=1/p=1/0.0328\approx30.5\ \text{Пк}.
$$

It would seem that it is enough to calculate the speed $V$ in units of pc/year ($3.2\cdot10^{-5}$ pc/year is obtained), divide 30.5 pc by this value and get an answer: approximately 960,000 years. Alas, we forgot that Ruchbah also moves relative to the Sun, as indicated by the radial velocity given in the table $V_r=-6.7\ \mathrm{km/s}$ (the star is approaching us). Thus, this star is approaching us, earlier it was at a farther distance and it took more time to overcome the path of the Solar system. Relative Ruchbah, the comet moves at a speed

$$
V_1=V_\infty+V_r\approx24.3\ \mathrm{km/s},
$$

Or in another units, about $3.2\cdot10^{-5}$ pc/year. Dividing 30.5 pc by this value, we get the answer: about 1.2 million years.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-3">

### 5.3.

As we previously found out, the distance to Ruchbah is:

$$
D=1/p=1/0.0328\approx30.5\ \mathrm{pc}.
$$

The sun at such a distance will have a magnitude

$$
\begin{aligned}m_1&=m_0+5^{\mathrm m}\log(D/R_0)=-26.8^{\mathrm m}-5^{\mathrm m}\log(pR_0)\\
&=-26.8^{\mathrm m}+5^{\mathrm m}\log(206265/0.0328)\\
&=-26.8^{\mathrm m}+34.0^{\mathrm m}\approx7.2^{\mathrm m}.\end{aligned}
$$

Невооружённым глазом можно видеть только звёзды $7.2^{\mathrm m}$ и ярче. Ответ: «No».

With the naked eye, only stars $7.2^{\mathrm m}$ and brighter can be seen. The answer is «No».

</ProblemSection>
