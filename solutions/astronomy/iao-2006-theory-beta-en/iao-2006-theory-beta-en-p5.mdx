These are two photos of the Moon taken by the same camera mounted on a lunar satellite. The first photo has been made while the satellite was near its periselenion and the second one near the aposelenion. Estimate from these data the maximum possible period of orbit around the Moon of this satellite. Consider the lunar orbit around Earth as circular.

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

The hemisphere of the Moon at photos is about the same as we see it from the Earth.

Taking into account that the Moon is faced to us by one side all the times, it is possible to draw a conclusion that photographing was made from the points of space located approximately between the Earth and the Moon.

From the ratio of the sizes of images ($d_1\approx46.5$ mm, $d_2\approx40.5$ mm) we can find that the ratio of distances from the Moon to the satellite in periselenion “P” and aposelenion “A” is equal to $P/A=0.87$.

The satellite may be the satellite of only the Moon in the case even at the maximal distance from the Moon it would be in sphere of an attraction of the Moon, instead of the Earth. In other words, in case of the satellite in aposelenion is on a line the Moon – Earth, it should be closer to the Moon than the internal Lagrange point.

Let us find the position of this Lagrange point, the distance “$x$” from the center of the Moon. This point is rotating around the Earth with same angular speed $\omega$ as the Moon. Equation of the balance of a body in the Lagrange point is:

$$
\frac{GM}{(R-x)^2}-\frac{Gm}{x^2}=\omega^2(R-x),
$$

where $M$ and $m$ – masses of Earth and Moon, $R$ – distance from the Earth to Moon. Since we have estimating type of the problem, let us consider that $x$ many times less than $R$, and reformat the first term as:

$$
\frac{GM}{(R-x)^2}=\frac{GM}{R^2}+\frac{2GMx}{R^3},
$$

so

$$
\frac{GM}{R^2}+\frac{2GMx}{R^3}-\frac{Gm}{x^2}=\omega^2(R-x).
$$

Equation of rotating of the Moon around the Earth is $GM/R^2=\omega^2R$ and we have: $\omega=\sqrt{GM/R^3}$.

By substituting this value to the third formula we have:

$$
\frac{GM}{R^2}+\frac{2GMx}{R^3}-\frac{Gm}{x^2}=\frac{GM}{R^3}(R-x),
$$

or $3GMx/R^3=Gm/x^2$.

So $x=R\sqrt[3]{m/(3M)}$.

By substituting the value $R=384400$ km and $m/M=1/81{,}3$, we can receive $x=R/6{,}25=61500$ km.

So the distance $A$ in aposelenion of the satellite orbit should not exceed 61 500 km, and one in periselenium should not exceed $P=P/A\times61500\ \mathrm{km}=53300$ km. So the major axis of the satellite’s orbit should not exceed $a=(1/2+P/2A)\times61500\ \mathrm{km}=57400$ km. The sidereal period of a satellite with such a major axis is equal to

$$
T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{a^3}{Gm}}
$$

or 14.3 days. The correct answer (taking into account that we made only estimations) – about 14 days.
