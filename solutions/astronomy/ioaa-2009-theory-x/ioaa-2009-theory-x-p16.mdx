## Условие

A projectile which starts from the surface of the Earth at the sea level is launched with the initial speed of $v_0=\sqrt{GM/R}$ and with the projecting angle (with respect to the local horizon) of $\theta=\pi/6$. $M$ and $R$ are the mass and radius of the Earth respectively. Ignore the air resistance and rotation of the Earth.

**a** Show that the orbit of the projectile is an ellipse with a semi-major axis of $a=R$.

**b** Calculate the highest altitude of the projectile with respect to the Earth surface (in the unit of the Earth radius).

**c** What is the range of the projectile (distance between launching point and falling point) in the units of the earth radii?

**d** What is eccentricity (e) of this elliptical orbit?

**e** Find the time of flight for the projectile.

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a** $a=R$
- **b** $R/2$
- **c** $2\pi R/3$
- **d** 0.5
- **e** 55.7 min

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-a">

### a)

Total energy of the projectile is

$$
E=\frac12mv_0^2-\frac{GMm}{R}=-\frac{GMm}{2R}<0.
$$

$E<0$ means that orbit might be ellipse or circle. As $\theta>0$, the orbit is an ellipse. Total energy for an ellipse is

$$
E=-\frac{GMm}{2a}.
$$

Then

$$
a=R.
$$

(7 points)

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-b">

### b)

In figure (1) we have

$$
OA+O'A=2a,\qquad O'A=a.
$$

In $OAO'$ triangle it is obvious that

$$
OC=CO'.
$$

Then $C$ must be the center of the ellipse with the initial velocity vector $v_0$ parallel to the ellipse major-axis ($LH$).

In figure (2)

$$
HM=CH-CM=a-(R-R\sin\theta)=R-R+R\sin\theta=R\sin\theta=\frac R2.
$$

(15 points)

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-c">

### c)

Range of the projectile is $\widehat{AB}$

$$
\widehat{AB}=2\left(\frac\pi2-\theta\right)R=(\pi-2\theta)R=\frac{2\pi}{3}R.
$$

(6 points)

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-d">

### d)

Start with ellipse equation in polar coordinates

$$
r=\frac{a(1-e^2)}{1+e\cos\varphi}.
$$

For point A

$$
R=\frac{R(1-e^2)}{1-e\cos(\frac\pi2+\theta)}.
$$

$$
e=\sin\theta=\frac12.
$$

(5 points)

Source note: the second denominator is printed with a minus sign, although this conflicts with the displayed result. The source formula is retained.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-e">

### e)

Using Kepler's second law

$$
\frac{\Delta S}{S_0}=\frac{\Delta T}{T}.
$$

$$
\Delta S=S_{AOBH}=S_{\triangle AOB}+\frac{S_0}{2}=2\times\frac{bae}{2}+\frac{\pi ab}{2}=bae+\frac{\pi ab}{2}.
$$

$$
\frac{\Delta S}{S}=\frac{bae+\frac{\pi ab}{2}}{\pi ab}=\frac{e+\frac\pi2}{\pi}=\frac{0.5+\frac\pi2}{\pi}.
$$

Kepler's third law

$$
T=\sqrt{\frac{4\pi^2R^3}{GM}}=84.5\ \mathrm{min}.
$$

$$
\Delta T=T\times\frac{0.5+\frac\pi2}{\pi}=55.7\ \mathrm{min}.
$$

(12 points)

</ProblemSection>
