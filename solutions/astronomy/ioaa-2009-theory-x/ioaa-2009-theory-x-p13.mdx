## Условие

Assume that you are living in the time of Copernicus and do not know anything about Kepler's laws. You might calculate Mars-Sun distance in the same way as he did. After accepting the revolutionary belief that all the planets are orbiting around the Sun, not around the Earth, you measure that the orbital period of Mars is 687 days, then you observe that 106 days after opposition of Mars, the planet appears in quadrature. Calculate Mars-Sun distance in astronomical unit (AU).

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- 1.52 AU

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

We know the orbital period of Mars then the angle $\angle M_1SM_2$ can be determined simply

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '46.4%', '--fig-max': '297px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/ioaa-2009-theory-x/p13-sol-fig1.png" alt="Earth and Mars at opposition and quadrature." width="928" height="768" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

$$
\angle M_1SM_2=\frac{106}{687}\times360=55.5^\circ.
$$

(2 points)

By the same way we determine $\angle E_1SE_2$:

$$
\angle E_1SE_2=\frac{106}{365}\times360=104.5^\circ.
$$

(2 points)

Then angle $\angle M_2SE_2$ is:

$$
\angle M_2SE_2=104.5-55.5=49.0^\circ.
$$

(2 points)

$$
\cos(\angle M_2SE_2)=\frac{SE_2}{SM_2}.
$$

(2 points)

$$
r_{\mathrm{mars}}=\frac{SM_2}{SE_2}=\frac1{\cos(\angle M_2SE_2)}=\frac1{\cos49.0^\circ}=1.52\ \mathrm{AU}.
$$

(2 points)

</Spoiler>
