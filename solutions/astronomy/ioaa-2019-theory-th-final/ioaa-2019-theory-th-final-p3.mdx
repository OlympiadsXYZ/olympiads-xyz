The first image of a black hole was constructed recently by the international team of the Event Horizon Telescope (EHT). The imaged area surrounds the supermassive black hole in the centre of the galaxy M87. The observations resulting in the final image were carried out at a wavelength $\lambda = 1.3\ \mathrm{mm}$ where the interstellar extinction is not prohibitively large.

<ProblemSection id="a">

### a) (6 т.)

How large an instrument would be needed to resolve the shadow (in effect the photon capture radius, which is three times the size of event horizon) of a supermassive black hole in the centre of a galaxy? Express the result as a function of the distance $d$ and the mass $M$ of the black hole.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="b">

### b)

Give the size of the instrument in units of Earth radius for

**i.** the supermassive black hole in the centre of M87,
($d_{\text{BH-M87}} = 5.5 \times 10^7\ \mathrm{ly}$, $M_{\text{BH-M87}} = 6.5 \times 10^{9}\ M_{\odot}$) **[1 т.]**

**ii.** and Sgr A*, the supermassive black hole of our own galaxy, Milky Way.  
($d_{\text{Sgr A*}} = 8.3\ \mathrm{kpc}$, $M_{\text{Sgr A*}} = 3.6 \times 10^{6}\ M_{\odot}$) **[1 т.]**

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="c">

### c) (2 т.)

What type of technology is needed for the development of such an instrument? **Mark the letter of your answer with × on the answer sheet.**

(A) Gravitational lensing by dark matter
(B) Interferometry with an array of radio telescopes
(C) Photon deceleration in a dense environment
(D) Reducing the effect of incoming wavefront distortions
(E) Neutrino focusing with strong electromagnetic fields

</ProblemSection>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-a">

### a)

The scale of the black hole shadow is set by the Schwarzschild radius $R_{\mathrm{S}} = 2GM/c^2$. The light ring radius is $3R_{\mathrm{S}}$. (2 p)  
For a diffraction limited instrument the angular resolution is bounded by

$$
\theta_{\mathrm{res}} = \frac{2R}{d} \geq \frac{1.22\lambda}{D} \quad (3.1) \quad (2\ \mathrm{p})
$$

where $\lambda$ is the wavelength and $D$ is the diameter of the instrument.  
Using the expression for the Schwarzschild radius:

$$
D \geq \frac{1.22\lambda}{6R_{\mathrm{S}}}\,d = [\frac{1.22c^2\lambda d}{12GM}] \quad (3.2) \quad (2\ \mathrm{p})
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-b">

### b)

For $\lambda = 1.3\ \mathrm{mm}$, this gives

(a) $D > [7200\ \mathrm{km}] \approx [1.1\ \mathrm{R}_{\oplus}]$ (1 p)  
(b) $D > [6400\ \mathrm{km}] \approx [1\ \mathrm{R}_{\oplus}]$ (1 p)

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-c">

### c)

(B) Interferometry with array of radio telescopes (2 p)

</ProblemSection>
