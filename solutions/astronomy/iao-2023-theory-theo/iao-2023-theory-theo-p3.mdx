### Русский

В ближайшем будущем люди посылают огромный космический корабль к планете в системе Альфа Центавра, пытаясь посетить инопланетян, проживающих на этой планете. У инопланетян голубая кожа и размеры любых органов примерно как у людей. Наблюдая за небом, они замечают, что космический корабль движется к ним по прямой линии с постоянной скоростью $0{,}152c$.

Для разгона и торможения корабль оснащён двумя мощными двигателями, каждый из которых вырабатывает конический пучок фотонов. Угол раствора конуса составляет 4,96 градуса. Выходная мощность одного двигателя равна $P=2{,}25\cdot10^{17}$ Вт, из которых 9,92% приходится на видимый свет.

**3.1.** На каком расстоянии тормозящий корабль сравняется по яркости с Солнцем для наблюдателей-центаврян?

**3.2.** Смогут ли при этом инопланетяне визуально различить на своём небе корабль и Солнце? Запишите ваши расчёты и ответьте (по-английски) “Yes” или “No”.

</ProblemSection>

### English

In the near future, humans will send a massive space ship towards a planet in the Alpha Centauri system, trying to visit the aliens residing on this planet. The aliens have blue skin and all their organs are similar in size to those of humans. When they observe the sky, they notice that the space ship travel towards them in a straight line with a constant speed of $0.152c$.

For acceleration and deceleration, the ship is equipped with two powerful engines, each of which produces a conic beam of pure photons. The cone angle of the beam is 4.96 degrees. The power output of an engine is $P=2.25\cdot10^{17}$ W, of which 9.92% is visible light.

**3.1.** Observed by the Centaurians, at what distance is the decelerating ship as bright as the Sun?

**3.2.** Herein, will the aliens be able to visually distinguish the ship and the Sun in their sky? Write down your calculations and answer (in English) with “Yes” or “No”.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-s31">

### 3.1.

Distance between the Sun and α Cen:

$$
r=\frac1\pi=\frac1{0.747}=1.34\ pc=4.36\ ly=276\,000\ au
$$

(the parallax of α Cen can be found in the table of stars and objects).

Flux of the Sun seen at α Cen:

$$
F_\odot=1367\ W/m^2\times\left(\frac{1\ au}{r}\right)^2
$$

(the value of the solar constant can be found in the table of constants).

Solid angle of the engine plume:

$$
s=\pi\frac{(4.96/2)^2}{(180/\pi)^2}=5.89\cdot10^{-3}
$$

Flux from the ship at a distance of $r_s$:

$$
F_s=\frac{2P\times0.0992}{sr_s^2}
$$

When the two fluxes equals, $F_\odot=F_s$.

$$
1367\ W/m^2\times\left(\frac{1\ au}{r}\right)^2=\frac{2P\times0.0992}{5.89\cdot10^{-3}\cdot r_s^2}\quad\Rightarrow\quad r_s=\sqrt{\frac{4.5\cdot10^{17}\ W\times0.0992\times(276\,000\ au/1\ au)^2}{1367\ W/m^2\times5.89\cdot10^{-3}}}
$$

$$
r_s=2.06\cdot10^{13}\ m=137\ au.
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-s32">

### 3.2.

Considering both the ship and Alpha Centauri move at constant speed, the relative direction of them ($A_iS_i$) remains the same to the departure positions (AE). So seen at Alpha Centauri, the angular distance of the ship and the sun ($\angle EA_iS_i$) increases, and reaches maximum just before the arrival. At the arrival, the angular distance is equal to $\angle AER$, which is actually the angular movement of Alpha Centauri during the transit time. Since the distance of Alpha Centauri does not change much during the years, the angular movement can easily be estimated by proper motion multiplied by duration.

There is no aberration since there is no apparent motion.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '100%', '--fig-max': '1411px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/iao-2023-theory-theo/p3-travel.png" alt="Geometry of Earth, Alpha Centauri and ship rendezvous" width="4409" height="1026" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


TLDR: $A_iS_i\parallel AE$, $\max(\angle EAS)=\angle AER=$ proper motion × duration

Total proper motion:

$$
\mu=\sqrt{3.614''^2+0.803''^2}=3.7''/yr
$$

Ship transit time:

$$
t=\frac rv=\frac{4.36\ ly}{0.152c}=28.6\ yr
$$

Maximum angle:

$$
\angle REA=28.6\ yr\times3.7''/yr=106''.\qquad106''>1'
$$

It’s greater than the naked eye resolve angle of common practice.

Conclusion: **“Yes”**.

</ProblemSection>
