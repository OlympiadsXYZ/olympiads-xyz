<summary>Important Formulae</summary>

You might find the diagram of an elliptical orbit below useful in solving some of the questions:

**Elements of an elliptic orbit**:

| | |
|---|---|
| $a = \mathbf{OA}\ (= \mathbf{OP})$ | semi-major axis |
| $b = \mathbf{OB}\ (= \mathbf{OC})$ | semi-minor axis |
| $e = \sqrt{1 - \frac{b^2}{a^2}}$ | eccentricity |
| F | focus |
| P | periapsis (point nearest to F) |
| A | apoapsis (point furthest from F) |

**Kepler’s Third Law**: For an elliptical orbit, the square of the period, $T$, of an object about the focus is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis, $a$ (as defined above), such that

$$
T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{GM}a^3\,,
$$

where $M$ is the total mass of the system (typically dominated by the central object) and $G$ is the universal gravitational constant.

**Magnitudes**: The apparent magnitudes of two objects, $m_1$ and $m_0$, are related to their apparent brightnesses, $b_1$ and $b_0$, via the formula

$$
\frac{b_1}{b_0} = 10^{-0.4(m_1 - m_0)}\,.
$$

The absolute magnitude of an object, $\mathcal{M}$, is the same as its apparent magnitude when viewed from 10 pc, hence the relationship between apparent and absolute magnitude and distance is

$$
m - \mathcal{M} = 5\log\left(\frac{d}{10}\right),
$$

where $d$ is measured in parsecs.

## Условие

The speed of light is considered to be the speed limit of the Universe, however knots of plasma in the jets from active galactic nuclei (AGN) have been observed to be moving with apparent transverse speeds in excess of this, called superluminal speeds. Some of the more extreme examples can be appearing to move at up to 6 times the speed of light (see Figure 7).

This can be explained by understanding that the jet is offset by an angle $\theta$ from the sightline to Earth, and that the real speed of the plasma knot, $v$, is less than $c$, and from it we can define the scaled speed $\beta \equiv v/c$.

**a.** Show, with use of an appropriate diagram, that the apparent value of the scaled transverse speed (for a jet coming towards us) is

$$
\beta_{\mathrm{app}} = \frac{\beta\sin\theta}{1 - \beta\cos\theta}\,.
$$
**[5 т.]**

**b.** Determine the relationship between $\beta$ and $\theta$ that maximises $\beta_{\mathrm{app}}$ for a given value of $\beta$, and hence determine the minimum value of $\beta$ needed to give rise to superluminal apparent speeds (i.e. when $\beta_{\mathrm{app}}^{\mathrm{max}} > 1$). [You are given that a graph of $\beta_{\mathrm{app}}$ against $\theta$ has only one turning point in the range $0 < \theta < \pi$, and that it is a maximum.] **[4 т.]**

Superluminal jets are not limited just to AGN, as they have also been observed from systems within our own galaxy. A particularly famous one is the ‘microquasar’ GRS 1915+105, which is a low mass X-ray binary consisting of a small star orbiting a black hole. A symmetrical jet with components approaching and receding from us is observed (as expected for jets coming from the poles of the black hole), and the apparent transverse motion of material in those jets has been measured using very high resolution radio imaging. Fender *et. al* (1999) measure these motions to be $\mu_a = 23.6\ \mathrm{mas\ day^{-1}}$ and $\mu_r = 10.0\ \mathrm{mas\ day^{-1}}$ for the approaching and receding jet respectively (1 mas = 1 milliarcsecond, a unit of angle, and there are 3600 arcseconds in a degree) and the distance to the system as 11 kpc.

**c.** Calculate $\beta_{\mathrm{app}}$ for both jets, and use your formula from part b. to calculate the minimum value of $\beta$ to explain the apparent superluminal motion. **[5 т.]**

In practice, for a given $\beta_{\mathrm{app}}$ the values of $\beta$ and $\theta$ are degenerate and it is unlikely that the orientation of the jet is such that $\beta_{\mathrm{app}}$ has been maximised, so the value in part c. is just a lower limit. However, since there are two jets then if we assume that they are from the same event (and so equal in speed but opposite in direction) we can break this degeneracy.

**d.** Given that $\mu_a$ and $\mu_r$ can be calculated (in radians s$^{-1}$) as

$$
\mu_a = \frac{\beta\sin\theta}{1 - \beta\cos\theta}\frac{c}{D} \quad \text{and} \quad \mu_r = \frac{\beta\sin\theta}{1 + \beta\cos\theta}\frac{c}{D}\,,
$$

derive a formula for the distance, $D$, as a function of $\theta$, $\mu_a$ and $\mu_r$ (i.e. independent of $\beta$), and hence calculate $\theta$. **[4 т.]**

**e.** Show that $\beta\cos\theta$ can be expressed purely as a function of $\mu_a$ and $\mu_r$, and hence use your value of $\theta$ to calculate the value of $\beta$. **[4 т.]**

Since it is a binary system, we can gain information about the masses of the objects by looking at their period and radial velocity. Formally, the relationship is

$$
\frac{(M_{\mathrm{BH}}\sin i)^3}{(M_{\mathrm{BH}} + M_{\star})^2} = \frac{P_{\mathrm{orb}}K_d^3}{2\pi G}\,,
$$

where $M_{\mathrm{BH}}$ is the mass of the black hole, $M_{\star}$ is the mass of the orbiting star, $i$ is the inclination of the orbit, $P_{\mathrm{orb}}$ is the orbital period, and $K_d$ is the amplitude of the radial velocity curve. Normally the inclination can’t be measured, however if we assume that the orbit is perpendicular to the jets then $i = \theta$ and we can measure the mass of the black hole.

**f.** Greiner *et. al* (2001) measure $K_d = 140\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, $P_{\mathrm{orb}} = 33.5$ days, and a mass ratio for the two objects of $M_{\mathrm{BH}}/M_{\star} = 12.3$. Using the assumption that $i = \theta$, calculate $M_{\mathrm{BH}}$. Give your answer in $M_{\odot}$. **[3 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**Q4 – Superluminal Jets** **[25 marks]**

**a.**

Attempt at suitable diagram [be flexible] [2]

Apparent distance moved in time $\Delta t$,

$AB = d_{app} = v\sin\theta\times\Delta t$ [1]

Difference in distances moved by photons,

$BE = c\Delta t - v\cos\theta\times\Delta t$

So apparent time delay,

$\Delta t_{app} = \frac{BE}{c} = \Delta t - \beta\cos\theta\times\Delta t$ [1]

So apparent speed,

$v_{app} = \frac{d_{app}}{\Delta t_{app}} = \frac{\Delta t\times v\sin\theta}{\Delta t - \beta\cos\theta\times\Delta t}$ [1] **[5]**

$\therefore \beta_{app} = \frac{v_{app}}{c} = \frac{\beta\sin\theta}{1 - \beta\cos\theta}$

[Allow any convincing derivation, given that it was a ‘show that’ question. Any derivation without a diagram scores 3 marks max. Most diagrams with two sightlines, *v* & θ should score at least 1 mark]

**b.**

Want to find where $\frac{d\beta_{app}}{d\theta} = 0$, so need to differentiate our expression [with product / quotient rule]

$\frac{d\beta_{app}}{d\theta} = \frac{\beta\cos\theta(1 - \beta\cos\theta) - \beta\sin\theta(\beta\sin\theta)}{(1 - \beta\cos\theta)^2} = 0$ [1]

$\therefore$ ($\beta_{app}$ is maximised for a given value of $\beta$ when) $\beta = \cos\theta$ [1] **[2]**

Putting this into the formula from part a. and evaluating for when $\beta_{app} = 1$,

$\beta_{app}^{max} = \frac{\cos\theta\sin\theta}{1 - \cos\theta\cos\theta} = \cot\theta \quad \left(= \frac{\beta}{\sqrt{1 - \beta^2}}\right)$ [1]

If $\cot\theta = 1$, $\theta = \frac{\pi}{4} \quad \therefore \beta_{min} = \cos\theta = \cos\frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ (= 0.707) [1] **[2]**

**c.**

We need to convert μ from milliarcseconds a day to radians a second,

$v_a = \mu_ad_{system} = \frac{23.6\times10^{-3}}{3600}\times\frac{2\pi}{360}\times(11\times10^{3}\times3.09\times10^{16})$ [1]

$= 4.50\times10^{8}\ m\ s^{-1} \quad \therefore \beta_{app,a} = 1.50$ [1] **[2]**

Similarly, for the receding jet, $\beta_{app,r} = 0.636$ [2] **[2]**

[Values of $\mu_a$ and $\mu_r$ are $1.32\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$ and $5.61\times10^{-13}\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$ respectively]

$\beta_{app}^{max} = \cot\theta = 1.50 \quad \therefore \beta_{min,a} = \cos(\cot^{-1}1.50) = 0.832$ [1] **[1]**

[No requirement to use the data from the receding jet (which gives $\beta_{min,r} = 0.824$)]

**d.**

Rearranging the given formulae,

$D\mu_a(1 - \beta\cos\theta) = \beta c\sin\theta$ and $D\mu_r(1 + \beta\cos\theta) = \beta c\sin\theta$

$D\mu_a\mu_r\left(\frac{1}{\beta} - \cos\theta\right) = \mu_rc\sin\theta$ ① $\quad D\mu_a\mu_r\left(\frac{1}{\beta} + \cos\theta\right) = \mu_ac\sin\theta$ ② [1]

② – ① $\quad D\mu_a\mu_r(2\cos\theta) = (\mu_a - \mu_r)c\sin\theta$

$\therefore D = \frac{c}{2}\left(\frac{\mu_a - \mu_r}{\mu_a\mu_r}\right)\tan\theta$ [1] **[2]**

[This is just one approach – allow any reasonable attempt that gets the right answer]

$\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{2D}{c}\left(\frac{\mu_a\mu_r}{\mu_a - \mu_r}\right)\right)$

$= \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{2\times11\times10^{3}\times3.09\times10^{16}}{3.00\times10^{8}}\left(\frac{1.32\times10^{-12}\times5.61\times10^{-13}}{1.32\times10^{-12} - 5.61\times10^{-13}}\right)\right) \quad (= \tan^{-1}2.206)$ [1]

$= 65.6^{\circ} \quad (= 1.15\ \mathrm{rad})$ [1] **[2]**

**e.**

Rearranging the given formulae again,

$D\mu_a(1 - \beta\cos\theta) = \beta c\sin\theta$ ① and $D\mu_r(1 + \beta\cos\theta) = \beta c\sin\theta$ ②

② – ① $\quad D\mu_r + D\mu_r\beta\cos\theta + D\mu_a\beta\cos\theta - D\mu_a = 0$ [1]

$\therefore \beta\cos\theta = \frac{\mu_a - \mu_r}{\mu_a + \mu_r}$ [1] **[2]**

[This is just one approach – allow any reasonable attempt that gets the right answer]

$\beta\cos\theta = \frac{23.6 - 10.0}{23.6 + 10.0} = 0.405$ [1]

$\therefore \beta = \frac{0.405}{\cos 65.6^{\circ}} = 0.981$ [1] **[2]**

[When they were discovered, these blobs of plasma in the jet from the microquasar were the fastest moving that anyone had ever seen in our galaxy!]

**f.**

Rewriting the given equation in terms of the ratio $M_*/M_{BH}$,

$\frac{M_{BH}^3\sin^3\theta}{M_{BH}^2\left(1 + \frac{M_*}{M_{BH}}\right)^2} = \frac{P_{orb}K_d^3}{2\pi G} \quad \therefore M_{BH} = \frac{P_{orb}K_d^3}{2\pi G}\frac{\left(1 + \frac{M_*}{M_{BH}}\right)^2}{\sin^3\theta}$ [1]

$= \frac{(33.5\times24\times3600)\times(140\times10^{3})^3}{2\pi\times6.67\times10^{-11}}\times\frac{(1 + 12.3^{-1})^2}{\sin^3 65.6^{\circ}}$ [1]

$= 2.93\times10^{31}\ \mathrm{kg} = 14.7\,M_{\odot}$ [1] **[3]**

[Must be in $M_{\odot}$ for the final mark]

[At the time of discovery, this meant the black hole in the microquasar was the heaviest stellar black hole known. Subsequent measurements have made the distance a little closer than 11 kpc, and so the mass has been revised down, but until the discovery of the black holes behind detections of gravitational waves (with masses about double this) it was still one of heaviest known]
