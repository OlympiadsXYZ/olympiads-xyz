## Условие

Спътник се движи в орбита около планета, като равнината на тази орбита е перпендикулярна на повърхността на планетата в точката в която се намира наблюдателят. След извършване на необходимото скалиране, приемаме, че орбитата на спътника се описва от следното уравнение:

$$
9\left(\frac{x}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{3}y}{2}-4\right)^2+25\left(-\frac{\sqrt{3}x}{2}+\frac{y}{2}\right)^2=225
$$

Разгледайте орбитата в правоъгълна координата система, в която остта Ox лежи в хоризонталната равнина, а Oy е насочена към зенита. Нека с $r$ да означим радиуса на спътника. Да предположим, че периодът на околоосно въртене на планетата е много по-дълъг от орбиталния период на спътника. Атмосферната рефракция се пренебрегва.

**a.** Пресметнете голямата и малката полуос на елиптичната орбита

**b.** Пресметнете зенитното отстояние на перигея на орбитата, за наблюдателя.

**c.** Намерете $\tan\dfrac{\theta}{2}$, където $\theta$ е височината над хоризонта на точката от орбитата в която спътникът изглежда най-голям за наблюдателя.

<ProblemSection id="solution-polar-a">

### 1. Polar version — (a)

**Rewrite the orbit equation using polar coordinate**

$$
x=R(\alpha)\cos\alpha,\qquad y=R(\alpha)\sin\alpha
$$

Thus, if we let

$$
x^*=R(\alpha)\cos\left(\alpha-\frac\pi3\right)=\frac x2+\frac{y\sqrt3}2
$$

$$
y^*=R(\alpha)\sin\left(\alpha-\frac\pi3\right)=-\frac{x\sqrt3}2+\frac y2
$$

**10 points**

 the equation of the orbit can be written as

$$
9\left(R(\alpha)\cos\left(\alpha-\frac\pi3\right)-4\right)^2+25\left(R(\alpha)\sin\left(\alpha-\frac\pi3\right)\right)^2=225
$$

$$
9(x^*-4)^2+25(y^*)^2=225
$$

**10 points**

**Determine semi major and semi minor axis of the orbit**

Since $9(x^*-4)^2+25(y^*)^2=225$ is equivalent to

$$
\frac{(x^*-4)^2}{25}+\frac{(y^*)^2}{9}=1
$$

then, the semi major axis is 5, and (**5 points**) the semi minor axis is 3 (**5 points**).

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-polar-b">

### 1. Polar version — (b) (20 т.)

**Determine the zenith angle at perigee**

At the perigee, $\alpha-\frac\pi3=\pi$. Thus, the zenith angle at the perigee is

$$
\alpha-\frac\pi2=\pi+\frac\pi3-\frac\pi2=\frac{5\pi}6.
$$

### 1. Polar version — (c)

**Characterize the coordinates of the moon when it looks largest to the observer.**

Let $P(x_0,y_0)$ be the point where it looks largest to the observer. Since the distance between the moon and the observer decreases as it moves from apogee towards perigee, then the moon looks largest to the observer at the planet when it closest to the planet while the whole of the moon still can be seen in full. Hence, $y_0=r$.

$$
y_0=r=R(\pi-\alpha_0\alpha_0)\sin(\pi-\alpha_0)=R_0\sin(\pi-\alpha_0)
$$

$$
x_0=R_0\cos(\pi-\alpha_0)
$$

**10 points**

**Determine the coordinates of the point.**

Let the coordinate be $(x_0,r)$.

$$
9\left(\frac{x_0}2+\frac{\sqrt3r}2-4\right)^2+25\left(-\frac{\sqrt3x_0}2+\frac r2\right)^2=225
$$

Rewrite it as a quadratic equation in $x_0$.

$$
9(x_0+r\sqrt3-8)^2+25(-x_0\sqrt3+r)^2=900
$$

$$
84(x_0)^2+(-32\sqrt3r-144)x_0+(52r^2-144\sqrt3r+576=900)
$$

Solving for $x$, we get

$$
x_0=\frac{4r\sqrt3+18}{21}\pm\frac{15\sqrt{-r^2+4r\sqrt3+9}}{21}
$$

**20 points**

Then choose the smaller one

$$
x_0=\frac{4r\sqrt3+18}{21}-\frac{15\sqrt{-r^2+4r\sqrt3+9}}{21}
$$

**10 points**

**Find $\tan\frac\theta2$.** Hence

$$
\tan\frac{\theta_0}2=\tan\alpha_0=\frac r{x_0}=\frac{21r}{4r\sqrt3+18-15\sqrt{-r^2+4r\sqrt3+9}}
$$

**10 points**

### 3. Cartesian version — (c)

**Identify and characterized the coordinates when the planet looks largest.**

Since the distance between the moon and the observer decreases as it moves from apogee towards perigee, then the moon looks largest to the observer at the planet when it closest to the planet while the whole of the moon still can be seen in full. Hence, it is at necessary that $y=r$. Let the coordinates be $(x_0,r)$.

**10 points**

**Obtain expression for calculating distance from a point on an ellipse to the foci to set up an equation**

Consider standard ellipse

$$
\frac{(x^*-4)^2}{5^2}+\frac{(y^*)^2}{3^2}=1
$$

Choose any point $(x^*,y^*)$ on the ellipse. Let $d_1$ and $d_2$ be the distances from any point $(x^*,y^*)$ on the ellipse to the foci $(0,0)$ and $d(8,0)$; $d_1^2=(x^*)^2+(y^*)^2$ and $d_2^2=(x^*-8)^2+(y^*)^2$.

Thus,

$$
d_1^2=(x^*)^2+(y^*)^2=(x^*)^2+\left(9-\frac{9(x^*-4)^2}{5^2}\right)=\frac{16(x^*)^2+72x^*+81}{25}
$$

Therefore

$$
d_1^2=\frac{16(x^*)^2+72x^*+81}{25}=x^2+r^2
$$

where $x^*=\frac x2+\frac{r\sqrt3}2$. Therefore, we can obtain the value of $x$ by solving the above equation for $x$.

**10 points**

**Solve equation $\frac{16(x^*)^2+72x^*+81}{25}=x^2+r^2$ to obtain value of $x$.**

Substituting $x^*=\frac x2+\frac{r\sqrt3}2$ to the equation, we have

$$
25d_1^2=25x^2+25r^2=16\left(\frac x2+\frac{r\sqrt3}2\right)^2+72\left(\frac x2+\frac{r\sqrt3}2\right)+81=
$$

or

$$
25x^2+25r^2=4x^2+(8r\sqrt3+36)x+(12r^2+36\sqrt3r+81)
$$

$$
21x^2-(8r\sqrt3+36)x+(13r^2-36r\sqrt3-81)=0
$$

Then use quadratic formula to obtain $x$ in term of $r$. Choose smaller value of $x$ to get larger value of $\tan\frac\theta2=\frac rx$. The smaller one is

$$
x=\frac{4r\sqrt3+18}{21}-\frac{15\sqrt{-r^2+4r\sqrt3+9}}{21}
$$

**20 points**

**Find the expression of $\tan\frac\theta2$.** Hence

$$
\tan\frac\theta2=\frac rx=\frac{21r}{4r\sqrt3+18-15\sqrt{-r^2+4r\sqrt3+9}}
$$

**10 points**

$$
\frac{(x^*-4)^2}{25}+\frac{(y^*)^2}{9}=1
$$

by rotating the standard ellipse with respect to the origin, counterclockwise, (by $\pi/3$ radians). Then $c=\sqrt{a^2-b^2}=\sqrt{16}=4$.

For any point $(x,y)$ on the ellipse, let $(x^*,y^*)$ be its coordinates before the transformation. From the equation of the ellipse, we can easily see that

$$
x^*=\frac x2+\frac{\sqrt3y}2,\qquad y^*=-\frac{\sqrt3x}2+\frac y2

\begin{bmatrix}x^*\\y^*\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac12&\frac{\sqrt3}2\\-\frac{\sqrt3}2&\frac12\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac x2+\frac{\sqrt3y}2\\-\frac{\sqrt3x}2+\frac y2\end{bmatrix}
