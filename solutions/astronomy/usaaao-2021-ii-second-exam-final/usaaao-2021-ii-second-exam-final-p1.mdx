## Условие

**2 Short Questions**

Jupiter emits more energy to space than it receives from the Sun. The internal heat flux of Jupiter can be quantified by the “intrinsic” temperature of the planet $T_{\mathrm{int}}$. The effective temperature $T_{\mathrm{eff}}$ of a planet is related to its intrinsic temperature and equilibrium temperature $T_{\mathrm{eq}}$ by $T_{\mathrm{eff}}^4=T_{\mathrm{eq}}^4+T_{\mathrm{int}}^4$. Given that Jupiter’s albedo is 0.5, its emissivity is 1, its average separation from the Sun is 5.2 AU, and its effective temperature is 134 K, estimate its intrinsic temperature in Kelvin. You may use the Sun’s surface temperature equal to 5777 K.

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- 121 K

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

Solve for equilibrium temperature, by equating solar flux falling on Jupiter to a emission of a black-body at temperature $T_{\mathrm{eq}}$

$$
T_{\mathrm{eq}}=\left(\frac{R_{\mathrm{Sun}}}{a}\right)^{1/2}\left[\frac{(1-A)}{4\epsilon}\right]^{1/4}T_{\mathrm{Sun}},\qquad (1)
$$

Here, $A$ is the albedo, $\epsilon$ is the emissivity, $a$ is Jupiter-Sun separation.

$$
T_{\mathrm{eq}}=\left(\frac{6.96\times10^8\ \mathrm m}{5.2\times1.5\times10^{11}\ \mathrm m}\right)^{1/2}\left[\frac{(1-0.5)}4\right]^{1/4}5777\ \mathrm K=102\ \mathrm K.\qquad (2)
$$

Plug in to solve for intrinsic temperature

$$
T_{\mathrm{int}}=(T_{\mathrm{eff}}^4-T_{\mathrm{eq}}^4)^{1/4}=[(134\ \mathrm K)^4-(102\ \mathrm K)^4]^{1/4}=121\ \mathrm K.\qquad (3)
$$

Note: Accept slightly higher values if the student uses the Solar constant rather than Stefan-Boltzmann law to estimate the Sun’s emitted flux.


</Spoiler>
