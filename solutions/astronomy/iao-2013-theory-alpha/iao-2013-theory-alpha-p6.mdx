## Условие

An X-ray image of supernova remnant (SNR) Cas A located at a distance of d = 3400 pc was obtained using Chandra Space Observatory. The negative of this image is shown in Fig. SNR. The boundaries of the SNR region are marked by a circle. The scale of the image is shown in the upper left corner of the figure. A dot located close to the center of the circle is the neutron star – the remaining core of the collapsed star. The rectangular marks outside the circle are given for the reference when determining the center of the circle.

Assume that the amount of energy released in the supernova explosion was about $E_{\mathrm{SN}} \approx 10^{46}$ J, 1% of which drives the expansion of the remnant. The average density of the matter in the SNR is $\rho \approx 10^{-21}\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$.

**6.2.** Calculate the average velocity of the motion of the neutron star from the center of the SNR. **[2 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

Official English solution sketch. The proposed sketches are not full; the team leaders have to give more detailed explanations for students. But the correct solutions in the students’ papers (enough for 8 pts) may be shorter.

<ProblemSection id="solution-age">

### 6.1. Age of the supernova remnant

Assume that the SNR is a sphere with the linear radius R. Using a ruler we accurately measure the diameter of the circle and the scale length (e.g. in mm) in Fig. SNR (see the figure below, the initial figure was almost 2 times larger). From the measurements we get the radius of the circle $\rho=48.5\ \mathrm{mm}$ and 100″ scale length $l=28.5\ \mathrm{mm}$. With these data we can calculate the angular radius of the SNR

$$
\theta=100^{\prime\prime}\rho/l,
$$

$$
\theta=100^{\prime\prime}\times48.5/28.5\approx170^{\prime\prime}.
$$

If the radius R of the SNR is measured in au, the distance d in pc, and its angular radius $\theta$ in arcsec, then

$$
R=d\times\theta,
$$

$$
R=170^{\prime\prime}\times3400=578000\ \mathrm{AU}\approx8.6\cdot10^{16}\ \mathrm m.
$$

The mass of the SNR is confined within the sphere of the radius R.

$$
M=\rho\cdot\frac43\pi R^3,
$$

$$
M=10^{-21}\cdot4/3\cdot3.14\cdot(8.5\cdot10^{16})^3\approx2.7\cdot10^{30}\ \mathrm{kg}.
$$

The expansion speed of the SNR is calculated using the expression of the kinetic energy of the SNR

$$
E_{\mathrm{kin}}=\tfrac12Mv^2,
$$

$$
v=(2E_{\mathrm{kin}}/M)^{1/2}=(2\cdot0.01E_{\mathrm{SN}}/M)^{1/2},
$$

$$
v=(2\cdot0.01\cdot10^{46}\ \mathrm J/(2.7\cdot10^{30}\ \mathrm{kg}))^{1/2}\approx8.6\cdot10^6\ \mathrm{m/s}\approx10^7\ \mathrm{m/s}.
$$

As we see from the image, the darkest parts of it are distributed at distances about 60% of its radius. So we may propose that most mass of the SNR is distributed at these distances. Thus, by the above formulae we have found the average speed of matter at 0.6 R.

Assuming that the expansion velocity was constant we get the age of the SNR:

$$
t=0.6R/v,
$$

$$
t=0.6\times8.6\cdot10^{16}\ \mathrm m/(8.6\cdot10^6\ \mathrm{m/s})\approx6\cdot10^9\ \mathrm s\approx190\ \mathrm{yr}\approx200\ \mathrm{yr}.
$$

Taking into account that most data in the problem conditions was given very roughly, with maximum 1 significant digit (and even with zero significant digits as only order of magnitude is done: the energy was about $10^{46}$ J, 1% of which drives the expansion; the average density is $10^{-21}$ kg/m³), the answer should be expressed with only one significant digit.

So the answer is: ~200 years.

*Source note: the key uses $\rho$ both for the measured image radius and for the matter density, and uses $8.5\cdot10^{16}$ m in the mass calculation after $8.6\cdot10^{16}$ m above. These are retained.*

### 6.2. Neutron-star velocity

Using a ruler we measure the distance of the neutron star from the center of circle in Fig. SNR. Say that we get $\sigma=3\ \mathrm{mm}$. Then the angular distance of the neutron star from the center of the SNR is

$$
\delta=100^{\prime\prime}\sigma/l,
$$

$$
\delta=100^{\prime\prime}\times3/28.5\approx10.5^{\prime\prime}.
$$

The linear distance of the neutron star from the center of the SNR is

$$
S_N=\sigma\times d,
$$

$$
S_N=10.5\times3400\approx35700\ \mathrm{AU}\approx5.3\cdot10^{12}\ \mathrm{km}.
$$

The velocity of the motion of the neutron star is

$$
u=S_N/t,
$$

$$
u=5.3\cdot10^{12}\ \mathrm{km}/(6\cdot10^9\ \mathrm s)\approx900\ \mathrm{km/s}\approx10^3\ \mathrm{km/s}.
$$

Also taking into account that most data was done very roughly, the answer should be expressed also as an order of magnitude only or with only one significant digit.

The answer is: ~$10^3$ km/s.

*Source note: the key prints $S_N=\sigma d$ although the substituted 10.5 is the angular distance $\delta$; its notation is preserved.*
