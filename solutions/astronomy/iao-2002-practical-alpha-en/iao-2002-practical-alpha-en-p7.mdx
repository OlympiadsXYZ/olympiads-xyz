Sure, you know that detection in visible light of afterglow of gamma-ray burst (GRB) was one the most important astrophysical discoveries of the last years. The gamma-ray bursts are known since 1973. But exactly this discovery allowed us to understand that gamma-ray bursts are connected with explosions in distant galaxies. You are lucky because the powerful GRB was registered in the distant galaxy ($z\geq2.3$), in the constellation of Pisces in the beginning of this month (4th of October, 2002, after 12 h GMT). A after 12 min its optical afterglow was discovered. Observations began after several hours at the 1-m SAO telescope. One of the first runs of snapshots taken at SAO in three colors is shown on Fig.1. The Fig.2 shows variation of optical afterglow GRB021004 in the first 5 days.

<ProblemSection id="tasks">

### Tasks

- Plot a curve on the cross-bar paper for above-mentioned tabulated magnitude estimates depending on time.
- Fit the light curve with one or two dependencies of power kind:

$$
I=I_0(t-t_0)^{-\alpha}
$$

where $\alpha$ is the parameter of the power function and plot it on the profile.

- Estimate the magnitude at “moment of a gamma-ray burst” with your suggested curve $m_R(t)$.
- Is there any variation of the afterglow color during observations in three bands R, V and B?

</ProblemSection>
