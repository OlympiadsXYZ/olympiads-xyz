## Условие

We may consider, that the solar core consists of a mix of completely ionized hydrogen and helium, the part of atoms of helium is equal to $\alpha=0.08$ (that is, the numbers of atoms of hydrogen and helium is 92% : 8%). The temperature in the centre of solar core is equal to 15 million degrees and the density is $150\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$. Let us assume that there is a carbon star (100% of completely ionized carbon) with the same parameters (the same density and pressure in the center and the same mass). Find the temperature in the center of the core of this carbon star. Atomic numbers and atomic masses of hydrogen, helium and carbon are equal accordingly to: $Z_H=1$, $A_H=1$, $Z_{He}=2$, $A_{He}=4$, $Z_C=6$, $A_C=12$. One may assume the gas in cores as ideal.

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- $4 \times 10^{7}$ degrees

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**1β. Temperature of star’s core.** Gas in the cores of stars consists of ions (completely ionized atoms) and electrons. The number of protons of each atom number is equal to number of electrons. As all atoms in the cores are ionized, the number of free electrons corresponds to the number of protons in its nucleus, that is Z. If $n_H$, $n_{He}$ and $n_C$ are concentrations of ions of hydrogen, helium and carbon, the concentrations of corresponding electrons are equal to $Z_H\cdot n_H$, $Z_{He}\cdot n_{He}$ and $Z_C\cdot n_C$.

The total pressure of the gas in stars is equal to the sum of pressure of the gas of ions $p_i$ and the pressure of gas of electrons $p_e$,

$$
p=p_i+p_e.
$$

According to formulae for ideal gas

$$
p_i=n_i kT\qquad\text{and}\qquad p_e=n_e kT,
$$

where $n_i$ and $n_e$ are concentrations of ions and electrons respectively. The total pressure is equal to

$$
p=(n_i+n_e)kT.
$$

Then we write equations for the total pressures in the stars.

For the Sun:

$$
p_S=(n_H+Z_Hn_H+n_{He}+Z_{He}n_{He})kT_S=(2n_H+3n_{He})kT_S=(2(1-\alpha)n_S+3\alpha n_S)kT_S=(2+\alpha)n_SkT_S.
$$

For $\alpha=0.08$:

$$
p_S=2.08n_SkT_S.
$$

For the carbon star:

$$
p_C=(n_C+Z_Cn_C)kT_C=7n_CkT_C.
$$

Since these pressures are equal to each other (condition of the problem)

$$
p_S=p_C,
$$

we may write

$$
7n_CkT_C=2.08n_SkT_S,
$$
$$
T_C=0.297(n_S/n_C)T_S.
$$

Further we shall find a relation between the concentrations of ions in the solar core and the core of the carbon star, $n_S$ and $n_C$. Practically all mass of matter is in the nuclei of atoms, therefore the densities in the cores of each star are equal to the concentration of ions multiplied by the masses of ions which are proportional to the nuclear masses of the elements. As the density of substance in the solar core and the core of the carbon star are identical,

$$
A_Hn_H+A_{He}n_{He}=A_Cn_C,
$$
$$
A_H(1-\alpha)n_S+A_{He}\alpha n_S=A_Cn_C,
$$
$$
(1\cdot0.9+4\cdot0.1)n_S=12n_C,\qquad1.3n_S=12n_C,\qquad n_S=9.23n_C.
$$

Thus,

$$
T_C=0.297\cdot9.23T_S=2.74T_S.
$$
$$
T_C=2.74\cdot1.5\cdot10^7\,\mathrm K\approx4.1\cdot10^7\,\mathrm K.
$$

(Since we talk about millions of degrees, especially, about an estimations, it is without importance whether the temperature given is in degrees Celsius or Kelvin.)

Answer: the temperature in the core of the carbon star is about 40 million degrees.


</Spoiler>
