The light refraction in the atmosphere of a planet is an optical effect, which is due to the optical inhomogeneity of the atmosphere, thus the light propagation obey the Fermat principle. In this problem you have to find out if the is possible that due to the atmospheric refraction an astronaut could see his back. The atmospheric absorption is negligible and the structure of the atmosphere is stable. Here you have some theoretical issues to help you to solve the problem:

The situation you have to investigate is sketched in the figure 1.

The non-homogeneity of the atmosphere can be simulated by considering the atmosphere consisted of concentric spherical thin layers of thickness $\Delta r$. In one layer the refractive index is $n>1$ due to the presence of the gas molecules. The refractive index of vacuum is $n_{\rm vacuum}=1$.

The plane wave-front of the light AB has to remain normal on the surface of the planet and at the same distance from it, despite that the propagation occurs through an inhomogeneous atmosphere consisted of concentric spherical thin, homogenous layers.

The consequence of the model is that near the planet surface the following relationship occurred

$$
n-1=\alpha N,
$$

whith $N$ the gas molecules concentration, and $\alpha$ a constant.

For Earth atmosphere near the surface:

$$
n-1\approx11.35\cdot10^{-5}.
$$

In the table are given the data for 10 planets (8 from Solar System and 2 exoplanets).

$R$ – planet radius in m; $m$ – the mass of on gas molecule of dominant one near the surface of the planet in atomic mass unit ($1\,\mathrm{a.m.u.}=1.66\cdot10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$); $p$ – atmospheric pressure near the surface of the planet in bar ($1\,\mathrm{bar}=10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}=10^5\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-2}}$); $T$ – atmosphere temperature near the surface of the planet in K; $g$ – gravitational acceleration in $\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$; You will assume that the molecules are spheres with an effective diameter d. <ProblemSection id="solution-optics">

### a) The optical condition for the light refraction in the planet atmosphere

As seen in the figure 1 the plane wave front AB has to remain perpendicular to the surface of the planet, and at the same distance from that. If we consider the thin layer near the planet which can be considered homogenous the time of light travel will be the same on the circle sectors AA′ și BB′ corresponding to the same angle $\varphi$.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '47.2%', '--fig-max': '302px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/ioaa-2014-data-analysis-preliminary-p1/p1-sol-fig1-v2.png" alt="Fig. 1. Wave-front travel at radii R and R+Δr, with original velocity labels." width="944" height="948" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Fig. 1. Wave-front travel at radii R and R+Δr, with original velocity labels.</figcaption>
</figure>


$$
t=\frac{AA'}{v_R}=\frac{BB'}{v_{R+\Delta r}},
$$

$v_R$ – speed of light in the thin spherical homogenous layer near the planet surface – radius R;  
$v_{R+\Delta r}$ – speed of light in the thin spherical homogenous layer near the planet surface – radius $R+\Delta r$;

$$
v_{R+\Delta r}>v_R;\qquad n_R=\frac c{v_R};\quad n_{R+\Delta r}=\frac c{v_{R+\Delta r}}<n_R,
$$

$c$ speed of light in vacuum, $n_R$ refraction index of the atmosphere layer – radius R, $n_{R+\Delta r}$ refraction index of the atmosphere layer – radius $R+\Delta r$.

Results:

$$
AA'n_R=BB'n_{R+\Delta r};\quad n_{R+\Delta r}<n_R;
$$
$$
AA'=R\varphi;\quad BB'=(R+\Delta r)\varphi;
$$
$$
Rn_R=(R+\Delta r)n_{R+\Delta r};\quad n_{R+\Delta r}=n_R+\Delta n,
$$

where $\Delta n<0$ is the variance of the refraction index of the atmospheric gas associated with the height $\Delta r$;

$$
Rn_R=(R+\Delta r)(n_R+\Delta n);\quad\Delta r\,\Delta n\approx0;
$$
$$
-\frac{\Delta n}{n_R\Delta r}=\frac1R;
$$
$$
n_R=n;\quad-\frac{\Delta n}{n\Delta r}=\frac1R;\quad\frac{-\Delta n/n}{\Delta r}=\frac1R;\quad-\frac{\Delta n}{n\Delta r}=\frac1R.
$$

The condition of the occurrence of the refraction around the planet:

$$
-\frac{\Delta n/n}{\Delta r}=\frac1R.
$$

Using the following notations:

- theoretical curvature of the Earth surface $C_{\text{suprafata planeta}}=1/R$;
- the curvature of the light ray in the atmosphere layer near the Earth surface $C_{\rm light}=-\Delta n/(n\Delta r)$;

Thus:

$$
C_{\rm light}=C_{\text{surface of the planet}}.
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-thermo">

### b1) Relationship between the refractive index and the physical properties of the gas near the planet surface

According with the notations in figure 1 it can be obtain the equilibrium condition for the gas column near the Earth's surface.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '56.6%', '--fig-max': '363px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/ioaa-2014-data-analysis-preliminary-p1/p1-sol-fig2.png" alt="Fig. 1. Atmospheric gas column and force equilibrium." width="1134" height="823" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Fig. 1. Atmospheric gas column and force equilibrium.</figcaption>
</figure>


$$
\vec F_1+\vec G+\vec F_2=0;\quad F_1-G-F_2=0;
$$
$$
p\Delta S-Mg-(p+\Delta p)\Delta S=0;
$$
$$
p\Delta S-Mg-p\Delta S-\Delta p\Delta S=0;
$$
$$
-\Delta p\Delta S=Mg;\quad-\Delta p\Delta S=Nmg\Delta r\Delta S;\quad-\Delta p=Nmg\Delta r,
$$

where $m$ is the mass of a molecule, and $g$ is the gravitational acceleration at the base of the atmosphere:

$$
p=NkT;\quad\Delta p=kT\Delta N;\quad-kT\Delta N=Nmg\Delta r;
$$
$$
-\frac{\Delta N}{\Delta r}=N\frac{mg}{kT}.\qquad\text{10 points}
$$

According to the relationship $n-1=\alpha N$, and the fact that $n_{\rm vid}=1$ the coefficient $\alpha$ represents the volume.

$$
\langle N\rangle_{\rm SI}=\mathrm{m^{-3}};\qquad\langle\alpha\rangle_{\rm SI}=\mathrm{m^3}
$$

and $n>1$ being proportional to the concentration of the molecules in the atmosphere.

$$
n-1=\alpha N;\quad\Delta(n-1)=\Delta(\alpha N);\quad\Delta n=\alpha\Delta N;
$$
$$
\frac{\Delta n}{n}=\frac{\alpha\Delta N}{n};\quad n\approx1;\quad\frac{\Delta n}{n}=\frac{\alpha\Delta N}{n}\approx\alpha\Delta N.
$$

Using the relation (1):

$$
\frac{\Delta n}{n}=\alpha\Delta N;\quad\Delta N=\frac{\Delta n}{\alpha n};\quad-\frac{\Delta N}{\Delta r}=N\frac{mg}{kT};
$$
$$
-\frac{\Delta n/(\alpha n)}{\Delta r}=N\frac{mg}{kT};\qquad-\frac{\Delta n}{\alpha n\Delta r}=N\frac{mg}{kT}.
$$

The relationship between the light curvature and the physical properties of the atmosphere will be:

$$
C_{\rm light}=-\frac{\Delta n}{n\Delta r}=\alpha N\frac{mg}{kT}.\qquad (2)
$$

This one can be experimental determined for each planet atmosphere and compared with the each planet surface curvature.

$$
C_{\text{light,experimental}}=\alpha N\frac{mg}{kT};\qquad C_{\rm planet}=\frac1R.
$$

The condition is

$$
C_{\rm light}=C_{\rm planet}.\qquad (3)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-alpha">

### b2) The value of α, corelated with the volume of the gas molecule

$\alpha$ represents the volume of a gas molecule.

For Earth:

$$
n-1\approx11.35\cdot10^{-5}=\alpha N;\quad p=1\,\mathrm{bar}=10^5\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-2}};\quad T=300\,\mathrm K,
$$

so

$$
N=\frac p{kT}=\frac{10^5\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-2}}}{1.38\cdot10^{-23}\,\mathrm{J/K}\cdot300\,\mathrm K},
$$

where $k=1.38\cdot10^{-23}\,\mathrm{J/K}$ (constanta lui Boltzman);

$$
N\approx2.41\cdot10^{25}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}},
$$

Is the nytrogen concentration in the Earth atmosphere;

$$
11.35\cdot10^{-5}=\alpha\cdot2.41\cdot10^{25}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}};\quad\alpha\approx4.70\cdot10^{-30}\,\mathrm{m^3}.
$$

The volume of an nytrogen molecule with the diameter $d_{\rm N_2}\approx3\cdot10^{-10}$ m is:

$$
V_{1,\rm N_2}=\frac{4\pi}3\left(\frac{d_{\rm N_2}}2\right)^3=\frac{4\cdot3.14}3\frac{9\cdot10^{-30}}8\,\mathrm{m^3};
$$

Thus

$$
V_{1,\rm N_2}\approx4.71\cdot10^{-30}\,\mathrm{m^3}\approx\alpha.
$$

</ProblemSection>

### Earth

For Earth atmosphere considered full of nitrogen where $p=1$ bar and temperature $T=300$ K, the key again writes

$$
V_{1,\rm N_2}=\frac{4\pi}3\left(\frac{d_{\rm N_2}}2\right)^3=\frac{4\cdot3.14}3\frac{9\cdot10^{-30}}8\,\mathrm{m^3}.

$$

$$
V_{1,\rm N_2}\approx4.71\cdot10^{-30}\,\mathrm{m^3}\approx\alpha;
$$
$$

$$
$$
\alpha N\frac{mg}{kT}=11.35\cdot10^{-5}\frac{28\cdot1.66\cdot10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot9.8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}}{1.38\cdot10^{-23}\,\mathrm{J/K}\cdot300\,\mathrm K};
$$
$$

$$

</ProblemSection>

### e) Conclusion

For all the Solar System planets

$$
C_{\text{light,exp}}\ne C_{\text{surface planet}}.
$$

Thus the condition is not fullfiled.

For the exoplanets X and Y

$$
C_{\text{raza lumina,exp}}\approx C_{\text{suprafata planeta}}.
$$

Thus the astronaut can see its back.
