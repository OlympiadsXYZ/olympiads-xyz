## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

<ProblemSection id="solution-official-sketch">

### Official solution sketch

The maximum space distance from the Earth to the Venus ecliptic is (see figure)
$$
H=R_\oplus\times\sin i,
$$
where $R_\oplus$ is radius of the Earth orbit (1 AU), and $i$ is the orbital inclination of the terrestrial orbit to the plane of Venus ecliptic, which is evidently equal to the orbital inclination of the Venus orbit to the plane of the Earth’s ecliptic, i.e. $3.4°$.
$$
H=1\text{ AU}\times\sin3.4°=0.0593\text{ AU},
$$
[[figure p5-venus-geometry-astra-s17-r1]]

In the Venetian sky, the Earth in such position can be visible at the maximum distance at the configuration of opposition, when the distance between Venus and Earth is minimal and equals to
$$
L=R_\oplus-R_V=1\text{ AU}-0.723\text{ AU}=0.277\text{ AU}
$$
(all angles are small and we do not take into account the inclination in these calculations). The maximal distance in the sky (angle $\beta$) may be found from the equation
$$
H=L\times\tan\beta,
$$
$$
\operatorname{tg}\beta=H/L=0.0593\text{ AU}/0.277\text{ AU}=0{,}214,
$$
$$
\beta=12{,}1°.
$$
