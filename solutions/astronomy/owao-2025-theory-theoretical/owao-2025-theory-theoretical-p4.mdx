## Условие

An experimental gradiometer satellite consists of a thin, rigid, massless rod of length $L=40\ \mathrm m$ with small masses $m=500\ \mathrm{kg}$ at its ends. Its center moves in a circular orbit at an altitude $h=400\ \mathrm{km}$ above the Earth’s surface. In equilibrium, the rod is oriented radially toward the Earth’s center.

Assume that the Earth’s gravitational field is the same as that of a point mass:

$$
\mathbf g(\mathbf r)=-\frac{G\mathfrak M_\oplus}{r^3}\mathbf r.
$$

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '13%', '--fig-max': '84px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/owao-2025-theory-theoretical/p4-fig1.png" alt="Complete native tilted thin rod with both point masses m, rod length L and both endpoints." width="261" height="401" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

**a)** Determine the orbital period of the satellite, neglecting the rod’s length.

**b)** Find a small correction to the satellite’s circular orbital speed that comes from the fact that it isn’t a point, but has a finite length $L$:

$$
v\approx\sqrt{\frac{G\mathfrak M_\oplus}{R_\oplus+h}}\left(1+[\text{SOME NUMBER}]\frac{L^2}{(R_\oplus+h)^2}\right).
$$

**c)** Find the period of small oscillations of the satellite about the equilibrium in the orbital plane, assuming that the satellite’s center continues to move in a circular orbit.

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a)** $T_0=5.5\cdot10^3\ \mathrm s=92^{\mathrm m}\approx1.5^{\mathrm h}$.
- **b)** $[\text{SOME NUMBER}]=3/8$; $(v-v_0)/v_0=1.3\cdot10^{-11}$.
- **c)** $T_{\mathrm{osc}}=3.2\cdot10^3\ \mathrm s=53^{\mathrm m}\approx0.9^{\mathrm h}$.

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

a) The radius of satellite’s orbit is $r_0=R_\oplus+h$. For a circular orbit, the centripetal acceleration is provided by gravity:

$$
\frac{v_0^2}{r_0}=\frac{G\mathfrak M_\oplus}{r_0^2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad v_0=\sqrt{\frac{G\mathfrak M_\oplus}{r_0}},\quad\omega_0=\sqrt{\frac{G\mathfrak M_\oplus}{r_0^3}};
$$

$$
T_0=\frac{2\pi r_0}{v_0}=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{r_0^3}{G\mathfrak M_\oplus}}=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{(R_\oplus+h)^3}{G\mathfrak M_\oplus}}=
$$

$$
=2\times3.14\times\sqrt{\frac{(6.371\cdot10^6+0.4\cdot10^6)^3\ \mathrm{m^3}}{6.67\cdot10^{-11}\ \mathrm{m^3/(kg\cdot s^2)}\times5.97\cdot10^{24}\ \mathrm{kg}}}=5.5\cdot10^3\ \mathrm s=92^{\mathrm m}\approx1.5^{\mathrm h}.
$$

b) Let $\ell=L/2$ be the half-length of the rod. The positions of its endpoints are $\mathbf r_\pm=\mathbf r_{\mathrm{COM}}\pm\boldsymbol\rho$, where $|\boldsymbol\rho|=\ell$. By definition, their average gives the center of mass (COM):

$$
\mathbf r_{\mathrm{COM}}=\frac{\mathbf r_++\mathbf r_-}2,\qquad|\mathbf r_{\mathrm{COM}}|=r_0.
$$

Since internal forces cancel, Newton’s second law for the system gives

$$
2m\mathbf a_{\mathrm{COM}}=\mathbf F_++\mathbf F_-=m\mathbf g_++m\mathbf g_-\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathbf a_{\mathrm{COM}}=\frac{\mathbf g_++\mathbf g_-}2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a_{\mathrm{COM}}=\frac{g_++g_-}2.
$$

We now compute the gravitational acceleration at the endpoints using the binomial expansion, noting that $\ell/r_0\ll1$:

$$
g(r_0\pm\ell)=\frac{G\mathfrak M_\oplus}{(r_0\pm\ell)^2}=\frac{G\mathfrak M_\oplus}{r_0^2}\left(1\pm\frac\ell{r_0}\right)^{-2}=\frac{G\mathfrak M_\oplus}{r_0^2}\left[1\mp2\frac\ell{r_0}+3\frac{\ell^2}{r_0^2}+\cdots\right].
$$

“Averaging” the two values cancels the odd term:

$$
a_{\mathrm{COM}}=\frac{g(r_0+\ell)+g(r_0-\ell)}2\approx\frac{G\mathfrak M_\oplus}{r_0^2}\left(1+3\frac{\ell^2}{r_0^2}\right).
$$

*Alternative way.* Let us denote $x=\ell/r_0\ll1$. We start by simplifying the expression:

$$
\frac1{(1+x)^2}+\frac1{(1-x)^2}=\frac{(1-x)^2+(1+x)^2}{(1+x)^2(1-x)^2}=2\cdot\frac{1+x^2}{(1-x^2)^2}=2\cdot\frac{1+x^2}{1-2x^2+x^4}=
$$

$$
=2\cdot\frac{(1+x^2)(1+2x^2)}{(1-2x^2+x^4)(1+2x^2)}=2\cdot\frac{\boxed{1+3x^2}+2x^4}{\boxed1+(2x^2-3)x^4}\overset{\text{up to }x^2}\approx2\cdot(1+3x^2).
$$

This result simplifies the calculation of the center-of-mass acceleration:

$$
a_{\mathrm{COM}}=\frac12(g_++g_-)=G\mathfrak M_\oplus\cdot\frac12\left[\frac1{(r_0+\ell)^2}+\frac1{(r_0-\ell)^2}\right]=
$$

$$
=\frac{G\mathfrak M_\oplus}{r_0^2}\cdot\frac12\left[\frac1{(1+x)^2}+\frac1{(1-x)^2}\right]\approx\frac{G\mathfrak M_\oplus}{r_0^2}\left(1+3\frac{\ell^2}{r_0^2}\right).
$$

For a circular COM orbit,

$$
a_{\mathrm{COM}}=\frac{v^2}{r_0}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad v^2\approx\frac{G\mathfrak M_\oplus}{r_0}\left(1+3\frac{\ell^2}{r_0^2}\right)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad v\approx\sqrt{\frac{G\mathfrak M_\oplus}{r_0}}\left(1+\frac38\frac{L^2}{(R_\oplus+h)^2}\right).
$$

Thus, $[\text{SOME NUMBER}]=\frac38$, and the relative correction to the velocity is

$$
\frac{v-v_0}{v_0}=\frac38\left(\frac L{R_\oplus+h}\right)^2=\frac38\times\left(\frac{40\ \mathrm m}{6.371\cdot10^6\ \mathrm m+0.4\cdot10^6\ \mathrm m}\right)^2=1.3\cdot10^{-11}.
$$

c) When the rod is tilted by a small angle $\theta$, each end feels a force that is

- slightly stronger/weaker because of being nearer/farther, and
- points in a direction turned by a small angle because the end is shifted sideways.

The combination of these effects produces a net torque about the center of mass, which acts as a restoring torque:

$$
M=F_+\cdot\frac L2\sin\theta_+-F_-\cdot\frac L2\sin\theta_-,
$$

where

$$
F_\pm=m\cdot g(r_0\pm\ell\cos\theta),\qquad\theta_\pm\approx\theta\mp\frac{\ell\sin\theta}{r_0}.
$$

We begin by expanding the gravitational acceleration at each end of the rod $g(r_0\pm\ell\cos\theta)$, noting that $\ell/r_0\ll1$:

$$
g(r_0\pm\ell\cos\theta)=\frac{G\mathfrak M_\oplus}{(r_0\pm\ell\cos\theta)^2}=\frac{G\mathfrak M_\oplus}{r_0^2}\left(1\pm\frac{\ell\cos\theta}{r_0}\right)^{-2}=\frac{G\mathfrak M_\oplus}{r_0^2}\left[1\mp2\frac{\ell\cos\theta}{r_0}+\cdots\right].
$$

For small angles $\theta\ll1$, we use the approximations $\sin\theta\approx\theta$, $\cos\theta\approx1$. The restoring torque about the center of mass, calculated to first order, is

$$
M\approx\frac{G\mathfrak M_\oplus m}{r_0^2}\cdot\ell\theta\cdot\left[\left(1-2\frac\ell{r_0}\right)\cdot\left(1-\frac\ell{r_0}\right)-\left(1+2\frac\ell{r_0}\right)\cdot\left(1+\frac\ell{r_0}\right)\right]\approx-\frac{6G\mathfrak M_\oplus m}{r_0^2}\frac{\ell^2}{r_0}\theta.
$$

The moment of inertia of the two end masses about the COM in the orbital plane is

$$
J=2m\ell^2.
$$

Using the torque above, we obtain the equation of motion for a harmonic oscillator

$$
J\ddot\theta=M\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\ddot\theta+3\frac{G\mathfrak M_\oplus}{r_0^3}\theta=0.
$$

Therefore, the angular frequency and period of small librations are:

$$
\omega_{\mathrm{osc}}=\sqrt3\cdot\sqrt{\frac{G\mathfrak M_\oplus}{r_0^3}}=\sqrt3\omega_0,
$$

$$
T_{\mathrm{osc}}=\frac{2\pi}{\omega_{\mathrm{osc}}}=\frac{T_0}{\sqrt3}=\frac{5.5\cdot10^3\ \mathrm s}{1.73}=3.2\cdot10^3\ \mathrm s=53^{\mathrm m}\approx0.9^{\mathrm h}.
$$

**Elegant shortcut.** In the reference frame co-rotating with the rod’s center of mass, the effective potential combines Earth’s gravity and the centrifugal potential:

$$
U(r)=-\frac{G\mathfrak M_\oplus}r-\frac12\omega_0^2r^2.
$$

The center of mass is in mechanical equilibrium at the orbital radius $r=r_0$, satisfying:

$$
U'(r)\big|_{r=r_0}=0=\frac{G\mathfrak M_\oplus}{r_0^2}-\omega_0^2r_0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\omega_0^2=\frac{G\mathfrak M_\oplus}{r_0^3},
$$

which is the standard expression for the orbital angular velocity.

The tidal acceleration near $r_0$ is given by the second derivative of the potential. To first order:

$$
a_{\mathrm{tidal}}(r)\approx U''(r_0)\cdot(r-r_0)=\left(-\frac{2G\mathfrak M_\oplus}{r_0^3}-\omega_0^2\right)\cdot(r-r_0)=-\frac{3G\mathfrak M_\oplus}{r_0^3}\cdot(r-r_0).
$$

For small angular deflections, the system can be modeled as two simple pendulums of length $L/2$, oscillating in the effective tidal field, each in a uniform field with acceleration

$$
g_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{3G\mathfrak M_\oplus}{r_0^3}\cdot\frac L2.
$$

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '19%', '--fig-max': '122px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/owao-2025-theory-theoretical/p4-sol-fig1.png" alt="Complete effective tidal pendulum diagram: both masses, tilted full rod, fixed center label/arrow, dashed reference line, all upper/lower blue arrows and both geff labels." width="380" height="629" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


The corresponding oscillation period is

$$
T_{\mathrm{osc}}=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L/2}{g_{\mathrm{eff}}}}=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{r_0^3}{3G\mathfrak M_\oplus}}=\frac{T_0}{\sqrt3}.
$$
