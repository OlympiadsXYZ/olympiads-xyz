An hypothetical shuttle is launched to investigate the atmosphere (100% CO$_2$) of two extrasolar planets P$_1$ and P$_2$. The atmosphere is in static thermodynamic equilibrium. When the shuttle is near each planet, a radio probe is launched toward respective planet, in vertical direction (in the direction of the planet's radius). When the radio probe reaches constant velocity, it starts sending values of the pressure of the atmosphere. In Fig. 3.1 is plotted the atmospheric pressure values (in arbitrary units) as function of the time of descent for the planet P$_1$. When the probe touches the surface of planet P$_1$ it sends the value of the temperature $T_0=700\,\mathrm K$ and the value of the gravitational acceleration $g_0=10\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.

*The gravitational acceleration on each planet is assumed to be constant during uniform descent of the radio probes.*

### a)

Find the altitude $h_0$ from where the radio probe R$_1$ starts the uniform descent and thus starts the transmitting information.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="b">

### b)

Find the temperature of planet P$_1$ at the altitude $h=39.6\,\mathrm{km}$. You know: The universal gas constant $R=8.3\,\mathrm{J/(mol\,K)}$; the molar mass of CO$_2$, $\mu=44\,\mathrm{g/mol}$.

</ProblemSection>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

The matching official key is labelled preliminary Problem 3A. Marking scheme: a) 5 points, b) 10 points, c) 10 points. Printed approximations and the inconsistent temperature in the final table are retained.

<ProblemSection id="solution-a">

### a) Starting altitude

The pressure and the density of the atmosphere increase with the decrease of the altitude. The pressure at the surface of the planet is $p_{\max}$.

In figure 1 is represented a thin atmospheric layer with thickness $\Delta h$ near the surface of the planet P$_1$ where the atmosphere density can be considered $\rho_{\max}$. Inside this layer the pressure and the temperature are variable, reaching the values p$_{\max}$ and T$_0$ near the surface.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '65.7%', '--fig-max': '420px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/ioaa-2014-data-analysis-final/p2-sol-fig1.png" alt="Fig. 1. Thin atmospheric layer and pressure difference." width="1314" height="810" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Fig. 1. Thin atmospheric layer and pressure difference.</figcaption>
</figure>


The following relations can be written :

$$
p_{\max}=p+\Delta p;
$$
$$
\Delta p=\rho_{\max}g_0\Delta h,
$$

$\Delta p$ - the variation of the pressure due to the altitude variation $\Delta h$, $g_0$ the gravitational acceleration near the surface of planet P$_1$;

$$
\Delta h=\frac{\Delta p}{\rho_{\max}g_0}.
$$

The superior layer where the density is constant $\rho$ is represented too. At its base the pressure and temperature have the values $p$ and respectively $T$.

For a gas volume inside the near surface layer of the atmosphere,near the surface of the planet for $\nu$ mol of de gas according with Mendeleev – Klapeyron law

$$
p_{\max}V=\nu RT_0=\frac{m}{\mu}RT_0;
$$
$$
p_{\max}\mu=\frac{m}{V}RT_0=\rho_{\max}RT_0;
$$
$$
\rho_{\max}=\frac{p_{\max}\mu}{RT_0}.
$$

Just before touching the surface of the planet P$_1$ the radio- probe moves uniformly, so the distance $\Delta h$ is spend with speed $\vec{\mathrm v}$, in time $\Delta t$ :

$$
\Delta h=\mathrm v\Delta t;
$$
$$
\Delta h=\frac{\Delta p}{\rho_{\max}g_0};\ \rho_{\max}=\frac{p_{\max}\mu}{RT_0};
$$
$$
\frac{\Delta p}{\rho_{\max}g_0}=\mathrm v\Delta t;\ \mathrm v=\frac{\Delta p}{\rho_{\max}g_0\Delta t};\ \mathrm v=\frac{\Delta p}{\frac{p_{\max}\mu}{RT_0}g_0\Delta t};
$$
$$
\mathrm v=\frac{RT_0\Delta p}{g_0\mu p_{\max}\Delta t},(1)
$$

Where accordingly to fig .2 in the point F $(\tau;p_{\max})$, the trigonometric tangent of $\alpha$ is:

$$
\tan\alpha=\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}.
$$

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '63.8%', '--fig-max': '408px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/ioaa-2014-data-analysis-final/p2-sol-fig2.png" alt="Fig. 2. Tangent at the final pressure on P₁." width="1276" height="1149" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Fig. 2. Tangent at the final pressure on P₁.</figcaption>
</figure>


The rapport :

$$
\frac{\Delta p}{p_{\max}\Delta t},
$$

Can be determined in the graph in the point F :

$$
\mathrm v=\frac{\Delta p}{p_{\max}\Delta t}\frac{RT_0}{g_0\mu};
$$

| $\dfrac{\Delta p}{p_{\max}\Delta t}$ | $\dfrac{15\ \mathrm{u.c.}}{55\ \mathrm{u.c.}\ 250\ \mathrm s}=\dfrac{3}{2750}\dfrac{1}{\mathrm s}$ | $\dfrac{45\ \mathrm{u.c.}}{55\ \mathrm{u.c.}\ 750\ \mathrm s}=\dfrac{3}{2750}\dfrac{1}{\mathrm s}$ |
|---|---|---|

$$
\mathrm v=\frac{3}{2750}\frac{1}{\mathrm s}\frac{8{,}3\frac{\mathrm J}{\mathrm{molK}}700\ \mathrm K}{10\frac{\mathrm m}{\mathrm s^2}44\frac{10^{-3}\ \mathrm{kg}}{\mathrm{mol}}}=14{,}4\frac{\mathrm m}{\mathrm s};
$$
$$
h_0=\mathrm v\tau;\ \tau=3.750\ \mathrm s;
$$
$$
h_0=54.000\ \mathrm m=54\ \mathrm{km}.
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-b">

### b) Temperature at 39.6 km

b) Descending from the initial altitude $h_0=54\ \mathrm{km}$, with speed $\mathrm v=14{,}4\frac{\mathrm m}{\mathrm s}$, the radio-probe , after 1000 s arrives at the altitude $h=39{,}6\ \mathrm{km}$

$$
t=\frac{h_0-h}{\mathrm v}=\frac{14400\ \mathrm m}{14{,}4\frac{\mathrm m}{\mathrm s}}=1000\ \mathrm s.
$$

On Graph at altitude $h=39{,}6\ \mathrm{km}$, the atmospheric pressure is $p_h=4\ \mathrm{u.c.}$,- see point C on graph.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '60.2%', '--fig-max': '386px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/ioaa-2014-data-analysis-final/p2-sol-fig3-r4.png" alt="Fig. 3. Tangent at C and surface point F." width="1205" height="1034" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Fig. 3. Tangent at C and surface point F.</figcaption>
</figure>


In this point

$$
\tan\beta=\left(\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}\right)_{\mathrm C};
$$
$$
\left(\frac{\Delta p}{p\Delta t}\right)_{\mathrm C}=\frac{1}{p_h}\left(\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}\right)_{\mathrm C}=\frac{1}{4\ \mathrm{u.c.}}\frac{3\ \mathrm{u.c.}}{500\ \mathrm s}=\frac{3}{2000}\frac{1}{\mathrm s};
$$
$$
\left(\frac{p\Delta t}{\Delta p}\right)_{\mathrm C}=p_h\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta p}\right)_{\mathrm C}=4\ \mathrm{u.c.}\frac{500\ \mathrm s}{3\ \mathrm{u.c.}}=\frac{2000}{3}\mathrm s;
$$
$$
\mathrm v=\left(\frac{\Delta p}{p\Delta t}\right)_C\frac{RT_h}{g_0\mu};
$$
$$
\mathrm v=\frac{1}{p_h}\left(\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}\right)_C\frac{RT_h}{g_0\mu};
$$
$$
T_h=\frac{\mathrm v g_0\mu}{\frac{1}{p_h}\left(\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}\right)_C R};\ T_h=p_h\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta p}\right)_{\mathrm C}\frac{\mathrm v g_0\mu}{R};
$$
$$
T_h=\frac{2000}{3}\mathrm s\frac{14{,}4\frac{\mathrm m}{\mathrm s}10\frac{\mathrm m}{\mathrm s^2}44\frac{10^{-3}\ \mathrm{kg}}{\mathrm{mol}}}{8{,}3\frac{\mathrm J}{\mathrm{molK}}};
$$
$$
T_h=508{,}9\ \mathrm K.
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-c">

### c) Pressure and temperature on P₂

c) By using the same algorithm, corresponding to the points F, D, C, B şi A in graph on figure 4 :

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '61.1%', '--fig-max': '391px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/ioaa-2014-data-analysis-final/p2-sol-fig4.png" alt="Fig. 4. Linear pressure–time graph for P₂." width="1222" height="1021" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Fig. 4. Linear pressure–time graph for P₂.</figcaption>
</figure>


$$
v=\frac{\Delta p}{p_{\max}\Delta t}\frac{RT_0}{g_0\mu}=\frac{10}{50\cdot500}\frac{8.3\cdot750}{8\cdot44\cdot10^{-3}}\approx7\,\mathrm{m/s};
$$
$$
h_0=v\tau=7\cdot2000\,\mathrm m=14000\,\mathrm m=14\,\mathrm{km}.
$$

At F, $p=50$, $h_F=0$ and $T_F=750\,\mathrm K$.

$$
T_D=p_D\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta p}\right)_D\frac{vg_0\mu}{R}=593.7\,\mathrm K;
$$
$$
h_D=h_0-vt_D=14\,\mathrm{km}-7\cdot1500\,\mathrm m=3.5\,\mathrm{km}.
$$

Thus D has $p_D=40$, $h_D=3.5\,\mathrm{km}$, $T_D=593.7\,\mathrm K$.

$$
T_C=p_C\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta p}\right)_C\frac{vg_0\mu}{R}=445.3\,\mathrm K;
$$
$$
h_C=14\,\mathrm{km}-7\cdot1000\,\mathrm m=7\,\mathrm{km}.
$$

Thus C has $p_C=30$, $h_C=7\,\mathrm{km}$, $T_C=445.3\,\mathrm K$.

$$
T_B=p_B\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta p}\right)_B\frac{vg_0\mu}{R}=296.8\,\mathrm K;
$$
$$
h_B=14\,\mathrm{km}-7\cdot500\,\mathrm m=10.5\,\mathrm{km}.
$$

Thus B has $p_B=20$, $h_B=10.5\,\mathrm{km}$, $T_B=296.8\,\mathrm K$.

$$
T_A=p_A\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta p}\right)_A\frac{vg_0\mu}{R}=148.4\,\mathrm K;\qquad h_A=14\,\mathrm{km}.
$$

Thus A has $p_A=10$, $h_A=14\,\mathrm{km}$, $T_A=148.4\,\mathrm K$.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 0 | 10 | 14 | 148.4 |
| B | 500 | 20 | 10.5 | 296.8 |
| C | 1000 | 30 | 7 | 445.3 |
| D | 1500 | 40 | 3.5 | 593.3 |
| F | 2000 | 50 | 0 | 750 |
