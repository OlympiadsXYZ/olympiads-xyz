By observational facts, the scientists admit presence of a black-hole at the center of Milky Way.  
At the center of Milky Way, a hypothetical black-hole (Sagittarius A*) is located. A star S* is orbiting the black-hole SA*.

In the table 1 the following data is presented: the date and the angular position coordinates $(\alpha;\beta)$ of the star S* at different moments of the observation. The coordinates represent the angular distances of the projection of the star S* in the coordinates system (U, W), centered on the SA* (see figure 1).  
An angular distance of $\varphi=1\,\text{arcsec}$ corresponds to linear distance in the plane of the sky $d=41$ light days, therefore to a scale

$$
S_0=\frac d\varphi=41\,\frac{\text{light day}}{\text{arcsec}}.

### c)

Using your plot find out following elements of the real orbit of star S* around the black hole SA*:

I. $a$ – semi-major axis (in light days units); $b$ – small semi-minor axis (in light days units); $e$ – eccentricity;  
II. $r_{\min}$ – the minimum distance between S* and SA* (in light days units); $r_{\max}$ – the maximum distance between S* and SA* (in light days units);  
III. The distance from the observer to the S*;  
IV. the orbiting period of star S* around SA* (obtain the best possible result by taking as many measurements as possible and by taking their arithmetic mean);  
V. the total mass of the system “SA* – S*”.

Presenting the intermediate and final data in tables is recommended for an accurate evaluation.

### Numeric processing — a)

From the measurements done on paper system (X, Y):

$$
a_{\rm measured}\approx135\,\mathrm{mm};\quad b_{\rm measured}\approx66\,\mathrm{mm};\quad d_{\min}=17\,\mathrm{mm}.
$$

Corresponding to the numeric scale:

$$
a_{\rm measured}=\varphi_aS;\qquad b_{\rm measured}=\varphi_bS;\qquad d_{\min}^{\rm mes}=\varphi_{\min}S;
$$
$$
\varphi_a=0.1125\,\text{arcsec};\quad\varphi_b=0.055\,\text{arcsec};\quad\varphi_{d\min}=0{,}01416\,\text{arcsec}.
$$

The scale is $1\,\text{arcsec}\longleftrightarrow41$ light day. The source then lists $\varphi_a=0.1125\,\text{arcsec}\longleftrightarrow a$, $\varphi_b=0.055\,\text{arcsec}\longleftrightarrow b$, and $\varphi_{d\min}=0{,}01466\,\text{arcsec}\longleftrightarrow r_{\min}$.

$$
a=\frac{0.1125\,\text{arcsec}\cdot41\,\text{light days}}{1\,\text{arcsec}}=4.6125\,\text{light days};
$$
$$
b=\frac{0.055\,\text{arcsec}\cdot41\,\text{light days}}{1\,\text{arcsec}}=2.255\,\text{light days};
$$
$$
r_{\min}=\frac{0{,}0146\,\text{arcsec}\cdot41\,\text{light days}}{1\,\text{arcsec}}=0{,}5986\,\text{light days};
$$
$$
c=\sqrt{a^2-b^2}\approx4{,}0236\,\text{light days};
$$
$$
r_{\min}=a-c=0{,}5889\,\text{light days};\quad r_{\max}=a+c=8{,}6361\,\text{light days};
$$
$$
e=\sqrt{1-\frac{b^2}{a^2}}\approx0.87.
$$

**Source note:** the three different printed minimum-angle values (0.01416, 0.01466 and 0.0146) and two minimum-distance results are preserved.

<ProblemSection id="solution-mass">

### Numeric processing — c)

By using the III rd Kepler's law:

$$
T^2=\frac{4\pi^2}{K(M_{\rm SA*}+M_{\rm S*})}a^3;\qquad M_{\rm SA*}+M_{\rm S*}=\frac{4\pi^2a^3}{KT^2};
$$
$$
a=4.6125\,\text{light days};\quad T\approx15\,\text{years};\quad K=6{,}67\cdot10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\,m^2\,kg^{-2}};
$$
$$
1\,\mathrm{ld}\approx2{,}6\cdot10^{13}\,\mathrm m;\quad1\,\text{years}\approx3{,}1\cdot10^7\,\mathrm s;\quad4\pi^2\approx40;
$$
$$
(2{,}6)^3\approx17{,}6;\qquad(4.6125)^3\approx10^2;
$$
$$
M_{\rm SA*}+M_{\rm S*}=\frac{40\cdot10^2\cdot17{,}6\cdot10^{39}}{6{,}67\cdot10^{-11}\cdot225\cdot10^{15}}\,\mathrm{kg}\approx4{,}6\cdot10^{36}\,\mathrm{kg};
$$
$$
M_{\text{Sun}}\approx1{,}8\cdot10^{30}\,\mathrm{kg};\qquad M_{\rm SA*}+M_{\rm S*}\approx2{,}5\cdot10^6M_{\text{Sun}};
$$
$$
M_{\rm S*}\ll M_{\rm SA*};\qquad M_{\rm SA*}\approx2{,}5\cdot10^6M_{\text{Sun}}.
$$

The printed approximations, gravitational-constant symbol K and solar mass are retained.
