## Условие

The SAR21 is the Singapore Armed Forces’ weapon of choice. A variant of it features a 3x optical scope (called the sharpshooter scope) with a diameter of 25mm, and a length of 120mm. It is also the weapon of choice for an unfortunate astronomer stuck on an island Northeast of Singapore, as he reckons the scope has a greater light gathering power.

**a** Find the f-number of the sharpshooter scope. [1m] **[1 т.]**

**b** The limiting magnitude of stars on the island is 3.2. What is the new limiting magnitude when the sharpshooter scope is used? [2m] The diameter of the pupil of the human eye is 8mm (night adapted). **[2 т.]**

**c** The astronomer points the scope to the Moon. Given the Earth-Moon distance is 384400km, would he be able to resolve these two craters on the moon? [2m]

- a. Ross Crater (D = 24km). Fun fact: This crater is in Mare Tranquillitatis

- b. Rosse Crater (D = 12km). Fun fact: This crater is in Mare Nectaris **[2 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**Optics and detectors (6m)**

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-a">

### a)

From the given context, the scope is a **Galilean refractor**, as the image and source object are both upright in order for it to facilitate aiming in the battlefield, i.e.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '83.7%', '--fig-max': '536px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/sao-2016-4-q/q07-native-refractors.png" alt="Both full source refractor ray diagrams, native A. Galilean refractor and B. Newtonian refractor labels, upright/inverted objects, lenses and every ray arrow." width="1675" height="1032" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


Hence, *the focal point of the objective lens is behind the eyepiece lens*. [M1]

From the given magnification (3x), the ratio of the focal lengths of the objective to eyepiece focal lengths is3, i.e.

$$
\therefore\frac{f_o}{f_e}=3.
$$

 [B1]

Award this1m only if the participant has shown consideration of the above.

From a ray diagram, since the distance between the two lenses is the difference between their focal lengths, the objective focal length $f_o$ is

$$
\therefore f_o=L\times\frac32=120\,\mathrm{mm}\times\frac32=180\,\mathrm{mm}.
$$

Thus, the f-number is

$$
F=\frac{f_o}D=\frac{180\,\mathrm{mm}}{25\,\mathrm{mm}}=\mathbf{7.2}.
$$

**Common Mistakes:**

- Expression for f-number was inverted, giving extremely fast but unrealistic optics
- Misuse of terms *focal length* and *aperture*, by those with little understanding of optics

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-b">

### b)

For point sources (i.e. stars),

$$
\frac{LGP_{scope}}{LGP_{eye}}=\frac{A_{scope}}{A_{eye}}=\left(\frac{D_{scope}}{D_{eye}}\right)^2=\left(\frac{25\,\mathrm{mm}}{8\,\mathrm{mm}}\right)^2=\mathbf{9.765}.
$$

 [B1]

Hence, the new limiting magnitude is

$$
m'_{lim}=m_{lim}+2.5\log\left(\frac{LGP_{scope}}{LGP_{eye}}\right)=3.2+2.5\log(9.765)=\mathbf{5.67}.
$$

 [B1]

**Common Mistakes:**

- Light Gathering Power/light grasp was often incalculated incorrectly where employed
- Incorrectly converting the extra factor in LGP to a decrease in limiting magnitude instead of an *increase* (ability to detect fainter objects)
- Magnification does not affect the limiting magnitude obtained this way for point objects

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-c">

### c)

The angular resolution of the scope using the *Rayleigh criterion* is

$$
\vartheta_{lim}=\frac{1.22\lambda}D.
$$

Hence assuming the wavelength of visible light $\lambda=500\,\mathrm{nm}$, the linear resolution of the scope for small $\vartheta_{lim}$ is

$$
d_{lim}=\frac{1.22\lambda}D R=\frac{1.22(500\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm m)}{25\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm m}(384400\times10^3\,\mathrm m)=\mathbf{9.38\times10^3\,m}.
$$

 [M1]

Hence, since $d_{lim}<d_{Ross}$ and $d_{lim}<d_{Rosse}$, the scope can resolve both craters. [B1]

Only award full2m if the wavelength of visible light is reasonably estimated.

Other conclusions based on reasonable estimates of the wavelength of visible light between300nm and700nm are also accepted.

Correct conclusions on the basis of incorrect physics are awarded no marks.

**Common Mistakes:**

- Guessing an answer without quantitative proof nets zero marks.
- Some used the incorrect units for angular resolution
- Missing assumption for the wavelength of light in the visible spectrum/wavelength used is not part of visible spectrum

</ProblemSection>
