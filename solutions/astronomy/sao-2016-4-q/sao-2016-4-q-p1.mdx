The Hubble Space Telescope (HST), launched in 1990, will someday be decommissioned. It will fire its retrorockets, fall back into Earth's atmosphere and crash harmlessly into the Pacific Ocean. Let us study the dynamics of this operation. Assume that the HST is currently in a circular orbit around the Earth and that the retrorockets will fire in precisely the opposite direction of its motion. The HST has an orbital period of 95.6 minutes.

**a** What is the radius of the HST's orbit? [2m] **[2 т.]**

**b** What is the orbital velocity of the HST? [2m] **[2 т.]**

**c** What is the minimum ΔV required to crash the HST into the Earth's surface? [4m] Briefly explain why such a trajectory would be unwise. [1m] **[5 т.]**

**d** NASA suggests that the HST should impact the ocean at an angle of at most 75° from the vertical. What would be the ΔV required to accomplish this? Neglect air resistance. [5m] **[5 т.]**

**Celestial Mechanics (14m)**

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-a">

### a)

**(First principles)**

The gravitational force provides the centripetal force for the HST’s circular orbit, thus

$$
\frac{GM_em}{a^2}=\frac{mv^2}{a},\qquad\because v=a\omega,\quad\omega=\frac{2\pi}{T},\qquad\therefore GM_e=a\left(\frac{2\pi a}{T}\right)^2.
$$

**(Solution)**

Rearranging, Kepler’s Third Law: $T^2=\frac{4\pi^2}{GM_e}a^3$. [A1]

Hence, rearranging to find $a$,

$$
a=\sqrt[3]{\frac{GM_eT^2}{4\pi^2}}=\sqrt[3]{\frac{(6.674\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^2})(5.972\times10^{24}\,\mathrm{kg})(5736\,\mathrm s)^2}{4\pi^2}}=\mathbf{6.93\times10^6\,m}.
$$

 [B1]

Note the full equation is in fact, $T^2=\frac{4\pi^2}{G\mu}a^3$ where $\mu$ is the reduced mass; however since $M_e\gg m$ then $\mu\simeq M_e$.

Common Mistakes:

- There is no need to use the vis-viva equation for circular orbits – remembering it incorrectly is self-penalisation

- $T^2=a^3$ is incorrect here! This form of Kepler’s Third Law is only applicable to satellites of the Sun, i.e. planets, comets, where T and a are in units of yr and AU respectively.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-b">

### b)

For a circular orbit,

$$
v_0=\frac{2\pi a}{T}=\frac{2\pi(6.93\times10^6\,\mathrm m)}{5736\,\mathrm s}=\mathbf{7.59\times10^3\,m\,s^{-1}}.
$$

 [M1, A1]

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-c">

### c)

The problem can in fact be visualised as a **Hohmann Transfer Orbit**, with corresponding diagram (not to scale),

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '49.6%', '--fig-max': '318px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/sao-2016-4-q/q01-transfer-orbit.png" alt="Native complete Hohmann-transfer orbit, Earth, circular orbit, ellipse, radii and velocity arrows." width="993" height="649" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


Conserving orbital energy $U$,

$$
U=-\frac{GM_em}{2\left(\frac{a+R_e}{2}\right)}=\frac12mv_{ap}^2-\frac{GM_em}{a}.
$$

 [A1]

Solving for $v_{ap}$,

$$
2GM_e\left(\frac1a-\frac1{a+R_e}\right)=v_{ap}^2,
$$

$$
v_{ap}=\sqrt{\frac{2GM_eR_e}{a(a+R_e)}}.
$$

 [M1]

$$
=\sqrt{\frac{2(6.674\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^2})(5.972\times10^{24}\,\mathrm{kg})(6.371\times10^6\,\mathrm m)}{(6.926\times10^6\,\mathrm m)((6.926\times10^6\,\mathrm m)+(6.371\times10^6\,\mathrm m))}}=7.426\times10^3\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.
$$

 [A1]

$$
\Delta v=v_0-v_{ap}=(7.591\times10^3\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}})-(7.426\times10^3\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}})=\mathbf{165\,m\,s^{-1}}.
$$

 [B1]

**Such a trajectory would skim the Earth’s surface. As such it would be hard to control where the HST impacts the surface.** [A1]

There is no need to consider orbital angular momentum here as there is only one unknown, also $v_{ap}r_{ap}=v_pr_p$ gives no useful information.

**Common Mistakes:**

- $\Delta v$ is a scalar – it has no direction!
- Quoting Kepler’s first law in polar form or vis-viva equation without a supporting diagram or working
- Question does not involve escape velocity!
- Illogical conclusion unrelated to the question (e.g. damaging buildings)

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-d">

### d)

The diagram for this scenario is shown below.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '55.8%', '--fig-max': '357px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/sao-2016-4-q/q01-impact-orbit.png" alt="Complete native impact-angle trajectory and Earth with 75-degree angle and all velocity/radius labels." width="1117" height="808" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


Conserving orbital energy $U$,

$$
\frac12mv_{crash}^2-\frac{GM_em}{R_e}=\frac12mv_{ap}^2-\frac{GM_em}{a}.
$$

 [A1]

Conserving orbital angular momentum $L$,

$$
\vartheta=180^\circ-75^\circ=105^\circ,\qquad L=mv_{ap}a=mv_{crash}(\sin105^\circ)R_e,
$$

$$
\Rightarrow v_{crash}=\frac{a}{R_e(\sin75^\circ)}v_{ap}.
$$

 [A1]

Rearranging and substituting,

$$
v_{ap}^2\left(1-\left(\frac{a}{R_e(\sin75^\circ)}\right)^2\right)=2GM_e\left(\frac1a-\frac1{R_e}\right).
$$

 [M1]

$$
v_{ap}=\sqrt{\frac{2GM_e\left(\frac1a-\frac1{R_e}\right)}{1-\left(\frac{a}{R_e(\sin75^\circ)}\right)^2}}=\sqrt{\frac{2GM_e(R_e-a)}{aR_e\left(1-\frac{a^2}{R_e^2(\sin^275^\circ)}\right)}}
$$

$$
=\sqrt{\frac{2(6.674\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^2})(5.972\times10^{24}\,\mathrm{kg})((6.371\times10^6-6.926\times10^6)\,\mathrm m)}{(6.926\times10^6\,\mathrm m)(6.371\times10^6\,\mathrm m)\left(1-\frac{(6.926\times10^6\,\mathrm m)^2}{(6.371\times10^6\,\mathrm m)^2(\sin^275^\circ)}\right)}}=6.132\times10^3\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.
$$

 [A1]

$$
\Delta v=v_0-v_{ap}=(7.591\times10^3\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}})-(6.132\times10^3\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}})=\mathbf{1.46\times10^3\,m\,s^{-1}}.
$$

 [B1]

Recall $\mathbf L=\mathbf r\times\mathbf p$, hence $|\mathbf L|=L=mvr\sin\vartheta$, where $\vartheta$ is the angle between the vectors $\mathbf r$ and $\mathbf p$.

Recall $\sin105^\circ=\sin75^\circ$.

Also observe that as the impact angle from the vertical $\alpha$ decreases from $90^\circ$, the periapsis of the new orbit moves closer to the focus (Earth’s centre).

**Common Mistakes:**

- Note the actual angle $\vartheta$ between the $\mathbf r$ and $\mathbf v$ vectors is **105°**, not 75°
- Some candidates misinterpreted the question, calculating for the scenario where the HST impacts at 15° to the vertical (i.e. 75° to the horizontal).

</ProblemSection>
