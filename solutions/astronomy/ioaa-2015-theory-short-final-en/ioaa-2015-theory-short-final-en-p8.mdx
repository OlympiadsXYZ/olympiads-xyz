*SHORT PROBLEMS*

## Условие

A strong continuum radio signal from a celestial body has been observed as a burst with a very short duration of 700 µs. The observed flux density at a frequency of 1660 MHz is measured to be 0.35 kJy. If the distance from the source is known to be 2.3 kpc, estimate the brightness temperature of this source.

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- $1.5 \times 10^{26}$ K

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**Answer and Marking Scheme:**

**1** During 700 µs, the radio wave travels about r = c t = $3\times10^{10}\times700\times10^{-6} = 2.1\times10^{7}$ cm. The region from where the burst originates must be no larger than the distance that light can travel during the duration of the burst. So we estimate that r is the size of the source. (r = $2.1\times10^{7}$ cm, R = 2.3 kpc)

**[10]**

Flux density observed at 1660 MHz is

$S_{1660MHz} = 0.35$ kJy

$= 0.35\times10^{3}\times10^{-23}$ ergs s$^{-1}$ cm$^{-2}$ Hz$^{-1}$

$= 3.5\times10^{-21}$ ergs s$^{-1}$ cm$^{-2}$ Hz$^{-1}$

**[10]**

**2** The solid angle subtended by this source of radiation is

$\Omega = \pi\,(r/R)^2 = 3.14\,(2.1\times10^{7}/2.3\times10^{3}\times3.086\times10^{18})^2$

$= 2.75\times10^{-29}$ sr

**[30]**

**3** The flux density is related to the total brightness by the relation:

$S_{1660MHz} = B_{1660MHz}\,\Omega$

**[20]**

while at this frequency, the total brightness can be approximated from the Rayleigh-Jeans formula:

$B_{1660MHz} = 2kT_b\nu^2/c^2$

**[20]**

where $T_b$ is the brightness temperature. Then we have:

$T_b = S_{1660MHz}c^2/2k\nu^2\Omega$

$T_b = [3.5\times10^{-21}\times(3\times10^{10})^2]/[2\times(1.38\times10^{-16})\times(1660\times10^{6})^2\times(2.75\times10^{-29})]$

$= 1.5\times10^{26}$ K

**[10]**


</Spoiler>

13. Two protons collide to produce deuteron, positron, and neutrino in the center of a star. Write down the reaction equation and calculate the minimum temperature needed for this reaction. Assume that the neutrino mass is negligible.

Answer

**1** $p + p + \Delta E \rightarrow D + e^{+} + \nu_e$ **[20]**

**2** $\Delta E = (m_e + m_D - 2m_p)c^2 = (m_e + (m_p + m_n) - 2m_p)c^2 = (m_e + m_n - m_p)c^2$ **[20]**

**3** $\Delta E = (9.109\times10^{-31} + 1.675\times10^{-27} - 1.673\times10^{-27})\mathrm{kg}\,(2.99792\times10^{8}\mathrm{ms^{-1}})^2 = 2.6198\times10^{-13}\ J$ **[20]**

**4** $\Delta E \sim k_BT \rightarrow T \sim \frac{\Delta E}{k_B}$ **[20]**

**5** $T = \frac{2.6198\times10^{-13}\ J}{8.315\frac{J}{mol\ K}} = \frac{2.6198\times10^{-13}mol\ K}{8.315}\times6.02\times10^{23}\frac{1}{mol} = 1.894\times10^{10}\ K$ **[20]**

</details>
