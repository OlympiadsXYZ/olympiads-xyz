## Условие

The largest discovered now in our Solar system Transneptune body is the dwarf planet Eris (Эрида). Now Eris is near its aphelion point. Find the apparent stellar magnitude of Eris as visible from Earth. Approximately when will the next “Great opposition” of Eris be? What will the magnitude of Eris be as visible from Earth at this “Great opposition”?

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

Let us at first answer for the question about the “Great opposition”. And what does it mean the wo… “Great”? The Great oppositions appear for the situation when the distance to opposition planet is small… than during usual (mean) oppositions. In our case it is an opposition when the outer planet is near perihelion. Now Eris is near its aphelion point. From the value of the semiaxis of the Eris' orbit it … evident that its period is of order of centuries and we may talk about the period of the Great oppositions … “years” (in comparison with “weeks” for the Great opposition of Mars) near after of half revolution of th… dwarf planet around the Sun.

The period of Eris can be taken from the table (days should be recalculated into years)

$$
T_{Ed}=204852\ \text{days}/365.25\ \text{days/year}=560.85\ \text{years}\approx560\ \text{years}
$$

or found from the III Kepler law

$$
(T_{Ed}/T_E)^2=(A_{Ed}/A_E)^3,
$$

(here and below $A$ means the major semiaxis, $T$ is period, $e$ is eccentricity, $\alpha$ is albedo, $D$ are diameters … the bodies, $R$ are distances, and indexes E, Ed, Sa and S correspond to Earth, Eris, Saturn and Sun).

$$
T_{Ed}=T_E\cdot(A_{Ed}/A_E)^{3/2}=1\cdot(68.01)^{3/2}\ \text{years}\approx560\ \text{years}.
$$

So the years of the “Great oppositions of Eris” will be after $T_{Ed}/2\approx280$ years.

For the questions of the stellar magnitude there may be a few ways to solve this problem. The main two … them is to compare Eris with some other planet (data can be given from the table) or to calculate it direct… using formulae of the total solar irradiation.

Let us use the first one and compare Eris and, for example, Saturn.

The following aspects should be taken into account:

1. $R_{S\to}$. Distance from the Sun. The intensity of light coming to the bodies is reverse proportional to th… square of these distances.
2. $D$. Size (diameter) of the body. The intensity of light reflecting by the body is proportional to the square of these sizes.
3. $\alpha$. Albedo. The intensity of light reflecting by the body is proportional to the albedos.
4. $R_{E\to}$. Distance from the observer (i.e. from the Earth). The intensity of light coming to the telesco… is reverse proportional to the square of these distances.

The flux to Earth from Eris

$$
F_{Ed}\sim\alpha_{Ed}\cdot D_{Ed}^2\cdot(1/R_{E-Ed})^2\cdot(1/R_{S-Ed})^2.
$$

The flux to Earth from Saturn

$$
F_{Sa}\sim\alpha_{Sa}\cdot D_{Sa}^2\cdot(1/R_{E-Sa})^2\cdot(1/R_{S-Sa})^2.
$$

The ratio of fluxes

$$
F_{Ed}/F_{Sa}=(\alpha_{Ed}/\alpha_{Sa})\cdot(D_{Ed}^2/D_{Sa}^2)\cdot(R_{E-Sa}\cdot R_{S-Sa})^2/(R_{E-Ed}\cdot R_{S-Ed})^2.
$$

For the current situation the following distances should be used:

Since Eris is near its aphelion, $R_{S-Ed}=(1+e)A_{Ed}$, and $R_{E-Ed}=(1+e_{Ed})A_{Ed}\pm A_E$, but, since we do n… know the real configuration of Earth, Sun and Eris (including we do not know the inclination of the Eri… orbit), we should use the average value of $R_{E-Ed}=(1+e_{Ed})A_{Ed}$ as well.

The stellar magnitude for Saturn is given for the mean opposition, so we should not use eccentricity he… $R_{S-Sa}=A_{Sa}$ and $R_{E-Sa}=A_{Sa}-A_E$.

$$
F_{Ed}/F_{Sa}=(0.86/0.68)\cdot(2600/120536)^2\cdot(8.584\cdot9.584)^2/(1.434\cdot68.01)^4.
$$

$$
F_{Ed}/F_{Sa}\approx4.4\cdot10^{-8}.
$$

So the difference in magnitudes between the current visible Eris and Saturn in mean opposition is

$$
\Delta m=-2^{\mathrm m}.5\cdot\lg(F_{Ed}/F_{Sa})\approx18^{\mathrm m}.4.
$$

and the magnitude of Eris

$$
m_{Ed}=m_{Sa}+\Delta m=0^{\mathrm m}.7+18^{\mathrm m}.4\approx19^{\mathrm m}.1.
$$

The real values that can be found in reports of Eris observations are $18^{\mathrm m}.7\div18^{\mathrm m}.8$.

For the situation of the “Great opposition” of the Eris it is near its perihelion. $R_{S-Ed}=(1-e)A_E$… $R_{E-Ed}\approx(1-e_{Ed})A_{Ed}-A_E$ (here we write “$\approx$” since we do not know the inclination of the Eris' orbit) and n… changed values for Saturn to compare.

$$
F_{Ed}/F_{Sa}=(0.86/0.68)\cdot(2600/120536)^2\cdot(8.584\cdot9.584)^2/((0.566\cdot68.01\cdot(0.566\cdot68.01-1))^2).
$$

$$
F_{Ed}/F_{Sa}\approx1.91\cdot10^{-6}.
$$

$$
\Delta m=-2^{\mathrm m}.5\cdot\lg(F_{Ed}/F_{Sa})\approx14^{\mathrm m}.3.
$$

and the magnitude of Eris in the “Great opposition” is

$$
m_{Ed}=m_{Sa}+\Delta m=0^{\mathrm m}.7+14^{\mathrm m}.3\approx15^{\mathrm m}.0.
$$

And we should repeat that this way with comparing the Eris and Saturn it is not the only possible correct way for solution.
