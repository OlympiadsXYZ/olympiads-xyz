## Условие

According to an ancient legend of Middle Volga there was a constellation called White Leopard (Белый Барс – Pardus Album) in the sky in the very past, in which the number of stars were exactly equal to the number of letters in the Greek alphabet, and the stars had magnitudes $\alpha$ PaA – $+0.10^{m}$, $\beta$ PaA – $+0.20^{m}$, $\gamma$ PaA – $+0.30^{m}$, $\delta$ PaA – $+0.40^{m}$, and so on with adding $0.10^{m}$ till $\omega$ PaA. Calculate the total magnitude of the stars of this constellation.

### Solution

There are 24 letters in the Greek alphabet, so take the 24 magnitudes from +0.10 to +2.40.

The source first jokes: $0.10+0.20+\cdots+2.40=12\cdot2.50=30$ magnitudes — “Sorry, a joke.”

Really we must add to the first star's flux the other 23 fluxes, decreasing exponentially with pitch

$$
10^{0.4\cdot0.10}\approx1.09648.
$$

The infinite sum can be found by a geometric progression. The source prints

$$
\Phi(1\text{–}\infty)=\Phi(1)+\Phi(\infty)/1.09648,
$$

$$
\Phi(1\text{–}\infty)=\Phi(\infty)/1.04713=\Phi(1),
$$

$$
\Phi(1\text{–}\infty)=\frac{\Phi(1)}{1-1/1.04713}=\Phi(1)\cdot11.37.
$$

*Source discrepancies: the middle line is printed as an equality chain, and the subsequent denominator uses 1.04713 instead of the preceding pitch 1.09648. The printed multiplier 11.37 follows the latter pitch.*

The magnitude of the infinite series would be

$$
m(1\text{–}\infty)=0.10-2.5\log11.37\approx-2.54.
$$

There are only 24 stars. Subtract the flux of the analogous infinite series starting at the 25th star, magnitude 2.50. Every star in this second series is 2.40 magnitudes weaker; so is the series as a whole:

$$
m(25\text{–}\infty)=2.50-2.5\log11.37\approx-0.14.
$$

The source jokes again about subtracting magnitudes: $-2.54-(-0.14)=-2.40$ — “Sorry, a joke once again.” Fluxes must instead be subtracted. The second flux is $10^{0.4\cdot2.4}\approx9.12$ times weaker:

$$
\Phi(25\text{–}\infty)=\frac{\Phi(1\text{–}\infty)}{10^{0.96}}=\frac{\Phi(1)\cdot11.37}{9.12}\approx\Phi(1)\cdot1.246,
$$

$$
\Phi(1\text{–}24)=\Phi(1\text{–}\infty)-\Phi(25\text{–}\infty)=\Phi(1)(11.37-1.246)=\Phi(1)\cdot10.12.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{m(1\text{–}24)=0.10-2.5\log10.12\approx-2.41.}
$$

**Attention!** The second joke happens to give almost the right number, by pure coincidence. Not any solution giving the same numerical value is right; pay attention when checking.

**Other approaches.** Calculate each star's flux, add the fluxes, and recover the total apparent magnitude. A proper calculation gives exactly the same answer (easy to check in Excel), but is lengthy without a computer or programmable calculator.

A very rough linear approximation uses the “average” magnitude $(0.10+2.40)/2=1.25$, giving

$$
1.25-2.5\log24\approx1.25-3.45=-2.20.
$$

The source says this is very rough and not suitable as a workable solution, but “a little better than nothing”.
