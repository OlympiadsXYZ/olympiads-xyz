## Условие

<nav className="problem-sections-toc" aria-label="Части на задачата">

**В тази задача**

- [Source Hertzsprung–Russell diagram](#hr-diagram)

</nav>

A spiral galaxy consisting mainly of A7-A8 spectral class stars was discovered in the Southern Cross (Crux) constellation. The galaxy may be seen as oval of about 40 by 30 arcsec in the sky. The broadened H$\alpha$ line is observed at wavelengths approximately from 7054 Å to 7057 Å in the spectrum of the galaxy. Other lines in the spectrum are also shifted and broadened proportionally. Estimate the number of stars in the galaxy.

### Solution

First, the values are given with accuracy of one digit, and the wavelength difference can also be obtained with only one digit, so a more accurate final answer is inappropriate. The redshift is $z=\Delta\lambda/\lambda\approx0.075$, so the galaxy recedes with $v=zc=22500$ km/s. By Hubble's law, with $H=68$ km/s/Mpc,

$$
L=v/H=zc/H\approx320\ \mathrm{Mpc}.
$$

The angular size is 40″, so the source prints

$$
D=(40/205265)\cdot320\ \mathrm{Mpc}\approx62\ \mathrm{kpc}=1.9\cdot10^{21}\ \mathrm m,
$$

$$
R=D/2=31\ \mathrm{kpc}=1.0\cdot10^{21}\ \mathrm m.
$$

*The denominator 205265 is printed in the source.*

The spiral galaxy appears elliptical, so its plane is inclined to the line of sight at angle $\alpha$:

$$
\cos\alpha=30/40,\qquad\alpha\approx41^\circ.
$$

The spectral broadening is due to stellar motion. The solution text gives a range 6854 Å to 6857 Å, a broadening of 1.5 Å in either direction. The radial speed of the outermost stars is

$$
V_R=(1.5\ \mathring{\mathrm A}/7055\ \mathring{\mathrm A})c=64\ \mathrm{km/s},
$$

and their total speed is

$$
V=V_R/\sin\alpha=96\ \mathrm{km/s}.
$$

*Source discrepancy: the solution prose says 6854–6857 Å, while the question says 7054–7057 Å and the solution's velocity calculation uses 7055 Å.*

Assume the outermost stars move in circular orbits with this speed and the galactic mass is concentrated at the centre. Kepler's law gives

$$
V^2/R=GM/R^2,\qquad M=V^2R/G=1.34\cdot10^{41}\ \mathrm{kg}.
$$

For stars of spectral class A7–A8 take Altair as typical, with mass 1.7 solar masses, or $3.4\cdot10^{30}$ kg. Then

$$
N=M/M_A\approx4\cdot10^{10},
$$

or **about 40 billion stars**.

</ProblemSection>
