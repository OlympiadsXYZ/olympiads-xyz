## Условие

<nav className="problem-sections-toc" aria-label="Части на задачата">

**В тази задача**

- [Source sky map](#sky-map)

</nav>

Some time after the events described in the previous problem (nobody knows even the order of magnitude how long after – minutes, or hours, or days, or years ...), Venus at the point of eastern elongation came to close conjunction with Mars, which was located near the aphelion of its orbit. At the same time a total lunar eclipse occurred on the Earth.

**3.1** Draw the corresponding scheme.

**3.2** Explain, which animal (sitting at the same poles) may see this lunar eclipse. (At the very end of your explanation write as answer **B+** or **B−** for the Bear and **P+** or **P−** for the Penguin.) An artistic picture of the observations of the animals is welcome.

**3.3** Calculate in which constellation the eclipsed Moon was observed.

**3.4** Estimate minimal possible time passed from the situation of the previous problem to the situation of the current one.

### Solution

**3.1.** The corresponding scheme is supplied separately in the archive.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '53.3%', '--fig-max': '342px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/iao-2015-theory-beta/p3-conjunction-astra-s9-r1.png" alt="Official conjunction diagram: Sun, Earth, Moon, Venus, Mars, angles α β γ and directions1 and2" width="1068" height="1510" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


The angles are

$$
\alpha=\arccos\frac{A_V}{(1+e)A_M}=\arccos\frac{0.723}{1.093\cdot1.524}=64.3^\circ,
$$

$$
\beta=\arccos\frac{A_V}{A_E}=\arccos\frac{0.723}{1}=43.7^\circ.
$$

The source prints the following chain for the third angle:

$$
\gamma=180^\circ-\alpha-\beta=\arccos(A_V/A_E)=\arccos(0.723/1)=72^\circ.
$$

*Source discrepancy: the two arccos expressions repeat the preceding β calculation, while 72° belongs to $180^\circ-\alpha-\beta$.*

**3.3.** Mars is at aphelion, opposite its perihelion position. Mars at perihelion may be in great opposition for Earth observers in late August (August 25). Direction 1 (Mars→Sun) therefore corresponds to Sun→Earth on August 25. Direction 2 corresponds to Sun→Earth 72° earlier, or 71 days earlier, June 15. By the sky map this direction is in **Ophiuchus**, near the Sagittarius border.

**3.2.** The situation is thus in mid-June. The Sun shines for the Bear at the North Pole, while the Moon, on the opposite side of the celestial sphere during eclipse, can be observed by the Penguin at the South Pole. Answer: **B− P+**.

**3.4.** The first situation was near equinox, the second in mid-June. Both require the Moon near its orbital nodes. The nodes move in the opposite direction with a period of 18.6 years. The minimum interval can occur if the line of nodes moves from the end-September/end-March line to the mid-June/mid-December line, about 28% of a period:

$$
18.6\cdot0.28=5.2\ \text{years}.
$$

Thus the minimum is about 5 years and almost 2–3 months. In 5 years and exactly 3 months the number of synodic lunar periods is

$$
(365.25\cdot5.25)/29.53=64.94.
$$

For our situations it should be an integer and a half:

$$
64.5\cdot29.53=1904.7\ \text{days}.
$$

The source checks against March 20, 2015 (total solar eclipse near the North Pole and equinox). 1904.7 days later is June 5–6, 2020. It says that a lunar eclipse, “although not full but partial”, will occur on June 5 evening, with the Moon in Sagittarius. *This is the source's approximate check, explicitly not the total lunar eclipse assumed in the question; no stronger agreement is claimed.*
