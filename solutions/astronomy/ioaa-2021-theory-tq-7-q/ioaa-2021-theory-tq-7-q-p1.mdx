## Условие

Almost half of the stars that we see are either binary or multiple star systems. A well-known example of this is Menkalinan (Beta Aurigae), which was initially thought to be a single star, but today recognised as a binary system comprising two stars that we will refer to as Menkalinan A and B. In the following figure, a spectrum of the system (obtained by the observatory of the Universidad de los Andes, in Bogotá) is shown:

Spectrum of Menkalinan binary system in the region of $H\alpha$. Y-axis is for the relative flux, and X-axis measures wavelengths. Menkalinan A is marked as A in the graph, and Menkalinan B is marked as B.

Answer the following questions using the plot and noting that the wavelength of $H\alpha$ line in the laboratory frame is $656.28\ \mathrm{nm}$. Assume circular orbits, and assume that the binary system as a whole is at rest with respect to the observer.

**7.1** In the spectrum, we can see the $H\alpha$ line for each star in the system. Calculate the line-of-sight velocity of each star (km/s) and determine, at the time of this observation, which of the two stars is moving towards us. **[5 т.]**

**7.2** The binary system is located $81.1$ light years from Earth and has an orbital period of $3.96$ days. The semi-major axis for Menkalinan B (smaller star) was measured to be $3.35\,milliarcseconds$. If the mass ratio of the two components is $1.026$, find the total mass of the system (in solar masses). **[4 т.]**

**7.3** Calculate the individual masses of Menkalinan $A$ and $B$ in solar masses. **[2 т.]**

**7.4** Since Menkalinan $A$ and $B$ are main sequence stars, use the relation:

$$
\frac{L}{L_\odot} = \left(\frac{M}{M_\odot}\right)^{3.5}
$$

to estimate the luminosity of each star (in solar luminosity). **[2 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **7.1** $v_A=-82.23\ \mathrm{km/s},\quad v_B=+100.50\ \mathrm{km/s}$
- **7.2** $M_{\mathrm{tot}}=37.868M_\odot$
- **7.3** $m_A=19.177M_\odot,\quad m_B=18.691M_\odot$
- **7.4** $L_A=3.088\times10^4L_\odot,\quad L_B=2.823\times10^4L_\odot$

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

### 7.1

Observing the plot, it is possible to estimate the wavelength of both Hα lines and later find the velocity of each star by Doppler.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '65.5%', '--fig-max': '419px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/ioaa-2021-theory-tq-7-q/p1-sol-fig1.png" alt="Official spectrum annotated with vertical lines at the two measured H-alpha wavelengths, 6561 and 6565 angstroms." width="1310" height="1084" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


$$
v_A=\frac{6561\ \mathrm{\mathring A}-6562.8\ \mathrm{\mathring A}}{6562.8\ \mathrm{\mathring A}}\times(2.998\cdot10^5\ \mathrm{km\cdot s^{-1}})=-82.23\ \mathrm{km\cdot s^{-1}}
$$

[2 point]

$$
v_B=\frac{6565\ \mathrm{\mathring A}-6562.8\ \mathrm{\mathring A}}{6562.8\ \mathrm{\mathring A}}\times(2.998\cdot10^5\ \mathrm{km\cdot s^{-1}})=+100.50\ \mathrm{km\cdot s^{-1}}
$$

[2 point]

So, Menkalinan A is approaching and Menkalinan B is moving away from us. [1 point]

### 7.2

$$
m_A>m_B\Rightarrow\alpha_B>\alpha_A
$$

From the definition of CM:

$$
\frac{\alpha_B}{\alpha_A}=1.026
$$

[0.5 points]

$$
\alpha=(\alpha_A+\alpha_B)=\alpha_B\left(1+\frac{\alpha_A}{\alpha_B}\right)=1.975\alpha_B
$$

[0.5 points]

Applying the 3rd Kepler’s law, the total mass of the system is:

$$
M_{\mathrm{tot}}=\frac{4\pi^2}{G}\times\frac{\alpha^3\cdot d^3}{T^2}
$$

[1 point]

$$
M_{\mathrm{tot}}=\frac{4\pi^2}{G}\times\frac{(0.00662''\times\frac{1\ \mathrm{rad}}{206265''})^3\cdot(81.1\ \mathrm{ly}\cdot\frac{9.46\cdot10^{15}\ \mathrm{m}}{1\ \mathrm{ly}})^3}{(3.96\ \mathrm{d}\cdot\frac{86400\ \mathrm{s}}{1\ \mathrm{d}})^2}
$$

$$
M_{\mathrm{tot}}=7.528\cdot10^{31}\ \mathrm{kg}=37.868M_\odot
$$

[2 point]

### 7.3

$$
\frac{m_A}{m_B}=1.026
$$

$$
M_{\mathrm{tot}}=m_A+m_B=1.026m_B+m_B=2.026m_B=37.868M_\odot
$$

$$
m_B=18.691M_\odot
$$

[1 point]

$$
m_A=1.026m_B=19.177M_\odot
$$

[1 point]

### 7.4

Using the mass/luminosity relation given:

$$
\frac{L_A}{L_\odot}=\left(\frac{m_A}{M_\odot}\right)^{3.5}=\left(\frac{19.177M_\odot}{M_\odot}\right)^{3.5}\quad3.088\times10^4
$$

$$
L_A=3.088\times10^4L_\odot
$$

[1 point]

$$
\frac{L_B}{L_\odot}=\left(\frac{m_B}{M_\odot}\right)^{3.5}=\left(\frac{18.691M_\odot}{M_\odot}\right)^{3.5}=2.823\times10^4
$$

$$
L_B=2.823\times10^4L_\odot
$$

[1 point]


</Spoiler>
