<summary>Requirements</summary>

**Languages of the solutions. (For Practical and also for Observational rounds).** No text in any language is permitted to use in solutions. All the solution must be written using only drawing pictures, plotting graph, writing formulae, numerical values and standard international astronomy symbols (like ♂, ♎, α UMa, M31, $5^{\mathrm m}$). Numerical values must be written using standard symbols but not symbols of National alphabet. Jury of the Practical round will not take into account any text in any language in solution.

For example, the names of constellations, written in English, Russian or any other language will be not taken into account (should be only UMa, Ori, etc.).

A variable star was explored at the RTT-150 Russian-Turkish telescope in 2003. The observations were performed during whole night at the first time, the 1st table. The brightness of the variable star was measured occasionally during the other nights, and moments of maximal values of brightness ($15.59^{\mathrm m}$) were received on nights 2–5, the 2nd table. The time is in Julian days, and mv is the yellow spectral colour magnitude.

<ProblemSection id="part-7-1">

### 7.1.

Plot the light curve (magnitude vs time function), determine the moment of maximum in the first night and write result (in Julian days) in form of «T0 = …».

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="part-7-2">

### 7.2.

Calculate the period of the variable star using the data of all 5 nights. Here period means the time between two closest maximums of the brightness. But actually astronomers usually do not know how many periods were passed between two observed maximums, because appropriate weather can not be same every day and a few periods can pass between two observed maximums.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="part-7-3">

### 7.3.

What type of variable star is this – eclipsing binary or pulsating variable star. (Write in English: Eclipsing or Pulsating respectively.)

</ProblemSection>
