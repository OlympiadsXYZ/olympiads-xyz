## Условие

You have a disc of glass with thickness $b=40\ \mathrm{mm}$, from which one has to make (by grinding) a spherical mirror with a diameter of $D=500\ \mathrm{mm}$. What could the focal distances $F$ be of a mirror made from this disc?

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

It is evident that the depth “x” in the centre of the disc must be less than th… thickness $b=40\ \mathrm{mm}$. Let $R$ be the curvature radius of the mirror. It means (from the Pythagorean theorem… that the minimum $R$ is defined by the equation:

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '46%', '--fig-max': '294px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/iao-2009-theory-alpha-en/p5-mirror.png" alt="Original mirror geometry from the official key." width="920" height="700" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Original mirror geometry from the official key.</figcaption>
</figure>


$$
R^2=D^2/4+(R-b)^2
$$

$$
2Rb=b^2+D^2/4
$$

$$
R=D^2/8b+b/2
$$

The relation between the focal length $F$ and the curvature radius o… the mirrow is $R=2F$, i.e. for zero thickness in the centre of the dis…

$$
F=R/2=D^2/16b+b/4
$$

$$
F=500^2/(16\cdot40)+40/4\approx401\ \mathrm{mm}
$$

So, theoretically the focal length may be in the interval from 401 mm to infinity. Nevertheless, th… thickness in the centre of the disc cannot be zero, let us take it at least 2 mm. In this case we should us… $b^*=38\ \mathrm{mm}$ instead of $b=40\ \mathrm{mm}$

$$
F=R/2=D^2/16b^*+b^*/4
$$

$$
F=500^2/(16\cdot38)+38/4\approx421\ \mathrm{mm}
$$

The focal length may be in the interval from about 420 mm to infinity.
