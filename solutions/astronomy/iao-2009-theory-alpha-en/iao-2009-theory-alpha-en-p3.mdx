## Условие

Estimate the theoretical limit of the maximum magnitude of stars that the human eye can see under very optimal conditions. Take into account that the eye's retina “remembers” the image about 1/7 of a second. A $0^{\mathrm m}$ star sends us about $10^{10}$ photons/m$^2$ every second.

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

For detecting a star the human retina should uninterruptedly remember the image of the star. That is the eye should receive from this star at least 7 photons per second. Human eyepiece is about $d=6\ \mathrm{mm}$ in the very dark. So the limit of illumination is:

$$
7\ \text{photons}/\pi d^2/4\approx250\,000\ \text{photons}/\mathrm{m^2}\quad\text{(very roughly, of course)}.
$$

It is about $10\,000\,000\,000/250\,000=40\,000$ less than the illumination from $0^{\mathrm m}$ star. In stellar magnitudes the difference is:

$$
\Delta m=2^{\mathrm m}.5\lg40\,000\approx11^{\mathrm m}.5.
$$

So the theoretical limit for human eye in the very dark is to see stars of $11^{\mathrm m}.5$. Of course, it is quite far from reality. Nobody reported about possibility to see stars fainter than $8^{\mathrm m}$ even in the absolute dark.


</Spoiler>
