This means that after the New Moon day, the orbit of Moon is above the ecliptic. In other words, the ascending node is near the $\boxed{\text{New Moon}}$. **[4.0]**

<ProblemSection id="solution-d2-3">

### D2.3

The largest angular size of the Moon in the ephemerides is $2008.3''$ and the smallest angular size is $1763.7''$. The distance is inversely proportional to the angular size. Hence ratio of distance at perigee to the distance at apogee is:

$$
\text{Ratio} = \frac{r_{\mathrm{perigee}}}{r_{\mathrm{apogee}}} = \frac{\cancel{a_0}}{1+e} \times \frac{1-e}{\cancel{a_0}}
$$

**[2.0]**

$$
\therefore \frac{1-e}{1+e} = \frac{1763.7}{2008.3} = 0.87821
$$

$$
\therefore e = \frac{1 - 0.87821}{1 + 0.87821} = 0.064846
$$

$$
\boxed{e \simeq 0.065}
$$

**[2.0]**

**Rounding off is done to account for the fact that our data are not continuous, hence exact angular sizes at perigee and apogee are not known.**

**A non-rounded answer will also receive full credit.**

</ProblemSection>

### D2.5

The following diagram needs to be drawn.

Angular size of umbra is $\theta_{\mathrm{umbra}} = 2\angle BOQ$

Angular size of penumbra is $\theta_{\mathrm{penumbra}} = 2\angle AOQ$

We have

$$
\begin{aligned} QA &= QP + PA \\ &= OE + PE\tan\theta_1 \\ &\approx R_{\oplus} + d_{\mathrm{Moon}}\theta_1 && \text{since } PA \ll PE \\ &\approx R_{\oplus} + d_{\mathrm{Moon}}\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{Sun}}}{2} && \text{since } \theta_1 \approx \theta_2 \approx \theta_{\mathrm{Sun}}/2 \end{aligned}
$$

**[2.0]**

and

$$
\begin{aligned} QB &= QP - PB \\ &= OE - PE\tan\theta_2 \\ &\approx R_{\oplus} - d_{\mathrm{Moon}}\theta_2 \\ &\approx R_{\oplus} - d_{\mathrm{Moon}}\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{Sun}}}{2} \end{aligned}
$$

**[2.0]**

$$
\therefore \theta_{\mathrm{penumbra}} = 2\angle AOQ = 2\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{QA}{OQ}\right) \approx 2\frac{QA}{OQ} \qquad \text{since } QA \ll OQ
$$

$$
= 2\frac{R_{\oplus} + d_{\mathrm{Moon}}\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{Sun}}}{2}}{d_{\mathrm{Moon}}} = \frac{2R_{\oplus}}{d_{\mathrm{Moon}}} + \theta_{\mathrm{Sun}}
$$

**[1.0]**

and

$$
\theta_{\mathrm{umbra}} = 2\angle BOQ = 2\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{QB}{OQ}\right) \approx 2\frac{QB}{OQ}
$$

$$
= 2\frac{R_{\oplus} - d_{\mathrm{Moon}}\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{Sun}}}{2}}{d_{\mathrm{Moon}}} = \frac{2R_{\oplus}}{d_{\mathrm{Moon}}} - \theta_{\mathrm{Sun}}
$$

**[1.0]**

Subtracting,

$$
\theta_{\mathrm{penumbra}} - \theta_{\mathrm{umbra}} = 2\theta_{\mathrm{Sun}} \Rightarrow \theta_{\mathrm{penumbra}} = \theta_{\mathrm{umbra}} + 2\theta_{\mathrm{Sun}}
$$

**[1.0]**

We have,

$$
\theta_{\mathrm{umbra}} = 2.76\theta_{\mathrm{Moon}} \qquad \text{and } \theta_{\mathrm{Sun}} = 1915.0''
$$

From the given data, $\theta_{\mathrm{Moon}} = 2008.3''$. **[1.0]**

Therefore,

$$
\theta_{\mathrm{penumbra}} = 2.76\theta_{\mathrm{Moon}} + 2\frac{1915.0}{2008.3}\theta_{\mathrm{Moon}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\theta_{\mathrm{penumbra}} = 4.67\theta_{\mathrm{Moon}}}
$$

**[1.0]**

**Acceptable range: $4.57\theta_{\mathrm{Moon}}$ to $4.77\theta_{\mathrm{Moon}}$.**

### D2.9

On September 10, phase of Moon is 0.097 and elongation of Moon is 36.2°. Angular size of the Moon on this day is $1792.0''$. Therefore, distance to Moon (from Earth) on September 10 is

$$
d_{\mathrm{Moon,10}} = \frac{2 \times 1713 \times 206265}{1792.0} = 3.94 \times 10^5\ \mathrm{km}
$$

**[2.0]**

$$
\text{Let } \angle EMS = \phi_M, \quad \angle ESM = \phi_S, \quad \angle SEM = \phi_E
$$

$$
\therefore \phi_E = 36.2^{\circ}
$$

**[1.0]**

$$
\text{phase} = \frac{1 + \cos\phi_M}{2}
$$

**[2.0]**

$$
\therefore \phi_M = \cos^{-1}(2 \times \text{ phase } - 1) = \cos^{-1}(2 \times 0.097 - 1) = \cos^{-1}(-0.806) = 143.71^{\circ}
$$

**[2.0]**

$$
\phi_S = 180^{\circ} - \phi_E - \phi_M = 180^{\circ} - 36.2^{\circ} - 143.71^{\circ} = 0.09^{\circ}
$$

**[1.0]**

Now using sine rule,

$$
\frac{d_{\mathrm{Sun}}}{d_{\mathrm{Moon,10}}} = \frac{\sin\phi_M}{\sin\phi_S}
$$

**[2.0]**

$$
\therefore d_{\mathrm{Sun}} = \frac{3.94 \times 10^8 \times \sin 143.71^{\circ}}{\sin 0.09^{\circ}}
$$

$$
\boxed{d_{\mathrm{Sun}} = 1.48 \times 10^{11}\ \mathrm{m}}
$$

**[1.0]**
