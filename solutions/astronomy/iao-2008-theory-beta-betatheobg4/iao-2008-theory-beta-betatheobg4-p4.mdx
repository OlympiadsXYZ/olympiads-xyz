## Условие

В пътеводителите на остров Шпицберген може да се намери следната фраза: „бялата мечка скача на 8 метра без предупреждение“. Средата на XXVI век. С цел да населят Слънчевата система с фауна, биолозите планират да закарат бели мечки от Шпицберген на ледените астероиди от пояса на Куйпър. Но физиците предупреждават, че някои скокливи мечки могат да станат независими обекти от пояса на Куйпър.

Оценете на астероиди с какви размери (диаметър) могат да се настаняват бели мечки безопасно от гледна точка на физиците. Отговорът трябва да се представи във вид на формула-неравенство.

## Отговори

- Official key: D > √(3Lg/(πGρ)) ≈ 35 km, using the first cosmic speed and ice density 900 kg/m³.

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

Официална скица на решението, на оригиналния английски език.

<ProblemSection id="solution-jump-speed">

### Initial jump speed

So, the bear makes a start and flies... On Spitsbergen (that is, on the Earth) the length of jump is equal to

$$
L=V_{hor}\times t,\qquad t=2V_{vert}/g,
$$

so

$$
L=2V_{hor}V_{vert}/g.
$$
$$
V_{hor}=V_0\cos\alpha,\qquad V_{vert}=V_0\sin\alpha.
$$

$V_0$ here is the initial speed of jump of the bear from the surface, $g$ – acceleration of gravity at the Earth. If we consider that this speed is not depend on the angle $\alpha$ (in general it is not absolutely true, but we shall use this assumption in our model), the maximum length of jump is reached when $\alpha=45^\circ$,

$$
L=V_0^2/g.
$$

So, if $L=8$ m, we may obtain, that speed with which the bear makes a start from the Earth is approximately equal to

$$
V_0=(L\cdot g)^{1/2}\approx9\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-asteroid-size">

### Safe asteroid diameter

If this speed will be larger than the second cosmic speed for the asteroid, the bear will depart and surely become an independent object of the Kuiper belt. The second cosmic speed may be used for the solution. Nevertheless, the first cosmic speed is better in the situation. Even, if this speed will be less the second cosmic speed, but larger than the first cosmic speed, it would be quite uncomfortable for the bear after it has jumped of the surface: it will follow an orbit of suborbital flight during many hours.

The relationship between the first cosmic speed and the size and density of the asteroid is

$$
V_1^2/R=GM/R^2=G(4/3)\pi R^3\rho/R^2,
$$
$$
V_1^2=G(4/3)\pi R^2\rho=\pi GD^2\rho/3.
$$

The first cosmic speed for an asteroid of the Kuiper belt is equal

$$
V_1=D(\pi G\rho/3)^{1/2}.
$$

So

$$
D=V_1/(\pi G\rho/3)^{1/2}.
$$

Taking into account that the planned for settling by bears objects of the Kuiper belt consist basically of ice, their characteristic density is about $900\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. So we have that the first cosmic speed $V_1$ is equal to $V_0=(L\cdot g)^{1/2}=9\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for the asteroids with diameter of order

$$
D=V_0/(\pi G\rho/3)^{1/2}=(L\cdot g)^{1/2}/(\pi G\rho/3)^{1/2}=(3Lg/\pi G\rho)^{1/2}.
$$
$$
D\approx35\,\mathrm{km}.
$$

Thus, from the physicists’ point of view it is possible to place polar bears comfortably on ice asteroids with diameters more than about 35 km.

Answer: $D>(3Lg/\pi G\rho)^{1/2}\approx35$ km.

</ProblemSection>
