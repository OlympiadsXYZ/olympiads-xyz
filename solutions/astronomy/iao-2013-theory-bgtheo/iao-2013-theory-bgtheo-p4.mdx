## Условие

Радиоастрон е международен научен проект, възглавяван от Астрокосмическия център на Руската академия на науките. На 18 юли 2011 г. в елиптична орбита около Земята е бил изведен спътникът „Спектър-Р“ с 10-метров (в диаметър) космически радиотелескоп. Заедно с наземни радиотелескопи „Спектър-Р“ работи като интерферометър. Радиоастрон работи на стандартните радиоастрономически дължини на вълните 1.19-1.63 cm (K-диапазон), 6.2 cm (C-диапазон), 18 cm (L-диапазон) и 92 cm (P-диапазон). Понастоящем „Спектър-Р“ се движи по силно изтеглена елиптична орбита с период $\tau = 8.3$ денонощия и височина на перигея $h = 600\ \mathrm{km}$ над земната повърхност.

**1.1.** Оценете най-добрата разделителна способност (в ъглови секунди) на проекта Радиоастрон. С помощта на схематична рисунка обяснете в каква ситуация се реализира тя. **[3,5 т.]**

**1.2.** Оценете разделителната способност на Радиоастрон при наблюдение на обекти, намиращи се по направление на голямата ос на орбитата на „Спектър-Р“. Решението също придружете със схема. **[3 т.]**

<ProblemSection id="solution-resolution">

### Resolution and base

The resolution of the instrument is determined by the working wavelength $\lambda$, and the base $D$. For telescopes the base is just the diameter of the front lens or mirrors, and for interferometers it is the distance between its elements, or, rather, the projection of this distance on the plane perpendicular to the direction to the object under research.

$$
\delta=\lambda/D.
$$

Thus, the maximum resolution is achieved in the case of the minimum wavelength and the maximum base.

$$
\delta_{\min}=\lambda_{\min}/D_{\max}.
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-best-resolution">

### 1.1. Maximum resolving power

In our case it is achieved at the wavelength $\lambda_{\min}=1.2\ \mathrm{cm}$ and the base of the Earth–“Spektr-R”, in which “Spektr-R” is at apogee, and the ground-based telescope is at the opposite side of the globe; and while the observations are held in the directions perpendicular to the base of Earth–“Spektr-R”, that is perpendicular to the major axis of the orbit:

$$
D_{\max}=A+R,
$$

where A is the distance at apogee, and R is radius of the Earth. The apogee distance can be found as

$$
A=2a-(R+h),
$$

where a is semi-major axis of the orbit, which, in turn, follows Kepler’s third law. Comparing the motion of the satellite with the motion of the Moon we get:

$$
a^3/a_{\mathrm{Moon}}^3=\tau^2/T_{\mathrm{Moon}}^2;\qquad a=a_{\mathrm{Moon}}(\tau/T_{\mathrm{Moon}})^{2/3},
$$

$$
a=384\,400\ \mathrm{km}\times(8.3\ \mathrm{days}/27.32\ \mathrm{days})^{2/3}=173\,700\ \mathrm{km}.
$$

$$
D_{\max}=2a-(R+h)+R=2a-h,
$$

$$
D_{\max}=2\times173\,700\ \mathrm{km}-600\ \mathrm{km}\approx347\,000\ \mathrm{km}.
$$

$$
\delta_{\min}=1.2\ \mathrm{cm}/347\,000\ \mathrm{km}\approx3.5\cdot10^{-11}\ \mathrm{rad},
$$

or in angular seconds:

$$
\delta_{\min}=3.5\cdot10^{-11}\ \mathrm{rad}\times206265^{\prime\prime}/\mathrm{rad}\approx7\cdot10^{-6}\,{}^{\prime\prime}=7\ \mu\mathrm{as}.
$$

### 1.2. Observation along the major axis

In observations of objects located in the direction of the major axis of the orbit of “Spektr-R” the maximum base D will be equal to the sum of the semi-minor axis of the orbit and the radius of Earth:

$$
D_{\max}=b+R.
$$

Semi-minor axis can be found from the geometric properties of the ellipse:

$$
a^2=b^2+(a-P)^2,\qquad b^2=[a^2-(a-P)^2]^{1/2},
$$

$$
b\approx48\,900\ \mathrm{km}.
$$

$$
D_{\max}=48\,900\ \mathrm{km}+6\,400\ \mathrm{km}=55\,300\ \mathrm{km}.
$$

$$
\delta_{\min}=\lambda_{\min}/D_{\max}=1.2\ \mathrm{cm}/55\,300\ \mathrm{km}\approx2.2\cdot10^{-10}\ \mathrm{rad},
$$

or in angular seconds:

$$
\delta_{\min}=2.2\cdot10^{-11}\ \mathrm{rad}\times206265^{\prime\prime}/\mathrm{rad}\approx4.5\cdot10^{-5}\,{}^{\prime\prime}=45\ \mu\mathrm{as}.
$$

*Source note: the key prints $b^2$ on the left of the square-root formula, and $10^{-11}$ in the final angular conversion after $10^{-10}$ in the preceding line. These printed discrepancies are retained; its stated result is 45 μas.*
