## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

Calculate the angular distance, $\varphi$, (in degrees or minutes of arc), of two bright stars, whose coordinates are given in the *List of Bright stars*. Preferably these stars should be chosen to be far apart (e.g. $\alpha$ UMa [$a_1=11^h03^m$, $\delta_1=+61^\circ45^\prime$] and $\eta$ UMa [$a_2=13^h48^m$, $\delta_2=+49^\circ19^\prime$]).

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '48.4%', '--fig-max': '310px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/ioaa-2013-data-analysis-x/fig2.png" alt="Figure 1A. Calculating the distance scale." width="969" height="331" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


1\. In order to calculate the angular distance of the two stars, the coordinates should be converted to decimal degrees (e.g. $\alpha$ UMa [$a_1=165.75^\circ$, $\delta_1=+61.75^\circ$] and $\eta$ UMa [$a_2=207.0^\circ$, $\delta_2=+49.32^\circ$]). **(2.5 Point)**

2\. Use the *cosine law* to calculate the angular distance between the two stars:

$$
\varphi=\arccos(\sin\delta_1\times\sin\delta_2+\cos\delta_1\times\cos\delta_2\times\cos(a_1-a_2))\quad\to\quad\varphi=25.8448^\circ.
$$

**(10 Points)**

3\. Measure the distance, $d_0$, of the two stars in mm in Figure 1. $d_0=138\,\mathrm{mm}$. **(2.5 Points)**

4\. The photograph in Figure 1 does not have the same dimensions as the original photograph. Measure the length, $\ell$, of the photograph in mm. $\ell=140\,\mathrm{mm}$. This length corresponds to 22 mm. Convert $d_0$ to the distance, $d$, of the original photograph, $d=138\times\frac{22}{140}\,\mathrm{mm}$. $d=21.6857\,\mathrm{mm}$. **(1.5 Points)**

4\. The *image scale* $\left(\frac{\varphi}{d}\right)$ of the original photograph is

$$
\frac{\varphi}{d}=\frac{25.8448^\circ}{21.6857}=1.1918\ \frac{\mathrm{deg}}{\mathrm{mm}}.
$$

**(2.5 Points)**

5\. The focal length is given from equation (see Figure 2): $\tan\frac{\varphi}{2}=\frac{d}{2f}$ or $f=\frac{d}{2\tan(\varphi/2)}$ from which the focal length is calculated: $f=\frac{21.6857}{2\times0.2294}$ or **$f=47.3\,\mathrm{mm}$**. **(1.5 Points)**

(If errors are included **(4 Points)**)


</Spoiler>
