## Условие

An observer is located at latitude $\phi=+42.0^\circ$ (Northern Hemisphere). Assume the Earth is a perfect sphere and ignore atmospheric refraction.

Star A has equatorial coordinates

$$
\alpha=6^{\mathrm h}40^{\mathrm m},\qquad\delta=+62.0^\circ.
$$

On a particular night, the local sidereal time (LST) is

$$
\mathrm{LST}=5^{\mathrm h}10^{\mathrm m}
$$

at 10:00 PM local clock time.

You may assume that one sidereal day equals $23^{\mathrm h}56^{\mathrm m}$ of clock time.

**(a)** **(2 points)** Compute the hour angle $H$ of star A at 10:00 PM. State whether the star is east or west of the meridian at that moment.

**(b)** **(5 points)** Compute the altitude $h$ of star A at 10:00 PM. Give your answer in degrees.

**(c)** **(3 points)** How much clock time will pass after 10:00 PM until star A reaches upper culmination? Express your answer in hours, minutes, and seconds.

Suppose star B has declination $\delta=+30.0^\circ$.

**(d)** **(5 points)** For star B, compute the total clock-time duration during which the star is above the horizon during one sidereal day. Express your final answer in hours and minutes of clock time.

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**Solution:**

Given $\phi=+42.0^\circ$, $\delta=+62.0^\circ$, and $\alpha=6^{\mathrm h}40^{\mathrm m}$. At 10:00 PM, $\mathrm{LST}=5^{\mathrm h}10^{\mathrm m}$. Recall $H=\mathrm{LST}-\alpha$ and $H>0$ means west of the meridian.

(a) **Hour angle and sky side. (2 points)**

Convert to hours:

$$
\alpha=6^{\mathrm h}40^{\mathrm m}=6.666\overline6\,\mathrm h,\qquad\mathrm{LST}=5^{\mathrm h}10^{\mathrm m}=5.166\overline6\,\mathrm h.
$$

Thus

$$
H=5^{\mathrm h}10^{\mathrm m}-6^{\mathrm h}40^{\mathrm m}=-1^{\mathrm h}30^{\mathrm m}.
$$

Since $H<0$, the star is *east* of the meridian.

Equivalently, in degrees ($15^\circ$ per hour):

$$
H=-1.5\,\mathrm h\times15^\circ/\mathrm h=-22.5^\circ.
$$

**Grading:**

- 2 pts: Correct $H$ and correct east/west interpretation.
- 1 pt: Correct subtraction but wrong east/west interpretation.
- 1 pt: Arithmetic error in conversion but correct method.
- 0.5 pt: Correct formula $H=\mathrm{LST}-\alpha$ but major arithmetic mistake.
- 0 pts: Incorrect formula or no meaningful progress.

Accept answers in range: $-22.5^\circ\pm0.3^\circ$.

(b) **Altitude at 10:00 PM. (5 points)**

Using spherical law of cosines,

$$
\sin h=\sin\phi\sin\delta+\cos\phi\cos\delta\cos H.\qquad (3)
$$

Substitute $\phi=42^\circ$, $\delta=62^\circ$, $H=-22.5^\circ$:

$$
\sin h=\sin42^\circ\sin62^\circ+\cos42^\circ\cos62^\circ\cos(22.5^\circ).
$$

Numerically,

$$
\sin h\approx0.9131
$$

$$
h\approx\arcsin(0.9131)\approx65.9^\circ.
$$

$$
\boxed{h\approx65.9^\circ}
$$

Accept answers between $65^\circ$ and $67^\circ$.

**Grading:**

- 5 pts: Correct substitution, trig evaluation, and final altitude.
- 4 pts: Correct setup with minor arithmetic error.
- 3 pts: Correct formula but computational errors.
- 2 pts: Some progress towards Eq. 3 but incorrect substitution and arithmetic errors.
- 1 pt: Partial substitution or wrong angle sign.
- 0 pts: No correct use of relation.

**Note:** If a student forgets $\cos(-H)=\cos H$, do not deduct heavily unless the conceptual reasoning is affected.

(c) **Clock time to upper culmination. (3 points)**

Upper culmination occurs when $H=0$, i.e. when $\mathrm{LST}=\alpha$.

Currently $H=-1^{\mathrm h}30^{\mathrm m}$, so the LST must increase by

$$
\Delta H=1^{\mathrm h}30^{\mathrm m}=1.5\text{ sidereal hours}.
$$

A sidereal day is 24 sidereal hours $=23^{\mathrm h}56^{\mathrm m}$ of clock time.

Thus

$$
1\text{ sidereal hour}=\frac{23^{\mathrm h}56^{\mathrm m}}{24}\approx59^{\mathrm m}50^{\mathrm s}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\Delta t=1.5\times59^{\mathrm m}50^{\mathrm s}\approx1^{\mathrm h}29^{\mathrm m}45^{\mathrm s}.
$$

$$
\boxed{1^{\mathrm h}29^{\mathrm m}45^{\mathrm s}}
$$

**Grading:**

- 3 pts: Correct sidereal interval and correct elapsed time.
- 2 pts: Correct sidereal interval and recognizes conversion needed but performs it incorrectly.
- 1 pt: Correct sidereal interval but assumes 1 sidereal hour = 1 clock hour, leading to $1^{\mathrm h}30^{\mathrm m}$.

(d) **Time above the horizon. (5 points)**

Use the horizon condition

$$
\cos H_0=-\tan\phi\tan\delta,
$$

which comes from Eq. 3 with altitude $h=0$.

Substitute $\phi=42^\circ$, $\delta=30^\circ$:

$$
\cos H_0=-\tan42^\circ\tan30^\circ.
$$

Using

$$
\tan42^\circ\approx0.900,\qquad\tan30^\circ\approx0.577,
$$

$$
\cos H_0\approx-0.519.
$$

Thus

$$
H_0\approx\cos^{-1}(-0.519)\approx121.3^\circ.
$$

Convert to hours:

$$
H_0=\frac{121.3^\circ}{15^\circ/\text{hour}}\approx8.09\text{ hours}.
$$

The star is above the horizon for

$$
2H_0\approx16.18\text{ sidereal hours}.
$$

Convert to clock time using

$$
1\text{ sidereal hour}\approx59^{\mathrm m}50^{\mathrm s}.
$$

$$
16.18\times59^{\mathrm m}50^{\mathrm s}\approx16^{\mathrm h}08^{\mathrm m}.
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{Star is above the horizon for about }16^{\mathrm h}08^{\mathrm m}.}
$$

**Grading:**

- 5 pts: Correct setup of the horizon equation, correct computation of $H_0$, and correct duration above the horizon.
- 4 pts: Correct method but minor arithmetic error in $H_0$ or time conversion.
- 3 pts: Correct horizon equation but significant computational errors.
- 2 pts: Partial substitution into the horizon condition.
- 1 pt: Incorrect derivation of horizon condition from Eq. 3.
- 0 pts: No correct use of the horizon relation.
