*ΩWAO 2025 ◇ Practical Round ◇ Problems · 20 т.*

## Условие

Cape Fligely ($81^{\circ}\,51'\,\mathrm{N};\ \ 59^{\circ}\,14'\,\mathrm{E}$) is located on the northern coast of Rudolf Island in Franz Josef Land, Russian Federation. It is the northernmost point of Russia, Europe, and the Eurasian continent as a whole.

**a)** Find the distance from the cape to the North Pole of the Earth. **[2 т.]**

**b)** At what solar declination does the polar day begin at the cape? Assume that atmospheric refraction at the horizon is $35'$. **[2 т.]**

You are provided with a table [`eclipses.xlsx`](https://astroedu.ru/assets/problems/owao/2025/OWAO-2025-P-files.zip) of lunar eclipses of the 21st century. The table lists the eclipse date, Saros number, gamma$^1$, the moments of the Moon’s disk contacts with the Earth’s shadow (see Fig. 1), and the time of greatest eclipse.

**c)** How many total lunar eclipses will occur in the 21st century? partial? penumbral? **[4 т.]**

Point $\Xi\,(0;\ 59^{\circ}\,14'\,\mathrm{E})$ lies on the equator at the same longitude, somewhere in the Indian Ocean.

**d)** Determine how many total lunar eclipses will be visible from both Cape Fligely and point $\Xi$. List the corresponding event numbers.

Count an eclipse as visible if any part of totality occurs above the horizon. Ignore events visible only in partial phases. **[12 т.]**

$^1$Gamma is the distance of the center of the Moon’s disk from the center of the Earth’s umbral shadow, expressed in units of Earth’s equatorial radius. It is defined at the moment of greatest eclipse, when its absolute value reaches a minimum. For a lunar eclipse, it shows whether the Moon passes north or south of the center of the Earth’s shadow; a positive value means the Moon passes to the north of the center.

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи решението от архива">

**Solution**

a) The distance from Cape Fligely to the North Pole is simply the meridional arc:

$$
d = \frac{90^{\circ} - \varphi}{360^{\circ}} \cdot 2\pi R_\oplus = \frac{90^{\circ} - 81^{\circ}\,51'}{360^{\circ}} \times 2 \times 3.14 \times 6371\ \mathrm{km} \approx 906\ \mathrm{km}.
$$

b) The polar day begins when, at lower culmination, the upper limb of the Sun just touches the apparent horizon. In terms of the Sun’s center, this condition is

$$
h = -\left(\frac{\rho_\odot}{2} + r\right) = -\left(\frac{32'}{2} + 35'\right) = -51' = -0.85^{\circ},
$$

where $\rho_\odot$ is the Sun’s angular diameter and $r$ is the correction for atmospheric refraction. The corresponding solar declination is obtained from the formula for the altitude at lower culmination:

$$
\delta_\odot = h - \varphi + 90^{\circ} = -51' - 81^{\circ}\,51' + 90^{\circ} = +7.3^{\circ}.
$$

c) Although the eclipse type can in principle be inferred from the value of $\gamma$, a more straightforward approach is to classify it directly from the reported contact times:

- Penumbral: only penumbral contacts, which are not listed in the table;
- Partial: two umbral contacts are present (U1, U4);
- Total: all four umbral contacts are present.

The counts are shown in Table 1.

d) Given the complexity of a fully manual approach, two possible strategies can be pursued:

- to use astronomical software, such as the AstroPy package, to obtain precise results;
- to simplify the problem by considering only the dominant physical effects, thus obtaining an approximate answer with an associated uncertainty.

The code in Appendix A (page 16) demonstrates the first strategy, implemented in Python with AstroPy. Tables 1 and 2 demonstrate the results.

**Discussion**

How can the visibility of a lunar eclipse be assessed without relying on specialized software? The problem reduces to determining whether the Moon is above the local horizon during the total phase. This, in turn, depends on a number of astronomical and observational factors:

- the declination of the Moon, which varies with date;
- the hour angle of the Moon, determined by local time;
- the equation of time, which links apparent solar time to mean time;
- the angular diameter of the Moon;
- atmospheric refraction near the horizon;
- the diurnal parallax of the Moon;
- the eclipse *gamma* parameter, characterizing the Moon’s trajectory through the Earth’s shadow.

**Analysis for Cape Fligely**

At Cape Fligely’s extreme northern latitude, the Moon’s diurnal path is nearly parallel to the horizon. The dominant factor determining visibility is the Moon’s declination: a large arc of the ecliptic remains permanently above the horizon, while another part remains permanently below it.

Since a lunar eclipse always occurs at full Moon, when the Moon is in opposition to the Sun, the following conclusions hold:

- Winter eclipses: if the Moon’s declination is greater than about $+8^{\circ}$, the Moon does not set and the eclipse is entirely visible.
- Summer eclipses: if the Moon’s declination is less than

    $$h_{\mathrm{min}} - \varphi + 90^{\circ} = -0.85^{\circ} - 81^{\circ}51' + 90^{\circ} \approx -9^{\circ},$$

    then the Moon does not rise and the eclipse is entirely invisible.
- Near the equinoxes: visibility should be checked on a case-by-case basis.

**Analysis for point $\Xi$**

At the equator the situation is essentially reversed. The Moon rises and sets every day, spending on average about 12 hours above the horizon (slightly more when minor effects are considered). Consequently, the key factor for visibility is the local time of the eclipse contacts. Since a lunar eclipse occurs at full Moon, the event is visible whenever the Moon is above the horizon, i.e. roughly from six hours before until six hours after local midnight.

The local time at point $\Xi$ is related to UTC by

$$
T_\Xi = \mathrm{UTC} + \frac{\lambda}{360^{\circ}} \times 24^{\mathrm{h}} = \mathrm{UTC} + \frac{59^{\circ}\,14'}{360^{\circ}} \times 24^{\mathrm{h}} = \mathrm{UTC} + 3^{\mathrm{h}}\,57^{\mathrm{m}}.
$$

Neglecting the equation of time, this means that total eclipses are observable from point $\Xi$ if the totality occurs between about 14:00 and 02:00 UTC. Events close to these boundaries require special attention.

### A Analysis of Eclipse Visibility with AstroPy

```python
"""
Analysis of Lunar Eclipse Visibility
====================================

This script reads a catalog of 21st century lunar eclipses from
‘eclipses.xlsx‘, extracts the times of totality, and determines
which eclipses are visible from:
    - Cape Fligely
    - Point Xi on the equator

Visibility is defined as the Moon’s upper limb being above the
geometric horizon, corrected for refraction and the Moon’s
radius.
"""

import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import astropy.units as u
from astropy.time import Time, TimeDelta
from astropy.coordinates import EarthLocation, AltAz, get_body


# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# Load and preprocess the eclipse catalog
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
def load_eclipses(path: str = "eclipses.xlsx") -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Load the lunar eclipse catalog from Excel and standardize columns.

    Parameters
    ----------
    path : str
        Path to the Excel file.

    Returns
    -------
    df : pandas.DataFrame
        DataFrame with standardized columns and auxiliary fields.
    """
    df = pd.read_excel(path, skiprows=1)

    df = df.rename(columns={
        "Unnamed: 0": "num",
        "U1": "U1", "U2": "U2", "U3": "U3", "U4": "U4",
        "Unnamed: 10": "maxt"
    })

    months = {m: i for i, m in enumerate(
        ["January","February","March","April","May","June",
         "July","August","September","October","November","December"], 1)}
    df["Month_num"] = df["Month"].map(months)

    df["date"] = pd.to_datetime(
        dict(year=df["Year"], month=df["Month_num"], day=df["Day"]),
        errors="coerce"
    )

    def frac_to_time(frac):
        if pd.isna(frac):
            return np.nan
        seconds = float(frac) * 24 * 3600
        h = int(seconds // 3600)
        m = int((seconds % 3600) // 60)
        s = int(seconds % 60)
        return f"{h:02d}:{m:02d}:{s:02d}"

    df["maxt_str"] = df["maxt"].apply(frac_to_time)

    return df


# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# Convert tabulated times into astropy.Time arrays
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
def build_times(df: pd.DataFrame):
    """
    Construct astropy.Time objects for total eclipses only.

    Returns
    -------
    df_tot : pandas.DataFrame
        Subset containing only total eclipses (U2 and U3 defined).
    t2, t3, tmax : astropy.Time
        Times of second contact, third contact, and maximum eclipse.
    """
    df_tot = df[df["U2"].notna() & df["U3"].notna()].copy()

    def to_iso(dates, times):
        iso_list = []
        for d, t in zip(dates, times):
            if pd.isna(t) or pd.isna(d):
                iso_list.append("NaT")
            else:
                iso_list.append(f"{d.strftime(’%Y-%m-%d’)}T{t}")
        return np.array(iso_list, dtype=str)

    iso_u2  = to_iso(df_tot["date"], df_tot["U2"])
    iso_u3  = to_iso(df_tot["date"], df_tot["U3"])
    iso_max = to_iso(df_tot["date"], df_tot["maxt_str"])

    t2   = Time(iso_u2,  format="isot", scale="utc", out_subfmt="date_hms")
    t3   = Time(iso_u3,  format="isot", scale="utc", out_subfmt="date_hms")
    tmax = Time(iso_max, format="isot", scale="utc", out_subfmt="date_hms")

    # adjust for midnight crossings
    one_day = TimeDelta(1*u.day)
    t3[tmax > t3] = t3[tmax > t3] + one_day
    t2[tmax < t2] = t2[tmax < t2] - one_day

    return df_tot, t2, t3, tmax


# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# Visibility criterion
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
def visible_mask_for_site(t2, t3, lat_deg, lon_deg=59 + 14/60, height_m=0,
                          n_samples=11, h_thresh_deg=-(35+16)/60.0):
    """
    Check eclipse visibility over the full totality interval [U2, U3]
    Samples each eclipse at ‘n_samples‘ evenly spaced times
    and returns True if the Moons center altitude exceeds ‘h_thresh_deg‘

    Parameters
    ----------
    t2, t3 : astropy.Time
        Start and end of totality.
    lat_deg, lon_deg : float
        Geographic coordinates of the site (degrees).
    height_m : float
        Elevation above sea level.
    n_samples : int, optional
        Number of samples per eclipse (inclusive of endpoints), default 11.
    h_thresh_deg : float, optional
        Altitude threshold (degrees) for the Moons center.

    Returns
    -------
    mask : ndarray of bool
        True if any sample during totality is above the threshold
        (i.e., visible).
    """
    loc = EarthLocation(
        lat=lat_deg*u.deg,
        lon=lon_deg*u.deg,
        height=height_m*u.m
    )

    frac = np.linspace(0.0, 1.0, n_samples)
    dt = t3 - t2
    t_grid = t2[:, None] + frac[None, :] * dt[:, None]

    alt = get_body("moon", t_grid.reshape(-1), location=loc)\
        .transform_to(AltAz(obstime=t_grid.reshape(-1), location=loc))\
        .alt.deg.reshape(len(t2), n_samples)

    return (alt > h_thresh_deg).any(axis=1)


# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# Main pipeline
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
def analyze_visibility(path="eclipses.xlsx"):
    """
    Analyze visibility of total lunar eclipses from Cape Fligely
    and point Xi.
    """
    df = load_eclipses(path)
    df_tot, t2, t3, tmax = build_times(df)

    events = df_tot["num"].to_numpy()

    # two sites
    vis_XI = visible_mask_for_site(t2, t3, lat_deg=0.0)
    vis_FL = visible_mask_for_site(t2, t3, lat_deg=81 + 51/60.0)

    xi_list  = events[vis_XI].tolist()
    fl_list  = events[vis_FL].tolist()
    both     = sorted(set(xi_list) & set(fl_list))

    print("Total eclipses:", len(events))
    print("Visible from point Xi:", len(xi_list))
    print("Visible from Cape Fligely:", len(fl_list))
    print("Visible from both:", len(both))

    return xi_list, fl_list, both


# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# Entry point
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
if __name__ == "__main__":
    xi_list, fl_list, both = analyze_visibility("eclipses.xlsx")
```
