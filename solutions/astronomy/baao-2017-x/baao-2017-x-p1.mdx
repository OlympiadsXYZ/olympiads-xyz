**Elements of an elliptic orbit**:

**Keplers Third Law**: For an elliptical orbit, the square of the period, $T$, of orbit of an object about the focus is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis, $a$ (the average of the minimum and maximum distances from the Sun). The constant of proportionality is $4\pi^2/GM$, where $M$ is the mass of the central object and $G$ is the universal gravitational constant.

**Magnitudes**: The apparent magnitudes of two objects, $m_1$ and $m_0$, are related to their apparent brightnesses, $b_1$ and $b_0$, via the formula:

## Условие

On Earth the Global Positioning System (GPS) requires a minimum of 24 satellites in orbit at any one time (there are typically more than that to allow for redundancies, with the current constellation having more than 30) so that at least 4 are visible above the horizon from anywhere on Earth (necessary for an x, y, z and time co-ordinate). This is achieved by having 6 different orbital planes, separated by $60^{\circ}$, and each orbital plane has 4 satellites.

The orbits are essentially circular with an eccentricity $< 0.02$, an inclination of $55^{\circ}$, and an orbital period of exactly half a sidereal day (called a semi-synchronous orbit). The receiving angle of each satellite’s antenna needs to be about $27.8^{\circ}$, and hence about 38% of the Earth’s surface is within each satellite’s footprint (see Figure 1), allowing the excellent coverage required.

**a.** Given that the Earth’s sidereal day is 23h 56 mins, calculate the orbital radius of a GPS satellite. Express your answer in units of $\mathrm{R}_{\oplus}$. **[3 т.]**

**b.** How long would it take a radio signal to travel directly between a satellite and its closest neighbour in its orbital plane (assuming they’re evenly spaced)? How far would a car on a motorway (with a speed of $30\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$) travel in that time? [This can be taken to be a very crude estimate of the positional accuracy of the system for that car.] **[3 т.]**

In the future we hope to colonise Mars, and so for navigation purposes it is likely that a type of GPS system will eventually be established on Mars too. Mars has a mass of $6.42\times10^{23}\ \mathrm{kg}$, a mean radius of $3390\ \mathrm{km}$, a sidereal day of 24h 37 mins, and two (low mass) moons with essentially circular orbits and semi-major axes of $9377\ \mathrm{km}$ (Phobos) and $23\,460\ \mathrm{km}$ (Deimos).

**c.** Using suitable calculations, explore the viability of a 24-satellite GPS constellation similar to the one used on Earth, in a semi-synchronous Martian orbit, by considering:

**(i)** Would the moons prevent such an orbit? **[4 т.]**

**(ii)** How would the GPS positional accuracy compare to Earth? **[3 т.]**

**(iii)** What would the receiving angle of each satellite’s antenna need to be, and what would be the associated satellite footprint? By comparing these with the ones utilised by Earth’s GPS, make a final comment on the viability of future Martian GPS. **[7 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**Q1 - Martian GPS** **[Total = 20]**

*a. Given that the Earth’s sidereal day is 23h 56 mins, calculate the orbital radius of a GPS satellite. Express your answer in units of* $\mathrm{R}_{\oplus}$.

$T = \frac{1}{2}\times(23\mathrm{h}\ 56\mathrm{m}) = 718\ \mathrm{mins} = 43080\ \mathrm{s}$ [1]

$T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{GM}a^3 \qquad \therefore a = \sqrt[3]{\frac{G\mathrm{M}_{\oplus}T^2}{4\pi^2}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{6.67\times10^{-11}\times5.97\times10^{24}\times43080^2}{4\pi^2}}$ [1]

$= 2.66\times10^{7}\ \mathrm{m}$

$= 4.17\ \mathrm{R}_{\oplus}$ [1] **[3]**

*b. How long would it take a radio signal to travel directly between a satellite and its closest neighbour in its orbital plane (assuming they’re evenly spaced)? How far would a car on a motorway (with a speed of 30 m s⁻¹) travel in that time? [This can be taken to be a very crude estimate of the positional accuracy of the system for that car.].*

Closest neighbour in orbital plane should be 90° away (since evenly spaced) so can use Pythagoras

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '25.2%', '--fig-max': '161px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/baao-2017-x/p1-sol-fig1.png" alt="Quarter circle of radius a with two perpendicular radii of length a (right angle marked) and the chord x joining their ends." width="504" height="502" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

$\therefore x = \sqrt{2a^2} = 3.76\times10^{7}\ \mathrm{m}$ [1]

Time for the signal to travel that distance:

$t = \frac{x}{c} = \frac{3.76\times10^{7}}{3.00\times10^{8}} = 0.125\ \mathrm{s}$ [1] **[2]**

Distance travelled by a car on a motorway in that time:

$d = vt = 30\times0.125 = 3.76\ \mathrm{m}$ [1] **[1]**

[In practice the positional accuracy of a GPS system is much harder to calculate; at low speeds it is typically a function of fluctuations and reflections of the signal within the atmosphere, as well as the presence of objects that might block the signal]

*c. Using suitable calculations, explore the viability of a 24-satellite GPS constellation similar to the one used on Earth, in a semi-synchronous Martian orbit, by considering:*

*i. Would the moons prevent such an orbit?*

$T = \frac{1}{2}\times(24\mathrm{h}\ 37\mathrm{m}) = 738.5\ \mathrm{mins} = 44310\ \mathrm{s}$ [1]

$T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{GM}a^3 \qquad \therefore a = \sqrt[3]{\frac{G\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{M}}T^2}{4\pi^2}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{6.67\times10^{-11}\times6.42\times10^{23}\times44310^2}{4\pi^2}}$ [1]

$= 1.29\times10^{7}\ \mathrm{m}$ [1] **[3]**

This is about ½ the distance to Diemos and about 3500 km (about $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{M}}$) away from Phobos so the moons should not provide a problem for any GPS satellite constellation [1] **[1]**

*ii. How would the GPS positional accuracy compare to Earth?*

(Using similar reasoning to part b.)

$x = 1.82\times10^{7}\ \mathrm{m}$ [1]

$t = 0.061\ \mathrm{s} \qquad (\text{or } d = 1.82\ \mathrm{m})$ [1] **[2]**

The positional accuracy is about twice as good as on Earth [1] **[1]**

*iii. What would the receiving angle of each satellite’s antenna need to be, and what would be the associated satellite footprint? By comparing these with the ones utilised by Earth’s GPS, make a final comment on the viability of future Martian GPS.*

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '37%', '--fig-max': '237px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/baao-2017-x/p1-sol-fig2.png" alt="Two diagrams of a planet (circle) and a satellite at distance a from its centre, with tangent lines from the satellite to the circle. Top: radius R perpendicular to the tangent, half-angle θ at the satellite, distance a. Bottom: same geometry with angle θ at the centre, the distances R-h and h marked along the axis." width="740" height="850" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

Receiving angle:

$2\theta = 2\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{M}}}{a}\right) = 2\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{3.39\times10^{6}}{1.29\times10^{7}}\right)$ [1]

$= 30.6^{\circ}$ [1] **[2]**

The area of a 'zone' of a sphere is $2\pi Rh$ where $h$ is the radial height of the zone. From the geometry of the situation:

$\sin\theta = \frac{R-h}{R} \qquad \therefore h = R(1-\sin\theta)$ [1]

$= 0.74\ \mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{M}}$ [1]

Fraction of surface area:

$\frac{\text{Area of zone}}{\text{Surface area of Mars}} = \frac{2\pi\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{M}}(0.74\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{M}})}{4\pi\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{M}}^{2}} = 36.8\%$ [1] **[3]**

Receiving angle similar to Earth's so can use current GPS satellite technology [0.5]

Satellite footprint similar to Earth's so should get sufficient coverage [0.5]

Martian GPS system is viable [1] **[2]**
