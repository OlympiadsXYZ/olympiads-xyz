**Elements of an elliptic orbit**:

$a = \mathbf{OA}\ (= \mathbf{OP})$ semi-major axis

$a = \mathbf{OB}\ (= \mathbf{OC})$ semi-minor axis

A apoapsis (point furthest from F)

**Keplers Third Law**: For an elliptical orbit, the square of the period, $T$, of orbit of an object about the focus is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis, $a$ (the average of the minimum and maximum distances from the Sun). The constant of proportionality is $4\pi^2/GM$, where $M$ is the mass of the central object and $G$ is the universal gravitational constant.

**Magnitudes**: The apparent magnitudes of two objects, $m_1$ and $m_0$, are related to their apparent brightnesses, $b_1$ and $b_0$, via the formula:

## Условие

In order to move a spacecraft between orbits we must apply a thrust using rockets, which changes the velocity of the spacecraft by $\Delta v$. In this question we will ignore changes in the mass of the spacecraft due to the burning of fuel.

For an object of mass $m$ in a circular orbit of radius $r$ around an object with mass $M$ (where $m \ll M$) the orbital velocity, $v_{\mathrm{orb}}$, is given by the formula $v_{\mathrm{orb}} = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}$.

**a.** Show that $v_{\mathrm{orb}}$ in low Earth orbit (LEO; about $200\ \mathrm{km}$ above the surface), is about $8\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$. This is an estimate of the $\Delta v$ the rockets need to provide for the spacecraft to reach LEO. **[2 т.]**

An economical route to take when travelling between planets is called a Hohmann transfer orbit. This is an ellipse for which the perihelion coincides with the inner planetary orbit (with radius $r_{\mathrm{A}}$) and the aphelion coincides with the outer planetary orbit (with radius $r_{\mathrm{B}}$). It is achieved by increasing the velocity of the spacecraft at point A by $\Delta v_{\mathrm{A}}$ before then increasing it again at point B by $\Delta v_{\mathrm{B}}$.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '67.7%', '--fig-max': '434px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/baao-2017-x/p2-fig1.png" alt="Two concentric circles: the inner labelled “Initial orbit” with radius rA, the outer labelled “Final orbit” with radius rB. A dashed half-ellipse labelled “Transfer orbit” runs from point A at the bottom of the inner circle to point B at the top of the outer circle. Arrows ΔvA at A (pointing right) and ΔvB at B (pointing left); a dotted vertical line through A and B." width="1355" height="987" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Figure 2: A diagram of a Hohmann transfer orbit between an inner and outer planet</figcaption>
</figure>

For an ellipse with semi-major axis $a$ it can be shown that the velocity $v$, at a distance $r$ from mass $M$, can be written as:

$$
v^2 = GM\left(\frac{2}{r}-\frac{1}{a}\right)
$$

**b.** Derive expressions for $\Delta v_{\mathrm{A}}$ and $\Delta v_{\mathrm{B}}$ by comparing their circular orbital speeds with their transfer orbit speeds. Simplify your final expressions to include $G$, $\mathrm{M}_{\odot}$, $r_{\mathrm{A}}$ and $r_{\mathrm{B}}$ only. **[3 т.]**

**c.** Approximating Mars’ orbit as circular with a radius of $1.52\ \mathrm{AU}$, calculate the $\Delta v$ to go from Earth LEO to Mars i.e. $\Delta v = |\Delta v_{\mathrm{A}}| + |\Delta v_{\mathrm{B}}|$. Compare your answer to the $\Delta v$ to reach Earth LEO. **[4 т.]**

**d.** Derive an expression for the total time spent on the transfer orbit, $t_{\mathrm{H}}$, and calculate it for an Earth to Mars transfer. Give your answer in months. (Use 1 month = 30 days.) **[3 т.]**

**e.** Hence calculate the direct distance between Earth and Mars at the moment the spacecraft reaches Mars. How long would it take a radio message from the spacecraft to reach Earth? **[3 т.]**

**f.** How long would any astronauts on board the spacecraft need to wait until they could use a Hohmann transfer orbit to return to Earth? Hence calculate the total duration of the mission. **[5 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **c.** 5.58 km s⁻¹
- **d.** 8.56 months
- **e.** x = 2.41 × 10¹¹ m (= 1.62 AU); t = 803 s (= 13.4 minutes)
- **f.** wait 14.8 months (= 443 days); total 31.9 months (= 956 days = 2.66 years)

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**Q2 - Hohmann Transfer** **[Total = 20]**

*a. Show that* $v_{orb}$ *in low Earth orbit (LEO; about 200 km above the surface) is about 8 km s⁻¹. This is an estimate of the Δv the rockets need to provide for the spacecraft to reach LEO.*

$v_{\mathrm{orb}} = \sqrt{\frac{G\mathrm{M}_{\oplus}}{\mathrm{R}_{\oplus}+r}} = \sqrt{\frac{6.67\times10^{-11}\times5.97\times10^{24}}{6.37\times10^{6}+200\times10^{3}}}$ [1]

$= 7790\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}} \quad (= 7.79\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}} \approx 8\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}})$ [1] **[2]**

[In practice a *Δv* of 8 km s⁻¹ assumes no external forces, but atmospheric drag can increase the necessary *Δv* by 1.3 – 1.8 km s⁻¹. When travelling between objects in space, however, such drag forces are absent and so the *Δv* calculated is much more accurate]

*b. Derive expressions for* $\Delta v_A$ *and* $\Delta v_B$ *by comparing their circular orbital speeds with their transfer orbit speeds. Simplify your final expressions to include G,* $\mathrm{M}_{\odot}$, $r_A$ *and* $r_B$ *only.*

$\Delta v_{\mathrm{A}} = \sqrt{G\mathrm{M}_{\odot}\left(\frac{2}{r_{\mathrm{A}}}-\frac{1}{a}\right)} - \sqrt{G\mathrm{M}_{\odot}\left(\frac{1}{r_{\mathrm{A}}}\right)} = \sqrt{\frac{G\mathrm{M}_{\odot}}{r_{\mathrm{A}}}}\left(\sqrt{2-\frac{r_{\mathrm{A}}}{a}}-1\right)$ [1]

But $2a = r_{\mathrm{A}} + r_{\mathrm{B}}$

$\therefore \Delta v_{\mathrm{A}} = \sqrt{\frac{G\mathrm{M}_{\odot}}{r_{\mathrm{A}}}}\left(\sqrt{2-\frac{2r_{\mathrm{A}}}{r_{\mathrm{A}}+r_{\mathrm{B}}}}-1\right) = \sqrt{\frac{G\mathrm{M}_{\odot}}{r_{\mathrm{A}}}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2r_{\mathrm{B}}}{r_{\mathrm{A}}+r_{\mathrm{B}}}}-1\right)$ [1] **[2]**

Similarly:

$\Delta v_{\mathrm{B}} = \sqrt{\frac{G\mathrm{M}_{\odot}}{r_{\mathrm{B}}}}\left(1-\sqrt{2-\frac{2r_{\mathrm{B}}}{r_{\mathrm{A}}+r_{\mathrm{B}}}}\right) = \sqrt{\frac{G\mathrm{M}_{\odot}}{r_{\mathrm{B}}}}\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{2r_{\mathrm{A}}}{r_{\mathrm{A}}+r_{\mathrm{B}}}}\right)$ [1] **[1]**

[These equations have been written so that the change in speed is positive, however give full credit for reversed signs (so long as they are consistent)]

*c. Approximating Mars' orbit as circular with a radius of 1.52 AU, calculate the Δv to go from Earth LEO to Mars i.e.* $\Delta v = |\Delta v_A| + |\Delta v_B|$. *Compare your answer to the Δv to reach Earth LEO.*

$\Delta v_{\mathrm{A}} = \sqrt{\frac{6.67\times10^{-11}\times1.99\times10^{30}}{1.49\times10^{11}}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2\times1.52}{1+1.52}}-1\right)$

$= 2935\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\ (= 2.94\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}})$ [1]

$\Delta v_{\mathrm{B}} = \sqrt{\frac{6.67\times10^{-11}\times1.99\times10^{30}}{1.52\times1.49\times10^{11}}}\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{2\times1}{1+1.52}}\right)$

$= 2642\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\ (= 2.64\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}})$ [1]

$\Delta v = 2.94 + 2.64$

$= 5.58\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ [1] **[3]**

This is less than the *Δv* to get into LEO [1] **[1]**

(So most of the effort needed in going to Mars simply comes from leaving Earth)

[The *Δv* calculated here would be for the spacecraft to enter a circular orbit around the Sun at the same distance as Mars, but this would not constitute landing – the extra *Δv* to get to the Martian surface increases the total for the whole transfer to roughly 8 km s⁻¹ (same as to get into LEO). Other, more complicated routes can be taken, some of which offer substantial efficiencies so the *Δv* for the trip can be much lower (although the time taken to complete the manoeuvre will be longer, and the *Δv* to reach LEO will still be the biggest single step)]

*d. Derive an expression for the total time spent on the transfer orbit,* $t_H$*, and calculate it for an Earth to Mars transfer. Give your answer in months. (Use 1 month = 30 days).*

From Kepler's third law: $T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{GM}a^3$

Since the spacecraft only covers half of the ellipse the time on the journey is half the period, and given that $2a = r_{\mathrm{A}} + r_{\mathrm{B}}$ then:

$t_{\mathrm{H}} = \pi\sqrt{\frac{(r_{\mathrm{A}}+r_{\mathrm{B}})^3}{8G\mathrm{M}_{\odot}}}$ [1] **[1]**

$= \pi\sqrt{\frac{\left((1+1.52)\times1.49\times10^{11}\right)^3}{8\times6.67\times10^{-11}\times1.99\times10^{30}}} = 2.22\times10^{7}\ \mathrm{s}$ [1]

$= 8.56\ \mathrm{months}$ [1] **[2]**

*e. Hence calculate the direct distance between Earth and Mars at the moment the spacecraft reaches Mars. How long would it take a radio message from the spacecraft to reach Earth?*

Initially Earth is at A. When the spacecraft reaches B (after 8.56 months), the Earth has moved round the Sun in its orbit and is now at A'.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '33%', '--fig-max': '211px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/baao-2017-x/p2-sol-fig1.png" alt="Hohmann transfer diagram (Initial orbit, Final orbit, Transfer ellipse, ΔVA at A, ΔVB at B, radii rA and rB) with the Earth’s later position A' marked in red on the inner orbit, the angle θ between A' and the vertical line to B, and a red dotted line x from A' to B." width="660" height="492" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

Angle between A' and B:

$\theta = \left(\frac{8.56-6}{12}\right)\times360^{\circ} = 76.7^{\circ}$ [1]

Using the cosine rule:

$x^2 = r_A^2 + r_B^2 - 2r_Ar_B\cos\theta$

$\therefore x = 2.41\times10^{11}\ \mathrm{m} \quad (= 1.62\ \mathrm{AU})$ [1] **[2]**

Since radio waves travel at the speed of light the time taken by the message is:

$t = \frac{2.41\times10^{11}}{3.00\times10^{8}} = 803\ \mathrm{s} \quad (= 13.4\ \mathrm{minutes})$ [1] **[1]**

[In practice, due to the eccentricity of Mars' orbit, the signal transmission time varies depending on the year the spacecraft was launched. When the rover Curiosity arrived at Mars the engineers described the landing as 7 minutes of terror, since the signal from the spacecraft would take 14 minutes to reach Earth but the time to transverse the Martian atmosphere was only 7 minutes (hence the process had to be completely automated)]

*f. How long would any astronauts on board the spacecraft need to wait until they could use a Hohmann transfer orbit to return to Earth? Hence calculate the total duration of the mission.*

During the transfer the Earth moves by $180^{\circ} + \theta = 257^{\circ}$, so the spacecraft should launch from Mars when Earth is at position A" (exhibiting symmetry with when it arrived).

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '33%', '--fig-max': '211px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/baao-2017-x/p2-sol-fig2.png" alt="The same Hohmann transfer diagram with the Earth’s launch position A' marked in red on the inner orbit at angle θ to the right of the vertical line through B." width="660" height="478" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

Since the planets move anti-clockwise in this diagram the angle covered by Earth from A' to A" is $360^{\circ} - 2\theta = 206.5^{\circ}$ [1]

From Kepler's third law, the period of Mars $= \sqrt{1.52^3} = 1.87\ \mathrm{years}$ [1]

Therefore, the relative angular velocity of Earth if Mars' motion is subtracted out is:

$\omega_{\mathrm{rel}} = \frac{360^{\circ}}{12} - \frac{360^{\circ}}{1.87\times12} = 14.0^{\circ}\ \mathrm{month^{-1}}$ [1]

(allow any equivalent units e.g. 168° year⁻¹, 9.38 × 10⁻⁸ rad s⁻¹ etc.)

Consequently, the time the astronauts need to wait for Earth to get from position A' to A" is:

$\frac{206}{14.0} = 14.8\ \mathrm{months} \quad (= 443\ \mathrm{days})$ [1] **[4]**

Thus the total duration of a return mission to Mars is:

$t = 14.8 + (2\times8.56) = 31.9\ \mathrm{months} \quad (= 956\ \mathrm{days} = 2.66\ \mathrm{years})$ [1] **[1]**

[Shorter missions are possible, but would require a greater *Δv* and hence need much more fuel - any future mission will have to balance the cost (and mass) of more fuel on a fast trip with the cost (and mass) of more supplies on a slow trip]
