### 7.2

Read off from the grid, and coordinates $(\lambda_A,\varphi_A,\lambda_B,\varphi_B)$ can be easily obtained as (127.60°E, 36.47°N) for point A, and (129.45°E, 36.10°N) for point B.

Then one should first convert the differences in angle into distance, and then use Pythagorean principle, such that

$$
L=[\{(129.45-127.60)\times110\times(4.8/5.8)\}^2+\{(36.47-36.10)\times110\}^2]^{1/2},
$$

where the conversion factor 4.8/5.8 for longitudinal distance per one degree longitude can be read off from the correctly scaled gridded paper or using $\sim\sin(90°-36°)$, and one can use 110 km / (latitude degree). Then $L=173.5$ km.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-s7-3">

### 7.3

Once points I, A & B are correctly marked, the distance $I_A$ and $I_B$ can be calculated using Pythagorean principle just as in problem 2, which are 91.1 km and 200.8 km. And using altitudes observed from point I, one can get

$$
h_A=91.1\,\mathrm{km}\times\tan35°=63.8\,\mathrm{km},\quad h_B=200.8\,\mathrm{km}\times\tan10°=35.4\,\mathrm{km}.
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-s7-4">

### 7.4

If the fireball hits the surface, the full projected trajectory from point A to the hitting point (let’s call it C), AC, is the multiple of the projected distance AB by a factor of $63.8/(63.8-35.4)=2.2465$. The same multiplication factor can be used to get the trajectory on the longitude and latitude separately, such that

$$
\lambda_C=127.6+2.2465(129.45-127.6)=131.76°
$$

$$
\varphi_C=36.49+2.2465(36.11-36.49)=35.64°
$$
