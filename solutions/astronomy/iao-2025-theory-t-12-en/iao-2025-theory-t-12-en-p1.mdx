## Условие

In the year when the Bengali calendar (\*) came into operation, court astronomers measured the maximum height of the center of the Sun during the summer and winter solstices. In the first case, they received the value $h_1 = 89^{\circ}27'$, in the second $h_2 = 42^{\circ}11'$. Both times the Sun was south of zenith. Find:

**1.1.** at what latitude the observations were made, **[3 т.]**

**1.2.** the angle of inclination of the ecliptic to the plane of the celestial equator in the specified epoch. **[2 т.]**

**1.3.** What area has the Northern circumpolar zone (the territory inside the Arctic circle) gained (the “+” sign in the answer) or lost (the “–” sign in the answer) since then to the present? **[3 т.]**

(\*) According to the Bengali calendar, the New Year begins in mid-April, the year 1432 is now, and today (November 8) is the 21$^{\mathrm{st}}$ of the Seventh Month.

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-1-1">

### 1.1.

The latitude may be found by the formula

$$
\varphi=90^\circ-(h_1+h_2)/2=24^\circ11'.
$$

(Probably the observations were done on the territory of Murshidabad, the medieval city and capital of the former Gauda Kingdom).

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-1-2">

### 1.2.

The angle of inclination of the ecliptic to the plane of the celestial equator at that time was equal to:

$$
\varepsilon=(h_1-h_2)/2=23^\circ38'.
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-1-3">

### 1.3.

The area that the northern circumpolar zone has lost is the ring between the Arctic Circle in 594 and the Arctic Circle in 2025, that is, between latitudes $\varphi_1=90^\circ-23^\circ38'=66^\circ22'$ and $\varphi_2=90^\circ-23^\circ26'=66^\circ34'$. If $R$ is the radius of the Earth, $\varphi$ is expressed in radians, and $\varphi_0=66^\circ28'$ is the mean value of $\varphi$, then the area of this ring is:

$$
S=R\cos\varphi_0\cdot R(\varphi_1-\varphi_2)=23^\circ38'.
$$

$$
S=(6371\,\mathrm{km})^2\cdot0.399\cdot(12'/3438'/\mathrm{rad})\approx56500\text{ sq.km.}
$$

Answer: $-56500\text{ sq.km.}$

*Transcription note: the supplied sketch prints the first area formula ending in $23^\circ38'$, omits a circumference factor, and then gives the positive numerical area and negative final answer above. These inconsistencies are retained rather than silently repaired.*

</ProblemSection>
