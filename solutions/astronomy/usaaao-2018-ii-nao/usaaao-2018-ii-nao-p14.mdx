## Условие

Suppose that at some time in the very recent past all the hydrogen and helium (baryon density $\rho_b = 4.2 \times 10^{-31}$ g/cm$^{3}$, about 75% hydrogen (=1 baryon) by mass and 25% helium (=4 baryons) by mass) in the universe had been instantly fused into iron in stars, and the released energy thermalized into black body radiation. (Note that the binding energy per nucleon of ${}^{56}_{26}$Fe is 8.8 MeV and that of ${}^{4}_{2}$He is 7.1 MeV.)

**(a)** Calculate the current temperature of this black body radiation. **[5 т.]**

**(b)** At what wavelength would the black body spectrum peak, and what region of the electromagnetic spectrum would this be? **[5 т.]**

**(c)** The mean bolometric luminosity per unit volume emitted by stars in the universe today is about $3 \times 10^{8}\ L_{\odot}/\mathrm{Mpc}^3$. How long would it takes stars at this rate to fuse all the hydrogen and helium in the universe? Compare to the present age of the universe. **[5 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **(a)** 4.4 K
- **(b)** λ = 6.59 × 10⁻² cm, microwave.
- **(c)** 71100000000000000000 s

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**(a)** Answer: $T = 4.40K$.

Without considering intermediary products, we have these two reactions:

$$56\ {}^{1}_{1}\mathrm{H} \rightarrow {}^{56}_{26}\mathrm{Fe}$$

$$14\ {}^{4}_{2}\mathrm{He} \rightarrow {}^{56}_{26}\mathrm{Fe}$$

We can calculate the energy released in each of these reactions by computing the binding energy on each side. There is no binding energy for a hydrogen atom, so the energy released in the first reaction is $56 \times 8.8$ MeV = 492.8 MeV per Fe. For the second reaction, the binding energy will by ($56 \times 8.8$ MeV) - ($14 \times 4 \times 7.1$ MeV) = 95.2 MeV per Fe.

To find how much iron is actually produced, we need to determine the number densities of hydrogen and helium using the mass fraction given in the question:

$$n_H = \frac{0.75\rho_b}{m_H}$$

$$n_{He} = \frac{0.25\rho_b}{m_{He}}$$

where $m_H$ and $m_{He}$ are the masses of hydrogen and helium respectively. Then , the total energy density released is:

$$u = \frac{492.8MeV \times n_H}{56} + \frac{95.2MeV \times n_{He}}{14}$$

To get the temperature, we use:

$$u = aT^4$$

where $a$ is the radiation constant. This gives us a temperature of 4.40 K.

**(b)** Answer: $\lambda = 6.59 \times 10^{-2}$ cm, microwave.

Using Wien’s displacement law:

$$\lambda = \frac{b}{T}$$

where $b = 2.898 \times 10^{-3}$ m K, we get a wavelength of $6.59 \times 10^{-2}$ cm, which is in the microwave region of the electromagnetic spectrum.

**(c)** Answer: $7.11 \times 10^{19}$ seconds (2250 Gyr)

The time it would take is given by the energy density (found in part (a)), divided by the rate of fusion, i.e. the luminosity given in the problem:

$$t = \frac{u}{L}$$

This gives us $7.11 \times 10^{19}$ seconds, or about 2250 Gyr. This is much longer than the current age of the universe (13.7 Gyr).
