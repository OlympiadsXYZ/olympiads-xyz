## Условие

Mars orbits the Sun at an average distance of $2.28 \times 10^{11}$ m and has a radius of $3.39 \times 10^{6}$ m. The Sun has a luminosity of $3.828 \times 10^{26}$ W. How much solar energy falls on the surface of Mars each second? Ignore any effects of Mars’ thin atmosphere.

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- 21200000000000000 W

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

At the distance of Mars’ orbit, the Sun’s energy output is spread over a sphere with an area of

$$A = 4\pi r^2 = 4\pi(2.28 \times 10^{11}\ \mathrm{m})^2 = 6.53 \times 10^{23}\ \mathrm{m}^2.$$

Dividing the luminosity of the Sun by this area gives,

$$\frac{L_{\odot}}{A} = \frac{3.828 \times 10^{26}\ \mathrm{W}}{6.53 \times 10^{23}\ \mathrm{m}^2} = 586\ \frac{\mathrm{W}}{\mathrm{m}^2}.$$

Mars presents a circular area to the Sun of

$$A_{\mathrm{Mars}} = \pi r_{\mathrm{Mars}}^2 = \pi(3.39 \times 10^{6}\ \mathrm{m})^2 = 3.61 \times 10^{13}\ \mathrm{m}^2.$$

Therefore, the total energy that falls on the surface of Mars will be

$$\frac{L_{\odot}}{A}A_{\mathrm{Mars}} = \left(586\ \frac{\mathrm{W}}{\mathrm{m}^2}\right)(3.61 \times 10^{13}\ \mathrm{m}^2) = 2.12 \times 10^{16}\ \mathrm{W}.$$


</Spoiler>
