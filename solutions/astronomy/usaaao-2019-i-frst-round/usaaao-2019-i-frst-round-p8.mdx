## Условие

Suppose a spacecraft were orbiting in a low Earth orbit at an altitude of 400 km. The spacecraft makes a single orbital maneuver to place it into a Mars transfer orbit. Delta-v ($\Delta v$) refers to the change in velocity during an orbital maneuver. What is the $\Delta v$ required for this trans-Mars injection? The semimajor axes of the orbits of Earth and Mars are $1.496 \times 10^8$ km and $2.279 \times 10^8$ km, respectively.

A. 2.94 km/s

B. 3.57 km/s

C. 6.12 km/s

D. 10.85 km/s

E. 11.24 km/s

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- 3.57 km/s

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

*Solution:* **B: 3.57 km/s**

A Hohmann transfer orbit from Earth to Mars would have a semimajor axis of

$$
a = \frac{1}{2}\left(1.496 \times 10^8 + 2.279 \times 10^8\right) = 1.888 \times 10^8\ \mathrm{km}
$$

On the Earth side of the transfer orbit, the spacecraft would need a velocity of

$$
v_1 = \sqrt{GM_{\odot}\left(\frac{2}{1.496 \times 10^{11}} - \frac{1}{a}\right)} = 32.722\ \mathrm{km/s}
$$

The orbital velocity of the earth around the sun is

$$
v_{\oplus} = \sqrt{\frac{GM_{odot}}{1.496 \times 10^{11}}} = 29.779\ \mathrm{km/s}
$$

The hyperbolic excess velocity is therefore

$$
v_{\infty} = v_1 - v_{\oplus} = 2.943\ \mathrm{km/s}
$$

However, the spacecraft must first escape the gravitational influence of the earth. Its current orbital velocity is

$$
v_{orb} = \sqrt{\frac{GM_{\oplus}}{1000 \times (6371 + 400)}} = 7.670\ \mathrm{km/s}
$$

The escape velocity is

$$
v_{\mathrm{esc}} = \sqrt{2}v_{orb} = 10.847\ \mathrm{km/s}
$$

Hyperbolic excess velocity is related to escape and perigee velocities by

$$
v_p^2 = v_{esc}^2 + v_{\infty}^2
$$

Therefore,

$$
v_p = \sqrt{v_{esc}^2 + v_{\infty}^2} = 11.239\ \mathrm{km/s}
$$

Finally, $\Delta v = v_p - v_{orb} = 3.569$ km/s.
