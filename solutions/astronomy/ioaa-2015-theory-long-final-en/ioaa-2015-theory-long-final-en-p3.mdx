Suppose a static spherical star consists of $N$ neutral particles with radius $R$ (see Figure 3).

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '54.2%', '--fig-max': '347px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/ioaa-2015-theory-long-final-en/p3-fig1.png" alt="Sphere of radius R centred at the origin of x, y, z axes, with a dotted equator; a dashed radius R makes polar angle θ with the z axis and its projection onto the x-y plane makes azimuthal angle ϕ." width="1085" height="855" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Figure 3</figcaption>
</figure>

with $0 \le \theta \le \pi$ , $0 \le \phi \le 2\pi$ , satisfying the following equation of states

$$
PV = N k \,\frac{T_R - T_0}{\ln(T_R/T_0)} \qquad (1)
$$

where $P$ and $V$ are the pressure inside the star and the volume of the star respectively, $k$ is the Boltzmann constant. $T_R$ and $T_0$ are the temperatures at the surface $r = R$ and the temperature at the center $r = 0$ respectively. Assume that $T_R \le T_0$.

**a.** Simplify the stellar equation of state (1) if $\Delta T = T_R - T_0 \approx 0$ (this is called ideal star)  
(Hint: Use the approximation $\ln(1 + x) \approx x$ for small $x$)

Suppose the star undergoes a quasi-static process, in which it may slightly contract or expand, such that the above stellar equation of state (1) still holds.

The star satisfies first law of thermodynamics

$$
Q = \Delta M c^2 + W \qquad (2)
$$

where $Q$, $M$, and $W$ are heat, mass of the star, and work respectively, while $c$ is the light speed in the vacuum and $\Delta M \equiv M_{\mathrm{final}} - M_{\mathrm{initial}}$ .

In the following we assume $T_0$ to be constant, while $T_R \equiv T$ varies.

**b.** Find the heat capacity of the star at constant volume $C_v$ in term of $M$ and at constant pressure $C_p$ expressed in $C_v$ and $T$ (Hint: Use the approximation $(1 + x)^n \approx 1 + nx$ for small $x$)

Assuming that $C_v$ is constant and the gas undergo the isobaric process so the star produces the heat and radiates it outside to the space.

**c.** Find the heat produced by the isobaric process if the initial temperature and the final temperature are $T_i$ and $T_f$ , respectively.

**d.** Suppose there is an observer far away from the star. Using information from part c., estimate the distance of the observer from the star.

For the next parts, assume the star is the Sun.

**e.** If the sunlight is monochromatic with frequency $5 \times 10^{14}$ Hz, estimate the number of photons radiated by the Sun per second.

**f.** Calculate the heat capacity $C_v$ of the Sun assuming its surface temperature varies from 5500 K to 6000 K in one second.

## Решение

<Warning title="Непълно решение">
The revised official key leaves final problem 3(d) blank (15 points). The earlier image edition gives a distance derivation only after adding a 0.1% temperature-error assumption absent from the final question; it is retained separately, not presented as a complete final-edition answer.

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

<ProblemSection id="solution-revised-a">

### Revised marking scheme — a. (15 т.)

Defining $\Delta T=T_R-T_0$ and $\Delta T\approx0$, we have

$$
PV=Nk\frac{\Delta T}{\ln(1+\Delta T/T_0)}\qquad (3)
$$

using $\ln(1+\Delta T/T_0)\approx\Delta T/T_0$, we then obtain

$$
PV=NkT_0\qquad (4)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-revised-b">

### Revised marking scheme — b. (30 т.)

The internal energy of the star is $U=Mc^2$ ($U(T)=M(T)c^2$ for ideal star). Thus, the constant volume heat capacity of the star has the form

$$
C_v=\left(\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta T}\right)_V=\left(\frac{\Delta M}{\Delta T}\right)_Vc^2
$$

for small $\Delta T$. Then, using first law of thermodynamics, the constant pressure heat capacity of the star is

$$
C_p=\left(\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta T}\right)_P=\left(\frac{\Delta M}{\Delta T}\right)_Vc^2+P\frac{\Delta V}{\Delta T}=C_v+P\frac{\Delta V}{\Delta T}
$$

for small $\Delta T$. Defining $\Delta T=T_2-T_1$, then

$$
P\Delta V=Nk\left(\frac{T_1-T_0+\Delta T}{\ln((T_1+\Delta T)/T_0)}-\frac{T_1-T_0}{\ln(T_1/T_0)}\right)
$$

Using the approximation

$$
\ln((T_1+\Delta T)/T_0)\approx\ln\left(\frac{T_1}{T_0}\right)+\frac{\Delta T}{T_1}
$$

$$
\left(1+\frac{\Delta T}{T_1\ln(T_1/T_0)}\right)^{-1}\approx1-\frac{\Delta T}{T_1\ln(T_1/T_0)}
$$

then we have

$$
P\frac{\Delta V}{\Delta T}=\frac{Nk}{\ln(T/T_0)}\left(1-\frac{(T-T_0)/T}{\ln(T/T_0)}\right)
$$

where $T_1\equiv T$. Finally, we obtain

$$
C_p=\left(\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta T}\right)_P=\left(\frac{\Delta M}{\Delta T}\right)_Vc^2+P\frac{\Delta V}{\Delta T}=C_v+\frac{Nk}{\ln(T/T_0)}\left(1-\frac{(T-T_0)/T}{\ln(T/T_0)}\right)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-revised-c">

### Revised marking scheme — c. (20 т.)

Since $C_v$ is constant, the heat produced by the star is given by

$$
Q_H=C_v(T_f-T_i)+P\Delta V
$$

$$
=C_v(T_i-T_f)+Nk\left(\frac{T_f-T_0}{\ln(T_f/T_0)}-\frac{T_i-T_0}{\ln(T_i/T_0)}\right)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-revised-e">

### Revised marking scheme — e. (10 т.)

Energy per second radiated by the Sun

$$
L_\odot=Nh\nu
$$

where $N$ is the number of photon. Thus

$$
N=\frac{L_\odot}{h\nu}=\frac{3.96\times10^{26}}{6.6261\times10^{-34}\times5\times10^{14}}=1.195\times10^{45}\ \text{photons}
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-revised-f">

### Revised marking scheme — f. (10 т.)

Energy per second radiated by the Sun is proportional to mass defect of the Sun

$$
L_\odot=\Delta Mc^2
$$

Thus,

$$
C_v=\frac{\Delta Mc^2}{\Delta T}=\frac{L_\odot}{\Delta T}=\frac{3.96\times10^{26}}{6000-5500}\ \mathrm{J/K}=7.92\times10^{23}\ \mathrm{J/K}
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-earlier-isothermal">

### Earlier image edition — b. Isothermal work (15 т.)

Find the work of the star when it expands from $V_1$ to $V_2$ in isothermal process where $T_R$ and $T_0$ are constants.

If the gas undergoes isothermal process where $T_R$ and $T_0$ are constant, then the work has the form

$$
W=\int_{V_1}^{V_2}P\,dV=Nk\frac{T_R-T_0}{\ln(T_R/T_0)}\ln\left(\frac{V_2}{V_1}\right)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-earlier-distance">

### Earlier image edition — e. Distance with 0.1% temperature error (15 т.)

Suppose there is an observer far away from the star. Related to point d., estimate the distance of the observer if the observer has 0.1% error in measuring the effective temperature around the star.

The error 0.1%, namely

$$
\frac{T_f-T_{\mathrm{eff}}}{T_f}=\frac1{1000}
$$

then $T_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{999}{1000}T_f$. From Stefan law of black body radiation

$$
\frac{Q_H}{4\pi r_0^2}=\sigma T_{\mathrm{eff}}^4=\sigma\left(\frac{999}{1000}\right)^4T_f^4
$$

where $r_0$ is the observer's distance which is given by

$$
r_0=\left(\frac{Q_H}{4\pi\sigma}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{1000}{999}\right)^2T_f^{-2}
$$

</ProblemSection>
