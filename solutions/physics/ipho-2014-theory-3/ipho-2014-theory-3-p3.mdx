An electric current flowing through a gas is called a gas discharge. There are many types of gas discharges including glow discharge in lighting lamps, arc discharge in welding and the well known spark discharge that occurs between the clouds and the earth in the form of lightning.

<ProblemSection id="part-a">

### Part A. Non-self-sustained gas discharge (4,8 т.)

In this part of the problem the so-called non-self-sustained gas discharge is studied. To maintain it a permanent operation an external ionizer is needed, which creates $Z_{\mathrm{ext}}$ pairs of singly ionized ions and free electrons per unit volume and per unit time uniformly in the volume.

When an external ionizer is switched on, the number of electrons and ions starts to grow. Unlimited increase in the number densities of electrons and ions in the gas is prevented by the recombination process in which a free electron recombines with an ion to form a neutral atom. The number of recombining events $Z_{\mathrm{rec}}$ that occurs in the gas per unit volume and per unit time is given by

$$
Z_{\mathrm{rec}}=rn_en_i,
$$

where $r$ is a constant called the recombination coefficient, and $n_e,n_i$ denote the electron and ion number densities, respectively.

Suppose that at time $t=0$ the external ionizer is switched on and the initial number densities of electrons and ions in the gas are both equal to zero. Then, the electron number density $n_e(t)$ depends on time $t$ as follows:

$$
n_e(t)=n_0+a\tanh bt,
$$

where $n_0,a$ and $b$ are some constants, and $\tanh x$ stands for the hyperbolic tangent.

**A1** Find $n_0,a,b$ and express them in terms of $Z_{\mathrm{ext}}$ and $r$. **[1,8 т.]**

Assume that there are two external ionizers available. When the first one is switched on, the electron number density in the gas reaches its equilibrium value of be $n_{e1}=12\cdot10^{10}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}$. When the second external ionizer is switched on, the electron number density reaches its equilibrium value of $n_{e2}=16\cdot10^{10}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}$.

**A2** Find the electron number density $n_e$ at equilibrium when both external ionizers are switched on simultaneously. **[0,6 т.]**

**Attention!** In what follows it is assumed that the external ionizer is switched on for quite long period of time such that all processes have become stationary and do not depend on time. Completely neglect the electric field due the charge carriers.

Assume that the gas fills in the tube between the two parallel conductive plates of area $S$ separated by the distance $L\ll\sqrt S$ from each other. The voltage $U$ is applied across the plates to create an electric field between them. Assume that the number densities of both kinds of charge carriers remain almost constant along the tube.

Assume that both the electrons (denoted by the subscript $e$) and the ions (denoted by the subscript $i$) acquire the same ordered speed $v$ due to the electric field strength $E$ found as

$$
v=\beta E,
$$

where $b$ is a constant called charge mobility.

**A3** Express the electric current $I$ in the tube in terms of $U,\beta,L,S,Z_{\mathrm{ext}},r$ and $e$ which is the elementary charge. **[1,7 т.]**

**A4** Find the resistivity $\rho_{\mathrm{gas}}$ of the gas at sufficiently small values of the voltage applied and express it in terms of $\beta,L,Z_{\mathrm{ext}},r$ and $e$. **[0,7 т.]**

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="part-b">

### Part B. Self-sustained gas discharge (5,2 т.)

In this part of the problem the ignition of the self-sustained gas discharge is considered to show how the electric current in the tube becomes self-maintaining.

**Attention!** In the sequel assume that the external ionizer continues to operate with the same $Z_{\mathrm{ext}}$ rate, neglect the electric field due to the charge carriers such that the electric field is uniform along the tube, and the recombination can be completely ignored.

For the self-sustained gas discharge there are two important processes not considered above. The first process is a secondary electron emission, and the second one is a formation of electron avalanche. The secondary electron emission occurs when ions hit on the negative electrode, called a cathode, and the electrons are knocked out of it to move towards the positive electrode, called an anode. The ratio of the number of the knocked electrons $\dot N_e$ per unit time to the number of ions $\dot N_i$ hitting the cathode per unit time is called the coefficient of the secondary electron emission, $\gamma=\dot N_e/\dot N_i$.

</figure>


The formation of the electron avalanche is explained as follows. The electric field accelerates free electrons which acquire enough kinetic energy to ionize the atoms in the gas by hitting them. As a result the number of free electrons moving towards the anode significantly increases. This process is described by the Townsend coefficient $\alpha$, which characterizes an increase in the number of electrons $dN_e$ due to moving $N_e$ electrons that have passed the distance $dl$, i.e.

$$
\frac{dN_e}{dl}=\alpha N_e.
$$

The total current $I$ in any cross section of the gas tube consists of the ion $I_i(x)$ and the electron $I_e(x)$ currents which, in the steady state, depend on the coordinate $x$, shown in the figure above. The electron current $I_e(x)$ varies along the $x$-axis according to the formula

$$
I_e(x)=C_1e^{A_1x}+A_2,
$$

where $A_1,A_2,C_1$ are some constants.

**B1** Find $A_1,A_2$ and express them in terms of $Z_{\mathrm{ext}},\alpha,e,L,S$. **[2 т.]**

The ion current $I_i(x)$ varies along the $x$-axis according to the formula

$$
I_i(x)=C_2+B_1e^{B_2x},
$$

where $B_1,B_2,C_2$ are some constants.

**B2** Find $B_1,B_2$ and express them in terms of $Z_{\mathrm{ext}},\alpha,e,L,S,C_1$. **[0,6 т.]**

**B3** Write down the condition for $I_i(x)$ at $x=L$. **[0,3 т.]**

**B4** Write down the condition for $I_i(x)$ and $I_e(x)$ at $x=0$. **[0,6 т.]**

**B5** Find the total current $I$ and express it in terms of $Z_{\mathrm{ext}},\alpha,\gamma,e,L,S$. Assume that it remains finite. **[1,2 т.]**

Let the Townsend coefficient $\alpha$ be constant. When the length of the tube turns out greater than some critical value, i.e. $L>L_{\mathrm{cr}}$, the external ionizer can be turned off and the discharge becomes self-sustained.

**B6** Find $L_{\mathrm{cr}}$ and express it in terms of $Z_{\mathrm{ext}},\alpha,\gamma,e,L,S$. **[0,5 т.]**

</ProblemSection>

## Решение

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-part-a">

### Part A. Non-self-sustained gas discharge

**A1** Let us derive an equation describing the change of the electron number density with time. It is determined by the two processes; the generation of ion pairs by external ionizer and the recombination of electrons with ions. At ionization process electrons and ions are generated in pairs, and at recombination process they disappear in pairs as well. Thus, their concentrations are always equal at any given time, i.e.

$$
n(t)=n_e(t)=n_i(t).\qquad (A1.1)
$$

Then the equation describing the number density evolution of electrons and ions in time can be written as

$$
\frac{dn(t)}{dt}=Z_{\mathrm{ext}}-rn(t)^2.\qquad (A1.2)
$$

It is easy to show that at $t\to0$ the function $\tanh bt\to0$, therefore, by virtue of the initial condition $n(0)=0$, one finds

$$
n_0=0.\qquad (A1.3)
$$

Substituting $n_e(t)=a\tanh bt$ in (A1.2) and separating it in the independent functions (hyperbolic, or $1$ and $e^x$), one gets

$$
a=\sqrt{\frac{Z_{\mathrm{ext}}}{r}},\qquad (A1.4)
$$

$$
b=\sqrt{rZ_{\mathrm{ext}}}.\qquad (A1.5)
$$

**A2** According to equation (A1.4) the number density of electrons at steady-state is expressed in terms of the external ionizer activity as

$$
n_{e1}=\sqrt{\frac{Z_{\mathrm{ext}\,1}}r},\qquad (A2.1)
$$

$$
n_{e2}=\sqrt{\frac{Z_{\mathrm{ext}\,2}}r},\qquad (A2.2)
$$

$$
n_e=\sqrt{\frac{Z_{\mathrm{ext}\,1}+Z_{\mathrm{ext}\,2}}r}.\qquad (A2.3)
$$

Thus, the following analogue of the Pythagorean theorem is obtained as

$$
n_e=\sqrt{n_{e1}^2+n_{e2}^2}=20.0\cdot10^{10}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}.\qquad (A2.4)
$$

**A3** In the steady state, the balance equations of electrons and ions in the tube volume take the form

$$
Z_{\mathrm{ext}}SL=rn_en_iSL+\frac{I_e}e,\qquad (A3.1)
$$

$$
Z_{\mathrm{ext}}SL=rn_en_iSL+\frac{I_i}e.\qquad (A3.2)
$$

It follows from equations (A3.1) and (A3.2) that the ion and electron currents are equal, i.e.

$$
I_e=I_i.\qquad (A3.3)
$$

At the same time the total current in each tube section is the sum of the electron and ion currents

$$
I=I_e+I_i.\qquad (A3.4)
$$

By definition of the current density the following relations hold

$$
I_e=\frac I2=en_evS=e\beta n_eES,\qquad (A3.5)
$$

$$
I_i=\frac I2=en_ivS=e\beta n_iES.\qquad (A3.6)
$$

Substituting (A3.5) and (A3.6) into (A3.1) and (A3.2), the following quadratic equation for the current is derived

$$
Z_{\mathrm{ext}}SL=rSL\left(\frac I{2e\beta ES}\right)^2+\frac I{2e}.\qquad (A3.7)
$$

The electric field strength in the gas is equal to

$$
E=\frac UL\qquad (A3.8)
$$

and solution to the quadratic equation (A3.7) takes the form

$$
I=\frac{e\beta^2U^2S}{rL^3}\left(-1\pm\sqrt{1+\frac{4rZ_{\mathrm{ext}}L^4}{\beta^2U^2}}\right).\qquad (A3.9)
$$

It is obvious that only positive root does make sense, i.e.

$$
I=\frac{e\beta^2U^2S}{rL^3}\left(\sqrt{1+\frac{4rZ_{\mathrm{ext}}L^4}{\beta^2U^2}}-1\right).\qquad (A3.10)
$$

**A4** At low voltages (A3.10) simplifies and gives the following expression

$$
I=2Ue\beta\sqrt{\frac{Z_{\mathrm{ext}}}r}\frac SL,\qquad (A4.1)
$$

which is actually the Ohm law. Using the well-known relation

$$
R=\frac UI\qquad (A4.2)
$$

together with

$$
R=\rho\frac LS,\qquad (A4.3)
$$

one gets

$$
\rho=\frac1{2e\beta}\sqrt{\frac r{Z_{\mathrm{ext}}}}.\qquad (A4.4)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-solution-part-b">

### Part B. Self-sustained gas discharge

**B1** Consider a gas layer located between $x$ and $x+dx$. The rate of change in the electron number inside the layer due to the electric current is given for a small time interval $dt$ by

$$
dN_e^I=\frac{I_e(x+dx)-I_e(x)}e\,dt=\frac1e\frac{dI_e(x)}{dx}\,dx\,dt.\qquad (B1.1)
$$

This change is due to the effect of the external ionization and the electron avalanche formation. The external ionizer creates the following number of electrons in the volume $Sdx$

$$
dN_e^{\mathrm{ext}}=Z_{\mathrm{ext}}S\,dx\,dt,\qquad (B1.2)
$$

whereas the electron avalanche produces the number of electrons found as

$$
dN_e^a=\alpha N_e\,dl=n_eS\,dx\,v\,dt=\alpha\frac{I_e(x)}e\,dx\,dt.\qquad (B1.3)
$$

The balance equation for the number of electrons is written as

$$
dN_e^I=dN_e^{\mathrm{ext}}+dN_e^a,\qquad (B1.4)
$$

which results in the following differential equation for the electron current

$$
\frac{dI_e(x)}{dx}=eZ_{\mathrm{ext}}S+\alpha I_e(x).\qquad (B1.5)
$$

On substituting $I_e(x)=C_1e^{A_1x}+A_2$, one derives

$$
A_1=\alpha,\qquad (B1.6)
$$

$$
A_2=-\frac{eZ_{\mathrm{ext}}S}{\alpha}.\qquad (B1.7)
$$

**B2** Given the fact that the ions flow in the direction opposite to the electron motion, the balance equation for the number of ions is written as

$$
dN_i^I=dN_i^{\mathrm{ext}}+dN_i^a,\qquad (B2.1)
$$

where

$$
dN_i^I=\frac{I_i(x)-I_i(x+dx)}e\,dt=-\frac1e\frac{dI_i(x)}{dx}\,dx\,dt,\qquad (B2.2)
$$

$$
dN_i^{\mathrm{ext}}=Z_{\mathrm{ext}}S\,dx\,dt,\qquad (B2.3)
$$

$$
dN_i^a=\alpha\frac{I_e(x)}e\,dx\,dt.\qquad (B2.4)
$$

Hence, the following differential equation for the ion current is obtained

$$
-\frac{dI_i(x)}{dx}=eZ_{\mathrm{ext}}S+\alpha I_e(x).\qquad (B2.5)
$$

On substituting the previously found electron current together with the ion current, $I_i(x)=C_2+B_1e^{B_2x}$, yields

$$
B_1=-C_1,\qquad (B2.6)
$$

$$
B_2=\alpha.\qquad (B2.7)
$$

**B3** Since the ions starts to move from the anode located at $x=L$, the following condition holds

$$
I_i(L)=0.\qquad (B3.1)
$$

**B4** By definition of secondary electron emission coefficient the following condition should be imposed

$$
I_e(0)=\gamma I_i(0).\qquad (B4.1)
$$

**B5** Total current in each tube section is the sum of the electron and ion currents:

$$
I=I_e+I_i=C_2-\frac{eZ_{\mathrm{ext}}S}{\alpha}.\qquad (B5.1)
$$

After substituting the boundary conditions (B3.1) and (B4.1):

$$
C_2-C_1e^{\alpha L}=0\qquad (B5.2)
$$

and

$$
C_1-\frac{eZ_{\mathrm{ext}}S}{\alpha}=\gamma(C_2-C_1).\qquad (B5.3)
$$

Solving (B5.2) and (B5.3) one can obtain:

$$
C_2=\frac{eZ_{\mathrm{ext}}S}{\alpha}\left(\frac1{e^{-\alpha L}(1+\gamma)-\gamma}\right).\qquad (B5.4)
$$

So the total current:

$$
I=\frac{eZ_{\mathrm{ext}}S}{\alpha}\left(\frac1{e^{-\alpha L}(1+\gamma)-\gamma}-1\right).\qquad (B5.5)
$$

**B6** When the discharge gap length is increased, the denominator in formula (B5.1) decreases. At that moment, when it turns zero, the electric current in the gas becomes self-sustaining and external ionizer can be turned off. Thus,

$$
L_{\mathrm{cr}}=\frac1\alpha\ln\left(1+\frac1\gamma\right).\qquad (B6.1)
$$

</ProblemSection>

### Supplementary presentation: B7

Slide 11 gives the following steps for its additional B7 question:

$$
\alpha=\alpha_1p\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha_2p}{E}\right),\qquad U=EL,
$$

$$
U=\frac{\alpha_2pL}{\ln\left(\dfrac{\alpha_1pL}{\ln(1+1/\gamma)}\right)}.
$$

With $z=pL$, the condition $dU/dz=0$ gives

$$
z_{\min}=\frac e{\alpha_1}\ln(1+1/\gamma).
$$

The final boxed expression is printed as

$$
U_{\min}=\frac{e\alpha_1}{\alpha_2}\ln\left(1+\frac1\gamma\right)=122\ \mathrm V.
$$

**Source note:** The slide reverses the ratio of $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ in this final expression relative to its preceding formula for $U$. The numerical constants needed to reproduce 122 V are not supplied on the slide. Here $e$ in the exponential minimum is Euler’s number; the exam uses $e$ for elementary charge. Slides 10–11 also display a total-current formula different from (B5.5). The complete competition solution above retains the exam’s formula.
