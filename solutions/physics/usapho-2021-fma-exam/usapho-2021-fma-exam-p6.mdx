## Условие

A cylindrical bucket of negligible mass has radius $R$ and height $h$, and is open at the top. It is submerged in water of density $\rho$, with its top a distance $H$ below the surface. How much work is needed to pull the bucket slowly up so that its bottom is just above the lake surface?

</figure>

(A) $\rho g \pi R^2 h(H - h)$

(B) $\rho g \pi R^2 h^2$

(C) $\rho g \pi R^2 (H - h)^2$

(D) $\rho g \pi R^2 h^2/2$

(E) $\rho g \pi R^2 h(H - h)/2$

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- D

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

While the bucket is in the lake, it is full of water, and the weight of the water is exactly cancelled by the buoyant force. Thus, no work is required to pull the bucket until its top is at the lake surface. From this point onward, the force linearly increases. The average force required to pull the bucket out of the water is $mg/2$ where $m = \pi \rho R^2 h$ is the mass of the water in the bucket, and the distance is $h$, giving total work $\rho g \pi R^2 h^2/2$.

The problem can also be solved using energy conservation. After the bucket has been lifted, a mass $m$ of water has been raised from the water surface to height $h/2$ above it, giving work $mgh/2$ and the same result.


</Spoiler>
