## Условие

A bacterium cell swims by rotating its bundle of flagella to counter a viscous drag force in the medium. The drag force $F(R, v, \eta)$ only depends on the typical length scale of the cell $R$, its speed $v$, and the viscosity of the fluid $\eta$, which has units of $\mathrm{kg/(m \cdot s)}$. It is observed under a microscope that a cell of length 1 $\mu$m swims at about 20 $\mu$m/s. Estimate the speed of a cell of length 0.5 $\mu$m, assuming cells of all sizes generate the same amount of force from their flagella.

(A) 5 $\mu$m/s

(B) 10 $\mu$m/s

(C) 40 $\mu$m/s

(D) 80 $\mu$m/s

(E) There is not enough information to decide.

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- C

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

Using dimensional analysis, we can see that force scales as $F \sim Rv\eta$. To see this you can try:

$$
[\mathrm{m}]^{\mathrm{a}}[\mathrm{m/s}]^{\mathrm{b}}[\mathrm{kg/(m \cdot s)}]^{\mathrm{c}} = [\mathrm{N}] = [\mathrm{kg \cdot m/s^2}].
$$

The only solution is $a = b = c = 1$. Therefore, in the same medium, when the cell length reduces by $1/2$, its speed doubles.


</Spoiler>
