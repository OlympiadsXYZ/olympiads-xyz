## Условие

Alice and Bethany stand side by side on the Earth’s equator. If Alice jumps directly upward, in her frame of reference, to a small height $h$ much less than the radius of the Earth, she will land a distance $D$ to the west of Bethany. If Alice had instead jumped to a height $2h$, how far to the west of Bethany would she land? Neglect air resistance.

(A) $D/\sqrt{2}$

(B) $D$

(C) $\sqrt{2}\,D$

(D) $2D$

(E) $2^{3/2}D$

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- E

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

The distance to the left of the starting position is given by $\Delta v\, t$, where $\Delta v = v_{\mathrm{ave}} - v_0$ is the average change in tangential velocity due to the jump and $t$ is the total time of the jump. We can compute the proportionality between $\Delta v$ and $h$ using conservation of angular momentum,

$$
v_{\mathrm{ave}}(R + h_{\mathrm{ave}}) \approx v_0 R \implies v_{\mathrm{ave}} \approx \frac{v_0 R}{R + h_{\mathrm{ave}}}.
$$

We use an $\approx$ because $[v \cdot (R + h)]_{\mathrm{ave}} \neq v_{\mathrm{ave}} \cdot (R + h_{\mathrm{ave}})$, but they both exhibit the same scaling. Then,

$$
\Delta v = v_0 - v_{\mathrm{ave}} = \frac{v_0 h}{R + h_{\mathrm{ave}}} \implies \Delta v \propto h.
$$

From basic kinematics, $t \propto h^{1/2}$, so $\Delta v\, t \propto h^{3/2}$, so the answer is (E).


</Spoiler>
