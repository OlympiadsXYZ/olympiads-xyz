## Условие

In this problem you have to investigate basic characteristics and stability conditions of atomic nuclei. Let an atomic nucleus contain $A$ nucleons ($A$ is the atomic weight of the corresponding element), namely, $Z$ protons ($Z$ is the atomic number in the table of chemical elements) and $N = A - Z$ neutrons. The expression for the total energy of the nucleus can be written as
$$
E = \left(Zm_p + Nm_n\right)c^2 + E_p(A,Z) = Mc^2, \qquad (1)
$$
where $M$ denotes the nucleus mass, $m_p$ is the mass of the free proton, $m_n$ is the mass of the free neutron, $c$ stands for the speed of light, and $E_p$ is the interaction potential energy of the nucleons in the nucleus.

The interaction potential energy of the nucleons can be described by the following semi-empirical formula proposed by Weizsäcker
$$
E_p(A,Z) = -a_1A + a_2A^{2/3} + a_3\frac{Z^2}{A^{1/3}} + a_4\frac{(A/2-Z)^2}{A}, \qquad (2)
$$
where $a_1 = 15.8\ \mathrm{MeV}$, $a_2 = 16.8\ \mathrm{MeV}$, $a_3 = 0.72\ \mathrm{MeV}$, $a_4 = 23.5\ \mathrm{MeV}$.

Weizsacker semiempirical formula corresponds to one of the simplest model of the atomic nucleus, the so-called spherical liquid droplet model, which relies on the analogy between a nucleus and a droplet of an ordinary liquid. The mass and the charge of a nucleus is assumed to be uniformly distributed inside a sphere of some radius, and the nucleon fluid is characterized by some parameter $\sigma$ which is an analogue of the surface tension of the liquid.

The formula for the potential energy $E_p$ takes into account the following contributions:

- surface energy, which takes into account the surface tension of the nuclear matter in the liquid droplet model;
- the energy of the Coulomb repulsion of the protons within the nucleus;
- the exchange interaction energy reflecting the trend towards the stability of nuclei at *N=Z;*
- direct dependence on the number of nucleons $A$ due to nuclear forces.

At the derivation of his semiempirical formula Weizsäcker used an experimentally established fact that the following dependence holds for nucleus radius on the number of nucleons
$$
R(A) = R_0A^{1/3}, \qquad (3)
$$
where $R_0$ is a constant.

Starting from the above statements, find answers to the following questions:

**3.1** Find the electrostatic energy $E_C$ of a ball of radius $R$, uniformly charged with the net charge $Q$. Express your answer in terms of the charge $Q$, the dielectric constant $\varepsilon_0$ and the radius $R$ of the ball. **[2 т.]**

**3.2** Find the formula and the numerical value of the coefficient $R_0$ in formula (3). **[1 т.]**

**3.3** Find the formula and the numerical value of the density $\rho_m$ of the nuclear matter. **[1 т.]**

**3.4** Find the formula and the numerical value of the surface tension $\sigma$ of the nucleon liquid. **[1 т.]**

Suppose now that the nucleus fisses (is divided) into two fragments with atomic weights $kA$ and $(1-k)A$ respectively, where $0<k<1$. Assume that the electric charge and the number of neutrons are distributed between the fragments similar to the atomic weight.

**3.5** Nuclear fission (division into fragments) becomes energetically favorable under the condition $Z^2/A > f(k)$. Find an expression for the function $f(k)$ and plot its graph schematically. **[2 т.]**

**3.6** Find the limiting value $(Z^2/A)_0$ at which the spontaneous fission is still theoretically possible. **[0,5 т.]**

Under the above condition in 3.5, a nucleus can stay stable for quite a long time. For example, the half-life of Uranium-235 nucleus is equal to 713 million years. Consequently, an instantaneous fission is prevented by an energy barrier, which disappears at some critical value $(Z^2/A)_{crtitcal}$. In fact, a nucleus will only fiss if a significant deviation of its shape from the ball occurs.

For the sake of simplicity assume that a spherical nucleus undergoes such a deformation that its surface turns into a prolate ellipsoid of rotation, which is described in the Cartesian coordinates by the equation
$$
\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{a^2} + \frac{z^2}{b^2} = 1, \qquad (4)
$$
where $a$ is a minor, and $b$ is a major semi-axes of the ellipsoid, respectively.

The volume of a prolate ellipsoid is given by
$$
V = \frac{4}{3}\pi a^2b, \qquad (5)
$$
and its surface area can be calculated via the formula
$$
S = 2\pi a\left(a + \frac{b^2}{\sqrt{b^2-a^2}}\arcsin\left(\frac{\sqrt{b^2-a^2}}{b}\right)\right). \qquad (6)
$$

Let a spherical nucleus undergo such a deformation that $b = R(1+\varepsilon)$ and $a = R(1-\lambda)$, so that $\varepsilon, \lambda \ll 1$, and $R$ is the initial radius of the nuclear droplet.

**3.7** Find the relation between $\varepsilon$ and $\lambda$. **[0,5 т.]**

Calculations show that the energy of the electrostatic interaction of protons of the deformed nucleus is equal to $E_C^{deformed} = E_C\left(1-\frac{1}{6}\varepsilon(\varepsilon+\lambda)\right)$.

**3.8** Find the expression and calculate the numerical value of $(Z^2/A)_{crtitcal}$. **[2 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **3.1** $E_C = \frac{3Q^2}{20\pi\varepsilon_0R}$
- **3.2** $R_0 = \frac{3e}{20\pi\varepsilon_0a_3}$ ≈ $1.2 \times 10^{-15}$ m
- **3.3** $\rho_m = \frac{3m}{4\pi R_0^3}$ ≈ $2.3 \times 10^{17}$ kg/m³
- **3.4** $\sigma = \frac{ea_2}{4\pi R_0^2}$ ≈ $1.5 \times 10^{17}$ N/m
- **3.5** $f(k) = -\frac{a_2\left(1-k^{2/3}-(1-k)^{2/3}\right)}{a_3\left(1-k^{5/3}-(1-k)^{5/3}\right)}$
- **3.6** 16
- **3.7** $\varepsilon = 2\lambda$
- **3.8** $(Z^2/A)_{critical} = \frac{128\pi^2\varepsilon_0\sigma R_0^3}{3e^2}$ ≈ $37$

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

3.1 [2 points] Let us calculate the total electrostatic energy of the protons in the nucleus. Within the droplet model the nuclear charge $Ze$ is uniformly distributed inside a ball of radius $R$, so that its volume density is the same everywhere and equals to
$$
\rho_q = \frac{3Q}{4\pi R^3}. \qquad (1)
$$
Using the Gauss theorem, we find the electric field inside and outside of the ball
$$
E(r)4\pi r^2 = \frac{1}{\varepsilon_0}\rho_q\frac{4\pi}{3}r^3, \qquad (2)
$$
$$
E(r)4\pi r^2 = \frac{1}{\varepsilon_0}\rho_q\frac{4\pi}{3}R^3. \qquad (3)
$$
Hence we get
$$
E(r) = \left\{ {\displaystyle \frac{\rho_q r}{2\varepsilon_0},\qquad r \le R} \atop {\displaystyle \frac{\rho_q R^3}{2\varepsilon_0 r^2},\quad r > R} \right.. \qquad (4)
$$
Total electrostatic energy is given by the integral
$$
E_C = \int_0^{\infty} w4\pi r^2dr = \int_0^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon_0E^2}{2}4\pi r^2dr = \frac{3Q^2}{20\pi\varepsilon_0R}. \qquad (5)
$$

3.2 [1 point] From (5), $Q = Ze$ and $R(A) = R_0A^{1/3}$ one can see that the electrostatic energy corresponds to the third term in the Weizsacker semiempirical formula, so
$$
a_3\frac{Z^2}{A^{1/3}} = \frac{3Z^2e}{20\pi\varepsilon_0R_0A^{1/3}}, \qquad (6)
$$
whence
$$
R_0 = \frac{3e}{20\pi\varepsilon_0a_3} = 1.2\times 10^{-15}\ \mathrm{m}. \qquad (7)
$$

3.3 [1 point] The density of the nuclear matter is given by
$$
\rho_m = \frac{3Am}{4\pi R^3} = \frac{3m}{4\pi R_0^3} = 2.3\times 10^{17}\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}. \qquad (8)
$$

3.4 [1 point] The surface energy depends on the surface tension
$$
E_{sur} = \sigma S = 4\pi\sigma R^2 = 4\pi\sigma R_0^2A^{2/3}. \qquad (9)
$$
We conclude that the surface energy corresponds to the second term of the semi-empirical formula of Weizsäcker
$$
4\pi\sigma R_0^2A^{2/3} = ea_2A^{2/3}, \qquad (10)
$$
whence
$$
\sigma = \frac{ea_2}{4\pi R_0^2} = 1.5\times 10^{17}\ \mathrm{N/m}. \qquad (11)
$$

3.5 [2 points] Nuclear fission becomes exoteric only if the potential energy of the nuclei decreases, that is,
$$
E_p(A,Z) - E_p(kA,kZ) - E_p((1-k)A,(1-k)Z) > 0, \qquad (12)
$$
which yields
$$
\frac{Z^2}{A} > f(k) = -\frac{a_2\left(1-k^{2/3}-(1-k)^{2/3}\right)}{a_3\left(1-k^{5/3}-(1-k)^{5/3}\right)}. \qquad (13)
$$

3.6 [0.5 points] The function $f(k)$ is symmetric with respect to the point $k = 0.50$, so the minimum is achieved exactly at this point, which corresponds to
$$
(Z^2/A)_0 = 16. \qquad (14)
$$

3.7 [0.5 points] Since the nucleus is treated as a liquid, its volume should not change. Using the formula for the volume of an ellipsoid and the fact that $\varepsilon, \lambda \ll 1$ we obtain
$$
V = \frac{4\pi}{3}R^3\left(1+\varepsilon-2\lambda\right) = \frac{4\pi}{3}R^3, \qquad (15)
$$
whence
$$
\varepsilon = 2\lambda. \qquad (16)
$$

3.8 [2 points] Using Taylor's series expansion for small deformations of the nucleus and taking into account (16), the surface area of the liquid increases by
$$
\Delta S = \frac{32}{5}\pi R^2\lambda^2 = \frac{32}{5}\pi R_0^2A^{2/3}\lambda^2, \qquad (17)
$$
and an increase in the surface energy is equal to
$$
\Delta E_{surf} = \sigma\Delta S = \frac{32}{5}\pi\sigma R_0^2A^{2/3}\lambda^2. \qquad (18)
$$
The Coulomb interaction energy of the protons is decreased by the value
$$
\Delta E_C = \frac{3Z^2e^2}{120\pi\varepsilon_0R}\varepsilon(\varepsilon+\lambda) = \frac{3Z^2e^2}{20\pi\varepsilon_0R_0A^{1/3}}\lambda^2. \qquad (19)
$$
A nucleus is unstable under the condition
$$
\Delta E_C > \Delta E_{surf}, \qquad (20)
$$
whence
$$
(Z^2/A)_{crtitcal} = \frac{128\pi^2\varepsilon_0\sigma R_0^3}{3e^2} = 37. \qquad (21)
$$
