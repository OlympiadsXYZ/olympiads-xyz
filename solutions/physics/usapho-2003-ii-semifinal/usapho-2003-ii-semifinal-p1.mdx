## Условие

A satellite of mass $m$ is in a circular orbit of radius $r$ around a star of mass $M$.

**a.** What is the magnitude of the satellite’s momentum $p$? Express your answer in terms of given quantities and constants. **[5 т.]**

In Parts b, c, and d you should express the impulse vector $\vec{I}$ in terms of the satellite’s original momentum vector $\vec{p}$.

**b.** We wish to give the satellite a single impulse of minimum possible magnitude, causing it to escape from the star. What impulse $\vec{I}_e$ is necessary? **[5 т.]**

**c.** We wish to give the satellite a single impulse of minimum possible magnitude, causing it to crash into the star. What impulse $\vec{I}_{C0}$ is necessary if you neglect the radius of the star? **[5 т.]**

**d.** Suppose that the star has a radius $R$. Now what impulse $\vec{I}_{CR}$ is necessary to crash the satellite into the star? Your answer should also involve the ratio $r/R$. **[10 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a.** $p = m\sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}$
- **b.** $\vec{I}_e = \left(\sqrt{2} - 1\right)\vec{p}$
- **c.** $\vec{I}_{C0} = -\vec{p}$
- **d.** $\vec{I}_{CR} = -\vec{p}\left(1 - \sqrt{\frac{2}{1 + r/R}}\right)$

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

a. Setting the mass times centripetal acceleration equal to the gravitational force
$$m\frac{v^2}{r} = G\frac{Mm}{r^2}.$$
Solving for speed
$$v = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}},$$
and multiplying by mass to get the momentum
$$p = mv = m\sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}$$

b. To escape from the star the total energy must be at least zero. Writing the escape speed $v_e$,
$$\tfrac{1}{2}mv_e^2 - G\frac{Mm}{r} = 0.$$
Solving for $v_e$, and multiplying by mass to get the needed momentum,
$$p_e = mv_e = m\sqrt{\frac{2GM}{r}} = \sqrt{2}p.$$
This is greater than p above, so the impulse must be in the direction of $\vec{p}$.
$$\vec{I}_e = \vec{p}_e - \vec{p} = \left(\sqrt{2} - 1\right)\vec{p}$$

c. Since angular momentum $L = mvr$ must be conserved, the only way to crash into a star with $R = 0$ is with $L = 0$. The satellite’s momentum $mv_{C0}$ must be slowed to zero.
$$\vec{I}_{C0} = m\vec{v}_{C0} - \vec{p} = 0 - \vec{p} = -\vec{p}.$$

d. Letting $v_r$ be the new speed at orbital radius $r$ and $v_R$ be the speed at stellar radius $R$, angular momentum conservation yields
$$mv_r r = mv_R R.$$
Solving for $v_R$
$$v_R = v_r\frac{r}{R}. \qquad \mathrm{(A1\text{-}1)}$$
Mechanical energy is also conserved.
$$\tfrac{1}{2}mv_r^2 - G\frac{Mm}{r} = \tfrac{1}{2}mv_R^2 - G\frac{Mm}{R}$$
Regrouping terms and canceling $m$,
$$G\frac{M}{R} - G\frac{M}{r} = \tfrac{1}{2}v_R^2 - \tfrac{1}{2}v_r^2.$$
Substituting (A1-1)
$$2GM\left(\frac{1}{R} - \frac{1}{r}\right) = v_r^2\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^2 - v_r^2$$
$$\frac{2GM}{rR}(r - R) = \frac{v_r^2}{R^2}\left(r^2 - R^2\right)$$
Multiplying by $R$ and dividing by $(r - R)$
$$\frac{2GM}{r} = \frac{v_r^2}{R}(r + R)$$
Solving for $v_r$ and multiplying by mass to get $p_r$,
$$p_r = mv_r = m\sqrt{\frac{2GMR}{r(r + R)}} = m\sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{1 + \frac{r}{R}}} = p\sqrt{\frac{2}{1 + \frac{r}{R}}}$$
This is less than the original orbital momentum. Finding the impulse,
$$\vec{I}_{CR} = \vec{p}_r - \vec{p} = \vec{p}\sqrt{\frac{2}{1 + r/R}} - \vec{p} = -\vec{p}\left(1 - \sqrt{\frac{2}{1 + r/R}}\right)$$


</Spoiler>
