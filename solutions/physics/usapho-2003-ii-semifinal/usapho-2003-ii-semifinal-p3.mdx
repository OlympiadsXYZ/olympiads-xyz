A conducting slab has infinite extent in the $x$ and $y$ directions and thickness $L$ in the $z$ direction as shown in Figure a above. The slab is centered at $z = 0$ and carries a uniform current density $\vec{J} = J\hat{i}$ where $\hat{i}$, $\hat{j}$, and $\hat{k}$ are unit vectors in the $x$, $y$, and $z$ directions, respectively.

**a.** Find the magnetic field $\vec{B}$ at all points. **[10 т.]**

**b.** A square wire loop of side $a$ is placed a distance $b$ above the slab (as shown in Figure b above). The loop has unit normal vector $\hat{n} = \cos\theta\hat{i} + \sin\theta\hat{j}$ and applied current $I$. (Figure b depicts the special case $\theta = 0$.) What is the net force and the net torque on the loop as a function of $\theta$? **[10 т.]**

**c.** The applied current $I$ is now removed from the loop described above and the current density in the slab $\vec{J} = J\hat{i}$ is reduced to zero over time $T$. The wire used to construct the loop has resistance per unit length $S$. How much charge flows through each cross section of the loop wire due to the reduction in current density? **[5 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a.** B = ½μ₀JL ĵ for z < −L/2; B = −μ₀Jz ĵ for −L/2 < z < L/2; B = −½μ₀JL ĵ for z > L/2
- **b.** $\vec{F}_{net} = 0,\quad \vec{\tau}_{net} = -\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_0 JLIa^2\cos\theta\,\hat{k}$
- **c.** $Q = \frac{\mu_0 aLJ}{8S}\sin\theta$

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

a. The slab has infinite extent in the $x$ and $y$ direction. Therefore the magnetic field $\vec{B}$ can depend at most on $z$. By the right hand rule and symmetry $\vec{B}$ is to the left for positive $z$ and to the right for negative $z$.
$$
\vec{B} = -B(z)\hat{j} \qquad z > 0
$$
$$
\vec{B} = +B(z)\hat{j} \qquad z < 0
$$
$\vec{B}$ can be found from Ampere’s law. To find $\vec{B}$ outside the slab, $z > L/2$, use the loop shown in Figure a. The loop segments parallel to the $z$ direction are perpendicular to $\vec{B}$ and will not contribute.
$$
\oint \vec{B}\cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_o I_{enc}
$$
$$
Bw + Bw = \mu_o(LwJ)
$$
$$
B = \tfrac{1}{2}\mu_o LJ \qquad \text{for } \lvert z\rvert > L/2.
$$
To find $\vec{B}$ inside the slab, $z < L/2$, use the loop shown in Figure b. Once again, the loop segments parallel to the $z$ direction are perpendicular to $\vec{B}$ and will not contribute.
$$
\oint \vec{B}\cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_o I_{enc}
$$
$$
Bw + Bw = \mu_o(2zwJ)
$$
$$
B = \mu_o Jz \qquad \text{for } \lvert z\rvert < L/2.
$$
Including the direction,
$$
\vec{B} = \tfrac{1}{2}\mu_o JL\hat{j} \qquad -L/2 > z,
$$
$$
\vec{B} = -\mu_o Jz\hat{j} \qquad +L/2 > z > -L/2,
$$
$$
\vec{B} = -\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_o JL\hat{j} \qquad z > L/2
$$

b. In the region of the loop, $\vec{B}$ is uniform, $\vec{B} = -\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_o JL\hat{j}$. The force on the top segment is equal and opposite to the force on the bottom segment. The force on the left segment it equal and opposite to the force on the right segment. The net force on the loop is zero.
$$
\vec{F}_{net} = 0.
$$
The net torque is given by
$$
\vec{\tau}_{net} = \vec{\mu}\times\vec{B}
$$
where $\vec{\mu}$, the magnetic dipole moment is
$$
\vec{\mu} = IA\hat{n} = Ia^2\left(\cos\theta\hat{i} + \sin\theta\hat{j}\right).
$$
$$
\vec{\tau}_{net} = Ia^2\left(\cos\theta\hat{i} + \sin\theta\hat{j}\right)\times\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_o JL\hat{j}\right)
$$
$$
\vec{\tau}_{net} = -\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_o JLIa^2\left(\cos\theta\hat{i}\times\hat{j} + \sin\theta\hat{j}\times\hat{j}\right)
$$
$$
\vec{\tau}_{net} = -\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_o JLIa^2\cos\theta\hat{k}
$$

c. $\vec{B}$ is uniform so the flux through the loop is
$$
\Phi = \vec{B}\cdot A\hat{n} = -\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_o JL\hat{j}\cdot a^2\left(\cos\theta\hat{i} + \sin\theta\hat{j}\right)
$$
$$
\Phi = -\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_o JLa^2\sin\theta
$$
Using Faraday’s Law, the emf
$$
\mathcal{E} = -\frac{\Delta\Phi}{\Delta t} = \tfrac{1}{2}\mu_o La^2\sin\theta\frac{\Delta J}{\Delta t}.
$$
The emf is also
$$
\mathcal{E} = IR = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta t}R
$$
where
$$
R = (4a)S.
$$
$$
\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta t} = \frac{1}{2R}\mu_o La^2\sin\theta\frac{\Delta J}{\Delta t} = \frac{1}{8aS}\mu_o La^2\sin\theta\frac{\Delta J}{\Delta t}.
$$
Since $J$ has been reduced to zero over time $T$, the charge $Q$ flowing in time $T$ is
$$
Q = \frac{\mu_o aLJ}{8S}\sin\theta
$$
