## Условие

An ideal gas has a heat capacity per unit mole at constant pressure of $C_\mathrm{P} = 41.9$ J/(mol·K) and a heat capacity per unit mole at constant volume of $C_\mathrm{V} = 33.5$ J/(mol·K). An 0.100-mole sample of this gas goes through the accompanying cycle. Process 1–>2 is isobaric (constant pressure), 2–>3 is adiabatic (no heat enters or leaves), and 3–>1 is isothermal (constant temperature).

| | |
|---|---|
| $T_1 = 300$ K | $T_2 = 400$ K |
| $V_1 = 0.0118\ \mathrm{m^3}$ | $V_2 = 0.0157\ \mathrm{m^3}$ |

<figure>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-2003-ii-semifinal/p4-fig1.png" alt="P–V diagram (P vertical, V horizontal) of a cycle: horizontal isobar from point 1 to point 2 (arrow to the right), a steep adiabat from 2 down to point 3 (arrow downward), and an isotherm from 3 back up to 1 (arrow toward 1)." />
</figure>

**a.** Find the work done by the gas during each process ($W_{1\text{–>}2}$, $W_{2\text{–>}3}$, and $W_{3\text{–>}1}$). **[15 т.]**

**b.** What is the efficiency of this cycle? **[10 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a.** W(1→2) = +83.1 J, W(2→3) = +335 J, W(3→1) = −358 J
- **b.** 0.14

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

a. Process $1\to 2$ takes place at constant pressure. The work done is
$$W_{1\to 2} = p\Delta V.$$
For a constant pressure process, the ideal gas law yields $p\Delta V = nR\Delta T$
and
$$W_{1\to 2} = nR\Delta T = (0.10\ \mathrm{mol})(8.31\ \mathrm{J/mol\cdot K})(400\ \mathrm{K} - 300\ \mathrm{K}) = +83.1\ \mathrm{J}.$$

Process $2\to 3$ is adiabatic, $Q = 0$. Combining this with the first law of thermodynamics,
$$Q = W + \Delta U.$$
The work done is the negative of the change in internal energy.
$$W_{2\to 3} = -\Delta U = -nC_V\Delta T = -nC_V\left(T_3 - T_2\right).$$
Since process $3\to 1$ is isothermal, $T_3 = T_1 = 300\ \mathrm{K}$.
And
$$W_{2\to 3} = -(0.10\ \mathrm{mol})(33.5\ \mathrm{J/mol\cdot K})(300\ \mathrm{K} - 400\ \mathrm{K}) = +335\ \mathrm{J}.$$

For the isothermal process $3\to 1$, the work done is
$$W_{3\to 1} = nRT\ln\left(\frac{V_1}{V_3}\right).$$
Since $2\to 3$ is adiabatic,
$$p_2V_2^{\gamma} = p_3V_3^{\gamma}$$
with $\gamma = \frac{C_P}{C_V} = \frac{41.9}{33.5} = \frac{5}{4}$. Combining this with the ideal gas law $pV = nRT$,
$$nRT_2V_2^{\gamma-1} = nRT_3V_3^{\gamma-1}.$$
Solving for $V_3$,
$$V_3^{\gamma-1} = \left(\frac{T_2}{T_3}\right)V_2^{\gamma-1}$$
$$V_3 = \left(\frac{T_2}{T_3}\right)^{1/(\gamma-1)}V_2 = \left(\frac{T_2}{T_3}\right)^4V_2 = \left(\frac{400}{300}\right)^4\left(0.0157\ \mathrm{m^3}\right) = 0.0496\ \mathrm{m^3}$$
Finally,
$$W_{3\to 1} = (0.10\ \mathrm{mol})(8.31\ \mathrm{J/mol\cdot K})(300\ \mathrm{K})\ln\left(\frac{0.0118}{0.0496}\right) = -358\ \mathrm{J}.$$

b. The efficiency is the total work done divided by the heat input. Heat is input during process $1\to 2$ and leaves during $3\to 1$.
$$Q_{in} = Q_{1\to 2} = nC_p\Delta T = (0.10\ \mathrm{mol})(41.9\ \mathrm{J/mol\cdot K})(100\ \mathrm{K}) = +419\ \mathrm{J}$$
$$W_{net} = W_{1\to 2} + W_{2\to 3} + W_{3\to 1} = +83\ \mathrm{J} + 335\ \mathrm{J} - 358\ \mathrm{J} = 60\ \mathrm{J}$$
$$e = \frac{60}{419} = 0.14$$


</Spoiler>
