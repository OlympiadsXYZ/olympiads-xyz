## Условие



**a.** In classical mechanics, the kinetic energy $K$, momentum $p$, and mass $m$ of a particle are related by $K = \frac{p^2}{2m}$. Derive the relationship between the total energy $E$, momentum $p$, and mass $m$ of a particle in special relativity? **[10 т.]**

Hint: Express $E^2$ in terms of $p^2$. In addition to $m$, the expression may also include $c$. Relativistic expressions for the total energy $E$ and momentum $p$ of a particle of mass $m$ moving at speed $v$ are
$$E = \frac{mc^2}{\sqrt{1 - (v/c)^2}} \qquad\qquad p = \frac{mv}{\sqrt{1 - (v/c)^2}}$$

Particle A (mass $1000\ \mathrm{MeV}/c^2$) is at rest at the center of a spherical gamma detector, which completely surrounds it except for a small hole. We accelerate particle B (mass $500\ \mathrm{MeV}/c^2$) to a total energy of $700\ \mathrm{MeV}$, sending it through the hole towards particle A. When the particles collide, a single, excited particle of unknown mass, C\*, is produced. C\* quickly decays to its unexcited state, C (mass $1300\ \mathrm{MeV}/c^2$), emitting a single gamma ray.

**b.** Find the mass of C\*. **[10 т.]**

**c.** If the gamma is emitted directly away from the hole, what energy does the detector record? **[15 т.]**

**d.** The experiment is repeated, and the detector records an energy of $300\ \mathrm{MeV}$. At what angles did particle C and the gamma emerge? Assume that C\* decays at the center of the detector. Let an angle of 0 be directly away from the hole, and an angle of $\pi$ be directly towards the hole. **[15 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a.** $E^2 = (cp)^2 + \left(mc^2\right)^2$
- **b.** 1628 MeV/c^2
- **c.** 397 MeV
- **d.** gamma at 1.365 rad and C at 0.601 rad from the axis, on opposite sides of the axis

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

a. Squaring the relativistic expressions for $E$ and $p$,
$$E^2 = \frac{m^2c^4}{1 - (v/c)^2} \qquad\qquad p^2 = \frac{m^2v^2}{1 - (v/c)^2}.$$
Multiplying the $p^2$ equation by $c^2$ and subtracting from $E^2$
$$E^2 - c^2p^2 = \frac{m^2c^4}{1 - (v/c)^2} - \frac{m^2v^2c^2}{1 - (v/c)^2} = \frac{m^2c^4\left(1 - (v/c)^2\right)}{1 - (v/c)^2} = m^2c^4$$
$$E^2 = (cp)^2 + \left(mc^2\right)^2$$

b. Since A is at rest, $E_A = m_Ac^2 = 1000\ \mathrm{MeV}$ and $p_A = 0$;
and
$$cp_B = \sqrt{E_B^2 - m_B^2c^4} = \sqrt{(700\ \mathrm{MeV})^2 - (500\ \mathrm{MeV})^2} = 490\ \mathrm{MeV}.$$
By conservation of energy and momentum,
$$E_{C*} = E_A + E_B = 1000\ \mathrm{MeV} + 700\ \mathrm{MeV} = 1700\ \mathrm{MeV}$$
$$cp_{C*} = cp_A + cp_B = 0 + 490\ \mathrm{MeV} = 490\ \mathrm{MeV}$$
Thus
$$m_{C*}c^2 = \sqrt{E_{C*}^2 - (cp_{C*})^2} = \sqrt{(1700\ \mathrm{MeV})^2 - (490\ \mathrm{MeV})^2} = 1628\ \mathrm{MeV}.$$

c. Again, using conservation of energy and momentum,
$$E_C + E_\gamma = E_{C*}$$
$$cp_C + cp_\gamma = cp_{C*}$$
Since the gamma is massless, $E_\gamma^2 = (cp_\gamma)^2$. It is emitted along the direction of travel of C\*, so we select the positive root, $cp_\gamma = E_\gamma$. Thus, rearranging,
$$E_C = E_{C*} - E_\gamma$$
$$cp_C = cp_{C*} - E_\gamma$$
We need a fourth relationship,
$$E_C^2 - (cp_C)^2 = \left(m_Cc^2\right)^2.$$
$$\left(E_{C*} - E_\gamma\right)^2 - \left(cp_{C*} - E_\gamma\right)^2 = \left(m_Cc^2\right)^2$$
$$E_{C*}^2 - 2E_{C*}E_\gamma + E_\gamma^2 - (cp_{C*})^2 + 2cp_{C*}E_\gamma - E_\gamma^2 = \left(m_Cc^2\right)^2$$
$$E_{C*}^2 - (cp_{C*})^2 + 2cp_{C*}E_\gamma - 2E_{C*}E_\gamma = \left(m_Cc^2\right)^2$$
Since
$$E_{C*}^2 - (cp_{C*})^2 = \left(m_{C*}c^2\right)^2,$$
the prior equation becomes,
$$\left(m_{C*}c^2\right)^2 - 2E_\gamma\left(E_{C*} - cp_{C*}\right) = \left(m_Cc^2\right)^2$$
$$E_\gamma = \frac{\left(m_{C*}c^2\right)^2 - \left(m_Cc^2\right)^2}{2\left(E_{C*} - cp_{C*}\right)} = \frac{(1628\ \mathrm{MeV})^2 - (1300\ \mathrm{MeV})^2}{2(1700\ \mathrm{MeV} - 490\ \mathrm{MeV})} = 397\ \mathrm{MeV}$$

d. Let the gamma be emitted at an angle $\theta_\gamma$ to the axis, and C at an angle $\theta_C$. We measure the two angles on opposite sides of the axis so that both are in the interval $[0, \pi]$.
From conservation of energy,
$$E_C = E_{C*} - E_\gamma = 1700\ \mathrm{MeV} - 300\ \mathrm{MeV} = 1400\ \mathrm{MeV};$$
thus
$$cp_C = \sqrt{E_C^2 - \left(m_Cc^2\right)^2} = \sqrt{(1400\ \mathrm{MeV})^2 - (1300\ \mathrm{MeV})^2} = 520\ \mathrm{MeV}$$
And again,
$$cp_\gamma = E_\gamma = 300\ \mathrm{MeV}.$$
Then from conservation of momentum,
$$p_\gamma\sin\theta_\gamma - p_C\sin\theta_C = 0$$
$$p_\gamma\cos\theta_\gamma + p_C\cos\theta_C = p_{C*}$$
Rearranging,
$$\left(p_C\sin\theta_C\right)^2 + \left(p_C\cos\theta_C\right)^2 = \left(p_\gamma\sin\theta_\gamma\right)^2 + \left(p_{C*} - p_\gamma\cos\theta_\gamma\right)^2$$
$$p_C^2 = p_{C*}^2 + p_\gamma^2 - 2p_{C*}p_\gamma\cos\theta_\gamma$$
$$\cos\theta_\gamma = \frac{p_{C*}^2 + p_\gamma^2 - p_C^2}{2p_{C*}p_\gamma} = \frac{(490\ \mathrm{MeV/c})^2 + (300\ \mathrm{MeV/c})^2 - (520\ \mathrm{MeV/c})^2}{2(490\ \mathrm{MeV/c})(300\ \mathrm{MeV/c})} = 0.204$$
$$\theta_\gamma = \cos^{-1}0.204 = 1.365\ \mathrm{rad}.$$
Then,
$$\sin\theta_C = \frac{p_\gamma}{p_C}\sin\theta_\gamma = \frac{300\ \mathrm{MeV/c}}{520\ \mathrm{MeV/c}}\sin 1.365 = 0.565$$
Thus,
$$\theta_C = 0.601\ \mathrm{rad}.$$


</Spoiler>
