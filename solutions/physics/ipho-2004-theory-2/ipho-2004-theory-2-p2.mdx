*Theoretical Question 2: Rising Balloon · 10 т.*

## Условие

A rubber balloon filled with helium gas goes up high into the sky where the pressure and temperature decrease with height. In the following questions, assume that the shape of the balloon remains spherical regardless of the payload, and neglect the payload volume. Also assume that the temperature of the helium gas inside of the balloon is always the same as that of the ambient air, and treat all gases as ideal gases. The universal gas constant is $R$ =8.31 J/mol·K and the molar masses of helium and air are $M_H = 4.00\times 10^{-3}$ kg/mol and $M_A$ = $28.9\times 10^{-3}$ kg/mol, respectively. The gravitational acceleration is $g = 9.8$ m/s$^2$.

**[Part A ]**

**(a)** Let the pressure of the ambient air be $P$ and the temperature be $T$ . The pressure inside of the balloon is higher than that of outside due to the surface tension of the balloon. The balloon contains $n$ moles of helium gas and the pressure inside is $P+\Delta P$ . Find the buoyant force $F_{\mathrm{B}}$ acting on the balloon as a function of $P$ and $\Delta P$ . **[1,5 т.]**

**(b)** On a particular summer day in Korea, the air temperature $T$ at the height $z$ from the sea level was found to be $T(z) = T_0(1 - z/z_0)$ in the range of $0 < z < 15$ km with $z_0$ =49 km and $T_0$ =303 K. The pressure and density at the sea level were $P_0$ = 1.0 atm = $1.01\times 10^5$ Pa and $\rho_0$ = 1.16 kg/m$^3$, respectively. For this height range, the pressure takes the form

$$
P(z) = P_0(1 - z/z_0)^{\eta} . \qquad (2.1)
$$

Express $\eta$ in terms of $z_0$, $\rho_0$, $P_0$, and $g$, and find its numerical value to the *two* significant digits. Treat the gravitational acceleration as a constant, independent of height. **[2 т.]**

**[Part B ]**

When a rubber balloon of spherical shape with un-stretched radius $r_0$ is inflated to a sphere of radius $r$ ($\ge r_0$), the balloon surface contains extra elastic energy due to the stretching. In a simplistic theory, the elastic energy at constant temperature $T$ can be expressed by

$$
U = 4\pi r_0^2\kappa RT\,(2\lambda^2 + \frac{1}{\lambda^4} - 3) \qquad (2.2)
$$

where $\lambda \equiv r/r_0$ ($\ge 1$) is the size-inflation ratio and $\kappa$ is a constant in units of mol/m$^2$.

**(c)** Express $\Delta P$ in terms of parameters given in Eq. (2.2), and sketch $\Delta P$ as a function of $\lambda = r/r_0$ . **[2 т.]**

**(d)** The constant $\kappa$ can be determined from the amount of the gas needed to inflate the balloon. At $T_0$ =303 K and $P_0$ =1.0 atm = $1.01\times 10^5$ Pa, an un-stretched balloon ($\lambda = 1$) contains $n_0$ =12.5 moles of helium. It takes $n$ =3.6 $n_0$ =45 moles in total to inflate the balloon to $\lambda$ =1.5 at the same $T_0$ and $P_0$ . Express the balloon parameter $a$ , defined as $a = \kappa/\kappa_0$ , in terms of $n$ , $n_0$ , and $\lambda$ , where $\kappa_0 \equiv \dfrac{r_0 P_0}{4RT_0}$ . Evaluate $a$ to the two significant digits. **[1,5 т.]**

**[Part C]**

A balloon is prepared as in (d) at the sea level (inflated to $\lambda = 1.5$ with $n = 3.6n_0 = 45$ moles of helium gas at $T_0$ =303 K and $P_0$ =1 atm=$1.01\times 10^5$ Pa). The total mass including gas, balloon itself, and other payloads is $M_{\mathrm{T}} = 1.12$ kg. Now let the balloon rise from the sea level.

**(e)** Suppose that the balloon eventually stops at the height $z_f$ where the buoyant force balances the total weight. Find $z_f$ and the inflation ratio $\lambda_f$ at that height. Give the answers in two significant digits. Assume there are no drift effect and no gas leakage during the upward flight. **[3 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **(a)** $F_B = M_A n g \frac{P}{P+\Delta P}$
- **(b)** $\eta = \frac{\rho_0 z_0 g}{P_0} = 5.5$
- **(c)** $\Delta P = \frac{4\kappa RT}{r_0}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{1}{\lambda^7}\right)$
- **(d)** $a = \frac{n/(n_0\lambda^3)-1}{\lambda^{-1}-\lambda^{-7}} = 0.110$
- **(e)** $z_f = 11\ \mathrm{km},\quad \lambda_f = 2.1$

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**1. Answers**

(a)
$$
F_B = M_A n g\frac{P}{P+\Delta P}
$$

(b)
$$
\gamma = \frac{\rho_0 z_0 g}{P_0} = 5.5
$$

(c)
$$
\Delta P = \frac{4\kappa RT}{r_0}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{1}{\lambda^7}\right)
$$

(d) $a$ =0.110

(e) $z_f$ =11 km, $\lambda_f$ =2.1.

**2. Solutions**

**[Part A]**

(a) [1.5 points]

Using the ideal gas equation of state, the volume of the helium gas of $n$ moles at pressure $P+\Delta P$ and temperature $T$ is

$$
V = nRT/(P+\Delta P) \qquad (a1)
$$

while the volume of $n'$ moles of air gas at pressure $P$ and temperature $T$ is

$$
V = n'RT/P . \qquad (a2)
$$

Thus the balloon displaces $n' = n\dfrac{P}{P+\Delta P}$ moles of air whose weight is $M_A n' g$ .

This displaced air weight is the buoyant force, i.e.,

$$
F_B = M_A n g\frac{P}{P+\Delta P} . \qquad (a3)
$$

(Partial credits for subtracting the gas weight.)

(b) [2 points]

The pressure difference arising from a height difference of $z$ is $-\rho g z$ when the air density $\rho$ is a constant. When it varies as a function of the height, we have

$$
\frac{dP}{dz} = -\rho g = -\frac{\rho_0 T_0}{P_0}\frac{P}{T}g \qquad (b1)
$$

where the ideal gas law $\rho T/P$ = constant is used. Inserting Eq. (2.1) and $T/T_0 = 1 - z/z_0$ on both sides of Eq. (b1), and comparing the two, one gets

$$
\gamma = \frac{\rho_0 z_0 g}{P_0} = \frac{1.16\times 4.9\times 10^4\times 9.8}{1.01\times 10^5} = 5.52 . \qquad (b2)
$$

The required numerical value is 5.5.

**[Part B]**

(c) [2 points]

The work needed to increase the radius from $r$ to $r+dr$ under the pressure difference $\Delta P$ is

$$
dW = 4\pi r^2\Delta P dr , \qquad (c1)
$$

while the increase of the elastic energy for the same change of $r$ is

$$
dW = \left(\frac{dU}{dr}\right)dr = 4\pi\kappa RT(4r - 4\frac{r_0^{\,6}}{r^5})dr . \qquad (c2)
$$

Equating the two expressions of $dW$ , one gets

$$
\Delta P = 4\kappa RT(\frac{1}{r} - \frac{r_0^{\,6}}{r^7}) = \frac{4\kappa RT}{r_0}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{1}{\lambda^7}\right). \qquad (c3)
$$

This is the required answer.

The graph as a function of $\lambda$ (>1) increases sharply initially, has a maximum at $\lambda = 7^{1/6}$ =1.38, and decreases as $\lambda^{-1}$ for large $\lambda$ . The plot of $\Delta P/(4\kappa RT/r_0)$ is given below.

(d) [1.5 points]

From the ideal gas law,

$$
P_0V_0 = n_0RT_0 \qquad (d1)
$$

where $V_0$ is the unstretched volume.

At volume $V = \lambda^3V_0$ containing $n$ moles, the ideal gas law applied to the gas inside at $T = T_0$ gives the inside pressure $P_{\mathrm{in}}$ as

$$
P_{\mathrm{in}} = nRT_0/V = \frac{n}{n_0\lambda^3}P_0 . \qquad (d2)
$$

On the other hand, the result of (c) at $T = T_0$ gives

$$
P_{\mathrm{in}} = P_0 + \Delta P = P_0 + \frac{4\kappa RT_0}{r_0}(\frac{1}{\lambda} - \frac{1}{\lambda^7}) = (1 + a(\frac{1}{\lambda} - \frac{1}{\lambda^7}))P_0 . \qquad (d3)
$$

Equating (d2) and (d3) to solve for $a$ ,

$$
a = \frac{n/(n_0\lambda^3) - 1}{\lambda^{-1} - \lambda^{-7}} . \qquad (d5)
$$

Inserting $n/n_0$ =3.6 and $\lambda$ =1.5 here, $a$ =0.110.

**[Part C]**

(e) [3 points]

The buoyant force derived in problem (a) should balance the total mass of $M_{\mathrm{T}}$ =1.12 kg. Thus, from Eq. (a3), at the weight balance,

$$
\frac{P}{P+\Delta P} = \frac{M_{\mathrm{T}}}{M_A n} . \qquad (e1)
$$

On the other hand, applying again the ideal gas law to the helium gas inside of volume $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 = \lambda^3\frac{4}{3}\pi r_0^{\,3} = \lambda^3V_0$, for arbitrary ambient $P$ and $T$ , one has

$$
(P+\Delta P)\lambda^3 = \frac{nRT}{V_0} = P_0\frac{T}{T_0}\frac{n}{n_0} \qquad (e2)
$$

for $n$ moles of helium. Eqs. (c3), (e1), and (e2) determine the three unknowns $P$ , $\Delta P$ , and $\lambda$ as a function of $T$ and other parameters. Using Eq. (e2) in Eq. (e1), one has an alternative condition for the weight balance as

$$
\frac{P}{P_0}\frac{T_0}{T}\lambda^3 = \frac{M_{\mathrm{T}}}{M_A n_0} . \qquad (e3)
$$

Next using (c3) for $\Delta P$ in (e2), one has

$$
P\lambda^3 + \frac{4\kappa RT}{r_0}\lambda^2(1 - \lambda^{-6}) = P_0\frac{T}{T_0}\frac{n}{n_0}
$$

or, rearranging it,

$$
\frac{P}{P_0}\frac{T_0}{T}\lambda^3 = \frac{n}{n_0} - a\lambda^2(1 - \lambda^{-6}) , \qquad (e4)
$$

where the definition of $a$ has been used again.

Equating the right hand sides of Eqs. (e3) and (e4), one has the equation for $\lambda$ as

$$
\lambda^2(1 - \lambda^{-6}) = \frac{1}{an_0}(n - \frac{M_{\mathrm{T}}}{M_A}) = 4.54. \qquad (e5)
$$

The solution for $\lambda$ can be obtained by

$$
\lambda^2 \approx 4.54/(1 - 4.54^{-3}) \approx 4.54 :\ \lambda_f \cong 2.13. \qquad (e6)
$$

To find the height, replace $(P/P_0)/(T/T_0)$ on the left hand side of Eq. (e3) as a function of the height given in (b) as

$$
\frac{P}{P_0}\frac{T_0}{T}\lambda^3 = (1 - z_f/z_0)^{\gamma - 1}\lambda_f^3 = \frac{M_{\mathrm{T}}}{M_A n_0} = 3.10 . \qquad (e7)
$$

Solution of Eq. (e7) for $z_f$ with $\lambda_f$ =2.13 and $\gamma - 1$ =4.5 is

$$
z_f = 49\times\left(1 - (3.10/2.13^3)^{1/4.5}\right) = 10.9\ (\mathrm{km}). \qquad (e8)
$$

The required answers are $\lambda_f = 2.1$, and $z_f = 11$ km.
