## Условие

**Part B**

**Virial Reality**

The ideal gas law states that $PV_m=RT$, where $V_m=V/n$ is the volume per mole of gas. However, any real gas will exhibit deviations from the ideal gas law, described by the virial expansion,

$$
PV_m=RT\left(1+\frac{B(T)}{V_m}+\frac{C(T)}{V_m^2}+\cdots\right).
$$

For gases with low density, the higher-order terms are negligible, so in this problem we will neglect all of the temperature-dependent terms in parentheses except for $B(T)/V_m$. The table below shows measurements of $B$ for nitrogen gas ($N_2$) at atmospheric pressure, $P=1.01\times10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}$.

| $T$ (K) | $B$ ($\mathrm{cm^3/mol}$) |
| --- | --- |
| 100 | $-160$ |
| 200 | $-35$ |
| 300 | $-4.2$ |
| 400 | 9.0 |
| 500 | 16.9 |
| 600 | 21.3 |

**a.** According to the ideal gas law, what is the value of $V_m$ at temperatures 100 K, 300 K, and 600 K? Give your answers in SI units.

**b.** What is the percentage change in $V_m$ at these temperatures if one accounts for $B(T)$?

**c.** In 1910, van der Waals was awarded the Nobel Prize for formulating the equation

$$
\left(P+\frac a{V_m^2}\right)(V_m-b)=RT
$$

which accurately describes many real gases. According to this equation, what is the form of $B(T)$? You may assume that $b\ll V_m$.

**d.** Using the data above, extract the values of $a$ and $b$. Give your answers in SI units.

**e.** In this problem, we have neglected terms in the virial expansion beyond $B(T)$, which is a good approximation as long as the volume correction due to $B(T)$ itself is small. Assuming the van der Waals equation holds, numerically estimate the temperature range within which the volume correction due to $B(T)$ is at most 10%, for nitrogen gas at atmospheric pressure.

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

<ProblemSection id="solution-a">

### a.

Plugging in the values and converting to $\mathrm{m^3/mol}$ gives

$$
V_m=\frac{RT}P=\begin{cases}8.23\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m^3/mol}&T=100\ \mathrm K\\2.47\times10^{-2}\ \mathrm{m^3/mol}&T=300\ \mathrm K\\4.94\times10^{-2}\ \mathrm{m^3/mol}&T=600\ \mathrm K\end{cases}
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-b">

### b.

The fractional change in volume is

$$
\frac{\Delta V_m}{V_m}\approx\frac{B(T)}{V_m}\approx\begin{cases}-1.9\%&T=100\ \mathrm K\\-0.02\%&T=300\ \mathrm K\\0.04\%&T=600\ \mathrm K.\end{cases}
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-c">

### c.

We have

$$
P+\frac a{V_m^2}=\frac{RT}{V_m-b}\approx\frac{RT}{V_m}\left(1+\frac b{V_m}\right).
$$

Rearranging yields

$$
PV_m\approx RT\left(1+\frac b{V_m}-\frac a{V_mRT}\right)
$$

from which we conclude

$$
B(T)=b-\frac a{RT}.
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-d">

### d.

First, we convert the data points to SI units, giving

| $T$ (K) | $B$ ($\mathrm{m^3/mol}$) |
| --- | --- |
| 100 | $-1.6\times10^{-4}$ |
| 200 | $-3.5\times10^{-5}$ |
| 300 | $-4.2\times10^{-6}$ |
| 400 | $9.0\times10^{-6}$ |
| 500 | $1.69\times10^{-5}$ |
| 600 | $2.13\times10^{-5}$ |

Now, plotting $B$ versus $1/T$, the slope is $-a/R$ and the $y$-intercept is $b$.

[[figure:p4-key-fit]]

The final result is

$$
a=0.18\ \mathrm{J\,m^3/mol^2},\qquad b=6\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{m^3/mol}.
$$

Any answer within 20% of this is acceptable.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-e">

### e.

We are looking for the temperature range where

$$
|B(T)|\lesssim\frac{V_m}{10}\approx\frac{RT}{10P}.
$$

At high temperatures, $B(T)$ always yields a small correction for nitrogen at atmospheric pressure. At low temperatures, $B(T)$ is dominated by the $-a/RT$ term, so we require

$$
T\gtrsim\frac{\sqrt{10Pa}}R=50\ \mathrm K.
$$

Any answer within 25% of this is acceptable.

</ProblemSection>
