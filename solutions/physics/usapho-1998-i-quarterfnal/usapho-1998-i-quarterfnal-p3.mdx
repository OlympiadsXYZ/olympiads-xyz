## Условие

Two small but heavy metal beads, each of mass $m$, are soldered to a thin rigid rod of length $2L$. One bead is soldered halfway along the rod, and the second bead is soldered at one end of the rod. The other end of the rod is connected to the ceiling by a frictionless pivot, making a pendulum of the rod and two beads (see the sketch). The mass of the rod is negligible, as is air resistance. This pendulum is pulled to one side such that it is initially horizontal ($\theta=90^\circ$). The pendulum is then released and swings down, with $\theta$ decreasing from $90^\circ$ through $0^\circ$.

**(a, 9)** Find the speed of each bead when the rod is vertical ($\theta=0^\circ$), assuming for now that neither section of the rod breaks. **[9 т.]**

**(b, 16)** Either section of the rod will break if subjected to a tension force of $4.2mg$ or greater. In the motion described above, does either section break? If so, find the angle at which it breaks. **[16 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **(a, 9)** $v_1=[(6/5)gL]^{1/2},\quad v_2=2[(6/5)gL]^{1/2}$
- **(b, 16)** The upper rod breaks at θ=41.4°; the lower rod does not break.

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**(a, 9)** One may apply conservation of energy, $E_i=E_f$, **(2)**

and obtain (choosing P.E. = 0 at $\theta=0$):

$$
(2m)g(2L)=[\tfrac12mv_1^2+mg(2L-L\cos\theta)]+[\tfrac12mv_2^2+mg(2L)(1-\cos\theta)]
$$

which simplifies to

$$
0=\tfrac12mv_1^2+\tfrac12mv_2^2-3mgL\cos\theta.
$$

**(3)**

We note that for all $\theta$, $v_1=L\omega$ and $v_2=(2L)\omega=2v_1$. **(2)**

Hence, $v_1=[(6/5)gL\cos\theta]^{1/2}$ and $v_2=2[(6/5)gL\cos\theta]^{1/2}$ **(1)**

so that, at $\theta=0$, $v_1=[(6/5)gL]^{1/2}$ and $v_2=2[(6/5)gL]^{1/2}$ **(1)**

**(b, 16)**

Consider the bottom mass (mass #2) [6 points to be earned here]:

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '8.9%', '--fig-max': '58px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-1998-i-quarterfnal/p3-sol-fig1-astra-r6-20260925.png" alt="Free-body diagram of lower bead with tension T2 and weight mg." width="180" height="437" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


The radial component of $\mathbf F=m\mathbf a$ becomes

$T_2-mg\cos\theta=mv_2^2/2L$ **(3)**

Using the result $v_2^2=(24/5)gL\cos\theta$ from part (a), one finds

$T_2=(17/5)mg\cos\theta$ **(2)**

which is $<4.2mg$ for all $\theta$, so the lower rod does not break. **(1)**

[Note: if the student says “we need consider only the top rod because if either rod breaks it must be the top one,” award the (1) point for this conclusion; however, the the student will still need to find $T_2$ as a function of $\theta$ in order to determine the angle where the upper rod breaks.]

Consider next the upper mass (mass #1) [10 points to be earned here]:

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '16.3%', '--fig-max': '105px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-1998-i-quarterfnal/p3-sol-fig2-astra-r6-20260925.png" alt="Free-body diagram of upper bead with tensions T1 and T2 and weight mg." width="327" height="463" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


The radial component of $\mathbf F=m\mathbf a$ becomes

$T_1-T_2-mg\cos\theta=mv_1^2/L$ **(5)**

Using $v_1^2=(6/5)gL\cos\theta$ and $T_2=(17/5)mg\cos\theta$, one obtains

$T_1=(28/5)mg\cos\theta$. **(3)**

Thus, $T_1$ will equal $4.2mg$ when $\theta=41.4^\circ$. **(2)**


</Spoiler>
