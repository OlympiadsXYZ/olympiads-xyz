A wooden 45° right triangle has uniform mass $M$ and two equal sides of length $L$. The coordinates of the triangle’s center of gravity, measured with respect to the axes shown in Fig. 4-1 above, are

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '27.8%', '--fig-max': '178px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-2002-i-quarterfnal/p4-fig3.png" alt="The triangle resting with its hypotenuse-side leg on a plane inclined at angle θ to the horizontal, the angle θ marked at the lower right." width="556" height="565" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Fig. 4-3</figcaption>
</figure>

$$x_{cg} = L/3 \qquad\qquad y_{cg} = L/3.$$

The triangle is placed on a rough horizontal surface and a horizontal force is applied at the top of the triangle, as shown in Fig. 4-2. The force is gradually increased until the triangle just starts to tip over without sliding. The force is then removed and the surface is inclined as shown in Fig 4-3. The triangle is placed on the inclined plane. For what range of angle $\theta$, measured with respect to the horizontal, can you be certain that the triangle will not slide?

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- $\theta \le \tan^{-1}(1/3) \approx 0.32\ \mathrm{rad} = 18.4^{\circ}$

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**4.** Let $F$ be the magnitude of the horizontal force when the triangle just starts to tip over. The forces acting on the triangle when it is on the horizontal surface are shown in Figure 4-4.

Each force identified 1 point. **[4]**

Since the triangle is just starting to tip over and is not sliding, the conditions of static equilibrium still apply and, in addition, $\quad f_s \le \mu F_N.$ **[1]**

*(Students should not be penalized for using “=” instead of “$\le$” in intermediate steps. Finding the upper limit of the range is a valid technique.)*

The forces in the $x$-direction must sum to zero. $\quad F = f_s \le \mu F_N$ **[1]**

The forces in the $y$-direction must sum to zero. $\quad F_N = W$ **[1]**

The sum of the torques must also be zero. Taking torques about the lower left-hand corner,

$$0 = \sum \tau_i$$ **[1]**

Each torque can be written $\quad \tau_i = R_i F_i \sin\theta_i, \quad$ or $\quad \tau_i = R_{\perp i} F_i$ **[1]**

where $R_{\perp i}$ is the perpendicular distance from the axis to line of action of the force.

$$0 = \sum \tau_i = FL - x_{cg}W$$ **[2]**

$$x_{cg}W = FL \le \mu WL$$ **[1]**

$$\mu \ge x_{cg}/L$$ **[1]**

$$\mu \ge 1/3$$ **[1]**

Let $\theta_m$ be the maximum inclination angle for which the triangle will not slide. The parallel and perpendicular components of the forces acting on the triangle are shown in Figure 4-5.

Each correct force component 1 point. **[4]**

The components perpendicular to the plane must sum to zero. $\quad F_N = W\cos\theta_m$ **[1]**

The components parallel to the plane must sum to zero. $\quad \mu F_N = W\sin\theta_m.$ **[1]**

Combining the last two equations: $\quad W\sin\theta_m = \mu W\cos\theta_m$

$$\tan\theta_m = \mu \ge x_{cg}/L$$ **[1]**

We can only be certain that $\theta_m$ is no smaller than $\quad \theta_m = \tan^{-1}\left(x_{cg}/L\right)$ **[1]**

The range we can be certain of has $\theta_m$ as its maximum

$$\theta \le \tan^{-1}\left(x_{cg}/L\right) - \tan^{-1}(1/3) = 0.32\ \text{radians} = 18.4^{\circ}$$ **[3]**

*$\{$Must have inequality for the full 3 points. Only one point for “=” instead of “$\le$”.$\}$*
