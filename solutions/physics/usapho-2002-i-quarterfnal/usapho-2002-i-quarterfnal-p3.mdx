## Условие

An air track glider is fitted with a launcher that fires a projectile with velocity $v_{\mathrm{o}}$ perpendicular to the glider. The launch point LP is marked on the glider, as shown in the diagrams. The glider is placed on a very long air track, which is inclined at an angle $\theta$ to the horizontal. With the air turned on, the system can be considered frictionless. With the air turned off, the coefficient of friction between the track and glider is $\mu$. The projectile is launched at the instant the glider has velocity $v$ down the track and lands a distance $x$ down the track. While the projectile is in the air, the point LP moves a distance $x_{\mathrm{LP}}$. What is $\Delta x = x - x_{\mathrm{LP}}$, if:

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a.** $\Delta x = \frac{2\mu v_o^{\,2}}{g\cos\theta}$
- **b.** $\Delta x = 0$

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**3. a.** Selecting the $x$-axis parallel to the inclined plane and $y$-axis perpendicular to it, the forces acting on the glider are shown as components to the right. Since the glider has no acceleration in the $y$-direction (perpendicular to the plane),

$$
F_N = mg\cos\theta
$$
**[2]**

The acceleration in the $x$-direction can be obtained from Newton’s second law. $\quad \sum F = ma$ **[2]**

$$
ma_x = mg\sin\theta - f_k
$$
**[1]**

Subbing in for friction $\quad f_k = \mu F_N = \mu mg\cos\theta$ **[1]**

$$
a_x = g\sin\theta - \mu g\cos\theta.
$$
**[1]**

The glider has initial velocity $v$ at time $t = 0$. The displacement of the glider and point LP at any time is given by

$$
x_{LP} = vt + \tfrac{1}{2}a_x t^2
$$
**[1]**

$$
x_{LP} = vt + \tfrac{1}{2}(g\sin\theta - \mu g\cos\theta)t^2
$$
**[1]**

The only force acting on the projectile is gravity. The projectile moves in two dimensions both parallel to the plane and perpendicular to it. The projectile has acceleration components

$a_x = g\sin\theta \qquad a_y = -g\cos\theta$ **[2]**

and initial velocity components $\quad v_x = v \qquad v_y = v_o$ **[2]**

Its $x$ and $y$ displacements are given by $\quad x = vt + \tfrac{1}{2}g\sin\theta t^2 \qquad y = v_o t - \tfrac{1}{2}g\cos\theta t^2$ **[2]**

The $x$-displacement of the projectile relative to point LP is

$$
\Delta x = x - x_{LP} = vt + \tfrac{1}{2}g\sin\theta t^2 - \left[vt + \tfrac{1}{2}(g\sin\theta - \mu g\cos\theta)t^2\right] = \tfrac{1}{2}\mu g\cos\theta t^2
$$
**[2]**

When the projectile lands, $\quad y = 0$ **[1]**

$$
0 = v_o t - \tfrac{1}{2}g\cos\theta t^2
$$

Solving for $t$ and assuming $t \ne 0$

$$
t = \frac{2v_o}{g\cos\theta}
$$
**[1]**

$$
\Delta x = \tfrac{1}{2}\mu g\cos\theta\left(\frac{2v_o}{g\cos\theta}\right)^2 = \frac{2\mu v_o^{\,2}}{g\cos\theta}
$$
**[1]**

**b.** If the air is on the system can be assumed to be frictionless, $\mu = 0$. **[1]**

$$
\Delta x = 0
$$
**[4]**

The projectile lands at LP.
