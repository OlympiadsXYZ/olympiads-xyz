We study a fictitious spherical asteroid called *Balkania*. It is initially homogeneous: iron is uniformly dispersed in silicate material. A collision heats it and produces partial melting. Iron droplets may then sink and form a metallic core. The model is deliberately simplified; any additional assumption must be stated clearly.

Subparts marked $\star$ are more difficult. They may be left for a second pass; no later question depends on them unless the required result is supplied again.

**Data for Balkania.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Initial radius | $R = 2.0 \times 10^5\ \mathrm{m}$ |
| Initial iron volume fraction | $\phi = 1/8$ |
| Silicate density | $\rho_s = 3.0 \times 10^3\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ |
| Iron density | $\rho_f = 7.0 \times 10^3\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ |
| Mean specific heat capacity | $c = 1.0 \times 10^3\ \mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$ |
| Initial temperature | $T_0 = 1250\ \mathrm{K}$ |
| Onset-of-melting temperature | $T_s = 1400\ \mathrm{K}$ |
| Effective latent heat of melting | $L = 2.5 \times 10^5\ \mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}}$ |
| Thermal diffusivity | $\kappa = 1.0 \times 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$ |
| Surface emissivity | $\varepsilon = 0.80$ |

**Collision.** The impactor is spherical, has radius $b = 8.0 \times 10^4\ \mathrm{m}$, and has the same composition and mean density as Balkania. Its speed at infinity is $u_\infty = 4.0 \times 10^3\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Balkania is treated as fixed during the approach. The collision is perfectly inelastic: the bodies remain joined. A fraction $\chi = 0.50$ of the kinetic-energy loss during the collision is converted into uniform internal energy of the merged body; mass loss is neglected.

**Iron droplets.** Their radius is $a = 5.0 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m}$. The dynamic viscosity of the molten silicate is $\eta = 20\ \mathrm{Pa\,s}$.

<ProblemSection id="i">

### I. Impact heating and partial melting

**1.** Determine Balkania's mean density $\rho$. Then calculate the iron *mass* fraction.

**2.** Let $M$ be the mass of Balkania and $m$ the mass of the impactor.

**2. a)** Calculate $M$, $m$, and the ratio $\mu = m/M$.

**2. b)** Using conservation of the impactor's mechanical energy during the approach, show that

$$
v_{\mathrm{imp}}^2 = u_\infty^2 + \frac{2GM}{R + b},
$$

where $v_{\mathrm{imp}}$ is its speed just before contact. Calculate $v_{\mathrm{imp}}$ and comment on the importance of gravitational acceleration.

**3.** Let $V$ be the common speed of the two bodies immediately after the collision.

**3. a)** Using conservation of momentum, express $V$. Show that the kinetic-energy loss during the collision is

$$
E_{\mathrm{loss}} = \frac{1}{2}\frac{Mm}{M + m}v_{\mathrm{imp}}^2.
$$

**3. b)** Show that the deposited energy per unit mass of the merged body is

$$
q_{\mathrm{imp}} = \frac{\chi}{2}\frac{\mu}{(1 + \mu)^2}v_{\mathrm{imp}}^2,
$$

and calculate it.

**3. c)** The energy $c(T_s - T_0)$ first heats the body to $T_s$. The remaining energy causes melting at approximately constant temperature. Determine the molten fraction $x$. Iron droplets can move when $x \geq x_c = 0.30$. Does the collision trigger differentiation?

**3. d)** $\star$ Neglecting gravitational acceleration during the approach, determine the minimum impactor radius $b_{\mathrm{min}} < R$ required to reach exactly $x = x_c$.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="ii">

### II. Sinking of iron droplets

In Parts II–IV, to keep the model analytically simple, use Balkania's initial values $R$ and $M$ and neglect the mass increase caused by the impactor. The gravitational field is approximated by that of the initial homogeneous sphere of density $\rho$. You may use the result that a homogeneous spherical shell produces no gravitational field at an interior point.

**4.** At a distance $r \leq R$ from the centre, show that the gravitational field strength is

$$
g(r) = \frac{4\pi G\rho}{3}r.
$$

Calculate the surface acceleration $g(R)$.

**5.** After differentiation, all the iron forms a pure spherical core of radius $R_c$. By conserving the total iron volume, determine $R_c/R$.

**6.** An iron droplet of volume $\mathcal{V}$ is at distance $r$ from the centre.

**6. a)** Taking account of weight and buoyancy, show that the inward resultant force has magnitude

$$
F = (\rho_f - \rho_s)\mathcal{V}g(r).
$$

**6. b)** At low Reynolds number, the Stokes drag force has magnitude $F_D = 6\pi\eta a v$. Assuming terminal speed, show that

$$
v_t(r) = \frac{2(\rho_f - \rho_s)a^2}{9\eta}g(r).
$$

**6. c)** Calculate $v_t(R)$. Check the consistency of the Stokes regime using

$$
\mathrm{Re} = \frac{2\rho_s a v_t}{\eta}.
$$

**7.** $\star$ Over a very short time interval, take the position $r$ and hence $v_t$ as constant. Establish the equation of motion of a droplet released from rest in the form

$$
\frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{v_t - v}{\tau}.
$$

Express and calculate $\tau$. Estimate the distance $v_t(R)\tau$ and justify the terminal-speed assumption used in Question 6.

**8.** Consider a droplet initially at the surface. Since $g(r) \propto r$, write $v_t(r) = kr$.

**8. a)** Obtain an expression for $k$, then calculate the settling time $t_{\mathrm{set}}$ from $R$ to $R_c$.

**8. b)** Deduce the dependence of $t_{\mathrm{set}}$ on $a$ and $\eta$. Determine the minimum droplet radius $a_{100}$ that reaches the core in less than 100 yr, with all other data unchanged.

**9.** $\star$ At the end of differentiation, calculate the field $g_f(R_c)$ just outside the core, neglecting the field of the outer mantle. Compare it with the initial homogeneous field $g_i(R_c)$. Does the calculation of $t_{\mathrm{set}}$ tend to overestimate or underestimate the actual final settling time?

</ProblemSection>

### III. Energy released by differentiation

Assume radial ordering is preserved: the iron initially contained inside a sphere of radius $r$ is found, after differentiation, inside a pure iron sphere of radius $r_f$.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '60.8%', '--fig-max': '389px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/balkanski-2026-cgp/p2-fig3.png" alt="Left: a sphere with a grey shell of radius r („initial state, dispersed iron shell“); arrow labelled $r_f = \phi^{1/3} r = r/2$ to the right: a sphere with a small dark shell of radius r_f inside the core („final state, shell inside the core“). Below: dV_f = φ4πr²dr and the note „Conservation of iron volume imposes the same radial scale factor on every shell.“" width="1217" height="552" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Figure II.3 – Radial mapping of iron shells during differentiation.</figcaption>
</figure>

**10.** Show that $r_f = r/2$.

**11.** For a small iron volume initially at distance $r$, approximate its displacement as a motion from $r$ to $r/2$ in the initial field.

**11. a)** Show that the gravitational work released per unit iron volume is

$$
w(r) = (\rho_f - \rho_s)\int_{r/2}^{r} g(s)\,ds = \frac{\pi}{2}G\rho(\rho_f - \rho_s)r^2.
$$

**11. b)** The iron volume initially contained in the shell $[r, r + dr]$ is $dV_f = \phi 4\pi r^2 dr$. Hence obtain the total released energy,

$$
E_{\mathrm{diff}} = \frac{\pi^2}{20}G\rho(\rho_f - \rho_s)R^5.
$$

**12.** Assume that this energy is fully converted into heat within the asteroid.

**12. a)** Calculate the mean temperature rise $\Delta T_{\mathrm{diff}} = E_{\mathrm{diff}}/(Mc)$. Compare it with the impact heating.

**12. b)** Determine the radius $R_\star$ for which differentiation alone would produce a mean temperature rise of 100 K, with all other data unchanged. Interpret the result.

</ProblemSection>

### IV. Does the asteroid cool too quickly?

**13.** At temperature $T_s$, radiation received from space is neglected.

**13. a)** Calculate the initial radiated power

$$
P_{\mathrm{rad}} = 4\pi R^2\varepsilon\sigma T_s^4.
$$

**13. b)** In an isothermal model, the molten fraction $x$ first solidifies at $T \simeq T_s$. Taking the radiated power as constant and equal to its value in part a), estimate the time $t_{\mathrm{sol}}$ required to remove the latent heat $MxL$.

**13. c)** $\star$ After solidification, write the differential energy balance governing $T(t)$ when the radiated power varies as $T^4$. Using the mathematical hints, calculate the time $t_{\mathrm{sens}}$ required to cool from 1400 K to 1300 K. Estimate $t_{\mathrm{iso}} = t_{\mathrm{sol}} + t_{\mathrm{sens}}$ and compare it with $t_{\mathrm{set}}$.

**14.** In a solid or a liquid without efficient mixing, heat diffuses over a distance of order

$$
\ell \simeq \sqrt{\kappa t}.
$$

**14. a)** Calculate $\ell$ during $t_{\mathrm{set}}$ and compare it with $R$. Also estimate the diffusion time $R^2/\kappa$ across the entire asteroid.

**14. b)** The exact volume fraction in the surface layer of thickness $\ell$ is

$$
f_{\mathrm{layer}} = 1 - \left(1 - \frac{\ell}{R}\right)^3.
$$

Estimate it, then explain how a cold crust can coexist with an interior that remains partially molten. Conclude whether the core can form within the diffusive model.

**14. c)** $\star$ How would very efficient internal convection modify this conclusion? A qualitative discussion is sufficient.

</ProblemSection>

**Constantes communes / Common constants**

$$
G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}},\qquad \sigma = 5.67 \times 10^{-8}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}\,K^{-4}},
$$

$$
1\ \mathrm{an} = 1\ \mathrm{yr} = 3.16 \times 10^{7}\ \mathrm{s},\qquad \ln 2 = 0.693,\qquad 1\ \mathrm{rad} = 57.3^{\circ}.
$$

**Français**

Les relations ci-dessous peuvent être utilisées directement. Elles ne remplacent pas le choix et la justification du modèle physique.

**Problème I – petites variations et optique**

Pour $|\theta| \ll 1\ \mathrm{rad}$ :

$$
\sin\theta \simeq \tan\theta \simeq \theta.
$$

Au premier ordre :

$$
F(z + \Delta z) - F(z) \simeq F'(z)\Delta z,
$$

$$
\Delta\left(\frac{1}{z}\right) \simeq -\frac{\Delta z}{z^2}.
$$

**Problème II – seuil, équations et intégrales**

Pour la question 3d) :

$$
q_c = c(T_s - T_0) + x_c L,
$$

$$
y = \frac{2q_c}{\chi u_\infty^2},\qquad \frac{\mu}{(1+\mu)^2} = y,
$$

$$
y\mu^2 + (2y - 1)\mu + y = 0.
$$

La racine physique vérifie $0 < \mu < 1$.

Si $dv/dt = (v_t - v)/\tau$ et $v(0) = 0$ :

$$
v(t) = v_t\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right).
$$

Si $dr/dt = -kr$ :

$$
\ln(r_2/r_1) = -k(t_2 - t_1).
$$

Intégrales utiles :

$$
\int_{r/2}^{r} s\,ds = \frac{3r^2}{8},\qquad \int_0^R r^4\,dr = \frac{R^5}{5},
$$

$$
\int T^{-4}\,dT = -\frac{1}{3T^3}.
$$

Pour $|z| \ll 1$ : $(1 - z)^3 \simeq 1 - 3z$.

**English**

The relations below may be used directly. They do not replace the choice and justification of a physical model.

**Problem I – small changes and optics**

For $|\theta| \ll 1\ \mathrm{rad}$:

$$
\sin\theta \simeq \tan\theta \simeq \theta.
$$

To first order:

$$
F(z + \Delta z) - F(z) \simeq F'(z)\Delta z,
$$

$$
\Delta\left(\frac{1}{z}\right) \simeq -\frac{\Delta z}{z^2}.
$$

**Problem II – threshold, equations, and integrals**

For Question 3d):

$$
q_c = c(T_s - T_0) + x_c L,
$$

$$
y = \frac{2q_c}{\chi u_\infty^2},\qquad \frac{\mu}{(1+\mu)^2} = y,
$$

$$
y\mu^2 + (2y - 1)\mu + y = 0.
$$

The physical root satisfies $0 < \mu < 1$.

If $dv/dt = (v_t - v)/\tau$ and $v(0) = 0$:

$$
v(t) = v_t\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right).
$$

If $dr/dt = -kr$:

$$
\ln(r_2/r_1) = -k(t_2 - t_1).
$$

Useful integrals:

$$
\int_{r/2}^{r} s\,ds = \frac{3r^2}{8},\qquad \int_0^R r^4\,dr = \frac{R^5}{5},
$$

$$
\int T^{-4}\,dT = -\frac{1}{3T^3}.
$$

For $|z| \ll 1$: $(1 - z)^3 \simeq 1 - 3z$.
