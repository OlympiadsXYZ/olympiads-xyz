## Условие

<nav className="problem-sections-toc" aria-label="Части на задачата">

</nav>

This problem studies a simplified phase-detection autofocus system of the type used in single-lens reflex cameras. The main camera objective is modelled as a thin converging lens $(L)$ with optical centre $O$, object and image foci $F$ and $F'$, and image focal length $f' = \overline{OF'} > 0$.

After unfolding the optical path produced by two redirecting mirrors, the autofocus module can be represented on the same axis as the objective. It contains a field lens $(LC)$ of focal length $f'_c = 12\ \mathrm{mm}$, two separator microlenses $(LS_1)$ and $(LS_2)$ of equal focal length $f'_s = 3\ \mathrm{mm}$, and two linear sensors $(C_1)$ and $(C_2)$. The microlens centres are separated by $2a = 2\ \mathrm{mm}$. Each sensor contains 48 equally spaced photodiodes along a $1\ \mathrm{mm}$ line; use the sampling pitch $d = 1\ \mathrm{mm}/48$.

An electronic unit compares the two recorded intensity profiles. Their relative shift gives the sign and magnitude of the focusing error; a controller then drives a servomotor that translates the focusing group.

<ProblemSection id="i">

### I. Formation of a sharp image

**1.** State the Gaussian conditions and approximations under which the thin lens $(L)$ is used.

**2.** The object is represented by a segment $(AB)$ perpendicular to the optical axis and its image by $(A'B')$. The point $C$ is where the image sensor meets the axis. Define

$$
p = \overline{OA},\qquad p' = \overline{OA'},\qquad x = \overline{CA},\qquad x_0 = \overline{CO},\qquad x' = \overline{CA'}.
$$

Algebraic distances are positive from left to right, in the direction of light propagation; transverse segments are positive upwards and angles are positive counterclockwise.

**2. a)** Starting from the Descartes thin-lens equation

$$
\frac{1}{-p} + \frac{1}{p'} = \frac{1}{f'},
$$

derive the transverse magnification relation connecting $\overline{AB}$, $\overline{A'B'}$, $p$ and $p'$, and hence obtain Newton's conjugation relation connecting $\overline{FA}$, $\overline{F'A'}$ and $f'$.

**2. b)** On the copy of the figure provided on the supplementary sheet, construct $(A'B')$ with a ruler using two principal rays. Check the position of $A'$ using Newton's relation. Is the image formed on the sensor?

**3.** Let $D = |\overline{AC}|$ be the object-to-sensor distance and $T = \overline{OC} = -x_0 > 0$ the lens-to-sensor distance.

**3. a)** Assuming that the objective lies between the object and sensor, obtain the two possible solutions for $T$. Retain the solution $T < D/2$, corresponding to the objective close to the sensor, and show that

$$
T = \frac{D - \sqrt{D^2 - 4Df'}}{2}.
$$

**3. b)** Find the condition on $D$ and $f'$ for these positions to be real. Give a geometrical interpretation of the limiting case.

**3. c)** Determine $T_\infty$ when the object is at infinity.

**3. d)** For $f' = 50\ \mathrm{mm}$, the objective must focus between infinity and $D_{\mathrm{min}} = 40\ \mathrm{cm}$. Calculate the required travel $\delta = T(D_{\mathrm{min}}) - T_\infty$. In which direction must the lens move as the object approaches?

**3. e)** A full-frame sensor of dimensions $36\ \mathrm{mm} \times 24\ \mathrm{mm}$ contains 24 million square pixels. The camera must resolve the $10\ \mathrm{cm}$ width of an object at $2.0\ \mathrm{km}$; require its image to span at least two pixels. Neglecting diffraction and aberrations, determine the minimum focal length. The numerical data in this part are independent of part d).

</ProblemSection>

### II. Study of the autofocus module

After unfolding the optical path, the system is represented below. The point $S$ is where the microlens plane meets the axis and $P$ is where the photosensor plane meets it.

When focus is correct, the intermediate image $(A'B')$ is formed in the plane of the field lens $(LC)$, whose optical centre is $O_c$. The microlenses lie in the image focal plane of $(LC)$, so $O_cS = f'_c$.

**4.** First consider the correctly focused configuration.

**4. a)** The plane containing $(LC)$ is conjugate to the photosensor plane through each microlens. Determine the distance $SP$.

**4. b)** On the corresponding figure on the supplementary sheet, complete the construction of the secondary images $(A''_1B''_1)$ and $(A''_2B''_2)$. The points $A''_1$ and $A''_2$, which are images of the axial point $A'$, lie at the respective centres of $(C_1)$ and $(C_2)$. Compare the orientation of the secondary images with that of $(A'B')$.

**4. c)** Explain the role of the field lens: why does it allow both microlenses to receive bundles from the same point of the intermediate image? Which measured quantity carries the defocus information?

**4. d)** For the axial point $A'$, use the geometry of the figure and the small-angle approximation to determine the two chief-ray angles $\beta_1$ and $\beta_2$ in terms of $a$ and $f'_c$. State their signs, then calculate their magnitude.

**5.** The objective remains fixed and the initially focused object moves by a small distance $\Delta x > 0$ towards the camera. The primary image point then moves by $\Delta x'$ beyond the sensor plane. Use $f' = 50\ \mathrm{mm}$ for numerical applications.

**5. a)** Starting from the thin-lens equation, show to first order that

$$
\Delta x' = \left(\frac{T}{D - T}\right)^2 \Delta x.
$$

For $D = 1.0\ \mathrm{m}$ and $\Delta x = 1.0\ \mathrm{cm}$, first calculate $\Delta x'$ using the exact value of $T$ obtained in Question 3a), then give the estimate obtained from $D \gg T$ and $T \simeq f'$.

**5. b)** $A'$, which lies to the right of $(LC)$, is a virtual object for that lens. If $A'_c$ is its image through $(LC)$, show that

$$
\overline{O_cA'_c} = \frac{f'_c\,\Delta x'}{f'_c + \Delta x'}.
$$

Justify the approximation $\overline{O_cA'_c} \simeq \Delta x'$.

**5. c)** $\star$ Using the approximation from part b), let $s = f'_c - \Delta x'$ be the new object distance for the microlenses. Use their conjugation relation to show that both secondary images undergo the same longitudinal displacement

$$
\Delta x''_1 = \Delta x''_2 = \left(\frac{f'_s}{f'_c - f'_s}\right)^2 \Delta x'.
$$

**5. d)** $\star$ Using the rays through the microlens centres, show to first order that

$$
\Delta\beta_1 = +\frac{a\,\Delta x'}{f'^2_c},\qquad \Delta\beta_2 = -\frac{a\,\Delta x'}{f'^2_c}.
$$

In the fixed photosensor plane, the centre of each profile follows the chief ray. Using the distance $SP$ found in Question 4a), show that

$$
\Delta y''_1 = SP\,\Delta\beta_1 = +\frac{a f'_s}{f'_c(f'_c - f'_s)}\Delta x',\qquad \Delta y''_2 = SP\,\Delta\beta_2 = -\frac{a f'_s}{f'_c(f'_c - f'_s)}\Delta x'.
$$

Calculate $\Delta x''_1$, $\Delta x''_2$, $\Delta y''_1$ and $\Delta y''_2$ numerically.

**5. e)** Describe the relative shift of the two intensity profiles on the sensors and calculate its value. Compare it with the photodiode pitch $d$. Briefly explain why correlation of the complete intensity profiles may nevertheless measure a shift smaller than one pitch. How does the sign of the shift distinguish focus in front of the sensor from focus behind it?

**6.** The objective is now moved so that the image is formed again on the sensor. The algebraic lens displacement $\Delta x_L$ is positive in the direction of light propagation.

**6. a)** What is the sign of $\Delta x_L$ when the object has approached, so that $\Delta x > 0$?

**6. b)** Linearising the thin-lens equation while the object and lens move simultaneously, show that

$$
\Delta x_L = K_L \Delta x,\qquad K_L = -\frac{T^2}{D(D - 2T)}.
$$

For $D = 1.0\ \mathrm{m}$ and $\Delta x = 1.0\ \mathrm{cm}$, first calculate $K_L$ and $\Delta x_L$ using the exact value of $T$, then give the estimate obtained from $D \gg T$ and $T \simeq f'$.

**7.** Describe a complete autofocus loop: acquisition of the two profiles, estimation of their relative shift, determination of the error sign, motor command, and stopping criterion. Also give one situation in which phase-detection autofocus performs poorly.

</ProblemSection>

**Français**

Les relations ci-dessous peuvent être utilisées directement. Elles ne remplacent pas le choix et la justification du modèle physique.

**Problème I – petites variations et optique**

Pour $|\theta| \ll 1\ \mathrm{rad}$ :

$$
\sin\theta \simeq \tan\theta \simeq \theta.
$$

Au premier ordre :

$$
F(z + \Delta z) - F(z) \simeq F'(z)\Delta z,
$$

$$
\Delta\left(\frac{1}{z}\right) \simeq -\frac{\Delta z}{z^2}.
$$

**Problème II – seuil, équations et intégrales**

Pour la question 3d) :

$$
q_c = c(T_s - T_0) + x_c L,
$$

$$
y = \frac{2q_c}{\chi u_\infty^2},\qquad \frac{\mu}{(1+\mu)^2} = y,
$$

$$
y\mu^2 + (2y - 1)\mu + y = 0.
$$

La racine physique vérifie $0 < \mu < 1$.

Si $dv/dt = (v_t - v)/\tau$ et $v(0) = 0$ :

$$
v(t) = v_t\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right).
$$

Si $dr/dt = -kr$ :

$$
\ln(r_2/r_1) = -k(t_2 - t_1).
$$

Intégrales utiles :

$$
\int_{r/2}^{r} s\,ds = \frac{3r^2}{8},\qquad \int_0^R r^4\,dr = \frac{R^5}{5},
$$

$$
\int T^{-4}\,dT = -\frac{1}{3T^3}.
$$

Pour $|z| \ll 1$ : $(1 - z)^3 \simeq 1 - 3z$.
