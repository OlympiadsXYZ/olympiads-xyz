## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи решението от архива">

### (a)

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '35.2%', '--fig-max': '226px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/inpho-2018-question/p3-key-equivalent.png" alt="Whole native equivalent ladder circuit, Req=X, r/R/X, open terminal circles and connecting wires." width="705" height="370" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


Let equivalent resistance be $X$

$$
r+\frac{RX}{R+X}=X
$$

$$
X=\frac{r\pm\sqrt{r^2+4rR}}2
$$

Taking positive sign

$$
X=\frac{r+\sqrt{r^2+4rR}}2
$$

### (b)

From Kirchhoff’s circuit law

$$
I_{n-1}=I_n+I'_n\qquad (1)
$$

Using Kirchhoff’s voltage law

$$
rI_n+RI'_{n+1}-RI'_n=0\qquad (2)
$$

From Eq. (1) we see that

$$
I'_{n+1}-I'_n=(I_n-I_{n+1})-(I_{n-1}-I_n)=2I_n-I_{n+1}-I_{n-1}
$$

So that Eq. (2) becomes

$$
I_{n+1}-\left(2+\frac rR\right)I_n+I_{n-1}=0\qquad (3)
$$

which is the required recursion relation.

### (c)

**First method:**

One can see that $I_{n-1}$, $I_n$, $I_{n+1}$ form geometric progression. Also

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '87.9%', '--fig-max': '563px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/inpho-2018-question/p3-key-geometric-circuits.png" alt="Both whole native I1/I2 geometric-progressions circuit panels and all currents/resistances." width="1759" height="293" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


$$
I_1=I_0\frac R{R+X}\qquad\text{(see the figure to the left)}
$$

$$
I_2=I_1\frac R{R+X}=I_0\left(\frac R{R+X}\right)^2\qquad\text{(see the figure to the right)}
$$

Hence

$$
I_n=I_0\left(\frac R{R+X}\right)^n
$$

For $r=R$,

$$
I_n=I_0\left(\frac{3-\sqrt5}2\right)^n=I_0k^n=I_0\left(\frac{\sqrt5-1}2\right)^{2n}
$$

Using Eq. (2)

$$
I'_n=I_0\left(\frac{\sqrt5-1}2\right)\left(\frac{3-\sqrt5}2\right)^{n-1}=I_0\left(\frac{\sqrt5-1}2\right)^{2n-1}
$$

**Second method:**

For $R=r$, Eq. (3) becomes

$$
I_{n+1}-3I_n+I_{n-1}=0\qquad (4)
$$

We can solve this linear recursion relation by assuming the ansatz $I_n\sim\rho^n$. This leads to

$$
\rho^2-3\rho+1=0
$$

so that we have

$$
\rho=\frac{3\pm\sqrt5}2
$$

This means that the general solution to (4) is

$$
I_n=A\left(\frac{3-\sqrt5}2\right)^n+B\left(\frac{3+\sqrt5}2\right)^n\qquad (5)
$$

The second term grows without bound with increasing $n$ - so that for an infinite ladder we must have $B=0$. Also when $n=0$, $I_0=A$. Thus

$$
I_n=I_0\left(\frac{3-\sqrt5}2\right)^n
$$

$$
I'_n=I_{n-1}-I_n=I_0\left(\frac{\sqrt5-1}2\right)\left(\frac{3-\sqrt5}2\right)^{n-1}
$$

### (d)

**First method:** Consider the end part of the ladder as shown below.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '73.5%', '--fig-max': '471px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/inpho-2018-question/p3-key-end-currents.png" alt="Whole native finite ladder end geometry, ABCDEF, primed/unprimed currents, all arrows/resistors/ellipses." width="1471" height="492" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


Voltage drop across AB $=2RI_N=I'_NR\Rightarrow I'_N=2I_N$ and $I_{N-1}=3I_N$.

Voltage drop across CD $=\frac{5R}3\,3I_N=I'_{N-1}R\Rightarrow I'_{N-1}=5I_N$ and $I_{N-2}=8I_N$.

Voltage drop across EF $=\frac{13R}8\,8I_N=I'_{N-2}R\Rightarrow I'_{N-2}=13I_N$ and $I_{N-3}=21I_N..$

This is depicted in figure below.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '73.5%', '--fig-max': '471px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/inpho-2018-question/p3-key-fibonacci-circuit.png" alt="Whole native finite ladder with Fibonacci current multipliers21/13/8/5/3/2/1 and ABCDEF nodes." width="1471" height="492" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


This looks like the terms of Fibonacci sequence. Fibonacci sequence is 1,1,2,3,5,8,13...etc in which $n$th term of Fibonacci sequence is the sum of previous two terms i.e. $F_n=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2}$, $F_1=F_2=1$. In other words:

$$
I_{N-1}=F_4,\ I_{N-2}=F_6,\ I_{N-3}=F_8,\ I_{N-4}=F_{10}\ldots
$$

In general

$$
\frac{I_{N-r}}{I_N}=F_{2r+2}\qquad (6)
$$

$$
\frac{I_n}{I_N}=F_{2(N-n)+2}\qquad (7)
$$

**Second method:** See Eq. 5. If $I_{N+1}=0$, we must have

$$
A\rho_1^{N+1}+B\rho_2^{N+1}=0
$$

where $\rho_{2,1}=(3\pm\sqrt5)/2$. Thus $B=-A(\rho_1/\rho_2)^{N+1}$ and thus

$$
I_n=A\left(\rho_1^n-\rho_2^n\left(\frac{\rho_1}{\rho_2}\right)^{N+1}\right)
$$

and thus

$$
\frac{I_n}{I_N}=\frac{\rho_1^n-\rho_2^n(\rho_1/\rho_2)^{N+1}}{\rho_1^N-\rho_2^N(\rho_1/\rho_2)^{N+1}}=\frac{\rho_1\rho_2}{\rho_2-\rho_1}\left[\rho_1^{n-N-1}-\rho_2^{n-N-1}\right]
$$

Substituting the values, we get

$$
\frac{I_n}{I_N}=\frac1{\sqrt5}\left[\left(\frac{3+\sqrt5}2\right)^{N+1-n}-\left(\frac{3-\sqrt5}2\right)^{N+1-n}\right]\qquad (8)
$$

Equations (7) and (8) are equivalent.

**Generalization**

if $r\ne R$:

Solution to Eq. (3) is

$$
\rho_{2,1}=\frac{b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4}}2\quad\text{where}\quad b=2+\frac rR
$$

Thus

$$
\frac{I_n}{I_N}=\frac{\rho_1\rho_2}{\rho_2-\rho_1}\left[\rho_1^{n-N-1}-\rho_2^{n-N-1}\right]\qquad (9)
$$

$$
=\frac1{\rho_2-\rho_1}\left[\rho_2^{N+1-n}-\rho_1^{N+1-n}\right]\qquad (10)
$$

Alternate form of Eqs. (7), (8) and (10) are also accepted.

**Source note:** Before Eq. (6), the source equates the currents $I_{N-1},I_{N-2},\ldots$ directly to dimensionless Fibonacci terms without an $I_N$ factor. The subsequent equations explicitly use current ratios. Both printed forms are retained.
