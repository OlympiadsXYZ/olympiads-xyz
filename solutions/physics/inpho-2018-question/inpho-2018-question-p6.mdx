Consider $n$ mole of a non-ideal (realistic) gas. Its equation of state maybe described by the Van der Waals equation

$$
\left(P+\frac{an^2}{V^2}\right)\left(\frac Vn-b\right)=RT
$$

where $a$ and $b$ are positive constants. We take one mole of the gas ($n=1$). You must bear in mind that one is often required to make judicious approximations to understand realistic systems.

### (b) Criticality (6 т.)

The Van der Waals gas exhibits phase transition. A typical isotherm at low temperature is shown in the figure. Here $L$ ($G$) represents the liquid (gas) phase and at $P_{LG}$ there are three possible solutions for the volume ($V_L$, $V_{LG}$, $V_G$). As the temperature is raised, at a certain temperature $T_c$, the three values of the volume merge to a single value, $V_c$ (corresponding pressure being $P_c$). This is called the point of criticality. As the temperature is raised further there exists only one real solution for the volume and the isotherm resembles that of an ideal gas.

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи решението от архива">

### (a) i.

$$
\beta=\frac1V\left(\frac{dV}{dT}\right)_p=\frac{V-b}{VT}\qquad (22)
$$

### (a) ii.

$$
\kappa=-\frac1V\left(\frac{dV}{dP}\right)_T=\frac{(V-b)^2}{VRT}\qquad (23)
$$

### (b) i.

At criticality, the curve is a cubic like function. Hence

$$
\left(\frac{dP}{dV}\right)_T=\left(\frac{d^2P}{dV^2}\right)_T=0
$$

Above condition along with Van der Waal’s equation can be used to obtain

$$
V_C=3b\qquad (24)
$$

$$
T_C=\frac{8a}{27Rb}\qquad (25)
$$

$$
P_C=\frac a{27b^2}\qquad (26)
$$

### (b) ii.

$a=0.37\ \mathrm{m^6\cdot Pa/mol^2}$ and $b=4.33\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{m^3/mol}$.

### (b) iii.

$$
b=N_Ad^3\quad\text{or}\quad N_A4\pi r^3/3
$$

$$
d=1.0\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm m\ \text{to}\ 6.0\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm m
$$

### (c) i.

$$
P_0V_G^2-RTV_G+a=0
$$

$$
V_G=\frac{RT}{2P_0}\left(1\pm\left(1-\frac{4aP_0}{R^2T^2}\right)^{1/2}\right)
$$

Taking positive sign since in the ideal gas limit $a\longrightarrow0$, $V_I=RT/P_0$ for one mole.

$$
V_G\simeq\frac{RT}{P_0}-\frac a{RT}
$$

### (c) ii.

$$
V_I=\frac{RT}{P_0}
$$

### (c) iii.

Comment 1: For ideal gas, $a/RT$ vanishes as $T\to\infty$.

$$
\frac{V_G-V_I}{V_I}=\frac{-aP_0}{(RT)^2}\simeq-5.50\times10^{-3}\ \text{to}\ -7.00\times10^{-3}
$$

Comment 2: Answer is negative, indicating attractive intermolecular forces.

### (d) i.

In this phase $P\ll a/V_L^2$. Hence

$$
\frac a{V_L^2}(V_L-b)=RT
$$

$$
V_L=\frac a{2RT}\left(1\pm\sqrt{1-\frac{4bRT}a}\right)
$$

Taking negative sign since as $T\to0$, $V_L\to b$.

$$
\begin{aligned}V_L&=\frac a{2RT}\left(1-\sqrt{1-\frac{4bRT}a}\right)\\&\simeq b\left(1+\frac{bRT}a\right)\end{aligned}
$$

### (d) ii.

$$
\rho_w\approx\frac{1.80\times10^{-2}}b=3.60\times10^2\ \text{to}\ 6.00\times10^2\ \mathrm{Kg/m^3}\qquad (27)
$$

### (d) iii.

$$
L\times\text{molar mass}=\int_{V_L}^{V_G}\frac a{V^2}\,dV\simeq\frac a{V_L}
$$

$$
L\simeq6.0\times10^5\ \text{to}\ 1.1\times10^6\ \mathrm{J/Kg}
$$

**Source note:** The density and heat-of-vaporization expressions are the approximations printed in the key. Its accepted ranges are retained in full; the source prints capital “Kg” in the two unit labels and upper-case critical subscripts $C$ in Eqs. (24)–(26).


</Spoiler>
