## Условие

A ramp with height $h$ is moving with fixed, uniform speed $v$ to the right. A small block of mass $m$ is placed at the top of the ramp, and is released at rest with respect to the ramp.

The block slides smoothly to the bottom of the ramp and onto the floor. How much kinetic energy does it gain in this process? Neglect friction.

(A) $mgh$

(B) $mgh + mv^2/2$

(C) $mgh + mv\sqrt{2gh}$

(D) $mgh + mv\sqrt{gh} + mv^2/2$

(E) $mgh + mv\sqrt{2gh} + mv^2$

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

In the frame of reference of the ramp, the block starts at rest, and ends up with horizontal velocity $\sqrt{2gh}$ by energy conservation. Thus, in the original frame, it has velocity $v + \sqrt{2gh}$, which means

$$
\Delta K = \frac{1}{2}m((v + \sqrt{2gh})^2 - v^2) = mgh + mv\sqrt{2gh}.
$$

In particular, $mgh$ is not the correct answer because the horizontal motion of the ramp also does work on the block as it slides.


</Spoiler>
