## Условие

An aeroplane flies along a horizontal path, emitting sound at a constant frequency $f_0$. A stationary observer with a detector on the ground directly beneath the flight path records the sound frequency as the plane passes overhead, which is plotted below. The speed of sound in the medium is $c_s=340\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.

**(a)** Your task is to measure the speed of the aeroplane, $v$, from the graph. Express $v$ in terms of quantities measurable from the graph, and define/mark these quantities on the graph reproduced in the Summary Answersheet. Calculate the value of $v$. **[5 т.]**

**(b)** Find the height $h$ of the plane’s flight path. **[5 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**(a)**

Let $\theta$ be the angle between the path of the aeroplane and the line joining the aeroplane and the observer. Using the Doppler formula, the expression for the apparent frequency when the observer is at rest is given by

$$
f'=f_0\frac{c_s}{c_s\mp v\cos\theta},
$$

where $c_s$ is the speed of sound. The negative and positive signs correspond to the source moving towards and away from the observer, respectively.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '56.3%', '--fig-max': '361px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/inpho-2026-question/p4-sol-fig1.png" alt="Native solution geometry: aeroplane velocity va, theta, line to observer O, flight path and h." width="1128" height="712" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


When the aeroplane is far away, $\cos\theta\approx1$. Hence, there will be no significant variation in the frequency heard by the observer. This occurs at $t<10\ \mathrm s$ and $t>28\ \mathrm s$ in the given graph.

A significant change occurs only when $\cos\theta$ varies (from 12–24 s). Let the frequencies shown in the graph be $f_l'$ and $f_r'$ when the aeroplane is at the extreme left and right, respectively, where $\cos\theta\approx1$. Then,

$$
f'=f_0\frac{c_s}{c_s\mp v},
$$

where the $+$ and $-$ signs give $f_r'$ and $f_l'$, respectively. The ratio of the two frequencies is

$$
\frac{f_l'}{f_r'}=\frac{c_s+v}{c_s-v}.
$$

Rearranging, we obtain

$$
v=c_s\frac{f_l'-f_r'}{f_l'+f_r'}.
$$

From the graph,

$$
f_l'\approx1220\ \mathrm{Hz},\qquad f_r'\approx760\ \mathrm{Hz},
$$

which gives $\boxed{v\approx79\ \mathrm{m/s}}$. Accepted range of $v=75$–$80\ \mathrm{m/s}$.

**(b)**

The frequency of the sound emitted by the engine is

$$
f_0=\frac{f_l'(c-v)}{c}\approx937\ \mathrm{Hz}.
$$

From the Doppler formula, this frequency is heard at $\theta=90^{\circ}$. Let $t$ be the observation time and $t'$ be the source emission time. Time $t'$ will include the time delay due to finite distance between the observer and the aeroplane.

$$
\frac{df'}{dt'}=f_0\frac{cv\sin\theta}{(c-v\cos\theta)^2}\frac{d\theta}{dt}.
$$

At $\theta=90^{\circ}$, $dt'=dt$. From the geometry shown below,

$$
\frac{d\theta}{dt}=\frac vh.
$$

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '40.7%', '--fig-max': '261px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/inpho-2026-question/p4-sol-fig2.png" alt="Native differential-angle construction including vdt, dtheta, h and observer." width="815" height="718" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


Thus,

$$
\left.\frac{df'}{dt}\right|_{\theta=90^{\circ}}=f_0\frac{cv}{c^2}\frac vh,
$$

$$
\Rightarrow\quad h=\frac{f_0v^2}{c\left.\dfrac{df'}{dt}\right|_{\theta=90^{\circ}}}.
$$

From the graph, the frequency near $t\approx18\ \mathrm s$ is approximately $940\ \mathrm{Hz}$. A tangent drawn at this point gives the slope.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '90.5%', '--fig-max': '580px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/inpho-2026-question/p4-sol-fig3.png" alt="Complete native frequency-time fit, every point, all ticks/units, whole tangent and construction triangle labelled(14.4,1200)/(20.0,800)." width="1812" height="1058" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


The slope at $\theta=90^{\circ}$ is approximately $-71.4$, which gives $\boxed{h\approx247\ \mathrm m}$. Depending on the slope calculated, an accepted range of $h$ is 185–250 m.

> Source note: the native key switches from $c_s$ to $c$ in (b), prints the derivative and height formula with the signs shown, then quotes a negative fitted slope and a positive height. It describes $t'$ as source emission time while saying that it includes the delay. These printed sign and time conventions are retained.
