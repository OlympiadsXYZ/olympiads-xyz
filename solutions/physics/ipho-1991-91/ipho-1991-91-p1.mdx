The figure 1.1 shows a solid, homogeneous ball radius $R$. Before falling to the floor its center of mass is at rest, but the ball is spinning with angular velocity $\omega_0$ about a horizontal axis through its center. The lowest point of the ball is at a height $h$ above the floor.

A spinning ball with angular velocity vector ω0 hangs at height h above the floor; after the bounce it leaves along a dashed line at angle θ to the vertical, follows a parabola reaching height αh, and falls again further along the floor." width="1592" height="1039" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Figure 1.1</figcaption>
</figure>

When released, the ball falls under gravity, and rebounds to a new height such that its lowest point is now $ah$ above the floor. The deformation of the ball and the floor on impact may be considered negligible. Ignore the presence of the air. The impact time, although, is finite.

The mass of the ball is $m$, the acceleration due the gravity is $g$, the dynamic coefficient of friction between the ball and the floor is $\mu_k$, and the moment of inertia of the ball about the given axis is:

$$
I = \frac{2mR^2}{5}
$$

You are required to consider two situations, in the first, the ball slips during the entire impact time, and in the second the slipping stops before the end of the impact time.

<ProblemSection id="situation-i">

### Situation I: slipping throughout the impact. (5,5 т.)

Find:

**a)** $\tan\theta$ , where $\theta$ is the rebound angle indicated in the diagram; **[2 т.]**

**b)** the horizontal distance traveled in flight between the first and second impacts; **[1,5 т.]**

**c)** the minimum value of $\omega_0$ for this situations. **[2 т.]**

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="situation-ii">

### Situation II: slipping for part of the impacts. (3,5 т.)

Find, again:

**a)** $\tan\theta$; **[2 т.]**

**b)** the horizontal distance traveled in flight between the first and second impacts. **[1,5 т.]**

</ProblemSection>

Taking both of the above situations into account, sketch the variation of $\tan\theta$ with $\omega_0$.

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **Situation I: slipping throughout the impact., a)** $\tan\theta = \mu_k\frac{(1+c)}{c}$

  c = √α is the restitution coefficient (official solution, eq. 18).
- **Situation I: slipping throughout the impact., b)** $d_I = 4\mu_k(1+c)\,ch$

  Official solution, eq. 22; c = √α.
- **Situation I: slipping throughout the impact., c)** $\omega_{0\,min} = \frac{7\mu_k(1+c)\sqrt{2gh}}{2R}$

  Official solution, eq. 20; c = √α.
- **Situation II: slipping for part of the impacts., a)** $\tan\theta = \frac{2\omega_0 R}{7c\sqrt{2gh}}$

  Official solution, eq. 19; c = √α.
- **Situation II: slipping for part of the impacts., b)** $d_{II} = \frac{4}{7}c\sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}}R\omega_0$

  Official solution; c = √α.

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

a) *Calculation of the velocity at the instant before impact*

Equating the potential gravitational energy to the kinetic energy at the instant before impact we can arrive at the pre-impact velocity $v_0$:

$$
mgh = \frac{mv_0^2}{2} \qquad (1)
$$

from which we may solve for $v_0$ as follows:

$$
V_0 = \sqrt{2gh} \qquad (2)
$$

b) *Calculation of the vertical component of the velocity at the instant after impact*

Let $v_{2x}$ and $v_{2y}$ be the horizontal and vertical components, respectively, of the velocity of the mass center an instant after impact. The height attained in the vertical direction will be $\alpha h$ and then:

$$
v_{2y}^2 = 2g\alpha h \qquad (3)
$$

from which, in terms of $\alpha$ (or the restitution coefficient $c = \sqrt{\alpha}$ ):

$$
v_{2y} = \sqrt{2g\alpha h} = cv_0 \qquad (4)
$$

c) *General equations for the variations of linear and angular momenta in the time interval of the Impact*

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '31.5%', '--fig-max': '202px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/ipho-1991-91/p1-sol-fig1.png" alt="Free-body diagram of the ball during impact: axes x (right) and y (down); a circle with upward force N (N ≫ mg) at the centre, weight mg downward and friction force fr along the floor to the right." width="631" height="358" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Figure 1.2 shows the free body of the ball during impact</figcaption>
</figure>

Considering that the linear impulse of the forces is equal to the variation of the linear momentum and that the angular impulse of the torques is equal to the variation of the angular momentum, we have:

$$
I_y = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} N(t)\,dt = mv_0 + mv_{2y} = m(1+c)\sqrt{2gh} \qquad (5)
$$

$$
I_x = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} f_r(t)\,dt = mv_{2x} \qquad (6)
$$

$$
I_\theta = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} Rf_r(t)\,dt = R\int_{t_1}^{t_2} f_r(t)\,dt = \mathrm{I}(\omega_o - \omega_2) \qquad (7)
$$

Where $I_x$, $I_y$ and $I_\theta$ are the linear and angular impulses of the acting forces and $\omega_2$ is the angular velocity after impact. The times $t_1$ and $t_2$ correspond to the beginning and end of impact.

*Variants*

At the beginning of the impact the ball will always be sliding because it has a certain angular velocity $\omega_0$. There are, then, two possibilities:

I. The entire impact takes place without the friction being able to spin the ball enough for it to stop at the contact point and go into pure rolling motion.

II. For a certain time $t \in (t_1, t_2)$, the point that comes into contact with the floor has a velocity equal to zero and from that moment the friction is zero. Let us look at each case independently.

Case I

In this variant, during the entire moment of impact, the ball is sliding and the friction relates to the normal force as:

$$
f_r = \mu_k N(t) \qquad (8)
$$

Substituting (8) in relations (6) and (7), and using (5), we find that:

$$
\mathrm{I}_x = \mu_k \int_{t_1}^{t_2} N(t)\,dt = \mu_k \mathrm{I}_y = \mu_k(1+c)\sqrt{2gh} = mv_{2x} \qquad (9)
$$

and:

$$
\mathrm{I}_\theta = \mathrm{R}\,\mu_k \int_{t_1}^{t_1} N(t)\,dt = \mathrm{R}\,\mu_k m(1+c)\sqrt{2gh} = \mathrm{I}(\mu_0 - \mu_2) \qquad (10)
$$

which can give us the horizontal component of the velocity $v_{2x}$ and the final angular velocity in the form:

$$
V_{2x} = \mu_k(1+c)\sqrt{2gh} \qquad (11)
$$

$$
\omega_2 = \omega_0 - \frac{\mu_k mR(1+c)}{I}\sqrt{2gh} \qquad (12)
$$

With this we have all the basic magnitudes in terms of data. The range of validity of the solution under consideration may be obtained from (11) and (12). This solution will be valid whenever at the end of the impact the contact point has a velocity in the direction of the negative $x$. That is, if:

$$
\omega_2\mathrm{R} > v_{2x}
$$

$$
\omega_0 - \frac{\mu_k mR(1+c)}{I}\sqrt{2gh} > \frac{\mu_k(1+c)}{R}\sqrt{2gh}
$$

$$
\omega_0 > \frac{\mu_k\sqrt{2gh}}{R}(1+c)\left(\frac{mR^2}{I}+1\right) \qquad (13)
$$

so, for angular velocities below this value, the solution is not valid.

Case II

In this case, rolling is attained for a time $t$ between the initial time $t_1$ and the final time $t_2$ of the impact. Then the following relationship should exist between the horizontal component of the velocity $v_{2x}$ and the final angular velocity:

$$
\omega_2\mathrm{R} = v_{2x} \qquad (14)
$$

Substituting (14) and (6) in (7), we get that:

$$
m\mathrm{R}v_{2x} = \mathrm{I}\left(\omega_0 - \frac{v_{2x}}{R}\right) \qquad (15)
$$

which can be solved for the final values:

$$
V_{2x} = \frac{I\omega_0}{mR + \frac{I}{R}} = \frac{I\omega_0 R}{mR^2 + I} = \frac{2}{7}\omega_0\mathrm{R} \qquad (16)
$$

and:

$$
\omega_2 = \frac{I\omega_0}{mR^2 + I} = \frac{2}{7}\omega_0 \qquad (17)
$$

*Calculation of the tangents of the angles*

Case I

For $\tan\theta$ we have, from (4) and (11), that:

$$
\tan\theta = \frac{v_{2x}}{v_{2y}} = \frac{\mu_k(1+c)\sqrt{2gh}}{c\sqrt{2gh}} = \mu_k\frac{(1+c)}{c}
$$

$$
\tan\theta = \mu_k\frac{(1+c)}{c} \qquad (18)
$$

i.e., the angle is independent of $\omega_0$.

Case II

Here (4) and (16) determine for $\tan\theta$ that:

$$
\tan\theta = \frac{v_{2x}}{v_{2y}} = \frac{I\omega_0 R}{I + mR^2}\,\frac{1}{c\sqrt{2gh}} = \frac{I\omega_0 R}{(I + mR^2)c\sqrt{2gh}}
$$

$$
\tan\theta = \frac{2\omega_0 R}{7c\sqrt{2gh}} \qquad (19)
$$

then (18) and (19) give the solution (fig. 1.3).

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '58%', '--fig-max': '372px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/ipho-1991-91/p1-sol-fig2.png" alt="Graph of tan θ against ω0: a straight line rising from the origin up to ω0 min (marked with a dashed vertical line), then a horizontal line for larger ω0." width="1161" height="835" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Figure 1.3</figcaption>
</figure>

We see that $\theta$ does not depend on $\omega_o$ if $\omega_0 > \omega_{0\,min}$; where $\omega_{0\,min}$ is given as:

$$
\omega_{o\,min} = \frac{\mu_k(1+c)\sqrt{2gh}\left(1 + \frac{mR^2}{I}\right)}{R}
$$

$$
\omega_{o\,min} = \frac{7\mu_k(1+c)\sqrt{2gh}}{2R} \qquad (20)
$$

*Calculation of the distance to the second point of impact*

Case I

The rising and falling time of the ball is:

$$
\mathrm{t}_v = 2\frac{v_{2y}}{g} = \frac{2c\sqrt{2gh}}{g} = 2c\sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}} \qquad (21)
$$

The distance to be found, then, is;

$$
d_I = v_{2x}t_v = \mu_k(1+c)\sqrt{2gh}\,2c\sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}}
$$

$$
d_I = 4\mu_k(1+c)\,ch \qquad (22)
$$

which is independent of $\omega_0$.

Case II

In this case, the rising and falling time of the ball will be the one given in (21). Thus the distance we are trying to find may be calculated by multiplying $t_v$ by the velocity $v_{2x}$ so that:

$$
d_{II} = v_{2x}t_v = \frac{I\omega_0}{mR^2 + I}2c\sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}} = \frac{2\omega_0 Rc}{1 + \frac{5}{2}}\sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}}
$$

$$
d_{II} = \frac{4}{7}c\sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}}R\omega_0
$$

Thus, the distance to the second point of impact of the ball increases linearly with $\omega_0$.
