## Условие

A cyclotron is a particle accelerator consisting of dipole magnets that create two semi-circular regions of magnetic field $B$, pointing uniformly down (toward the bottom of the page). These regions are called “Dees” due to their D shape. The two Dees are placed back-to-back, but with a small separation. Consider a small cyclotron of radius $R$. An AC potential $V=V_0\sin\omega t$, that is switched on at $t=0$, is applied across the Dee electrodes. The particles are accelerated whenever they cross the gap between the electrodes. The gap is small enough that any variation in $V(t)$ during the time it takes a particle to cross the gap may be neglected.

The cyclotron is filled with a variety of ions of different masses, but each has the same charge $q$. All ions start at the center of the cyclotron and are emitted when they reach the edge of the cyclotron. An ion will make many laps around the cyclotron before being emitted. Ignore relativistic effects in parts (a)–(c).

In terms of $\omega$, $q$, $V_0$, $R$, and $B$,

**a.** What is the maximum possible momentum of an emitted ion? **[5 т.]**

**b.** Of all of the possible ions that satisfy part (a), what is the kinetic energy of the ion emitted first? **[7 т.]**

**c.** At what time $t$ after turning on the AC potential are the ions from part (b) emitted? **[6 т.]**

**d.** Such a device won’t work well with electrons, as relativistic effects can’t be ignored for energies above one hundred thousand electron-volts. One possible correction is to create a magnetic field that is not constant, but varies with distance from the center of the cyclotron. This enhanced device is called an isochronous cyclotron. Find an expression for the necessary magnetic field $B$ in terms of $r$, $\omega$, $m$ (the mass of the electron), and $q$ (the charge on the electron). Relativistic effects must be considered. **[7 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-a">

### a. (5 т.)

The ions move, for the most part, in uniform circular motion under the influence of a magnetic force, so

$$
\frac{mv^2}{r}=qvB,\qquad (A4-1)
$$

or

$$
mv=qBr.\qquad (A4-2)
$$

When $r=R$ we have the maximum possible momentum, so

$$
p_{\max}=qBR.\qquad (A4-3)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-b">

### b. (7 т.)

Since

$$
K=\frac{p^2}{2m},\qquad (A4-4)
$$

we have

$$
K=\frac{q^2B^2R^2}{2m}.\qquad (A4-5)
$$

But

$$
m=qB\frac Rv=\frac{qB}{\omega},\qquad (A4-6)
$$

so

$$
K=\frac{qBR^2\omega}{2}.\qquad (A4-7)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-c">

### c. (6 т.)

The ions pick up this kinetic energy from the changing potential, collecting $2qV_0$ after every revolution. The number of revolutions required is then

$$
N=\frac{BR^2\omega}{4V_0},\qquad (A4-8)
$$

and since each revolution requires a time $2\pi/\omega$, the total time $t$ is given by

$$
t=\frac{\pi BR^2}{2V_0}.\qquad (A4-9)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-d">

### d. (7 т.)

The cyclotron works only because the revolution of the charged particle is constant in time. $p=qBr$ is relativistically correct, but now the velocity is given by the condition that $p=\gamma mv$. The angular frequency of revolution needs to be $\omega$ in order to get a boost each half-cycle, so

$$
v=r\omega.\qquad (A4-10)
$$

Then

$$
p=m\frac v{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}=\frac{mr\omega}{\sqrt{1-r^2\omega^2/c^2}}.\qquad (A4-11)
$$

Putting this into the momentum expression yields

$$
B=\frac{m\omega}{q}\left(1-\frac{r^2\omega^2}{c^2}\right)^{-1/2}.\qquad (A4-12)
$$

</ProblemSection>


</Spoiler>
