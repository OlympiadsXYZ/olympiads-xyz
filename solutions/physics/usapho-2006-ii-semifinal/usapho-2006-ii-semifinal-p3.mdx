## Условие

A hollow vertical cylinder contains a solid cylindrical piston with length $h$. The cylindrical piston is originally held in place by a retaining pin so that a fixed amount of gas at temperature $T_0$ and pressure $p_0$ is trapped beneath the piston. $T_0$ and $p_0$ are also the temperature and pressure of the gas outside the cylinder. The bottom of the piston is originally a height $y_0$ above the bottom of the cylinder.

When the retaining pin is removed, the piston falls under the influence of gravity, compressing the gas so that the piston drops to $y_1=y_0/8$ before bouncing back up. This process occurs so quickly that no heat enters or leaves the gas.

The piston bounces up and down, but eventually comes to rest at a height $y_2$ when the gas in the cylinder is once again in thermal equilibrium with the surroundings. The specific heat capacity at constant volume of the gas in the cylinder is $C_V=\frac32R$.

**a.** Find the density of the piston in terms of $p_0$, $g$, and $h$. **[12 т.]**

**b.** Find the numerical value of the ratio $y_2/y_0$. **[13 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-a">

### a. (12 т.)

First find $\gamma$:

$$
\gamma=\frac{C_P}{C_V}=\frac{C_V+R}{C_V}=\frac{\frac32+1}{\frac32}=\frac53.\qquad (A3-1)
$$

Fast processes are adiabatic processes, especially when we are told that no heat exchange occurs! This means that $pV^\gamma$ is a constant, and then

$$
p_0V_0^\gamma=p_1V_1^\gamma.\qquad (A3-2)
$$

But

$$
pV=nRT,\qquad (A3-3)
$$

so

$$
pV^\gamma=\frac{nRT}{V}V^\gamma=nRTV^{\gamma-1}\qquad (A3-4)
$$

is a constant. Finally, we can write

$$
T_0V_0^{\gamma-1}=T_1V_1^{\gamma-1}.\qquad (A3-5)
$$

Work is done on the gas by the falling cylinder and the external atmosphere. Hence,

$$
W=mg(y_0-y_1)+p_0(V_0-V_1),\qquad (A3-6)
$$

where $m$ is the mass of the piston. But the cross sections, $A$, are uniform, so we can write $V=Ay$ and then the work done is

$$
W=(mg+p_0A)(y_0-y_1).\qquad (A3-7)
$$

This work must equal the change in internal energy of the gas,

$$
W=\Delta U=nC_V\Delta T=nC_VT_0\left(\frac{T_1}{T_0}-1\right).\qquad (A3-8)
$$

But

$$
\frac{T_1}{T_0}=\left(\frac{V_0}{V_1}\right)^{\gamma-1},\qquad (A3-9)
$$

and

$$
nC_VT_0=\frac32nRT_0=\frac32p_0V_0,\qquad (A3-10)
$$

so

$$
(mg+p_0A)(y_0-y_1)=\frac32p_0Ay_0\left(\left(\frac{y_0}{y_1}\right)^{\gamma-1}-1\right),\qquad (A3-11)
$$

or

$$
(mg+p_0A)\left(1-\frac{y_1}{y_0}\right)=\frac32p_0A\left(\left(\frac{y_0}{y_1}\right)^{\gamma-1}-1\right).\qquad (A3-12)
$$

But $y_0=8y_1$, so

$$
(mg+p_0A)\left(\frac78\right)=\frac32p_0A(3).\qquad (A3-13)
$$

Solve for $m$, and

$$
m=\frac{29}{7}\frac{p_0A}{g}.\qquad (A3-14)
$$

We actually want the density, so

$$
\rho=\frac{m}{Ah}=\frac{29}{7}\frac{p_0}{gh}.\qquad (A3-15)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-b">

### b. (13 т.)

Eventually the gas temperature returns to $T_0$ and then

$$
p_0V_0=p_2V_2.\qquad (A3-16)
$$

The pressure will rise to the point that it can support the cylinder,

$$
mg=A(p_2-p_0),\qquad (A3-17)
$$

so

$$
mg=Ap_0\left(\frac{V_0}{V_2}-1\right).\qquad (A3-18)
$$

Rewriting in terms of the ratio $y_0/y_2$,

$$
\frac{y_0}{y_2}=\frac{mg}{p_0A}+1\qquad (A3-19)
$$

$$
=\frac{29}{7}+1,\qquad (A3-20)
$$

$$
=\frac{36}{7},\qquad (A3-21)
$$

and then

$$
\frac{y_2}{y_0}=\frac7{36}.\qquad (A3-22)
$$

</ProblemSection>
