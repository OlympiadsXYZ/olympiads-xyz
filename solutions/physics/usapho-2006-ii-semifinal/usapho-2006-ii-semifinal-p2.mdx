## Условие

A parallel plate capacitor is constructed from two vertical square conducting surfaces with sides of length $L$ separated horizontally by a distance $2d$. Assume that $d\ll L$, so that fringing effects can be neglected. The plates are charged to $Q$ and $-Q$, and then electrically isolated. A square conducting slab, also with sides of length $L$, but with thickness $d$, is then inserted vertically between the two plates without making contact with either plate. Assume that the slab moves only in the vertical direction. Give your answers to each part only in terms of the variables listed above and any appropriate fundamental constants.

**a.** Find an expression for the capacitance $C$ of the system assuming that the movable slab extends inward between the plates by a distance $y$. **[7 т.]**

**b.** Find an expression for the mass of the slab $m$ given that the equilibrium location (where the force of gravity on the slab is balanced by the attraction into the capacitor) for the slab is $y=L/2$. **[9 т.]**

**c.** The slab is perturbed vertically slightly from its equilibrium position so that it undergoes simple harmonic motion. Find an expression for the frequency of the motion of the slab. **[9 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-a">

### a. (7 т.)

Treat the system as two capacitors in parallel. One has a spacing of $2d$ and a surface area of $L(L-y)$, the other a spacing of $2d-d$ and a surface area of $Ly$. The capacitance is then found from the formula $\epsilon_0A/d$ and the rule for capacitors in parallel to be

$$
C=\frac{\epsilon_0L(L-y)}{2d}+\frac{\epsilon_0Ly}{d}=\frac{\epsilon_0L(L+y)}{2d}.\qquad (A2-1)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-b">

### b. (9 т.)

The potential energy stored in the capacitor is given by

$$
U=\frac12\frac{Q^2}{C},\qquad (A2-2)
$$

so the electric force of attraction on the slab is

$$
F=-\frac{\partial U}{\partial y}\qquad (A2-3)
$$

$$
=\frac{Q^2}{2C^2}\frac{\partial C}{\partial y}\qquad (A2-4)
$$

$$
=\frac{dQ^2}{\epsilon_0L(L+y)^2}.\qquad (A2-5)
$$

But this is equal to the force from gravity, $mg$, when $y=L/2$, so

$$
m=\frac{dQ^2}{\epsilon_0gL(L+L/2)^2}=\frac{4dQ^2}{9\epsilon_0gL^3}.\qquad (A2-6)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-c">

### c. (9 т.)

We need to expand the expression for the force about the point $y=L/2$. Letting $y=L/2+x$, we get

$$
F=\frac{dQ^2}{\epsilon_0L(3L/2+x)^2}\qquad (A2-7)
$$

$$
=\frac{4dQ^2}{9\epsilon_0L^3}\left(1+\frac{2x}{3L}\right)^{-2}\qquad (A2-8)
$$

$$
\approx\frac{4dQ^2}{9\epsilon_0L^3}\left(1-2\frac{2x}{3L}\right)\qquad (A2-9)
$$

$$
=\frac{4dQ^2}{9\epsilon_0L^3}-\frac{16dQ^2}{27\epsilon_0L^4}x.\qquad (A2-10)
$$

We know the first term in the last line balances gravity; it is the second term that is of interest. It is useful to write it in terms of the mass,

$$
\Delta F=-\frac{16dQ^2}{27\epsilon_0L^4}x=-\frac{4mg}{3L}x,\qquad (A2-11)
$$

so that the effective spring constant $k$ is given by

$$
k=\frac{4mg}{3L}.\qquad (A2-12)
$$

The frequency of small oscillations is then

$$
f=\frac1{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac km}=\frac1\pi\sqrt{\frac g{3L}}.\qquad (A2-13)
$$

</ProblemSection>


</Spoiler>
