## Условие

Four square metal plates of area $A$ are arranged at an even spacing $d$ as shown in the diagram. (Assume that $A \gg d^2$.)

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '31.5%', '--fig-max': '202px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-2008-ii-olympiad-semi-final/p1-fig1.png" alt="Four parallel square plates drawn in perspective, stacked one above another, labelled Plate 1 (top) to Plate 4 (bottom); arrows on the left mark the equal spacings d between neighbouring plates." width="631" height="344" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

Plates 1 and 4 are first connected to a voltage source of magnitude $V_0$, with plate 1 positive; plates 2 and 3 are then connected together with a wire. The wire is subsequently removed. Finally, the voltage source attached between plates 1 and 4 is replaced with a wire. The steps are summarized in the diagrams below.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '90.9%', '--fig-max': '582px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-2008-ii-olympiad-semi-final/p1-fig2.png" alt="Three side views of the four plates labelled Step 1, Step 2 and Step 3: in Step 1 a battery connects plates 1 and 4 and a wire joins plates 2 and 3; in Step 2 only the battery remains; in Step 3 the battery is replaced by a wire joining plates 1 and 4." width="1819" height="282" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

Find the resulting potential difference $\Delta V_{12}$ between plates 1 and 2; like wise find $\Delta V_{23}$ and $\Delta V_{34}$, defined similarly.

Assume, in each case, that a positive potential difference means that the top plate is at a higher potential than the bottom plate.

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- $\Delta V_{12} = \Delta V_{34} = V_0/6,\quad \Delta V_{23} = -\tfrac{1}{3}V_0$

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

We treat the plates as three capacitors in series. Each has an identical capacitance $C$. The figure below then show the three steps.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '91.2%', '--fig-max': '584px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-2008-ii-olympiad-semi-final/p1-sol-fig1.png" alt="Three circuit diagrams of capacitors C1, C2, C3 in series: left, a battery across the chain with C2 shorted by a wire; middle, C2 still shorted but no battery; right, the top of C1 joined to the bottom of C3 by a wire." width="1824" height="443" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

Since $C_2$ is shorted out originally, then effectively there are only two capacitors in series, so the voltage drop across each is $V_0/2$, where the a positive potential difference means that the top plate of any given capacitor is positive. The top plate of $C_1$ will then have a positive charge of $q_0 = CV_0/2$. Note that this means that the bottom plate of the top capacitor will have a negative charge of $-q_0$. Removing the shorting wire across $C_2$ will *not* change the charges or potential drops across the other two capacitors. Removing the source $V_0$ will also make no difference.

Shorting the top plate of $C_1$ with the bottom plate of $C_3$ *will* make a difference. Positive charge will flow out of top plate of $C_1$ into the bottom plate of $C_3$. Also, negative charge will flow out of the bottom plate of $C_1$ into the top plate of $C_2$. The result is that $C_1$ will acquire a potential difference of $V_1$, $C_2$ a potential difference of $V_2$, and $C_3$ a potential difference of $V_3$. Let the final charge on the *top* plate of each capacitor also be labeled as $q_1$, $q_2$, and $q_3$.

The last figure implies that

$$
V_1 + V_2 + V_3 = 0.
$$

By symmetry, we have

$$
V_1 = V_3.
$$

so

$$
2V_1 = -V_2.
$$

By charge conservation between the bottom plate of $C_1$ and the top plate of $C_2$ we have

$$
-q_0 = -q_1 + q_2.
$$

But $q = CV$, so

$$
-\frac{1}{2}V_0 = -V_1 + V_2
$$

Combining the above we get

$$
\begin{aligned} -\frac{1}{2}V_0 &= \frac{1}{2}V_2 + V_2, \\ -\frac{1}{3}V_0 &= V_2. \end{aligned}
$$

Finally, solving for $V_1$, we get $V_1 = V_0/6$.

Alternatively, we could focus on the plate arrangement and the fact that across a boundary $|\Delta E_\perp| = |\sigma/\epsilon_0|$, a consequence of Gauss's Law. Also, we have, for parallel plate configurations, $|\Delta V| = |Ed|$. Since $\epsilon_0$ and $d$ are the same for each of the three regions, it is sufficient to simply look at the electric fields.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '90.8%', '--fig-max': '581px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-2008-ii-olympiad-semi-final/p1-sol-fig2.png" alt="Three sketches of four horizontal plates with field arrows: left, downward fields E0 in the top and bottom gaps with the middle plates connected; middle, downward fields in the top and bottom gaps; right, downward fields E1 in the top and bottom gaps and upward field E2 in the middle gap, with the outer plates connected." width="1816" height="542" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

In the first picture we require that $2E_0 = V_0/d$. The charge density on the second plate requires that $\Delta E = E_0$. In the last picture we have $2E_1 + E_2 = 0$, since the potential between the top plate and the bottom plate is zero. But we also have, on the second plate, $\Delta E = E_1 - E_2$. Combining, $E_0 = -\frac{1}{2}E_2 - E_2 = -\frac{3}{2}E_2$, and therefore $V_2 = -\frac{1}{3}V_0$, and $V_1 = V_0/6$.


</Spoiler>
