*EIGHTH BALKAN PHYSICS OLYMPIAD (BPO-8) · 29 юни 2026 г. · 25 т.*

## Условие

The Istanbul Bosphorus Cross-Continental Swimming Race is an international open-water event in which swimmers cross from the Asian side of Istanbul to the European side. In this problem, the Bosphorus current is not neglected.

A simplified race course is modeled as a straight crossing of width

$$
D = 1450\ \mathrm{m}
$$

from point $A$ on the Asian shore to point $B$ on the European shore. The current flows parallel to the shores, from north to south, with constant speed $u = 0{,}65\ \mathrm{m/s}$. A swimmer swims with the constant speed $\mathrm{v} = 1{,}25\ \mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the water.

The swimmer wants to reach exactly point $B$, directly opposite to point $A$. Therefore, the swimmer must aim upstream at an angle $\theta$ with respect to the straight line $AB$.

**A)** Find the angle $\theta$ at which the swimmer must aim relative to the line $AB$ in order to arrive exactly at point $B$. **[6 т.]**

**B)** Determine the time required for the swimmer to cross the Bosphorus and reach point $B$. **[6 т.]**

**C)** A second swimmer ignores the current and swims directly toward point $B$. Determine the time needed to reach the European shore, as well as the downstream displacement from point $B$. **[6 т.]**

**D)** During a training session, three swimmers cross the same route. Their measured crossing times and downstream displacements are given below.

| Swimmer | Crossing time $t$ (s) | Downstream displacement $x$ (m) |
|---|---:|---:|
| S1 | 1320 | 0 |
| S2 | 1160 | 270 |
| S3 | 1450 | -110 |

Here positive $x$ means downstream from $B$, and negative $x$ means upstream from $B$.

For each swimmer, determine the average ground-velocity components

$$
v'_x = \frac{x}{t}, \qquad v'_y = \frac{D}{t},
$$

and decide which swimmer made the best current correction. **[7 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **A)** 31.3 °
- **B)** 1358 s
- **C)** t2 = 1160 s; 754 m downstream from point B
- **D)** S1

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

Choose the positive $x$-direction along the straight crossing from the Asian shore toward the European shore. Choose the positive $y$-direction upstream. Therefore the water current is

$$
\vec{u} = (0, -u), \qquad u = 0.65\ \mathrm{ms^{-1}}.
$$

The swimmer’s speed relative to the water is constant:

$$
v = 1.25\ \mathrm{ms^{-1}}.
$$

The width of the crossing is

$$
D = 1450\ \mathrm{m}.
$$

(a) Angle required to land exactly at point $B$ (6 points)

To land exactly at $B$, the swimmer must aim upstream so that the upstream component of his velocity relative to the water cancels the downstream current.

If the swimmer aims at angle $\theta$ above the line $AB$, the velocity components relative to the water are

$$
v_x = v\cos\theta,
$$

(1.5p) $$v_y = v\sin\theta.$$

The ground-velocity component in the $y$-direction is therefore

$$
v_{y,\mathrm{ground}} = v\sin\theta - u.
$$

For exact arrival at $B$,

(1.5p) $$v_{y,\mathrm{ground}} = 0.$$

Thus

$$
v\sin\theta - u = 0
$$

(1.5p) $$v\sin\theta = u,$$

(0.5p) $$\sin\theta = \frac{u}{v} = \frac{0.65}{1.25} = 0.52.$$

Therefore

(1.0p) $$\theta = \sin^{-1}(0.52) \approx 31.3^{\circ}.$$

Hence the swimmer must aim approximately

$$
\boxed{\theta \approx 31.3^{\circ}\ \text{upstream.}}
$$

(b) Time required to cross the Bosphorus (6 points)

The forward speed across the Bosphorus is the $x$-component of the ground velocity:

(2.0p) $$v_{x,\mathrm{ground}} = v\cos\theta.$$

Since

(1.0p) $$\cos\theta = \sqrt{1 - \sin^2\theta} = \sqrt{1 - (0.52)^2} = \sqrt{0.7296} \approx 0.8542,$$

we get

$$
v_{x,\mathrm{ground}} = 1.25(0.8542) \approx 1.068\ \mathrm{m/s}.
$$

The crossing time is

(2.0p) $$t = \frac{D}{v_{x,\mathrm{ground}}} = \frac{1450}{1.068} \approx 1.36 \cdot 10^3\ \mathrm{s}.$$

Thus

(1.0p) $$t \approx 1358\ \mathrm{s} \approx 22.6\ \mathrm{min}.$$

(c) Second swimmer who aims directly toward $B$

Now the swimmer does not correct for the current. Therefore the swimmer’s velocity relative to the water is completely in the $x$-direction:

$$
v_x = v = 1.25\ \mathrm{m/s}.
$$

The current gives the whole $y$-component of the ground velocity:

$$
v_y = -u = -0.65\ \mathrm{m/s}.
$$

*Time to reach the European shore.* Since the $x$-component is 1.25 ms$^{-1}$, the crossing time is

(2.0p) $$t_2 = \frac{D}{v} = \frac{1450}{1.25} = 1160\ \mathrm{s}.$$

Thus

(1.0p) $$\boxed{t_2 = 1160\ \mathrm{s}.}$$

*Downstream displacement.* The downstream drift is

(2.0p) $$d = ut_2 = 0.65(1160) = 754\mathrm{m}.$$

Therefore the swimmer lands

(1.0p) $$\boxed{754\mathrm{m}}$$

downstream from point $B$.

(d) Analysis of the GPS checkpoint data

For each swimmer, the average velocity components are obtained from

$$
v'_x = \frac{x}{t}, v'_y = \frac{D}{t},
$$

where $x$ is the final displacement parallel to the current direction relative to point $B$.

Swimmer S1

$$
t = 1320\mathrm{s}, \qquad x = 0.
$$

Hence

(1.0p) $$v'_x = \frac{0}{1320} = 0,$$

(1.0p) $$v'_y = \frac{1450}{1320} \approx 1.099\,\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s}}.$$

So

$$
\boxed{v'_x = 0, \qquad v'_y \approx 1.099\ \mathrm{ms^{-1}}.}
$$

Swimmer S2

$$
t = 1160\ \mathrm{s},\ x = 270\ \mathrm{m}.
$$

Thus

(1.0p) $$v'_x = \frac{270}{1160} \approx 0.233\ \mathrm{m/s},$$

(1.0p) $$v'_y = \frac{1450}{1160} \approx 1.25\,\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s}}.$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{v_x \approx 0.233\mathrm{ms^{-1}}, \qquad v_y = 1.25\mathrm{ms^{-1}}.}
$$

Swimmer S3

$$
t = 1450\ \mathrm{s}, \qquad x = -110\ \mathrm{m}.
$$

Thus

(1.0p) $$v'_x = \frac{-110}{1450} = -0.0759\ \mathrm{m/s},$$

(1.0p) $$v'_y = \frac{1450}{1450} \approx 1.00\,\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s}}.$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{v_x \approx -0.0759\ \mathrm{ms^{-1}}, \qquad v_y = 1.00\ \mathrm{ms^{-1}}.}
$$

The best current correction corresponds to the swimmer who lands closest to the target line through $B$. This means the swimmer with the smallest value of $|x|$.

The displacements are

$$
|x_{S1}| = 0, \qquad |x_{S2}| = 270\mathrm{m}, \qquad |x_{S3}| = 110\mathrm{m}.
$$

Therefore the best current correction is achieved by

(1.0p) $$\boxed{\text{S1.}}$$


</Spoiler>
