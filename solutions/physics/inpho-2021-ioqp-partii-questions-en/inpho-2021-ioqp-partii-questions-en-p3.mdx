## Условие

Consider an electron (mass $m$, magnitude of charge $e$) moving initially around a nucleus of charge $2e$ in a circular orbit of radius $10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m}$. In this problem we use SI units throughout and neglect all relativistic effects.

**(a)** Obtain the expression for the frequency, $f$, of the electron in the circular orbit (numerical value is not required). **[2 т.]**

From classical electrodynamics, we know that an accelerated electron radiates energy. The expression for the power $P$ of this radiation is given by

$$
P = K\epsilon_0^{w} e^{x} a^{y} c^{z}
$$

where $a$ is the acceleration, $c$ is the speed of light, $\epsilon_0$ is the permittivity of free space, and $K$ is a dimensionless constant.

**(b)** Obtain $\{w, x, y, z\}$ using dimensional analysis. **[2 т.]**

Due to the loss of energy through radiation, the electron does not remain in the circular orbit, and gradually spirals into the nucleus. Take the constant $K$ to be $5.31 \times 10^{-2}$.

**(c)** Let $T$ be the time it takes for the electron to reach the nucleus. Calculate $T$ if the radius of the nucleus is $10^{-14}\,\mathrm{m}$. **[5 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **(a)** $f = \frac{v}{2\pi r} = \left(\frac{2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 m}\right)^{1/2}\frac{e}{2\pi r^{3/2}}$
- **(b)** w = −1, x = 2, y = 2, z = −3
- **(c)** $5.26 \times 10^{-11}$ s

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**(a) Solution:**

The centripetal force for the circular motion of the electron is provided by the Coulomb attraction of the nucleus. Let $r$ be the radius of the circular orbit, and $v$ the speed of the electron in this orbit, then

$$
\frac{mv^2}{r} = \frac{2e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r^2} \qquad (3.1)
$$

$$
f = \frac{v}{2\pi r} = \left(\frac{2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 m}\right)^{1/2} \frac{e}{2\pi r^{3/2}} \qquad (3.2)
$$

**(b) Solution:** $w = -1, x = 2, y = 2, z = -3$

**(c) Solution:**

The total energy of an electron in the orbit is

$$
E(r) = -\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{e^2}{r} \qquad (3.3)
$$

$$
-\dot{E}(r) = -\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{e^2\dot{r}}{r^2} \qquad (3.4)
$$

the acceleration is

$$
a = \frac{v^2}{r} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{2e^2}{mr^2} \qquad (3.5)
$$

We use Eq. (3.5) in the power radiated, which yields the energy loss rate

$$
-\dot{E}(r) = -K\frac{1}{(4\pi\epsilon_0)^2}\frac{4e^6}{\epsilon_0 c^3 m^2 r^4} \qquad (3.6)
$$

Here negative sign indicates that the energy of the electron is decreasing. Combining Eqs. (3.4) and (3.6)

$$
r^2\,dr = -\frac{K4e^4}{(4\pi\epsilon_0)\epsilon_0 c^3 m^2}dt \qquad (3.7)
$$

Integrating the equation

$$
\int_{10^{-10}}^{10^{-14}} r^2\,dr = -\frac{K4e^4}{(4\pi\epsilon_0)\epsilon_0 c^3 m^2}\int_0^T dt \qquad (3.8)
$$

which yields

$$
T \sim \frac{10^{-30}}{48\pi}\frac{(4\pi\epsilon_0)^2\,c^3 m^2}{Ke^4} \sim 5.26 \times 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{s}. \qquad (3.9)
$$


</Spoiler>
