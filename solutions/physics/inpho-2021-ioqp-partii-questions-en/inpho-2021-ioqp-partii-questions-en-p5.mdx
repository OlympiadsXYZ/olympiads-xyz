## Условие

Two friends, Amina (A) and Beena (B), are sitting at diametrically opposite points of a merry-go-round (taken as a circular disk in the horizontal plane) of radius $R$ that is rotating at constant angular speed $\omega$ in the anticlockwise direction, when viewed from the top (see figure below).

When Amina is at the position A (as shown in the figure), she throws a ball with velocity $\vec{u}$ (relative to the merry-go-round) in such a manner that Beena catches it when she reaches the position C ($\angle BAC = \alpha$). Here $\vec{u}$ makes an angle $\theta$ with respect to the horizontal, and $\phi$ is the angle made by the horizontal projection of $\vec{u}$ with respect to the line AB. Neglect air resistance, friction, and the effect of throwing or catching the ball on the speed of the merry-go-round.

<figure>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/inpho-2021-ioqp-partii-questions-en/p5-fig1.png" alt="Figure labelled 'Top view' of the merry-go-round: a circle with centre O and diameter AB drawn dashed; point D at the top on the dashed vertical radius from O (right angle 90° marked at O); point C on the circle between D and B; dashed chord AC making angle α with AB at A; R marks the radius between A and O; a curved arrow labelled ω shows anticlockwise rotation." />
</figure>

**(a)** Determine $u$, $\theta$ and $\phi$, in terms of $R$, $\omega$, $\alpha$, and other relevant quantities. **[6 т.]**

**(b)** If Amina throws the ball with $\phi = 60^{\circ}$, and appropriate values of $\theta$ and $u$ such that Beena can catch it, what is the magnitude of the displacement, $s$, of the ball when it is caught by Beena? For this part only, take $R = 1.5\,\mathrm{m}$, and it is enough to state your answer within a range of $0.5\,\mathrm{m}$. **[3 т.]**

**(c)** Determine the speed of throwing $u_{\mathrm{D}}$ if Beena catches the ball at the point D ($\angle BOD = 90^{\circ}$), instead of C. **[0,5 т.]**

**(d)** What should be the angular speed $\omega_m$ of the merry-go-round for which the speed of throwing $u_{\mathrm{D}}$ will be minimum for Beena to catch the ball at the position D? What is this minimum speed of throwing $u_m$? **[3 т.]**

**(e)** Consider the case when Amina throws the ball when she is at A, and catches it herself when she reaches the point B (Beena is not involved in this case). Take the angular speed of the merry-go-round to be $\omega = \sqrt{g/R}$. Find $u$, $\theta$ and $\phi$ in this case. **[2,5 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**(a) Solution:**

Point of throwing: A; Point of catching: C

Position of $C$ at instant of projection: B

We take the point A as the origin and the $x$-axis along the diameter AB. The $y$-axis is in the horizontal plane, perpendicular to AB. The $z$-axis is taken along vertical direction.

Given, $\omega =$ angular speed of rotation; $\vec{u} =$ velocity of throwing

$\theta =$ Projection angle with respect to horizontal

$\phi =$ Projection angle with respect to diameter $AB$ ($x$-axis)

$\alpha = \angle BAC \quad \implies \beta = \angle BOC = 2\alpha$

Time of flight = time taken for $B$ to reach $C$ $= T = \dfrac{R\beta}{v_s} = \dfrac{R\beta}{R\omega} = \dfrac{2\alpha}{\omega}$

Equations of motion along three directions:

$x:\quad u_x \cdot T = AP$

$$
\implies (u\cos\theta\cos\phi)\cdot\frac{2\alpha}{\omega} = R + R\cos\beta = R(1 + \cos 2\alpha) = 2R\cos^2\alpha
$$

$$
\implies u\cos\theta\cos\phi = \frac{R\omega}{\alpha}\cos^2\alpha \qquad (5.1)
$$

$y:\quad (u_y - R\omega)\cdot T = CP$

$$
\implies (u\cos\theta\sin\phi - R\omega)\cdot\frac{2\alpha}{\omega} = R\sin\beta = 2R\sin\alpha\cos\alpha
$$

$$
\implies u\cos\theta\sin\phi = \frac{R\omega}{\alpha}\sin\alpha\cos\alpha + R\omega = \frac{R\omega}{\alpha}[\sin\alpha\cos\alpha + \alpha] \qquad (5.2)
$$

$z:\quad u_z \cdot T - \dfrac{1}{2}gT^2 = 0$

$$
\implies u\sin\theta = \frac{gT}{2} = \frac{g\alpha}{\omega} \qquad (5.3)
$$

Dividing eq. (5.2) by eq. (5.1),

$$
\tan\phi = \frac{\sin\alpha\cos\alpha + \alpha}{\cos^2\alpha} = \tan\alpha + \alpha\sec^2\alpha
$$

$$
\implies \boxed{\phi = \tan^{-1}(\tan\alpha + \alpha\sec^2\alpha)} \qquad (5.4)
$$

Squaring eqs. (5.1), (5.2), (5.3) and adding,

$$
u^2\cos^2\theta + u^2\sin^2\theta = \left(\frac{R\omega}{\alpha}\right)^2\left[\cos^4\alpha + \sin^2\alpha\cos^2\alpha + \alpha^2 + 2\alpha\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\right] + \left(\frac{g\alpha}{\omega}\right)^2
$$

$$
\implies u^2 = \left(\frac{R\omega}{\alpha}\right)^2\left[\cos^2\alpha + 2\alpha\sin\alpha\cos\alpha + \alpha^2\right] + \left(\frac{g\alpha}{\omega}\right)^2 \qquad (5.5)
$$

$$
\implies \boxed{u = \left[\left(\frac{g\alpha}{\omega}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{R\omega}{\alpha}\right)^2\left[\cos^2\alpha + 2\alpha\sin\alpha\cos\alpha + \alpha^2\right]\right]^{1/2}} \qquad (5.6)
$$

From (5.3) and (5.6),

$$
\boxed{\theta = \sin^{-1}\left[\frac{g\alpha}{\omega}\left[\left(\frac{g\alpha}{\omega}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{R\omega}{\alpha}\right)^2\left[\cos^2\alpha + 2\alpha\sin\alpha\cos\alpha + \alpha^2\right]\right]^{-1/2}\right]} \qquad (5.7)
$$

**(b) Solution:**

The displacement of the ball is the length of AC $= s = 2R\cos\alpha$.

Thus we need to determine $\alpha$ when $\phi = 60^{\circ}$. Equation (5.4) can be used for this. Note that values of $\theta$ and $u$ are not needed.

Putting $\phi = 60^{\circ}$ in equation (5.4), we have

$$
f(\alpha) = \tan\alpha + \alpha\sec^2\alpha = \tan 60^{\circ} = \sqrt{3}
$$

This equation cannot be solved analytically. We use trial values of $\alpha$ to find the solution by interpolation.

$$
f(\pi/6) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} + \frac{\pi}{6}\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^2 = 1.275 < \sqrt{3}
$$

$$
f(\pi/4) = 1 + \frac{\pi}{4}(\sqrt{2})^2 = 2.571 > \sqrt{3}
$$

Thus

$$
\frac{\pi}{6} < \alpha < \frac{\pi}{4}
$$

$$
\implies \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} > \cos\alpha > \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
$$

$$
\implies 2R\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} > 2R\cos\alpha > 2R\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
$$

$$
\implies \sqrt{3}R > s > \sqrt{2}R
$$

Putting $R = 1.5\,\mathrm{m}$,

$$
\boxed{2.1\,\text{metre} < s < 2.6\,\text{metre}}
$$

Any answer that encloses the actual value of $2.4\,\mathrm{m}$ and has a range $\leq 0.5\,\mathrm{m}$ is acceptable.

**(c) Solution:**

This is a special case of the above, where $\alpha = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$. Using the above results,

$$
\boxed{u_{\mathrm{D}} = \left[\left(\frac{g\pi}{4\omega}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{4R\omega}{\pi}\right)^2\left[\frac{1}{2} + \frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{\pi^2}{16}\right]\right]^{1/2}} \qquad (5.8)
$$

**(d) Solution:**

This can be determined by finding the minimum of $u_{\mathrm{D}}$, or equivalently, $u_{\mathrm{D}}^2$. From (5.8),

$$
u_{\mathrm{D}}^2 = \left(\frac{g\pi}{4\omega}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{4R\omega}{\pi}\right)^2\left[\frac{1}{2} + \frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{\pi^2}{16}\right]
$$

$$
\therefore \left.\frac{\mathrm{d}(u_{\mathrm{D}}^2)}{\mathrm{d}\omega}\right|_{\omega_m} = 0 \implies -\frac{(g\pi)^2}{8\omega_m^3} + \frac{32R^2\omega_m}{\pi^2}\left[\frac{1}{2} + \frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{\pi^2}{16}\right] = 0
$$

$$
\implies \omega_m^4 = \frac{g^2\pi^4}{256R^2\left[\frac{1}{2} + \frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{\pi^2}{16}\right]}
$$

$$
\implies \boxed{\omega_m = \frac{\pi}{4}\left[\frac{1}{2} + \frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{\pi^2}{16}\right]^{-1/4}\sqrt{\frac{g}{R}}}
$$

Also,

$$
\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}^2(u_{\mathrm{D}}^2)}{\mathrm{d}\omega^2}\right|_{\omega_m} = \frac{3(g\pi)^2}{8\omega_m^4} + \frac{32R^2}{\pi^2}\left[\frac{1}{2} + \frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{\pi^2}{16}\right] > 0.
$$

implying $u_{\mathrm{D}}^2$ is minimum at $\omega = \omega_m$.

$$
\therefore u_m^2 = gR\left[\frac{1}{2} + \frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{\pi^2}{16}\right]^{1/2} + gR\left[\frac{1}{2} + \frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{\pi^2}{16}\right]^{1/2}
$$

$$
\implies \boxed{u_m = \left(\frac{1}{2} + \frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{\pi^2}{16}\right)^{1/4}\sqrt{2gR}}
$$

Alternative solution without calculus

Observe that

$$
u_{\mathrm{D}}^2 = \frac{\lambda}{\omega^2} + \mu\omega^2
$$

where $\lambda = \left(\dfrac{g\pi}{4}\right)^2 > 0$, $\mu = \left(\dfrac{4R}{\pi}\right)^2\left[\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{\pi}{4} + \dfrac{\pi^2}{16}\right] > 0.$

We can write

$$
u_{\mathrm{D}}^2 = \left(\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{\omega} - \omega\sqrt{\mu}\right)^2 + 2\sqrt{\lambda\mu}.
$$

The first term can be made zero by the choice of

$$
\omega = \omega_m = \left(\frac{\lambda}{\mu}\right)^{1/4}
$$

leading to the minimum value of $u_{\mathrm{D}}^2$ as $u_m^2 = 2\sqrt{\lambda\mu}$. Upon substituting the values of $\lambda$ and $\mu$ the desired expressions are obtained.

**(e) Solution:**

This case is NOT a special case of the above.

Now $T = \dfrac{\tau}{2} = \dfrac{\pi}{\omega}$.

Further, $\omega = \sqrt{g/R}$.

The equations of motion are:

$$
x:\quad u_x \cdot T = AB \implies (u\cos\theta\cos\phi)\cdot\frac{\pi}{\omega} = 2R \implies u\cos\theta\cos\phi = \frac{2R\omega}{\pi} = \frac{2}{\pi}\sqrt{gR} \qquad (5.9)
$$

$$
y:\quad (u_y - R\omega)\cdot T = 0 \implies u\cos\theta\sin\phi = R\omega = \sqrt{gR} \qquad (5.10)
$$

$$
z:\quad u_z T - \frac{1}{2}gT^2 = 0 \implies u\sin\theta = \frac{gT}{2} = \frac{\pi g}{2\omega} = \frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{gR} \qquad (5.11)
$$

Dividing eq. (5.10) by eq. (5.9),

$$
\tan\phi = \frac{\pi}{2} \implies \boxed{\phi = \tan^{-1}\frac{\pi}{2} = 57.52^{\circ}}
$$

Squaring eqs. (5.10) and (5.9), and adding,

$$
u^2\cos^2\theta = gR\left[\frac{4}{\pi^2} + 1\right]
$$

$$
\implies \boxed{u = \sqrt{gR}\left[\frac{\pi^2}{4} + \frac{4}{\pi^2} + 1\right]^{1/2} = 1.97\sqrt{gR}} \qquad (5.12)
$$

Using eqs. (5.11) and (5.12),

$$
\sin\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}\left[\frac{\pi^2}{4} + \frac{4}{\pi^2} + 1\right]^{-1/2} \implies \boxed{\theta = \sin^{-1}(0.80) = 52.96^{\circ}}
$$
