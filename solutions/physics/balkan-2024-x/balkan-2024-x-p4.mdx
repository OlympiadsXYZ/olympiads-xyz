*BPO6 Problems 20-24 June 2024. Montenegro · 20 юни 2024 г. · 25 т.*

## Условие

**1. Description of the experiment**

A long plastic spring, the so called “slinky”, of mass $M = 72.3\ \mathrm{g}$, is composed of $N = 108$ identical circular turns. The slinky is positioned on a horizontal support, so that part of it is hanging freely, as depicted in Figure 1. After the spring oscillations damp completely, the length $\ell$ of the suspended part is measured as a function of the number $n$ of hanging turns ($n < N$; see the figure). The experimental data for $\ell$ at different $n$ are recorded in the Table at the end of the text.

**A.** From a theoretical viewpoint, the dependence of $\ell$ on $n$ is given by a quadratic function:

$$
\ell = An + Bn^2 \qquad (1)
$$

where $A$ and $B$ are constants characterizing the spring. Define a set of variables $x$ and $y$, which linearize equation (1), i.e. transform it to a linear dependence. **[2 т.]**

**B.** Determine constants $A$ and $B$ in equation (1) by means of a graphical analysis. Any auxiliary data, necessary for plotting the graph, must be recorded in the empty cells of Table 1. Use the graph paper to draw your graph. **Attach the last page of the text (containing the table) to your solution!** **[10 т.]**

**C.** Equation (1) can be derived under the assumptions that each single turn of the slinky is equivalent to a spring of stiffness $k_{\mathrm{s}}$ (known also as “spring constant” or “coefficient of elasticity”) and length $\ell_{\mathrm{s}}$ in the undeformed state. Perform a theoretical analysis and express constants $A$ and $B$ in equation (1) in terms of $k_{\mathrm{s}}$ and $\ell_{\mathrm{s}}$. Neglect the deformation of a given single turn by its own weight. **[5 т.]**

**D.** Calculate $k_{\mathrm{s}}$ and $\ell_{\mathrm{s}}$. **[3 т.]**

**E.** Calculate the length $L$ of whole slinky in the undeformed state, and its stiffness $K$. **[5 т.]**

**Useful information:**

1. The acceleration due to gravity is $g = 9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$.
2. Depending on the methodology of your solution, the following relation may be of use:

$$
1 + 2 + \cdots + n = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}
$$

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **A.** y = ℓ/n; x = n, so that y = A + Bx (alternatively y = ℓ/n²; x = 1/n, y = Ax + B).
- **B.** A ≈ 0.18 cm; B ≈ 0.022 cm
- **C.** $A = \ell_{\mathrm{t}} - \frac{Mg}{2Nk_{\mathrm{t}}},\quad B = \frac{Mg}{2Nk_{\mathrm{t}}}$
- **D.** k_s ≈ 15 N/m; ℓ_s ≈ 0.20 cm (2.0 mm)
- **E.** L ≈ 22 cm; K ≈ 0.14 N/m

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**A.** The simplest choice of variables is:

$$
y = \ell/n\,;\ x = n \qquad (1)
$$

In this case:

$$
y = A + Bx \qquad (2)
$$

Alternatively, the student may choose to work with:

$$
y = \ell/n^2\,;\ x = 1/n \qquad (3)
$$

and:

$$
y = Ax + B \qquad (4)
$$

though calculations in this case are more time consuming and the results are less accurate.

Figure 1 shows data points on a *x-y* graph and the line, approximating the data. If we measure coordinates of two pints of the fitting line, ($x_1$,$y_1$) and ($x_2$,$y_2$), then:

$$
y_1 = A + Bx_1;\ y_2 = A + Bx_2 \qquad (5)
$$

and the coefficients *A* and *B* could be calculated as:

$$
A = \frac{y_1x_2 - y_2x_1}{x_2 - x_1};\ B = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} \qquad (6)
$$

For the choice shown in Fig. 1, $x_1 = 3; y_1 = 0.25\ \mathrm{cm}$ and $x_2 = 48; y_2 = 1.25\ \mathrm{cm}$. Therefore:

$$
A \approx 0.18\ \mathrm{cm}
$$

$$
B \approx 0.022\ \mathrm{cm}
$$

Alternatively *A* could be determined as the *y*-coordinate of the intersection between the fitting line and the vertical axis. In this case, however, *A* is much less accurate and values between 0.15 mm and 0.20 mm could be reported by the students.

**C.** We will enumerate the hanging turns from 1 to *n* starting from the lowest free end toward the upper turn attached to the holder. The mass of one single turn is:

$$
m_{\mathrm{t}} = \frac{M}{N} \qquad (7)
$$

The deformation $\Delta\ell_i$ of *i*-th turn is due to the total weight $W = (i-1)m_{\mathrm{t}}g$ of all *i*–1 turns underneath. From the Hook’s law:

$$
\Delta\ell_i = \frac{W}{k_{\mathrm{t}}} = \frac{(i-1)Mg}{Nk_{\mathrm{t}}} \qquad (8)
$$

The total deformation of the hanging part is a sum of the deformation of individual turns:

$$
\Delta\ell = \Delta\ell_1 + \cdots + \Delta\ell_n = \frac{n(n-1)Mg}{2Nk_{\mathrm{t}}} \qquad (9)
$$

The measured length is a sum of the undeformed length $n\ell_{\mathrm{t}}$ of the hanging part and the deformation:

$$
\ell = n\ell_{\mathrm{t}} + \frac{n(n-1)Mg}{2Nk_{\mathrm{t}}} = \left(\ell_{\mathrm{t}} - \frac{Mg}{2Nk_{\mathrm{t}}}\right)n + \frac{Mg}{2Nk_{\mathrm{t}}}\,n^2 \qquad (10)
$$

By comparing with equation 1, we identify:

$$
A = \ell_{\mathrm{t}} - \frac{Mg}{2Nk_{\mathrm{t}}} = \ell_{\mathrm{t}} - B \qquad (11)
$$

and

$$
B = \frac{Mg}{2Nk_{\mathrm{t}}} \qquad (12)
$$

**D.** From the theoretical expressions for *A* and *B*, and by using their experimental values:

$$
k_{\mathrm{t}} = \frac{Mg}{2NB} = \frac{0.0723\ \mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}}{2 \cdot 108 \cdot 2.2 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{m}} \approx 15\ \mathrm{N/m} \qquad (13)
$$

and

$$
\ell_{\mathrm{t}} = A + B = 0.18\ \mathrm{cm} + 0.022\ \mathrm{cm} \approx 0.20\ \mathrm{cm}\ (2.0\ \mathrm{mm}) \qquad (14)
$$

**E.** The total length of the spring in undeformed state is:

$$
L = N\ell_{\mathrm{t}} = 108 \times 0.20\ \mathrm{cm} \approx 22\ \mathrm{cm} \qquad (15)
$$

If forces with opposite direction and the same magnitude *F* are applied to both ends of slinky, each single turn will be stretched by:

$$
\Delta\ell_{\mathrm{t}} = F/k_{\mathrm{t}} \qquad (16)
$$

The total deformation of the spring will be:

$$
\Delta L = N\Delta\ell_{\mathrm{t}} = NF/k_{\mathrm{t}} \qquad (17)
$$

Therefore, the stiffness of the whole slinky is:

$$
K = \frac{F}{\Delta L} = \frac{k_{\mathrm{t}}}{N} = \frac{15\ \mathrm{N/m}}{108} \approx 0.14\ \mathrm{N/m} \qquad (18)
$$
