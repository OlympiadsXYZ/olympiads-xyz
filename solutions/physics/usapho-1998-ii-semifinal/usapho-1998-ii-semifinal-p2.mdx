## Условие

Two uniformly charged balls each have mass $m$ and carry electric charge $q$. Each ball is tied to a string of length $L$ and negligible mass. The other ends of the strings are tied to a hook in the ceiling. When the balls are in equilibrium the strings stand apart with the angle $2\theta_o$ between them.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '24.9%', '--fig-max': '159px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-1998-ii-semifinal/p2-fig1-v2.png" alt="Two balls m,q suspended by length L strings separated by angle2thetao." width="498" height="529" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

**a.** Find an expression for $\theta$ in terms of the given quantities and known constants. Neglect the gravitational force which the balls exert on each other. **[7 т.]**

**b.** Now let both balls be displaced an additional angle $\delta\ll1$. Find the magnitude of the new electric force $F'$ which one ball exerts on the other one. In particular, show that $F'=F_o(1-2\delta\cot\theta_o)$ where $F_o$ is the electric force’s magnitude for the situation of part (a). Along with the binomial expansion, the following approximations may be useful:

$$
\sin(\theta_o+\delta)\simeq\sin\theta_o+\delta\cos\theta_o,
$$

$$
\cos(\theta_o+\delta)\simeq\cos\theta_o-\delta\sin\theta_o.
$$
**[6 т.]**

**c.** When the balls are released from the angle $\theta_o+\delta$, each ball undergoes an oscillation about its equilibrium position (assume that the balls move only in the original plane of the two strings). Find an expression for the period $T$ of this oscillation in terms of given quantities and known constants. Neglect air resistance. **[10 т.]**

**d.** Evaluate $T/T_o$ for $\theta_o=45$ degrees, where $T_o$ is the period of a simple pendulum of length $L$. **[2 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-a">

### a. (7 т.)

$$
\vec F=m\underbrace{\vec a}_{0}
$$

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '55.3%', '--fig-max': '354px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-1998-ii-semifinal/p2-fig2.png" alt="Equilibrium ball free-body diagram with tension, weight and electric force; x and y axes." width="1107" height="974" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


$x$: $qE_o-F_T\sin\theta_o=0$.

$y$: $F_T\cos\theta_o-mg=0$.

$$
\frac{qE_o}{mg}=\tan\theta_o.\qquad (1)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-b">

### b. (6 т.)


<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '29.2%', '--fig-max': '187px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-1998-ii-semifinal/p2-fig3.png" alt="Original and displaced balls with separations so and s prime and angle delta." width="585" height="558" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


$$
s_o=2L\sin\theta_o
$$

$$
s'=2L\sin(\theta_o+\delta)\simeq2L(\sin\theta_o+\delta\cos\theta_o)=s_o(1+\delta\cot\theta_o)
$$

$$
F'=qE'=\frac{kq^2}{s'^2}=\frac{kq^2}{s_o^2}(1+\delta\cot\theta_o)^{-2}\simeq F_o(1-2\delta\cot\theta_o).
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-c">

### c. (10 т.)

Apply $\vec\tau=I\vec\alpha$ about hook.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '39.8%', '--fig-max': '255px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-1998-ii-semifinal/p2-fig4-v2.png" alt="Displaced ball force and torque diagram about the hook." width="796" height="872" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


$$
qE'L\cos(\theta_o+\delta)-mgL\sin(\theta_o+\delta)=mL^2\ddot\delta
$$

$$
qE_o(1-2\delta\cot\theta_o)(\cos\theta_o-\delta\sin\theta_o)-mg(\sin\theta_o+\delta\cos\theta_o)=mL\ddot\delta.\qquad (2)
$$

Use Eq. (1), $qE_o\cos\theta_o=mg\sin\theta_o$. To $O(\delta)$, Eq. (2) becomes

$$
\ddot\delta+\frac gL\left(\frac1{\cos\theta_o}+2\cos\theta_o\right)\delta=0
$$

$$
\omega^2=\frac gL\left(\frac1{\cos\theta_o}+2\cos\theta_o\right)
$$

$$
T=\frac{2\pi}{\omega}=2\pi\sqrt{\frac Lg}\left(\frac1{\cos\theta_o}+2\cos\theta_o\right)^{-1/2}.
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-d">

### d. (2 т.)

At $\theta_o=45^\circ$, $\cos\theta_o=1/\sqrt2=\sin\theta_o$.

$$
T_o=2\pi\sqrt{\frac Lg},\qquad T=\frac{T_o}{\sqrt{\sqrt2+\frac2{\sqrt2}}}=\frac{T_o}{\sqrt{2\sqrt2}}.
$$

</ProblemSection>
