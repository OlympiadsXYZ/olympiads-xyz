## Условие

Two sirens located on the x-axis are separated by a certain distance $D$. As heard by an observer at rest, $f_L$ denotes the frequency emitted by the left-hand siren, and $f_R$ denotes the frequency emitted by the right-hand siren. You are moving with constant velocity at speed $v_o$ along the x-axis, and record the following observations:

When you are on the right side of both sirens, you hear a beat frequency of 1.01 Hz.

When you are on the left of both sirens, you hear a beat frequency of 0.99 Hz.

When you are between the sirens, the beat frequency is zero.

**a.** Determine in which direction you are moving along the x-axis. **[5 т.]**

**b.** Evaluate your speed $v_o$ as a fraction of the speed of sound. **[10 т.]**

**c.** Determine which frequency is greater, $f_L$ or $f_R$. **[5 т.]**

**d.** Determine the numerical values of $f_L$ and $f_R$. **[10 т.]**

**e.** Suppose you stop moving, and the siren that emits sound of frequency $f_L$ is taken aboard a helicopter. The helicopter hovers with zero velocity above you. The siren (assumed to be battery-powered) is dropped from the helicopter. In the case of negligible air resistance, sketch a graph of frequency as a function of time, that describes the frequency you hear from this falling siren. [If you did not obtain a value for $f_L$ in part (d), use 50 Hz.] Take the speed of sound to be 343 m/s. The siren falls for 30 seconds before striking the ground beside you. **[10 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-a">

### a. (5 т.)

The beat frequency is “blue shifted” on the [text missing in original scan], and “red shifted” on the L — hence moving [text missing in original scan].

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-b">

### b. (10 т.)

The beats are a “source” of frequency $\lvert f_L-f_R\rvert\equiv\Delta$ [remainder missing in original scan].

When on L: $\Delta f'_L=\Delta f_o(1-v/c)$, moving away; $c=$ speed of sound.

Using given data,

$$
0.99\text{ Hz}=\Delta f_o(1-v/c).\qquad (1)
$$

When on R: $\Delta f'_R=\Delta f_o(1+v/c)$, moving towards.

$$
1.01\text{ Hz}=\Delta f_o(1+v/c).\qquad (2)
$$

(1) and (2) $\Rightarrow$

$$
\beta\equiv\frac{1.01}{0.99}=\frac{1+v/c}{1-v/c}\qquad (3)
$$

$$
\Rightarrow\frac vc=0.01.\qquad (4)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-c">

### c. (5 т.)

In between, $f_R$ is red-shifted:

$$
f'_R=f_R-\epsilon_R\quad\text{for some }\epsilon_R>0.
$$

In between, $f_L$ is blue-shifted:

$$
f'_L=f_L+\epsilon_R\quad\text{for some }\epsilon_L>0.
$$

In between, $f'_L=f'_R$.

$$
f_L+\epsilon_R=f_R-\epsilon_R
$$

$$
\Rightarrow f_R=f_L+\epsilon_R+\epsilon_R>f_L
$$

so $f_R>f_L$.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-d">

### d. (10 т.)

In between,

$$
f'_L=f_L\left(1+\frac vc\right)\quad\text{blue shifted},\qquad f'_R=f_R\left(1-\frac vc\right)\quad\text{red-shifted}.
$$

And $f'_L=f'_R$, so $f_L(1+v/c)=f_R(1-v/c)$.

I.e.,

$$
\frac{f_R}{f_L}=\frac{1+v/c}{1-v/c}=\beta\quad\text{[from (3)]}.\qquad (5)
$$

Also, from either (1) or (2), and using (4) [$v/c=0.01$], it follows that

$$
f_R-f_L=1\text{ Hz}.\qquad (6)
$$

Solving (5) and (6) gives $f_R=50.5\text{ Hz}$, $f_L=49.5\text{ Hz}$.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-e">

### e. (10 т.)

Source moving: $v=-gt$.

$$
f'=f_o\left(1+\frac vc\right)^{-1}=f_o\left(1-\frac{gt}{c}\right)
$$

$g=9.8\text{ m/s}^2$, $f_o=50\text{ Hz}$, $c=343\text{ m/s}$.

$$
f'=\frac{50\text{ Hz}}{1-\frac{t}{35\text{ s}}}.
$$
