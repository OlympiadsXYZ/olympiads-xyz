## Условие

A certain metal has various energy levels for the electrons in its atoms. Assuming the energy of a free electron at rest is zero, the first five energy levels of the electrons in the metal’s atoms are as follows:

| State | Energy |
|---|---|
| State 1 | $-5$ eV |
| State 2 | $10$ eV |
| State 3 | $-15$ eV |
| State 4 | $-30$ eV |
| State 5 | $-50$ eV |

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '46.7%', '--fig-max': '299px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-1998-ii-semifinal/p3-fig1.png" alt="Energy diagram showing E=0 and negative5,10,15eV levels, etc." width="935" height="423" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

**a.** In this part of the problem we will see what the wave model of light predicts for the ability of light to free the electrons from the metal surface. Suppose the intensity of light irradiating the metal is 0.01 W/m$^2$ and the density of surface electrons is $5\times10^{19}$ electrons per square meter. Calculate the minimum time required to liberate an electron from the metal surface, assuming that this energy falling on the metal can be absorbed by the electrons, so that they will be ejected as soon as they acquire sufficient energy. **[7 т.]**

**b.** Unfortunately for the wave model’s result suggested by part (a), experiments show that, even when the intensity of the light is very low, some electrons are liberated in less than $10^{-9}$ s, provided the frequency of the light is above a specific minimum value. Calculate this minimum frequency for the metal described above. **[5 т.]**

**c.** Suppose the metal surface is irradiated with photons corresponding to the frequency $f=4.5\times10^{15}$ Hz. Calculate the kinetic energies of the electrons that originally occupied States 1 through 5. (If you like, you may leave your answers in units of electron volts.) **[8 т.]**

**d.** The electrons liberated from the metal by the photons of part (c) form an electric current. What minimum stopping voltage would be required to halt the motion of every liberated electron, and thus bring the current to zero? **[5 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-a">

### a. (7 т.)

Min. energy required is 5 eV.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '46.9%', '--fig-max': '300px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-1998-ii-semifinal/p3-fig2.png" alt="Light intensity and area with one surface electron." width="939" height="499" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


Area with 1 surface electron:

$$
A=(5\times10^{19}/\text{m}^2)^{-1}=2\times10^{-20}\text{ m}^2.
$$

$$
IA\Delta t=5\text{ eV}
$$

$$
\Delta t=\frac{5(1.6\times10^{-19}\text{ J})}{(2\times10^{-20}\text{ m}^2)(10^{-2}\text{ J}/(\text{s}\cdot\text{m}^2))}=4\times10^3\text{ s}\simeq1.1\text{ hr}.
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-b">

### b. (5 т.)

$$
hf=5\text{ eV}\Rightarrow f=\frac{5\text{ eV}}{4.14\times10^{-15}\text{ eV}\cdot\text{s}}=1.21\times10^{15}\text{ Hz}.
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-c">

### c. (8 т.)

$$
hf=K+\phi\qquad\text{(cons. of Energy)}
$$

$hf$: incoming photon energy; $K$: K.E. left over; $\phi$: what it costs to liberate electron.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '39.6%', '--fig-max': '254px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-1998-ii-semifinal/p3-fig3.png" alt="Work function phi between a negative energy state and zero." width="794" height="354" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


$$
K=hf-\phi=(4.14\times10^{-15}\text{ eV}\cdot\text{s})(4.5\times10^{15}\text{ Hz})-\phi=18.6\text{ eV}-\phi.
$$

| State | Kinetic energy |
|---|---|
| 1 | $K_1=18.6\text{ eV}-5\text{ eV}=13.6\text{ eV}$ |
| 2 | $K_2=8.6\text{ eV}$ |
| 3 | $K_3=3.6\text{ eV}$ |
| 4 | — |
| 5 | — |

K.E. cannot be $<0$.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-d">

### d. (5 т.)

$$
eV_{stop}=13.6\text{ eV}\Rightarrow V_{stop}=13.6\text{ Volts}.
$$

</ProblemSection>


</Spoiler>
