## Условие

Consider an electron that starts from rest, essentially infinitely far from a completely ionized, extremely massive nucleus containing $Z$ protons. The electron is captured by this nucleus. In the capture process, one photon is emitted. After being captured, the electron possesses angular momentum of magnitude $L$ as it orbits the nucleus. In parts (a) and (b) let us suppose the electron orbits classically in an elliptical orbit whose perigee (minimum distance from the nucleus) is $r_{min}$ and whose apogee (maximum distance from the nucleus) is $r_{max}=\beta r_{min}$. For numerical values use:

$$
L=1.4\times10^{-33}\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{s},\qquad\beta=2,\qquad r_{max}=2.5\times10^{-10}\text{ m}.
$$

**a.** Find the value of $Z$. **[15 т.]**

**b.** What is the wavelength of the single photon that was emitted during the capture process? **[10 т.]**

**c.** Now let us consider another heavy but completely ionized nucleus of mass M, where $M=121\text{ GeV}/c^2$. Let an electron be accelerated such that, as observed in the reference frame where the nucleus is originally at rest, the electron has 1 GeV of kinetic energy right before it impacts the nucleus. Upon entering the nucleus, the electron reacts with one of the protons in an inverse beta decay,

$$
(\text{electron})+(\text{proton})\to(\text{neutron})+(\text{neutrino}).
$$

The neutrino immediately escapes the nucleus. Let us approximate the mass $M'$ of the transformed nucleus as equal to $M$, since $\lvert M'-M\rvert\ll M$. The mass of the neutrino is very small; experimentally it cannot be above about $5\text{ eV}/c^2$, so let us assume that the neutrino’s mass is exactly zero. Find the neutrino’s energy in the case where it is emitted in the direction exactly opposite the direction of the incoming electron (that is, the angle between the incoming electron’s momentum and the outgoing neutrino’s momentum is 180 degrees). Describe any other approximations you make in addition to those already stated.

Here again are the numerical values for this problem:

$$
M=121\text{ GeV}/c^2\simeq M'
$$

neutrino mass $=0$

electron mass $m\simeq\frac12\text{ MeV}/c^2$

incoming electron’s kinetic energy: 1 GeV

Note: $1\text{ MeV}=10^6\text{ eV}$, and $1\text{ GeV}=10^9\text{ eV}$. **[15 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

$$
E=\frac12m\dot r^2+\frac{L^2}{2mr^2}-\frac{kZe^2}{r}\qquad\text{for orbiting electron}.
$$

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-a">

### a. (15 т.)

At apogee (2) and perigee (1), $\dot r=0$.

$$
E_1=E_2,\qquad L=\text{const}.
$$

$$
\frac{L^2}{2mr_1^2}-\frac{kZe^2}{r_1}=\frac{L^2}{2mr_2^2}-\frac{kZe^2}{r_2}\qquad\text{where }r_2=\beta r_1.
$$

$$
\frac{L^2}{2m}\left(\frac1{r_2^2}-\frac1{r_1^2}\right)-kZe^2\left(\frac1{r_2}-\frac1{r_1}\right)=0
$$

$$
\frac{L^2}{2mr_1^2}\left(\frac1{\beta^2}-1\right)-\frac{kZe^2}{r_1}\left(\frac1\beta-1\right)=0
$$

$$
\frac1{\beta^2}-1=\left(\frac1\beta-1\right)\left(\frac1\beta+1\right)
$$

$$
\frac{L^2}{2mr_1^2}\left(\frac1\beta+1\right)=\frac{kZe^2}{r_1}
$$

$$
Z=\frac{L^2(\frac1\beta+1)}{2mr_1ke^2}=28.
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-b">

### b. (10 т.)

$$
E_{final}+hf=\underbrace{E_{initial}}_0
$$

“initial” and “final” refer to electron’s energy.

$$
hf=-E_f=-\frac{L^2}{2mr_2^2}+\frac{kZe^2}{r_2}=8.6\times10^{-18}\text{ J}
$$

$$
\lambda=\frac{hc}{E}=2.3\times10^{-8}\text{ m}.
$$

</ProblemSection>

$$
M\simeq M'=121\text{ GeV}/c^2
$$

$$
E_e=K_e+m_ec^2=1\text{ GeV}+\tfrac12\text{ MeV}\simeq1\text{ GeV}\qquad\text{neglect electron mass}
$$

$$
M'c^2\gg K_e\Rightarrow M'\text{ kinetic energy }(K')\simeq\frac{p'^2}{2m}
$$

$\therefore$ The electron is relativistic, the nucleus non-relativistic.

[Need we consider electron’s electrical potential energy?

Estimate P.E. at surface of nucleus (order of magnitude)

$$
Z\sim50,\qquad r\sim10^{-14}\text{ m}
$$

$$
U=\frac{Zke^2}{r}\simeq\frac{(50)(9\times10^9)(1.6\times10^{-19})e}{10^{-14}\text{ m}}=7.2\times10^5\text{ eV}<1\text{ MeV}\ll K_e
$$

— may ignore P.E.]

Ok, here we go: (units: ignore c’s — $E$ in eV, $p$ in eV/$c$, $m$ in eV/$c^2$)

Cons. of Energy:

$$
K_e+M=E_\nu+M'+K',\qquad M\simeq M'
$$

$$
K_e\simeq E_\nu+K'\qquad (1)
$$

$$
\simeq E_\nu+\frac{p'^2}{2M'}.
$$

Cons. of momentum (one-dimensional):

$$
p_e=p'-p_\nu\Rightarrow p'=p_e+p_\nu.\qquad (2)
$$

(2) into (1) $\Rightarrow$

$$
K_e=E_\nu+\frac{(p_e+p_\nu)^2}{2M}
$$

$$
K_e=E_\nu+\frac1{2M}(p_e^2+p_\nu^2+2p_ep_\nu).\qquad (3)
$$

For neutrino,

$$
E_\nu^2-p_\nu^2=\underbrace{m_\nu^2}_0,\qquad p_\nu=E_\nu.
$$

For electron,

$$
E_e^2-p_e^2=m_e^2
$$

$$
(K_e+m_e)^2-p_e^2=m_e^2,\qquad m_e\ll K_e
$$

$$
K_e\simeq p_e.
$$

(3) becomes

$$
K_e=E_\nu+\frac1{2M}(K_e^2+E_\nu^2+2K_eE_\nu).
$$

Numerically,

$$
1\text{ GeV}=E_\nu+\frac1{242\text{ GeV}}(1\text{ GeV}^2+E_\nu^2+(2\text{ GeV})E_\nu)
$$

Neglect $1/242$.

$$
1\text{ GeV}\simeq\frac{E_\nu^2}{242\text{ GeV}}+E_\nu\left(1+\frac1{121}\right)
$$

Neglect $1/121$.

$$
E_\nu^2+242E_\nu-242\text{ GeV}=0
$$

$$
E_\nu=\left[-121\pm\frac12\sqrt{(242)^2+4(242)}\right]\text{ GeV}=-121\text{ GeV}+121.9\ldots\text{ GeV}\simeq1\text{ GeV}
$$

(+ root), a little less than 1 GeV. [The last handwritten digits of the intermediate 121.9… value are overwritten in the original scan.] Keeping $1/242$ and $1/121$ gives .98.
