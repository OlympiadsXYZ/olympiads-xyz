## Условие

A weightless rod is attached to a massive block with a mass M. An object with a mass m is attached to the rod by a weightless string of length R. The object can rotate in a vertical plane not touching the rod. The coefficient of friction between the block and the floor depends on the direction of sliding, $k1=\mu$, $k2 = \infty$. The object is launched with an initial velocity $V_0$ in the direction as shown in the picture 1. $V_0$ is sufficiently big so that the object makes at least one rotation around the rod.

In this problem you are asked to examine the movement of the system during the first rotation of the object. You can assume that the block does not detach from the floor and does not turn over and that the string is stretched while the object is moving. Let’s describe the position of the object by the angle $\varphi$ (Fig 1). Assume $M \gg m$.

**1.** **[*1 point*]** In this part of the question assume that the parameters are such that the block does not move at all. Derive the condition that will guarantee that the change in absolute velocity of the object is considerably smaller than $V_0$ in the process of rotation. **[1 т.]**

From this point onwards you can assume that the block’s velocity is considerably smaller than $V_0$, and that the change in absolute velocity of the object during first rotation is considerably smaller than $V_0$.

**2.** **[*2 points*]** Find the minimum initial velocity $V_0^{\min}$ which will cause the block to slide when the object is rotating. **[2 т.]**

**3. a)** For $V_0 > V_0^{\min}$ find:

**[*1 point*]** At which angle $\varphi_0$ sliding will start; **[1 т.]**

**3. b)** **[*1 point*]** At which angle $\psi$ the velocity of block’s motion *V* will be largest. **[1 т.]**

**4. a)** **[*1 point*]** Find $V_{max}$ which corresponds to the angle $\psi$ from b) above; **[1 т.]**

**4. b)** **[*1 point*]** Write down the condition for angle $\theta$, at which the block stops. **[1 т.]**

**5. a)** Numerical data:

$$
\mu = \frac{\pi}{2} - 1 \approx 0.57,
$$

$$
g = 10\ m/s^2,
$$

$$
M = 10\ kg
$$

$$
V_0 = 10\ m/s,
$$

$$
R = 0.1\ m,
$$

$$
m = 0.057\ kg.
$$

**[0.5 *points*]** Calculate $\varphi_0$, $\psi$ и $\theta$. **[0,5 т.]**

**5. b)** **[0.5 *points*]** Calculate $V_{max.}$ **[0,5 т.]**

**5. c)** **[*1 point*]** For the given numerical values, calculate the displacement of the block in one rotation of the object. **[1 т.]**

**5. d)** **[*1 point*]** In first non vanishing order, estimate the change in the velocity of the object at the lowest point after the first rotation in comparison to $V_0$. **[1 т.]**

***Note:***

The following formulas might be useful for solution:

$$
\cos\varphi + \mu\ \sin\varphi = \sqrt{1+\mu^2}\ \cos(\varphi - \alpha),
$$

$$
\cos\varphi - \mu\ \sin\varphi = \sqrt{1+\mu^2}\ \cos(\varphi + \alpha),
$$

where $\alpha = \operatorname{arctg}\mu = \arccos(1/\sqrt{1+\mu^2}) = \arcsin(\mu/\sqrt{1+\mu^2})$.

$$
\int\cos\varphi d\varphi = \sin\varphi,\ \int\sin\varphi d\varphi = -\cos\varphi
$$

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **1.** $\Delta V / V = 2gR/V_0^{2} \ll 1$
- **2.** $V_0^{\min} = \sqrt{\dfrac{MgR\mu}{m\sqrt{1+\mu^2}}}$
- **3. a)** $\varphi_0 = \operatorname{arctg}\mu + \arcsin\left(\dfrac{\mu Mg}{m\omega^2 R\sqrt{1+\mu^2}}\right)$
- **3. b)** $\psi = \operatorname{arctg}\mu + \pi - \arcsin\left(\dfrac{\mu Mg}{m\omega^2 R\sqrt{1+\mu^2}}\right)$
- **4. a)** $V_{\max} = 2\dfrac{T}{\omega M}\sqrt{1+\mu^2-\mu^2\left(\dfrac{Mg}{T}\right)^2} - \dfrac{\mu g}{\omega}\left(\pi - 2\arcsin\dfrac{\mu Mg}{T\sqrt{1+\mu^2}}\right)$
- **4. b)** $\dfrac{T}{\omega M}(\cos\varphi_0 + \mu\sin\varphi_0) + \dfrac{\mu g\varphi_0}{\omega} = \dfrac{T}{\omega M}(\cos\theta + \mu\sin\theta) + \dfrac{\mu g\theta}{\omega}$
- **5. a)** φ0 = 90°, ψ = 90° + 2arctg(0.57) ≈ 150°, θ = 180°
- **5. b)** 0.006 m/s
- **5. c)** 0.000053 m
- **5. d)** -0.0003

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

1. **[*1 point*]** From the energy conservation law for the object:

$$
V_0^{\,2} - V_{top}^{\,2} = 4gR \approx 2V_0\Delta V,\quad \Delta V / V = 2gR/V_0^{\,2} \ll 1
$$

2. **[2 *point*]** Maximal angle of deviation of the floor’s total reaction force (Normal force + friction force) from vertical is

$$
\alpha = \operatorname{arctg}\mu.
$$

Denote

$$
T = mV_0^{\,2}/R,\qquad \omega = V_0/R.
$$

From the picture above we can see (see Fig.2) that the maximum angle of deviation of

$$
M\vec{g} + \vec{T}
$$

is $\arcsin(T/Mg)$ when $T \le Mg$ ($M\vec{g} + \vec{T}$ is tangent to the circle). If the deviation angle of $M\vec{g} + \vec{T}$ from the vertical line is greater than $\alpha$, then the equilibrium is impossible and the block will start to slide. The condition that we are looking for is

$$
V_0^{\min} = \sqrt{\frac{MgR\mu}{m\sqrt{1+\mu^2}}}
$$

3. From Fig 2, the beginning of sliding corresponds to point B. The block accelerates on the portion of path *BC, and decelerates afterwards.*. Since the line intersects the rotation circle at two points, $\angle ABO < \pi/2$, and therefore from the sine theorem for the triangle *ABO* :

$$
\angle ABO = \arcsin\left(\mathrm{Mg}\frac{\sin\alpha}{m\omega^2\ R}\right) = \arcsin\left(\frac{\mu Mg}{m\omega^2\ R\sqrt{1+\mu^2}}\right)
$$

a) **[*1 point*]** Then

$$
\varphi_0 = \alpha + \angle ABO = \operatorname{arctg}\mu + \arcsin\left(\frac{\mu Mg}{m\omega^2\ R\sqrt{1+\mu^2}}\right).
$$

b) **[*1 point*]** Similarly

$$
\psi = \alpha + \pi - \angle ABO = \operatorname{arctg}\mu + \pi - \arcsin\left(\frac{\mu Mg}{m\omega^2\ R\sqrt{1+\mu^2}}\right).
$$

4. To calculate the acceleration we can first decompose the forces into x and y components and write down Newton’s second law

$$
F_{\mathrm{friction}} = \mu\ \mathrm{N} = \mu\,(M\mathrm{g} + T\cos\varphi)
$$

$$
M\,\mathrm{a}(\varphi) = T\sin\varphi - F_{\mathrm{friction}} = T\sin\varphi - \mu\,(M\mathrm{g} + T\cos\varphi)
$$

Then

$$
V(\varphi) = \int a(\varphi)dt = \int\frac{a(\varphi)d\varphi}{\omega}
$$

Integration gives us:

$$
V(\varphi) = -\left(\frac{T}{\omega M}(\cos\varphi + \mu\sin\varphi) + \frac{\mu g\varphi}{\omega}\right) + \left(\frac{T}{\omega M}(\cos\varphi_0 + \mu\sin\varphi_0) + \frac{\mu g\varphi_0}{\omega}\right).
$$

a) **[*1point*]** Then

$$
V_{\mathrm{max}} = \mathrm{v}(\psi) = 2\frac{T}{\omega M}\sqrt{1+\mu^2-\mu^2\left(\frac{Mg}{T}\right)^2}\ -\ \frac{\mu g}{\omega}\left(\pi - 2\arcsin\ \frac{\mu Mg}{T\sqrt{1+\mu^2}}\right)
$$

b) **[*1 point*]** The condition for $\theta$ : $V(\theta) = 0$

$$
\frac{T}{\omega M}(\cos\varphi_0 + \mu\sin\varphi_0) + \frac{\mu g\varphi_0}{\omega} = \frac{T}{\omega M}(\cos\theta + \mu\sin\theta) + \frac{\mu g\theta}{\omega}
$$

5. The values are such that $\dfrac{Mg}{T} = \dfrac{1}{\mu} = 1/(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - 1)$.

a) **[0.5 *point*]** Using this equality we get :

$$
\varphi_0 = 90^{0},\ \psi = 90^{0} + 2\operatorname{arctg}(0.57) \approx 150^{0},\ \theta = 180^{0}
$$

b) **[0.5 *point*]**

$$
V_{\mathrm{max}} = 2\frac{\mu g}{\omega}(\mu - \operatorname{arctg}\mu) \approx 6*10^{-3}\mathrm{m/s} \ll V_0
$$

c) **[*1 point*]** Displacement is

$$
\mathrm{S} = \int\frac{V(\varphi)d\varphi}{\omega}.
$$

Plug in

$$
\mu = \frac{\pi}{2} - 1,\ \varphi_0 = \frac{\pi}{2},\ \theta = \pi,
$$

We get

$$
\mathrm{S} = \left(\frac{\pi}{2} - 1\right)\left(2 + \frac{\pi^2}{8} - \pi\right)\mathrm{g}/\omega^2 \approx 5.3*10^{-5}\mathrm{m}.
$$

d) **[*1 point*]** The change in object’s velocity occurs because the kinetic energy is lowered due to work by friction force. In first nonvanishing order, the change in velocity is determined from the Law of change in energy, where the work of friction force is calculated assuming constant absolute velocity of the object:

$$
mV_0\Delta V = \int F_{friction}ds = -\int_{\varphi_0}^{\theta}\mu(\mathrm{Mg} + \mathrm{T}\cos\varphi)\,\mathrm{v}(\varphi)\frac{d\varphi}{\omega}.
$$

By order of magnitude

$$
\frac{\Delta V}{V_0} \propto -\frac{\mu MgS}{mV_0^{\,2}} \propto -5*10^{-4}.
$$

The exact value: $-3*10^{-4}$
