**Introduction**

Water sprayers are found at various places such as agriculture farms, green parks or urban areas for functional purposes or for aesthetic art installation. Consider a hemispherical shaped fountain sprayer of radius $r$ at the height $h$ from the ground as shown in a cross-sectional diagram in **Fig. 1**. Let the radius of the hemisphere to be small compared to the range $R$, hence can be treated as a point source. However there are $\rho(\theta)$ number of holes per unit area at angle $\theta$. The water spurts in all directions at the same initial velocity $v_o$.

### Part A: Uniformly Distributed Holes on the Surface of the Hemisphere (6 т.)

First consider the holes distribution on the surface of the hemisphere to be uniform (i.e. $\rho(\theta)$ is a constant).

**A.1** Express the instantaneous position $x(t)$ and $y(t)$ of a water element (can be treated as a stream of particles) launched at velocity $v_o$ at angle $\theta$. **[0,5 т.]**

**A.2** Derive the water trajectory relation $y = y(x, \theta)$ and express in a same type of trigonometric function (e.g. in terms of sine, cosine or tangent ONLY). Note that $\theta > 0$. **[1 т.]**

**A.3** The envelope is the minimal boundary which encloses all water trajectories (i.e. no water will be found beyond this boundary). Derive the equation for the envelope for this system. Express your answer as y(x). Sketch the envelope over the trajectories. **[3 т.]**

**A.4** Calculate the range $R = R(\theta)$ as a function of angle $\theta$. **[1 т.]**

**A.5** Letting $h \to 0$ in the range obtained in **A.4**, show that $R = \frac{v_o^2}{g} \sin 2\theta$. **[0,5 т.]**

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="part-b">

### Part B: Non-Uniformly Distributed Holes on the Surface of the Hemisphere (4 т.)

Now, consider the area of holes per unit area $\rho(\theta)$ to be non-uniformly distributed and depended on the angle $\theta$.

**B.1** Using the results from part **A.4** above, calculate the elemental area $dA_W$ of an annulus (ring) of radius $R$ and width $dR$ of the water hitting the ground. Express your answer in R, R' and d$\theta$. [hint: consider R'=$\frac{dR}{d\theta}$ ] **[1 т.]**

**B.2** Determine the relation for $\rho(\theta)$ that gives a uniform spray coverage pattern on the ground by considering elemental area on the hemisphere in proportional to the elemental area of the water spray on the ground. Express your answer in terms of R and its derivative. [hint: consider condition where $R'(\theta) > 0$]. **[1 т.]**

**B.3** Letting $h = 0$, calculate explicitly the relation for $\rho(\theta)$ that gives a uniform spray coverage pattern for $\theta < 45^{\circ}$. **[2 т.]**

</ProblemSection>

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **Part A: Uniformly Distributed Holes on the Surface of the Hemisphere, A.1** $x(t) = (v_o\cos\theta)t,\quad y(t) = h + (v_o\sin\theta)t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2$
- **Part A: Uniformly Distributed Holes on the Surface of the Hemisphere, A.2** $y = h + x\tan\theta - \frac{gx^2}{2v_o^2}(1 + \tan^2\theta)$
- **Part A: Uniformly Distributed Holes on the Surface of the Hemisphere, A.3** $y = h + \frac{v_o^2}{2g} - \frac{gx^2}{2v_o^2}$
- **Part A: Uniformly Distributed Holes on the Surface of the Hemisphere, A.4** $R = \frac{\tan\theta \pm \sqrt{\tan^2\theta + 4\frac{g}{2v_o^2}(1 + \tan^2\theta)h}}{\frac{g}{v_o^2}(1 + \tan^2\theta)}$
- **Part A: Uniformly Distributed Holes on the Surface of the Hemisphere, A.5** Доказателство (Eq. (1.22)–(1.24) в официалното решение).
- **Part B: Non-Uniformly Distributed Holes on the Surface of the Hemisphere, B.1** $dA_W = 2\pi R R'(\theta)\,d\theta$
- **Part B: Non-Uniformly Distributed Holes on the Surface of the Hemisphere, B.2** $\rho(\theta) \sim \frac{R(\theta)R'(\theta)}{r^2\cos\theta}$
- **Part B: Non-Uniformly Distributed Holes on the Surface of the Hemisphere, B.3** $\rho(\theta) \sim \frac{\sin(4\theta)}{\cos(\theta)}$

### Part A: Uniformly Distributed Holes on the Surface of the Hemisphere

**A.1** Given the radius of the hemisphere is small compared to the range, that we can treat all the different water sprouts emanate from a point source at the center of the hemisphere at the same initial velocity but at different angles $\theta$. Note that the distribution of the holes on the surface of the hemisphere is uniform.

Consider the motion in *x*- and *y*-directions with coordinate (*x*,*y*) as indicated in the **Fig. 1**.

Horizontal motion – no acceleration

$$
x = x(t) = (v_o \cos\theta)t \qquad (1.1)
$$

**[0.2pt]**

Vertical motion – ‘particle’ in free fall under gravity, $g$

$$
y = y(t) = h + (v_o \sin\theta)t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2 \qquad (1.2)
$$

**[0.3pt]** **[0,5 т.]**

**A.2** Equation of water trajectory (or path of the projectile) is obtained by eliminating time $t$ from equations (1.1) and (1.2). **[0.1pt]**

Using Eq. (1.1), we get $t = \frac{x}{v_0 \cos\theta}$ and substituting $t$ in Eq.(1.2) we obtain **[0.1pt]**

$$
y = h + x\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} - \frac{gx^2}{2v_o^2}\frac{1}{(\cos^2\theta)} \qquad (1.3)
$$

**[0.2pt]**

$$
= h + x\tan\theta - \frac{gx^2}{2v_o^2}(\sec^2\theta) \qquad (1.4)
$$

**[0.2pt]**

Using trigonometry identity: $\sec^2\theta = 1 + \tan^2\theta$, thus we write: **[0.1pt]**

$$
y = h + x\tan\theta - \frac{gx^2}{2v_o^2}(1 + \tan^2\theta) \qquad (1.5)
$$

**[0.3pt]**

or

$$
y' = y - h = x\tan\theta - \frac{gx^2}{2v_o^2}(1 + \tan^2\theta) \qquad (1.6)
$$

The equation of water trajectory is given by Eq. (1.5) (with reference to the ground) or Eq. (1.6) with reference to the base of the hemisphere. **[1 т.]**

**A.3** We rewrite the trajectory equation in the form of quadratic function in $\tan\theta$ **[0.2pt]**

$$
\left(\frac{gx^2}{2v_o^2}\right)\tan^2\theta - x\tan\theta + \left(y' + \frac{gx^2}{2v_o^2}\right) = 0 \qquad (1.7)
$$

**[0.5pt]**

where $y' = y - h$.

Let $\tan\theta = u$, thus we express Eq. (1.7) as a quadratic function in $u$:

$$
au^2 + bu + c = 0 \qquad (1.8)
$$

**[0.3pt]**

where

$$
a = \frac{gx^2}{2v_o^2}, \qquad b = -x, \qquad c = y' + \frac{gx^2}{2v_o^2}. \qquad (1.9)
$$

The quadratic equation Eq.(1.8) must have a real solution for $u$, i.e

$$
u = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} \qquad (1.10)
$$

**[0.2pt]**

Or the discriminant is non-negative, i.e. $b^2 - 4ac \geq 0$, which gives. **[0.2pt]**

$$
x^2 - 4\left(\frac{gx^2}{2v_o^2}\right)\left(y' + \frac{gx^2}{2v_o^2}\right) \geq 0 \qquad (1.11)
$$

**[0.4pt]**

Re-arranging Eq.(1.11) for y’ to get

$$
-y' \geq \frac{gx^2}{2v_o^2} - \frac{v_o^2}{2g} \qquad (1.12)
$$

or

$$
y' \leq \frac{v_o^2}{2g} - \frac{gx^2}{2v_o^2} \qquad (1.13)
$$

**[0.2pt]**

The water trajectory follows the inequality in (1.13) and the envelop of different water trajectories with different launch angles follow the parabolic equation:

$$
y' = \frac{v_o^2}{2g} - \frac{gx^2}{2v_o^2} \qquad (1.14)
$$

**[0.2pt]**

Or

$$
y = h + \frac{v_o^2}{2g} - \frac{gx^2}{2v_o^2} \qquad (1.15)
$$

**[0.3pt]**

The sketch of the envelop of water trajectory is shown below:

<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/apho-2024-theory-th/p1-sol-fig1.png" alt="Sketch: a hemispherical fountain on a vertical post of height h above the x axis, origin (0,0) at the foot of the post; blue parabolic water trajectories leave the hemisphere at various angles θ and land on the ground, one at horizontal distance R; a thick black parabola touching all trajectories from above is marked with an arrow and the label y = h + v_o²/2g − gx²/2v_o²; axes x and y." width="1515" height="779" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


**[0.5pt]** **[3 т.]**

**A.4** Recall the trajectory Eq. (1.5) and set $y = 0$ to obtain the horizontal range $x = R$:

$$
0 = h + R\tan\theta - \frac{gR^2}{2v_o^2}(1 + \tan^2\theta) \qquad (1.16)
$$

**[0.2pt]**

$$
\frac{g}{2v_o^2}(1 + \tan^2\theta)R^2 - (\tan\theta)R - h = 0 \qquad (1.17)
$$

**[0.2pt]**

which is a quadratic equation

$$
a'R^2 + b'R + c' = 0 \qquad (1.18)
$$

**[0.2pt]**

with

$$
a' = \frac{g}{2v_o^2}(1 + \tan^2\theta), \quad b' = -\tan\theta\,, \quad c = h., \qquad (1.19)
$$

The solution of Eq. (1.18) is the range of the water trajectory on the ground.

$$
R = \frac{-b' \pm \sqrt{b'^2 - 4a'c'}}{2a'} \qquad (1.20)
$$

**[0.1pt]**

$$
R = \frac{\tan\theta \pm \sqrt{\tan^2\theta + 4\frac{g}{2v_o^2}(1 + \tan^2\theta)h}}{\frac{g}{v_o^2}(1 + \tan^2\theta)} \qquad (1.21)
$$

**[0.3pt]** **[1 т.]**

**A.5** Letting $h = 0$ in Eq.(1.21) gives

$$
R = \frac{\tan\theta \pm \sqrt{\tan^2\theta}}{\frac{g}{v_o^2}(1 + \tan^2\theta)} \qquad (1.22)
$$

**[0.2pt]**

$$
R = \frac{2\tan\theta}{\frac{g}{v_o^2}(1 + \tan^2\theta)} = \frac{2\tan\theta}{\frac{g}{v_o^2}(\sec^2\theta)} \qquad (1.23)
$$

$$
= \frac{v_o^2}{g}2\sin\theta\cos\theta = \frac{v_o^2}{g}\sin 2\theta \qquad (1.24)
$$

**[0.3pt]** **[0,5 т.]**

### Part B: Non-Uniformly Distributed Holes on the Surface of the Hemisphere

**B.1** Recall the range from (**A.4**) above (Eq. (1.21))

$$
R = R(\theta) = \frac{\tan\theta \pm \sqrt{\tan^2\theta + 4\frac{g}{2v_o^2}(1 + \tan^2\theta)h}}{\frac{g}{v_o^2}(1 + \tan^2\theta)} \qquad (1.21)
$$

**[0.2pt]**

Let

$$
\frac{dR}{d\theta} = R'(\theta) \qquad (1.25)
$$

**[0.2pt]**

and thus

$$
dR = R'(\theta)d\theta. \qquad (1.26)
$$

**[0.2pt]**

There is no need to determine the R' explicitely. Answers can be left in R'. Consider an annulus (or ring) water on the ground with radius *R* and thickness *dR* where the element area of annulus $dA_W$ is given by

$$
dA_W = 2\pi R \times dR \qquad (1.27)
$$

**[0.2pt]**

$$
= 2\pi RR'(\theta)d\theta \qquad (1.28)
$$

**[0.2pt]** **[1 т.]**

**B.2** Number of holes in on the elemental area of annulus $dA_H$, on the hemisphere, of radius $r$ at angle $\theta$ with angular width $d\theta$ is

$$
dA_H = \rho(\theta) \times 2\pi(r\ \cos\theta)rd\theta \qquad (1.29)
$$

**[0.2pt]**

In order the water sprouts to spray uniformly on the ground $dA_W \sim dA_H$ such that

$$
dA_H = \rho(\theta) \times 2\pi(r\cos\theta)rd\theta \sim 2\pi RR'(\theta)d\theta \sim dA_W \qquad (1.30)
$$

**[0.4pt]**

And to ensure the expression is independent of $\rho(\theta)$, we express

$$
\rho\left(\theta\right) \sim \frac{R(\theta)R'(\theta)}{r^2\cos\theta} \qquad (1.31)
$$

**[0.3pt]**

where $R(\theta)$ and $R'(\theta)$ are given by Eq. (1.21) and Eq.(1.25), respectively.

If the distribution of the holes on the hemisphere follows Eq. (1.31), then the water spray on the ground will be uniform (independent of angle). The condition set for angle $\theta$ does not affect the general expression in (1.31). **[0.1pt]** **[1 т.]**

**B.3** When $h = 0$, the range become simple to estimate, namely from Eq. (1.24) we have

$$
R(\theta) = \frac{v_o^2}{g}\sin 2\theta
$$

**[0.2pt]**

and

$$
\frac{dR(\theta)}{d\theta} = \frac{2v_o^2}{g}\cos 2\theta \qquad (1.32)
$$

**[0.2pt]**

$$
dR = \frac{2v_o^2}{g}\cos 2\theta\ d\theta \qquad (1.33)
$$

**[0.1pt]**

Consider an annulus (or ring) water on the ground with radius $R$ and thickness $dR$ where the element area of annulus $dA_W$ is given by

$$
dA_W = 2\pi R \times dR
$$

$$
= 2\pi\left(\frac{v_o^2}{g}\sin 2\theta\right)\left(\frac{2v_o^2}{g}\cos 2\theta\right)d\theta \qquad (1.34)
$$

**[0.2pt]**

$$
= 2\pi\left(\frac{v_o^2}{g}\right)^2 2\sin 2\theta\cos 2\theta\ d\theta \qquad (1.35)
$$

**[0.2pt]**

$$
= 2\pi\left(\frac{v_o^2}{g}\right)^2 \sin 4\theta\ d\theta \qquad (1.36)
$$

**[0.1pt]**

Thus, elemental area of water annulus $dA_W$ follows

$$
dA_W \propto \sin 4\theta\ d\theta. \qquad (1.37)
$$

**[0.1pt]**

Recall from **B.2** the number of holes in on the elemental area of annulus $dA_H$, on the hemisphere, of radius $r$ at angle $\theta$ with angular width $d\theta$ is **[0.1pt]**

$$
dA_H = \rho(\theta) \times 2\pi(r\ \cos(\theta))rd\theta \sim \rho(\theta)\cos(\theta)d\theta.
$$

**[0.1pt]**

In order the water sprouts to spray uniformly on the ground $dA_W \propto dA_H$ such that **[0.1pt]**

$$
dA_H \sim \rho(\theta)\cos\theta d\theta \sim \sin 4\theta\ d\theta. \qquad (1.38)
$$

**[0.3pt]**

And to ensure the expression is independent of $\rho(\theta)$, we express

$$
\rho\left(\theta\right) \sim \frac{\sin(4\theta)}{\cos(\theta)}. \qquad (1.39)
$$

**[0.2pt]**

If the distribution of the holes per unit area on the hemisphere follows Eq. (1.39), then the water sprayed on the ground will be uniform (independent of angle). The condition set on the angle only to avoid duplication of water hitting the same area for $0^o < \theta \leq 45^o\ or\ 45^o < \theta < 90^o$. **[0.1pt]** **[2 т.]**
