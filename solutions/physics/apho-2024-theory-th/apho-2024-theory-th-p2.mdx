**Introduction**

When light is incident upon a dielectric interface, it will be reflected and refracted, depending on the incident angle of the light and the refractive indices of the dielectric media as shown in **Fig. 1**. The refraction of light is governed by the Snell’s law,

$$
n_1 \sin \phi_1 = n_2 \sin \phi_2 \qquad (1)
$$

where $n_1$ and $n_2$ are the refractive indices of the lower and upper parts of the boundary, $\phi_1$ and $\phi_2$ are the angles that the light ray makes with the normal of the boundary.

### Part C: Light Propagation Through an Optical Fibre (5 т.)

Optical fibre is formed by surrounding the medium with refractive index, $n_1$ with a lower refractive index medium, $n_2$, as shown in **Fig. 4** below. The medium with refractive index, $n_1$ is known as the fibre core, and the medium with refractive index, $n_2$ is known as the fibre cladding. The refractive index, $n_3$, is typically the refractive index of air ($n_3$ = 1).

Consider a ray that propagates along the meridional plane (plane that cuts across the fibre axis), formulate the relationship between the angle, $\theta_a$ and the refractive indices. **[2 т.]**

**C.2** Optical fibers are not always aligned in a straight line but need to be bent to go through different spaces. Given the fiber core diameter of 50 $\mu\mathrm{m}$, if the minimum bending radius on the optical fiber is 1.0 $\mathrm{cm}$, how much will $\theta_a$ change? **[2,6 т.]**

### Part A: Light Propagation Through a Semi-Sphere

**A.1** Using Snell’s law, larger angle of refraction corresponds to larger refractive index that the light sees in the semi-sphere ($n_1$). **[0.2pt]**

This means that the colour with a smaller angle of refraction propagates faster in the semi-sphere, which is colour *a*. **[0.3pt]** **[0,5 т.]**

**A.2** The light rays no longer exit the bottom of semi-sphere when the ray is refracted by $90^{\mathrm{o}}$ with respect to the normal. **[0.2pt]**

Using Snell’s law,

$$
n_x\sin\theta_i = n_2\sin(90^{\circ})
$$

**[0.2pt]**

Replace “x” with “a” and “b” for the condition of ray a and ray b, respectively.

For ray a,

$$
n_a\sin(50^{\circ}) = 1.0\sin(90^{\circ})
$$

$$
n_a = 1.305
$$

**[0.2pt]**

For ray b,

$$
n_b\sin(45^{\circ}) = 1.0\sin(90^{\circ})
$$

$$
n_b = 1.414
$$

**[0.2pt]**

Difference in refractive index, Δn = **0.109** **[0.2pt]** **[1 т.]**

</ProblemSection>

### Part B: Light Propagation Through a Cylindrical Rod

**B.1** Given $n_1$ = 1.5, $n_2$ = 1.0, $n_3$ = 1.4,

Identify 2 conditions that need to be obeyed: **[0.2pt]**

**#1: Light incident at point A should be totally reflected.**

$$
n_1\sin\phi_1 > n_2\sin(90^{\circ})
$$

$$
\phi_1 > \sin^{-1}\frac{1.0}{1.5} = 41.81^{\circ}
$$

**[0.3pt]**

The condition for angle $\theta_1 < 90^{\mathrm{o}} - 41.81^{\mathrm{o}} = 48.19^{\mathrm{o}}$ **[0.2pt]**

Using Snell’s law,

$$
\theta < \sin^{-1}\frac{1.5}{1.4}\sin(48.19^{\circ}) = 53^{\circ}
$$

**[0.2pt]**

**#2: Light incident at point B should be totally reflected.**

$$
n_1\sin\theta_2 > n_2\sin(90^{\circ})
$$

$$
\theta_2 > \sin^{-1}\frac{1.0}{1.5} = 41.81^{\circ}
$$

**[0.3pt]**

The condition for angle $q_1 > 41.81^{\mathrm{o}}$ **[0.2pt]**

Using Snell's Law

$$
\theta > \sin^{-1}\frac{1.5}{1.4}\sin(41.81^{\circ}) = 45.58^{\circ}
$$

**[0.2pt]**

Therefore, the incident angle, $q$, where light is totally reflected back to the polymer is $45.58^{\mathrm{o}} < q < 53^{\mathrm{o}}$. **[0.4pt]** **[2 т.]**

**B.2 (i)** When right end of rod is coated with oil with $n_4$ = 1.6, condition #2 changes. **[0.2pt]**

Light incident on point B will always be refracted. **[0.2pt]**

Light CANNOT be totally reflected back to the polymer. **[0.2pt]** **[0,6 т.]**

**B.2 (ii)** When the setup is placed in water, **$n_2$ = 1.33**. Both condition #1 and #2 change. **[0.2pt]**

#1:

$$
\phi_1 > \sin^{-1}\frac{1.33}{1.5} = 62.46^{\circ}
$$

**[0.2pt]**

$\theta_1 < 90^{\mathrm{o}} - 62.46^{\mathrm{o}} = 27.54^{\mathrm{o}}$

Using Snell’s law,

$$
\theta < \sin^{-1}\frac{1.5}{1.4}\sin(27.54^{\circ}) = 29.7^{\circ}
$$

#2:

$$
\theta_2 > \sin^{-1}\frac{1.33}{1.5} = 62.46^{\circ}
$$

Using Snell’s law,

$$
\theta > \sin^{-1}\frac{1.5}{1.4}\sin(62.46^{\circ}) = 71.81^{\circ}
$$

**[0.2pt]**

As conditions #1 and #2 does not overlap. **[0.1pt]**

Again, light CANNOT be totally reflected back to the polymer. **[0.2pt]** **[0,9 т.]**

### Part C: Light Propagation Through an Optical Fibre

**C.1**


<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '56.5%', '--fig-max': '362px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/apho-2024-theory-th/p2-sol-fig2.png" alt="Longitudinal cross section of an optical fibre: core n1 between two cladding layers n2, outside medium n3 on the left; a ray arrives at angle θa to the dashed z axis, is refracted into the core at angle θc and meets the core–cladding boundary at angle ϕc to the dashed normal; a blue dashed arrow shows the refracted ray running along the boundary." width="1131" height="513" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


In order for light to not refractive out from the fiber, the angle of refraction that the light makes with the normal of the fiber core/cladding boundary needs to be $90^{\mathrm{o}}$. Using Snell’s law,

$$
n_1\sin\phi_c = n_2\sin(90^{\circ}) \qquad (1)
$$

**[0.4pt]**

Taking the square of eq. (1), we have,

$$
n_1^2\sin^2\phi_c = n_2^2
$$

Using the trigonometry identity, we have, **[0.2pt]**

$$
n_1^2(1 - \cos^2\phi_c) = n_2^2
$$

Rearranging, we got,

$$
n_1^2\cos^2\phi_c = n_1^2 - n_2^2
$$

Or, **[0.2pt]**

$$
n_1^2\sin^2\theta_c = n_1^2 - n_2^2 \qquad (2)
$$

Taking the square root of eq. (2), we have,

$$
n_1\sin\theta_c = \sqrt{n_1^2 - n_2^2}
$$

**[0.4pt]**

With this, we can use Snell’s law to calculate the maximum angle of light incident,

$$
n_3\sin\theta_a = n_1\sin\theta_c = \sqrt{n_1^2 - n_2^2}
$$

**[0.4pt]**

$$
\theta_a = \sin^{-1}\frac{\sqrt{n_1^2 - n_2^2}}{n_3}
$$

**[0.4pt]**

or $\theta_a = \sin^{-1}\sqrt{{n_1}^2 - {n_2}^2}$ **[2 т.]**

**C.2**


<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '69%', '--fig-max': '442px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/apho-2024-theory-th/p2-sol-fig3.png" alt="A fibre with core n1 of width 50 μm between cladding layers n2 that bends downward on the right about a centre C far below; a ray enters at angle θa′, travels at θc and ϕc′ in the straight section, reaches point B on the lower boundary (angle ϕc′ to the normal) and point A on the upper boundary of the bent section (angle ϕc to the normal), where part of the light leaves the core; blue dotted lines join C to B and C to A." width="1382" height="1070" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


- Identifying the correct relationship between $\phi_c$and $\phi'_c$ **[0.4pt]**

- Find the relationship between $\phi_c$ and $\phi_c$’:

- Using the Law of Sine,

$$
\sin\phi_c = \frac{CB}{CA}\sin(\pi - \phi_c')
$$

**[0.4pt]**

- For light guiding at point A,

$$
\sin\phi_c = \frac{n_2}{n_1}
$$

**[0.4pt]**

CB = 10000-25 = 9975 $\mu$m

CA = 10000+25 = 10025 $\mu$m **[0.4pt]**

$$
\sin(\pi - \phi_c') = \sin(\phi_c')
$$

Therefore,

$$
\sin(\phi_c') = \frac{10025n_2}{9975n_1}
$$

**[0.4pt]**

Rearrange into eq.(1) and derive following part (a):

$$
a.n_1\sin(\phi_c') = n_2, \qquad where\ a = \frac{9975}{10025}
$$

$$
a^2\,n_1^2\sin^2(\phi_c') = n_2^2
$$

$$
a^2n_1^2(1 - \cos^2\phi'_c) = n_2^2
$$

$$
a^2n_1^2\cos^2\phi'_c = a^2n_1^2 - n_2^2
$$

$$
a^2n_1^2\sin^2\theta'_c = a^2n_1^2 - n_2^2
$$

$$
n_1\sin\theta'_c = \frac{1}{a}\sqrt{a^2n_1^2 - n_2^2}
$$

**[0.4pt]**

To calculate the new angle of acceptance,

$$
n_3\sin\theta'_a = n_1\sin\theta'_c = \frac{1}{a}\sqrt{a^2n_1^2 - n_2^2}
$$

$$
\theta'_a = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{10025}{9975n_3}\sqrt{a^2n_1^2 - n_2^2}\right)
$$

**[0.2pt]** **[2,6 т.]**

**C.3** For case C.1: State that $n_3$ = 1;

$$
\sqrt{n_1^2 - n_2^2} = \sqrt{1.45^2 - 1.44^2} = 0.17
$$

**[0.1pt]**

$$
\theta_a = \sin^{-1}0.17 = 9.79^{0}
$$

**[0.1pt]**

For case C.2: Substitute,

$$
\theta'_a = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{10025}{9975}\sqrt{\left(\frac{9975}{10025}\right)^2 1.45^2 - 1.44^2}\right)
$$

**[0.1pt]**

$$
= 5.15^{\circ}
$$

**[0.1pt]** **[0,4 т.]**

</ProblemSection>
