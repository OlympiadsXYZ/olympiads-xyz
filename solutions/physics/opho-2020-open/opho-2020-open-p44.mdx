## Условие

A planet has a radius of $10\ \mathrm{km}$ and a uniform density of $5\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$. A powerful bomb detonates at the center of the planet, releasing $8.93 \times 10^{17}\ \mathrm{J}$ of energy, causing the planet to separate into three large sections each with equal masses. You may model each section as a perfect sphere of radius $r'$. The initial and final distances between the centers of any two given sections is $2r'$. How long does it take for the three sections to collide again?

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- 136000 s

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**Solution:** Due to conservation of momentum, the three masses must form an equilateral triangle at all times. Let us determine the force as a function of $r$, the distance between each mass and the center.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '54.6%', '--fig-max': '349px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/opho-2020-open/p44-sol-fig1.png" alt="Three point masses at the vertices of an equilateral triangle, joined to the center by dotted lines; one dotted line is labelled r. From the left mass two arrows point towards the other two masses." width="1092" height="1268" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

The vector sum of the net force on any individual mass is

$$
F = \frac{Gm^2}{d^2}\sqrt{2 - 2\cos 120^{\circ}} = \frac{\sqrt{3}Gm^2}{d^2}
$$

where $d$ is the distance between the mass and the center.

$$
d^2 = 3r^2
$$

The net force is thus

$$
F = \frac{Gm^2}{\sqrt{3}r^2}
$$

The system behaves as if there was a stationary mass $m' = m/\sqrt{3}$ at the center, simplifying the problem greatly into a restricted two body system. Next, we need to figure out the height of the apoapsis. This can be done via conservation of energy.

$$
E_{\mathrm{binding,initial}} + E = 3E_{\mathrm{binding,final}} - \frac{Gm^2}{\sqrt{3}\ell} \implies \ell = -\frac{Gm^2}{\sqrt{3}\left(-\frac{3GM^2}{5R} + 8.93\cdot 10^{17} - \left(-3\frac{3Gm^2}{5r_f}\right)\right)} = 101{,}000\ \mathrm{m}
$$

If you have a stationary mass $M$ at the center. The time it takes for an object to fall into it is:

$$
T = \pi\sqrt{\frac{\ell^3}{8GM}}
$$

our time will be double this, and the mass in the center will be $M = m/\sqrt{3}$. So plugging in numbers gives:

$$
t = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{l^3}{8G\left(\frac{m}{\sqrt{3}}\right)}} = 138{,}000
$$

If we take into account a nonzero radius so final separation is $10/\cos(30^{\circ})$ km, then the answer should be:

$$
-2\int_{l}^{\frac{10000}{\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{xl}{2G\left(\frac{m}{\sqrt{3}}\right)(l - x)}}\right)dx = \boxed{136{,}000\ \mathrm{s}}
$$

Note if you set the upper bound to zero, you get the same answer as before. Both these answers will be accepted.


</Spoiler>
