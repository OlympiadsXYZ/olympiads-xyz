*XXII International Zhautykov Olympiad/Theoretical Competition · 11 януари 2026 г. · 10 т.*

## Условие

This problem consists of three independent parts.

**1.1** A thin cylindrical core of radius $R$ has a strip of foil tightly wound around it in a thin layer. The foil has thickness $\delta$, mass $m$, and length $L = 10.0\ \mathrm{m}$. One end of the foil is fixed to the core, while the other end is attached to the edge of a thin horizontal platform $\Pi$. Initially, the roll (the core with the wound foil) is held so that its axis $O$ is horizontal and at the same level as the platform. The roll is then released, and under the action of gravity it begins to unwind. Assuming that $\delta \ll R \ll L$, find the length $x_0$ of the unwound part of the foil for which the force $T_0$ acting on the platform is equal to the weight of the roll, $mg$. Also determine the acceleration $a$ and the velocity $v$ of the downward motion of the roll's center of mass at that instant. **[3 т.]**

**1.2** The readings of a multimeter ($\Omega$) operating in ohmmeter mode and connected to a circuit change with time according to the law $R_\Omega(t) = A - Be^{-t/\tau_0}$, where $A = 100\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, $B = 40\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, and $\tau_0 = 100\ \mathrm{s}$. Using these data, determine the values of the resistances $r$ and $R$, as well as the capacitance $C$ of the capacitor, which is initially uncharged. Also find the amount of heat $Q$ released in the resistor $R$ during the measurement time $t \gg \tau_0$.

**1.3** An observer (the eye) is located at a distance $L = 55\ \mathrm{cm}$ from a convex–concave lens and observes images of a distant street lamp formed due to reflections from both surfaces of the lens (see photo). The lamp and the eye are positioned close to the principal optical axis of the lens on the side of the concave surface. The radius of curvature of the concave surface is $r = 7\ \mathrm{cm}$, and the radius of curvature of the convex surface is $R = 28\ \mathrm{cm}$. Upon observation, two images of the lamp are visible: one real and the other virtual. The ratio of the apparent angular sizes of these images is $\gamma = \varphi_1/\varphi_2 = 0.1$, where $\varphi_1$ is the angular size of the real image and $\varphi_2$ is the angular size of the virtual image. Find the optical power $D$ of the lens. **[3 т.]**

**Problem 1 (10.0 points)**

**Problem 1.1 (3.0 points)**

Let the $x$-axis be directed downwards, and the initial position of the roll's center of mass correspond to the origin. If the velocity of the roll's center of mass at a given moment in time is $v$, then its kinetic energy is
$$
E_k = \frac{m}{L}(L-x)v^2. \qquad (1)
$$
On the other hand, the potential energy of the roll relative to the initial position is equal to
$$
E_p = -\frac{m}{L}g\frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{m}{L}g(L-x)x. \qquad (2)
$$
At the initial moment of time the total energy is zero, therefore, according to the conservation law we get
$$
E_k + E_p = 0, \qquad (3)
$$
from which the dependence of the speed of the roll center of mass on the coordinate is given by
$$
v^2(x) = g\left[x + \frac{x^2}{2(L-x)}\right]. \qquad (4)
$$
The acceleration of the roll is determined by the expression
$$
a(x) = \frac{dv}{dt} = v\frac{dv}{dx}, \qquad (5)
$$
which after substitution (4) gives the following dependence
$$
a(x) = \frac{g}{4}\left[1 + \frac{L^2}{(L-x)^2}\right]. \qquad (6)
$$
The total momentum of the roll is directed along the $x$-axis and is equal to
$$
p = \frac{m}{L}(L-x)v, \qquad (7)
$$
and therefore the change in momentum over time takes the form
$$
\frac{dp}{dt} = -\frac{m}{L}v^2 + \frac{m}{L}(L-x)a. \qquad (8)
$$
According to the conditions of the problem, the force acting on the platform from the roll is equal to the force of gravity, which means that the total external force on the roll itself is zero, and we obtain
$$
\frac{dp}{dt} = 0. \qquad (9)
$$
Solving together (4), (6), (8) and (9), we derive the quadratic equation
$$
x + \frac{x^2}{2(L-x)} = \frac{1}{2}\left(L + \frac{x^2}{2(L-x)}\right), \qquad (10)
$$
whose positive root equals to
$$
x_0 = \frac{3-\sqrt{3}}{3}L = 4.23\ \mathrm{m}. \qquad (11)
$$
Substituting into (4) and (6) provides the final answers
$$
v(x_0) = \frac{\sqrt{gL}}{\sqrt[4]{3}} = 7.52\ \mathrm{m/s}, \qquad (12)
$$
$$
a(x_0) = g = 9.80\ \mathrm{m/s^2}. \qquad (13)
$$

**Problem 1.2 (4.0 points)**

At the initial moment of time $t = 0$ the capacitor is not charged and the voltage drops across the connected resistances are the same, that is, they are connected in parallel, therefore
$$
\frac{1}{R_0} = \frac{1}{R} + \frac{1}{r}, \qquad (1)
$$
and the ohmmeter readings are
$$
R_0 = R(0) = A - B. \qquad (2)
$$
At time $t = \infty$ the capacitor is fully charged and no current flows through it, therefore
$$
R_\infty = r, \qquad (3)
$$
and the ohmmeter readings are
$$
R_\infty = R(\infty) = A, \qquad (4)
$$
Solving (1)-(4) together, we obtain
$$
r = 100\ \mathrm{k\Omega}, \qquad (5)
$$
$$
R = 150\ \mathrm{k\Omega}. \qquad (6)
$$
At any given moment in time, the voltage drop $U_r$ across the resistance $r$ is equal to the voltage drop $U_R$ across the resistance $R$ and the voltage drop $U_C$ across the capacitor $C$, that is,
$$
U_r = U_R + U_C. \qquad (7)
$$
On the other side according to Ohm's law we write
$$
U_R = I_c R, \qquad (8)
$$
$$
U_r = I_r r, \qquad (9)
$$
where $I_r$ denotes the current flowing through the resistor $r$, and $I_c$ designates the current flowing through the capacitor $C$ and the resistance $R$.  
The voltage across the capacitor is equal to
$$
U_C = \frac{q}{C}. \qquad (10)
$$
Note that the capacitor is charged due to the flow of current $I_0$ generated by the ohmmeter, that is,
$$
I_0 = I_r + I_C, \qquad (11)
$$
and the current flowing through the capacitor is equal to the derivative of its charge over time
$$
I_C = \frac{dq}{dt}. \qquad (12)
$$
From (7)-(12) we derive the differential equation for $q$
$$
\frac{dq}{dt} + \frac{q}{\tau_0} = I_0\frac{r}{R+r}, \qquad (13)
$$
whose solution yields the current through the capacitor
$$
I_c = I_0\frac{r}{R+r}e^{-t/\tau_0}. \qquad (14)
$$
Here $\tau_0 = C(R+r)$.  
Thus, the capacitance is found as
$$
C = \frac{\tau_0}{R+r} = 400\ \mathrm{\mu F}. \qquad (15)
$$
The amount of heat released by the resistor is determined by the Joule-Lenz law and is equal to
$$
Q = \int_0^\infty I_c^2 R\,dt = \frac{I_0^2 r^2 R \tau_0}{2(R+r)^2} = 12\ \mathrm{mJ}. \qquad (16)
$$
Note that, in principle, there is no need to consider equations (1)-(4) separately; to find unknown resistances, it is sufficient to find the dependence $R(t)$, which is obtained as
$$
R(t) = \frac{U_r}{I_0} = r - \frac{r^2}{R+r}e^{-t/\tau_0}. \qquad (17)
$$

**Problem 1.3 (3.0 points)**

A real image with a smaller angular size $\varphi_1$ is formed as a result of reflection at the air-glass boundary on the concave surface of the lens as from a concave mirror with a radius of curvature $r$ at a distance equal to the focal length $F_1$ of the mirror
$$
\frac{1}{F_1} = \frac{2}{r}, \qquad (1)
$$
which means the image is located at a distance from the observer
$$
a_1 = L - F_1. \qquad (2)
$$
Let $\varphi_0$ be the apparent angular size of the street lamp from the lens location. Then the linear size of the image of the lamp at the focus is
$$
l_1 = \varphi_0 F_1, \qquad (3)
$$
and therefore, the angular size of the visible image is found as
$$
\varphi_1 = \frac{l_1}{a_1}. \qquad (4)
$$
The second image, with a larger angular size of $\varphi_2$, is a virtual image of the lamp, formed as a result of reflection at the glass-air boundary from the convex surface of the lens. Considering that the rays that formed this image passed twice through a diverging lens with optical power $D$, and the focal length of the convex surface is $R/2$, the focal length of the system is
$$
-\frac{1}{F_2} = 2D + \frac{2}{R}, \qquad (5)
$$
and the image itself is located at a distance from the observer
$$
a_2 = L + F_2. \qquad (6)
$$
The linear size of the image of the lantern at focus is equal to
$$
l_2 = \varphi_0 F_2, \qquad (7)
$$
and therefore the angular size of the visible image is finally obtained as
$$
\varphi_2 = \frac{l_2}{a_2}. \qquad (8)
$$
Using the condition $\gamma = \varphi_1/\varphi_2$, the answer is derived as
$$
D = \frac{\gamma+1}{2L} - \frac{1}{R} - \frac{\gamma}{r} = -4\ \mathrm{dpt}. \qquad (9)
$$
