## Условие

*This problem inspired by the 2008 Guangdong Province Physics Olympiad*

Two infinitely long concentric hollow cylinders have radii $a$ and $4a$. Both cylinders are insulators; the inner cylinder has a uniformly distributed charge per length of $+\lambda$; the outer cylinder has a uniformly distributed charge per length of $-\lambda$.

An infinitely long dielectric cylinder with permittivity $\epsilon = \kappa\epsilon_0$, where $\kappa$ is the dielectric constant, has a inner radius $2a$ and outer radius $3a$ is also concentric with the insulating cylinders. The dielectric cylinder is rotating about its axis with an angular velocity $\omega \ll c/a$, where $c$ is the speed of light. Assume that the permeability of the dielectric cylinder and the space between the cylinders is that of free space, $\mu_0$.

**a.** Determine the electric field for all regions.

**b.** Determine the magnetic field for all regions.

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a.** $\mathbf{E} = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi r\epsilon_0}\hat{\mathbf{r}} \times \begin{cases} 0 & r < a \\ 1 & a < r < 2a \\ 1/\kappa & 2a < r < 3a \\ 1 & 3a < r < 4a \\ 0 & r > 4a \end{cases}$
- **b.** $\mathbf{B} = \frac{\mu_0\omega\lambda_i}{2\pi}\hat{\mathbf{z}} \times \begin{cases} 0 & r < 2a \\ 1 & 2a < r < 3a \\ 0 & r > 3a \end{cases},\quad \lambda_i = \left(1 - \frac{1}{\kappa}\right)\lambda$

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

a. Consider a Gaussian cylinder of radius $r$ and length $l$ centered on the cylinder axis. The electric field is radial, so Gauss’s Law states that

$$
\oint \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{A} = \frac{q_{\mathrm{in}}}{\epsilon_0} \quad\Rightarrow\quad 2\pi rEl = \frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{in}}l}{\epsilon_0}
$$

where $\lambda_{\mathrm{in}}$ is the linear charge density enclosed in the cylinder, so

$$
\mathbf{E} = \frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{in}}}{2\pi r\epsilon_0}\hat{\mathbf{r}}.
$$

The field due to the hollow cylinders alone is therefore

$$
\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{applied}} = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi r\epsilon_0}\hat{\mathbf{r}} \times \begin{cases} 0 & r < a \\ 1 & a < r < 4a \\ 0 & r > 4a. \end{cases}
$$

However, the field within the dielectric is reduced by a factor $\kappa$, so that in total

$$
\mathbf{E} = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi r\epsilon_0}\hat{\mathbf{r}} \times \begin{cases} 0 & r < a \\ 1 & a < r < 2a \\ 1/\kappa & 2a < r < 3a \\ 1 & 3 < r < 4a \\ 0 & r > 4a. \end{cases}
$$

b. We can apply the results of the previous section to obtain the enclosed charge density $\lambda_{\mathrm{in}}$ as a function of radius,

$$
\lambda_{\mathrm{in}} = \begin{cases} 0 & r < a \\ \lambda & a < r < 2a \\ \lambda/\kappa & 2a < r < 3a \\ \lambda & 3 < r < 4a \\ 0 & r > 4a. \end{cases}
$$

Defining

$$
\lambda_i = \left(1 - \frac{1}{\kappa}\right)\lambda
$$

we conclude that a charge density $-\lambda_i$ exists on the inner surface of the dielectric, a charge density $\lambda_i$ exists on the outer surface, and there is no charge on the interior.

As with the case of a very long solenoid, we expect the magnetic field to be entirely parallel to the cylinder axis $\hat{\mathbf{z}}$, and to go to zero for large $r$. Consider an Amperian loop of length $l$ extending along a radius, the inner side of which is at radius $r$ and the outer side of which is at a very large radius. We have on this loop

$$
\oint \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = \mu_0 I_{\mathrm{in}}.
$$

Letting $B$ be the magnitude of the magnetic field at radius $r$, we find

$$
B = \frac{\mu_0 I_{\mathrm{in}}}{l}.
$$

For $r > 3a$, $I_{\mathrm{in}} = 0$, since the charge on the hollow cylinders is not moving. For $2a < r < 3a$, the loop now encloses the outer surface of the dielectric. In time $\frac{2\pi}{\omega}$ a charge $\lambda_i l$ passes through the loop, so the current due to the outer surface is

$$
I_{\mathrm{out}} = \frac{\lambda_i l\omega}{2\pi}
$$

and thus this is $I_{\mathrm{in}}$ for $2a < r < 3a$. For $r < 2a$, the loop now encloses both surfaces of the dielectric; the inner surface contributes a current that exactly cancels the outer one, so again $I_{\mathrm{in}} = 0$. Putting this together,

$$
\mathbf{B} = \frac{\mu_0\omega\lambda_i}{2\pi}\hat{\mathbf{z}} \times \begin{cases} 0 & r < 2a \\ 1 & 2a < r < 3a \\ 0 & r > 3a. \end{cases}
$$
