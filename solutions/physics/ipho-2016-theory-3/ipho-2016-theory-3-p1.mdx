In this task, the physics of the particle accelerator LHC (Large Hadron Collider) at CERN is discussed. CERN is the world's largest particle physics laboratory. Its main goal is to get insight into the fundamental laws of nature. Two beams of particles are accelerated to high energies, guided around the accelerator ring by a strong magnetic field and then made to collide with each other. The protons are not spread uniformly around the circumference of the accelerator, but they are clustered in so-called bunches. The resulting particles generated by collisions are observed with large detectors. Some parameters of the LHC can be found in table 1.

| **LHC ring** | |
|---|---|
| Circumference of ring | $26659\ \mathrm{m}$ |
| Number of bunches per proton beam | $2808$ |
| Number of protons per bunch | $1.15 \times 10^{11}$ |
| **Proton beams** | |
| Energy of protons | $7.00\ \mathrm{TeV}$ |
| Centre of mass energy | $14.0\ \mathrm{TeV}$ |

Table 1: Typical numerical values of relevant LHC parameters.

Particle physicists use convenient units for the energy, momentum and mass: The energy is measured in electron volts [$\mathrm{eV}$]. By definition, $1\ \mathrm{eV}$ is the amount of energy gained by a particle with elementary charge, e, moved through a potential difference of one volt ($1\ \mathrm{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2s^{-2}}$).

The momentum is measured in units of $\mathrm{eV}/c$ and the mass in units of $\mathrm{eV}/c^2$, where $c$ is the speed of light in vacuum. Since $1\ \mathrm{eV}$ is a very small quantity of energy, particle physicists often use $\mathrm{MeV}$ ($1\ \mathrm{MeV} = 10^6\ \mathrm{eV}$), $\mathrm{GeV}$ ($1\ \mathrm{GeV} = 10^9\ \mathrm{eV}$) or $\mathrm{TeV}$ ($1\ \mathrm{TeV} = 10^{12}\ \mathrm{eV}$).

Part A deals with the acceleration of protons or electrons. Part B is concerned with the identification of particles produced in the collisions at CERN.

$$

**A3 (1.0 pt)** Derive an expression for the uniform magnetic flux density $B$ necessary to keep the proton beam on a circular track. The expression should only contain the energy of the protons $E$, the circumference $L$, fundamental constants and numbers. You may use suitable approximations if their effect is smaller than the precision given by the least number of significant digits. Calculate the magnetic flux density $B$ for a proton energy of $E = 7.00$ TeV.

**Solution A3:** **[1.0]**

Balance of forces:

$$
\frac{\gamma \cdot m_p \cdot v^2}{r} = \frac{m_p \cdot v^2}{r \cdot \sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}} = e \cdot v \cdot B \qquad \mathbf{0.3}
$$

In case of a mistake, partial points can be given for intermediate steps (up to max 0.2). Examples:

Example: Lorentz force **0.1**

Example: $\frac{\gamma \cdot m_p \cdot v^2}{r}$ **0.1**

Energy:

$$
E = (\gamma - 1) \cdot m_p \cdot c^2 \simeq \gamma \cdot m_p \cdot c^2 \rightarrow \gamma = \frac{E}{m_p c^2}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{E \cdot v}{c^2 \cdot r} = e \cdot B \qquad \mathbf{0.3}
$$

With

$$
v \simeq c \quad \text{and} \quad r = \frac{L}{2\pi}
$$

follows:

$$
\rightarrow B = \frac{2\pi \cdot E}{e \cdot c \cdot L} \qquad \mathbf{0.2}
$$

Solution:

$$
B = 5.50\mathrm{T} \qquad \mathbf{0.2}
$$

**A4 (1.0 pt)** An accelerated charged particle radiates energy in the form of electromagnetic waves. The radiated power $P_{rad}$ of a charged particle that circulates with a constant angular velocity depends only on its acceleration a, its charge $q$, the speed of light $c$ and the permittivity of free space $\epsilon_0$. Use a dimensional analysis to find an expression for the radiated power $P_{rad}$.

**Solution A4:** **[1.0]**

Ansatz:

$$
P_{rad} = a^{\alpha} \cdot q^{\beta} \cdot c^{\gamma} \cdot \epsilon_0^{\delta} \qquad \mathbf{0.2}
$$

Dimensions: $[a]=\mathrm{ms^{-2}}$, $[q]=\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{As}$, $[c]=\mathrm{ms^{-1}}$,$[\epsilon_0]=\mathrm{As(Vm)^{-1}}=\mathrm{A^2s^2(Nm^2)^{-1}}=\mathrm{A^2s^4(kgm^3)^{-1}}$

All dimensions correct **0.3**

Three dimensions correct **0.2**

Two dimensions correct **0.1**

---

if dimensions: N and Coulomb $[\epsilon_0]= \mathrm{C^2(Nm^2)^{-1}}$

$$
\frac{\mathrm{m}^{\alpha}}{\mathrm{s}^{2\alpha}} \cdot \mathrm{C}^{\beta} \cdot \frac{\mathrm{m}^{\gamma}}{\mathrm{s}^{\gamma}} \cdot \frac{\mathrm{C}^{2\delta}}{\mathrm{N}^{\delta} \cdot \mathrm{m}^{2\delta}} = \frac{\mathrm{N \cdot m}}{\mathrm{s}} \qquad \mathbf{0.1}
$$

From this follows:

$$
\mathrm{N} :\rightarrow \delta = -1, \quad \mathrm{C} :\rightarrow \beta + 2 \cdot \delta = 0, \quad \mathrm{m} :\rightarrow \alpha + \gamma - 2\delta = 1, \quad \mathrm{s} :\rightarrow 2 \cdot \alpha + \gamma = 1 \qquad \mathbf{0.2}
$$

Two equations correct **0.1**

And therefore:

$$
\rightarrow \alpha = 2, \beta = 2, \gamma = -3, \delta = -1 \qquad \mathbf{0.1}
$$

---

if dimensions: N and As $[\epsilon_0]=\mathrm{A^2s^2(Nm^2)^{-1}}$

$$
\frac{\mathrm{m}^{\alpha}}{\mathrm{s}^{2\alpha}} \cdot \mathrm{A}^{\beta} \cdot \mathrm{s}^{\beta} \cdot \frac{\mathrm{m}^{\gamma}}{\mathrm{s}^{\gamma}} \cdot \frac{\mathrm{A}^{2\delta} \cdot \mathrm{s}^{2\delta}}{\mathrm{N}^{\delta} \cdot \mathrm{m}^{2\delta}} = \frac{\mathrm{N \cdot m}}{\mathrm{s}} \qquad \mathbf{0.1}
$$

From this follows:

$$
N :\rightarrow \delta = -1, \quad \mathrm{A} :\rightarrow \beta + 2 \cdot \delta = 0, \quad \mathrm{m} :\rightarrow \alpha + \gamma - 2\delta = 1, \quad \mathrm{s} :\rightarrow -2 \cdot \alpha + \beta - \gamma + 2\delta = -1 \qquad \mathbf{0.2}
$$

Two equations correct **0.1**

And therefore:

$$
\rightarrow \alpha = 2, \beta = 2, \gamma = -3, \delta = -1 \qquad \mathbf{0.1}
$$

---

if dimensions: kg and As $[\epsilon_0]=\mathrm{A^2s^4(kg \cdot m^3)^{-1}}$

$$
\frac{\mathrm{m}^{\alpha}}{\mathrm{s}^{2\alpha}} \cdot \mathrm{A}^{\beta} \cdot \mathrm{s}^{\beta} \cdot \frac{\mathrm{m}^{\gamma}}{\mathrm{s}^{\gamma}} \cdot \frac{\mathrm{A}^{2\delta} \cdot \mathrm{s}^{4\delta}}{\mathrm{kg}^{\delta} \cdot \mathrm{m}^{3\delta}} = \frac{\mathrm{kg} \cdot m^2}{s^3} \qquad \mathbf{0.1}

From this follows:

$$
\mathrm{kg} :\rightarrow \delta = -1, \quad \mathrm{A} :\rightarrow \beta + 2 \cdot \delta = 0, \quad \mathrm{m} :\rightarrow \alpha + \gamma - 3\delta = 2, \quad \mathrm{s} :\rightarrow -2 \cdot \alpha + \beta - \gamma + 4\delta = -3 \qquad \mathbf{0.2}

And therefore:

$$
\rightarrow \alpha = 2, \beta = 2, \gamma = -3, \delta = -1 \qquad \mathbf{0.1}

**A5 (1.0 pt)** Calculate the total radiated power $P_{tot}$ of the LHC for a proton energy of $E = 7.00$ TeV (Note table 1). You may use appropriate approximations.

**A6 (1.5 pt)** Determine the time $T$ that the protons need to pass through this field.

**Solution A6:** **[1.5]**

2nd Newton's law

$$
F = \frac{dp}{dt} \quad \text{leads to} \qquad \mathbf{0.2}
$$

$$
\frac{V \cdot e}{d} = \frac{p_f - p_i}{T} \ \text{with} \ p_i = 0 \qquad \mathbf{0.3}
$$

Conservation of energy:

$$
E_{tot} = m \cdot c^2 + e \cdot V \qquad \mathbf{0.2}
$$

Since

$$
E_{tot}^2 = (m \cdot c^2)^2 + (p_f \cdot c)^2 \qquad \mathbf{0.2}
$$

$$
\rightarrow p_f = \frac{1}{c} \cdot \sqrt{(m \cdot c^2 + e \cdot V)^2 - (m \cdot c^2)^2} = \sqrt{2e \cdot m \cdot V + \left(\frac{e \cdot V}{c}\right)^2} \qquad \mathbf{0.2}
$$

$$
\rightarrow T = \frac{d \cdot p_f}{V \cdot e} = \frac{d}{V \cdot e}\sqrt{2e \cdot m_p \cdot V + \left(\frac{e \cdot V}{c}\right)^2} \qquad \mathbf{0.3}
$$

$$
T = 218\mathrm{ns} \qquad \mathbf{0.1}
$$

---

$$

$$
v(x) = c \cdot \sqrt{1 - \left(\frac{m_p \cdot c^2}{m_p \cdot c^2 + \frac{e \cdot V \cdot x}{d}}\right)^2} = c \cdot \frac{\sqrt{\left(m_p \cdot c^2 + \frac{e \cdot V \cdot x}{d}\right)^2 - (m_p \cdot c^2)^2}}{m_p \cdot c^2 + \frac{e \cdot V \cdot x}{d}}
$$

$$
= c \cdot \frac{\sqrt{\left(1 + \frac{e \cdot V \cdot x}{d \cdot m_p \cdot c^2}\right)^2 - 1}}{1 + \frac{e \cdot V \cdot x}{d \cdot m_p \cdot c^2}} \qquad \mathbf{0.2}
$$

$$
\text{Substitution} : \xi = \frac{e \cdot V \cdot x}{d \cdot m_p \cdot c^2} \qquad \frac{d\xi}{dx} = \frac{e \cdot V}{d \cdot m_p \cdot c^2} \qquad \mathbf{0.2}
$$

$$
\rightarrow t = \frac{1}{c}\int_0^b \frac{1 + \xi}{\sqrt{(1 + \xi)^2 - 1}} \frac{d \cdot m_p \cdot c^2}{e \cdot V} d\xi \qquad b = \frac{e \cdot V}{m_p \cdot c^2} \qquad \mathbf{0.2}
$$

$$
1 + \xi := \cosh(s) \qquad \frac{d\xi}{ds} = \sinh(s) \qquad \mathbf{0.1}
$$

$$
t = \frac{m_p \cdot c \cdot d}{e \cdot V}\int \frac{\cosh(s) \cdot \sinh(s) ds}{\sqrt{\cosh^2(s) - 1}} = \frac{m_p \cdot c \cdot d}{e \cdot V}[\sinh(s)]_{b_1}^{b_2} \qquad \mathbf{0.2}
$$

$$
\text{with} \quad b_1 = \cosh^{-1}(1), \quad b_2 = \cosh^{-1}\left(1 + \frac{e \cdot V}{m_p \cdot c^2}\right) \qquad \mathbf{0.1}
$$

$$
T = 218\mathrm{ns} \qquad \mathbf{0.1}
$$

---

**Alternative: differential equation** **[1.5]**

$$
F = \frac{\mathrm{d}p}{\mathrm{d}t} \qquad \mathbf{0.2}
$$

$$
\rightarrow \frac{V \cdot e}{d} = \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\frac{m \cdot v}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}}\right) = \frac{m \cdot a\left(1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}\right) + m \cdot a\frac{v^2}{c^2}}{\left(1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}} = \gamma^3 \cdot m \cdot a \qquad \mathbf{0.4}
$$

$$
a = \ddot{s} = \frac{V \cdot e}{d \cdot m}\left(1 - \frac{\dot{s}^2}{c^2}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}} \qquad \mathbf{0.3}
$$

$$
\text{Ansatz} : \ s(t) = \sqrt{i^2 \cdot t^2 + k} - l \ \text{ with boundary conditions } s(0) = 0, v(0) = 0 \qquad \mathbf{0.1}
$$

$$
\rightarrow s(t) = \frac{c}{V \cdot e}\left(\sqrt{e^2 \cdot V^2 \cdot t^2 + c^2 \cdot m^2 \cdot d^2} - c \cdot m \cdot d\right) \qquad \mathbf{0.2}
$$

$$
s = d \rightarrow T = \frac{d}{V \cdot e}\sqrt{\left(\frac{V \cdot e}{c}\right)^2 + 2V \cdot e \cdot m} \qquad \mathbf{0.2}
$$

$$
T = 218\mathrm{ns} \qquad \mathbf{0.1}
$$

---

**classical solution:** **[0.4]**

$$
F = \frac{V \cdot e}{d} \rightarrow \text{acceleration } a = \frac{F}{m_p} = \frac{V \cdot e}{m_p \cdot d} \qquad \mathbf{0.1}
$$

$$
d = \frac{1}{2} \cdot a \cdot T^2 \rightarrow T = \sqrt{\frac{2d}{a}} \qquad \mathbf{0.1}
$$

And hence for the time

$$
T = d \cdot \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot m_p}{V \cdot e}} \qquad \mathbf{0.1}
$$

$$
T = 194\mathrm{ns} \qquad \mathbf{0.1}
$$

### Part B. Particle identification (4 points)

**B1 (0.8 pt)** Express the particle rest mass $m$ in terms of the momentum $p$, the flight length $l$ and the flight time $t$ assuming that the particles with elementary charge $e$ travel with velocity close to $c$ on straight tracks in the ToF detector and that it travels perpendicular to the two detection planes (see Figure 2).

**Solution B1:** **[0.8]**

with velocity

$$
v = \frac{l}{t} \qquad \mathbf{0.1}
$$

relativistic momentum

$$
p = \frac{m \cdot v}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}} \qquad \mathbf{0.2}
$$

gets

$$
p = \frac{m \cdot l}{t \cdot \sqrt{1 - \frac{l^2}{t^2 \cdot c^2}}} \qquad \mathbf{0.2}
$$

$\rightarrow$ mass

$$
m = \frac{p \cdot t}{l} \cdot \sqrt{1 - \frac{l^2}{t^2 \cdot c^2}} = \frac{p}{l \cdot c} \cdot \sqrt{t^2 \cdot c^2 - l^2} \qquad \mathbf{0.3}
$$

---

**Alternative** **[0.8]**

with flight distance: $l$, flight time t gets:

$$
t = \frac{l}{(c \cdot \beta)} \qquad \mathbf{0.1}
$$

relativistic momentum

$$
p = \frac{m \cdot \beta \cdot c}{\sqrt{1 - \beta^2}}
$$

therefore the velocity:

$$
\beta = \frac{p}{\sqrt{m^2 \cdot c^2 + p^2}} \qquad \mathbf{0.2}
$$

insert into the expression for $t$:

$$
t = l\frac{\sqrt{m^2 \cdot c^2 + p^2}}{c \cdot p} \qquad \mathbf{0.2}
$$

$\rightarrow$ mass:

$$
m = \sqrt{\left(\frac{p \cdot t}{l}\right)^2 - \left(\frac{p}{c}\right)^2} = \frac{p}{l \cdot c}\sqrt{(t \cdot c)^2 - (l)^2} \qquad \mathbf{0.3}
$$

---

**non-relativistic solution:** **[0.0]**

flight time: $t = l/v$ velocity:

$$
v = \frac{p}{m} \rightarrow t = \frac{l \cdot m}{p} \quad \text{and} \quad m = \frac{p \cdot t}{l}
$$

this solution gives no points **0.0**

**B2 (0.7 pt)** Calculate the minimal length of a ToF detector that allows to safely distinguish a charged kaon from a charged pion given both their momenta are measured to be 1.00 GeV/c. For a good separation it is required that the difference in the time-of-flight is larger than three times the time resolution of the detector. The typical resolution of a ToF detector is 150 ps (1 ps = $10^{-12}$ s).

**Solution B2:** **[0.7]**

Flight time difference between kaon and pion

$$
\Delta t = 450\mathrm{ps} = 450 \cdot 10^{-12}\mathrm{s} \qquad \mathbf{0.1}
$$

Flight time difference between kaon and pion

$$
\Delta t = \frac{l}{cp}(\sqrt{m_\pi^2 \cdot c^2 + p^2} - \sqrt{m_K^2 \cdot c^2 + p^2}) = 450\mathrm{ps} = 450 \cdot 10^{-12}\mathrm{s} \qquad \mathbf{0.2}
$$

$$
\rightarrow l = \frac{\Delta t \cdot p}{\sqrt{m_K^2 + p^2/c^2} - \sqrt{m_\pi^2 + p^2/c^2}} \qquad \mathbf{0.2}
$$

$$
\sqrt{m_K^2 + p^2/c^2} = 1.115\ \mathrm{GeV}/c^2 \text{and} \sqrt{m_\pi^2 + p^2/c^2} = 1.010\ \mathrm{GeV}/c^2
$$

$$
l = 450 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot \frac{1}{1.115 - 1.010}\mathrm{s}\ \mathrm{GeV}c^2/(\mathrm{GeV}c) \qquad \mathbf{0.1}
$$

$$
l = 4285.710^{-12}\mathrm{s} \cdot c = 4285.7 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^8\mathrm{m} = 1.28\mathrm{m} \qquad \mathbf{0.1}
$$

**Penalty** for $< 2$ or $> 4$ significant digits **-0.1**

---

**Non-relativistic solution:** **[0.3]**

Flight time difference between kaon and pion

$$
\Delta t = \frac{l}{p}(m_K - m_\pi) = 450ps = 450 \cdot 10^{-12}\mathrm{s} \qquad \mathbf{0.1}
$$

length:

$$
l = \frac{\Delta tp}{m_K - m_\Pi} = \frac{450 \cdot 10^{-12}\mathrm{s} \cdot 1\mathrm{GeV}/c}{(0.498 - 0.135)\mathrm{GeV}/c^2} \qquad \mathbf{0.1}
$$

$$
l = 450 \cdot 10^{-12}/0.363 \cdot cs = 450 \cdot 10^{-12}/0.363 \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^8\mathrm{m}
$$

$$
l = 3716 \cdot 10^{-4}\mathrm{m} = 0.372\mathrm{m} \qquad \mathbf{0.1}
$$

**Penalty** for $< 2$ or $> 4$ significant digits **-0.1**
