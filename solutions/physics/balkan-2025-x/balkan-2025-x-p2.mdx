*BPO7 Problems 26-30 June 2025, Ohrid · 26 юни 2025 г. · 25 т.*

## Условие

An artificial satellite with mass $m \ll M$, where $M$ is the mass of the Earth, orbits the Earth on an elliptical trajectory, having the semi-major axis $a = \alpha R$, where $R$ is the mean Earth radius, $\alpha = 3$, and eccentricity $e = 1/2$. Also known is the numerical value of the first cosmic speed, $v_I = 7.91\ \mathrm{km/s}$.

**a)** Determine the speed of the satellite at apogee (point A) ($v_a$) and at the perigee (point B) ($v_p$). **[8 т.]**

A projectile with the mass $m_0 = m/\beta$, where $\beta = 3$, launched from the Earth, moves radially and undergoes a plastic collision with the satellite, when the satellite is at its apogee. After the collision, the eccentricity of the new orbit of the satellite becomes $e' = 3/4$.

**b)** Determine the tangential speed of the satellite immediately after the collision. **[2 т.]**

**c)** Determine the ratio of the satellite energy immediately after the collision with the projectile to the satellite energy immediately before the collision. **[7 т.]**

**d)** Determine the radial speed of the satellite immediately after the collision. **[3 т.]**

**e)** Determine the launching speed of the projectile. **[5 т.]**

***Notes:***

1) *An ellipse is the plane curve represented in the figure, for which* $\mathrm{F_1P + F_2P = const.}$*, for any point* P*. The point* O *is the center of the ellipse and the fixed points* F$_{1,2}$ *are the ellipse’s focal points. If the Earth is in* F$_1$*, for example, then* A *is called the apogee and B – the perigee. Other important quantities for an ellipse are:* $a = \mathrm{OA} = \mathrm{OB}$ *– semi-major axis,* $b = \mathrm{OC} = \mathrm{OD}$ *– semi-minor axis,* $c = \mathrm{OF_1} = \mathrm{OF_2}$ *– the linear eccentricity, while the nondimensional parameter characterizing the ellipse is* $e = c/a$*, called the ellipse eccentricity.*

2) *For a satellite – Earth system, there is a physical quantity called angular momentum* $L$ *with respect to the center of the Earth, which is conserved. Its conservation proves the second law of Kepler. Mathematically, the angular momentum has the magnitude (see the drawing below):*

$$L = mvr\sin\alpha,$$

*where* $m$ *is the satellite mass,* $v$ *– its speed, and* $r$ *– the distance from the center of the Earth (F$_1$) to the satellite.*

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a)** v_a = v_I/3 ≅ 2.64 km/s, v_p = v_I = 7.91 km/s
- **b)** 1.98 km/s
- **c)** 1.38
- **d)** 5.06 km/s
- **e)** 22.5 km/s

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

a) **(8 points)** Determine the speed of the satellite at apogee ($v_a$) and at the perigee ($v_p$).

**Solution:**

The first cosmic speed is the launching speed of a body from the Earth surface to make it orbit the Earth, at its surface:

$$\frac{mv_I^2}{R}=\gamma\frac{mM}{R^2},$$

(0.6 x 2)

where $\gamma$ is the gravitational constant, so

$$v_I=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma M}{R}}.$$

(0.4)

The energy of the satellite on its orbit is

$$E=-\gamma\frac{mM}{2a}.$$

(0.8)

At the apogee

$$E=\frac{mv_a^2}{2}-\gamma\frac{mM}{r_{max}},$$

(0.7 x 2)

where

$$r_{max}=a+c=a(1+e),$$

(0.4 x 2)

so

$$v_a=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma M}{a}\frac{1-e}{1+e}}.$$

(0.5)

or, using the first cosmic speed,

$$\boxed{v_a=v_I\sqrt{\frac{1}{\alpha}\frac{1-e}{1+e}}}.$$

(0.4)

Numerically,

$$\boxed{v_a=\frac{v_I}{3}=2.636\ \frac{\mathrm{km}}{\mathrm{s}}\cong 2.64\ \frac{\mathrm{km}}{\mathrm{s}}}.$$

(0.3)

At perigee, from angular momentum conservation (or from energy equation $\frac{mv_p^2}{2}-\gamma\frac{mM}{r_{min}}=-\gamma\frac{mM}{2a}$)

$$v_p=\frac{r_{max}}{r_{min}}v_a,$$

(1.0)

where

$$r_{min}=a-c=a(1-e).$$

(0.4)

Finally,

$$\boxed{v_p=v_I\sqrt{\frac{1}{\alpha}\frac{1+e}{1-e}}}.$$

(0.5)

Numerically,

$$\boxed{v_p=v_I=7.91\ \frac{\mathrm{km}}{\mathrm{s}}}.$$

(0.3)

b) **(2 points)** Determine the tangential speed of the satellite immediately after the collision.

**Solution:**

From momentum conservation on the tangential direction

$$\boxed{v_t'=\frac{mv_a}{m+m_0}=\frac{\beta v_a}{1+\beta}},$$

(1.4)

Numerically,

$$\boxed{v_t'=\frac{v_I}{4}=1.9775\ \frac{\mathrm{km}}{\mathrm{s}}\cong 1.98\ \frac{\mathrm{km}}{\mathrm{s}}}.$$

(0.3 x 2)

c) **(7 points)** Determine the ratio of the satellite energy immediately after the collision with the projectile to the satellite energy immediately before the collision.

**Solution:**

The satellite energy before the collision is

$$E=-\gamma\frac{mM}{2a}.$$

From energy and angular momentum conservation, it follows that

$$e'=\sqrt{1+\frac{2E'L'^2}{\gamma^2M^2(m+m_0)^3}},$$

(5.0)

where

$$L'=(m+m_0)a(1+e)v_t'=ma(1+e)v_a=m\sqrt{\gamma Ma(1-e^2)}.$$

(0.5)

[derivation:

$$E'=\frac{(m+m_0)v^2}{2}-\gamma\frac{(m+m_0)M}{r}$$

(1.0)

and

$$L'=(m+m_0)vr$$

(0.5)

at the apses. Eliminating $v$ between these equations, one gets

$$E'=\frac{L'^2}{2(m+m_0)r^2}-\gamma\frac{(m+m_0)M}{r},$$

or

$$2E'(m+m_0)r^2+2\gamma M(m+m_0)^2\cdot r-L'^2=0.$$

(0.5)

The solutions of this quadratic equation are

$$r_{min,max}=\frac{\gamma M(m+m_0)}{2E'}\left[-1\pm\sqrt{1+\frac{2E'L'^2}{\gamma^2M^2(m+m_0)^3}}\right].$$

(0.5)

Since

$$r_{min}+r_{max}=2a'=-\frac{\gamma M(m+m_0)}{E'}$$

(0.5 x 2)

and

$$r_{max}-r_{min}=2c'=-\frac{\gamma M(m+m_0)}{E'}\sqrt{1+\frac{2E'L'^2}{\gamma^2M^2(m+m_0)^3}},$$

(0.5 x 2)

then

$$e'=\frac{c'}{a'}=\sqrt{1+\frac{2E'L'^2}{\gamma^2M^2(m+m_0)^3}}$$

(0.5)

end of derivation]

So

$$E'=E\,\frac{1-e'^2}{1-e^2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\beta}\right)^3.$$

(0.7)

Under these conditions

$$\boxed{\frac{E'}{E}=\frac{1-e'^2}{1-e^2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\beta}\right)^3}.$$

(0.5)

Numerically,

$$\boxed{\frac{E'}{E}=\frac{112}{81}=1{,}38}.$$

(0.3)

d) **(3 points)** Determine the radial speed of the satellite immediately after the collision.

**Solution:**

Since

$$E'=\frac{(m+m_0)v'^2}{2}-\gamma\frac{(m+m_0)M}{a(1+e)},$$

(0.5)

then

$$v'^2=\frac{v_I^2}{\alpha}\left[\frac{2}{1+e}-\frac{1-e'^2}{1-e^2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\beta}\right)^2\right].$$

(1.0)

Under these conditions,

$$v_r'^2=v'^2-v_t'^2,$$

(0.7)

or

$$v_r'^2=\frac{v_I^2}{\alpha(1-e^2)\beta^2(1+\beta)^2}\left[e'^2(1+\beta)^4-(1+2\beta+e\beta^2)^2\right],$$

which gives

$$v_r'=\frac{v_I}{\beta(1+\beta)}\sqrt{\frac{e'^2(1+\beta)^4-(1+2\beta+e\beta^2)}{\alpha(1-e^2)}}.$$

(0.5)

Numerically,

$$v_r'=5.058\ \frac{\mathrm{km}}{\mathrm{s}}\cong 5.06\ \frac{\mathrm{km}}{\mathrm{s}}.$$

(0.3)

e) **(5 points)** Determine the launching speed of the projectile.

**Solution:**

From momentum conservation for the collision, on the radial direction, it follows that

$$v_p=v_r'\frac{m+m_0}{m_0}=v_r'(1+\beta),$$

(1.0 + 0.5)

or

$$v_p=\frac{v_I}{\beta}\sqrt{\frac{e'^2(1+\beta)^4-(1+2\beta+e\beta^2)}{\alpha(1-e^2)}}.$$

(0.5)

The energy conservation for the projectile before the collision is written

$$\frac{m_0v_{p0}^2}{2}-\frac{\gamma m_0M}{R}=\frac{m_0v_p^2}{2}-\frac{\gamma m_0M}{a(1+e)},$$

(1.5)

so

$$v_{p0}=\sqrt{v_p^2+2v_I^2\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha(1+e)}\right)}.$$

(0.7)

Finally,

$$v_{p0}=v_I\sqrt{\frac{e'^2(1+\beta)^4-(1+2\beta+e\beta^2)}{\alpha(1-e^2)\beta^2}+2\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha(1+e)}\right)}.$$

(0.5)

Numerically,

$$\boxed{v_{p0}=22.5\ \frac{\mathrm{km}}{\mathrm{s}}}.$$

(0.3)


</Spoiler>
