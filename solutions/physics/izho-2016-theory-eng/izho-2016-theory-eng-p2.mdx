## Условие

One of the widely known principles of the general physics is that every system tends to decrease its potential energy, and the stable equilibrium position corresponds to the state with the minimum of its value.

In this problem the interaction of the liquid with the solid surface is studied. To describe this interaction the following parameters are introduced:

$\sigma_0$ is the surface tension at the interface between the liquid and the air;

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '43.6%', '--fig-max': '280px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/izho-2016-theory-eng/p2-fig1.png" alt="A liquid drop on a solid surface with callouts σ0 (liquid–air surface), σ1 (liquid–solid interface) and σ2 (solid–air surface); the contact angle θ is marked at the edge of the drop." width="874" height="443" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

$\sigma_1$ is the surface tension at the interface between the liquid and solid;

$\sigma_2$ is the surface tension at the interface between the solid and the air;

$\theta$ is the contact angle (wetting angle).

The values $\sigma_0$, $\sigma_1$, $\sigma_2$ designate the surface energy per unit area of contact between media.

In all parts of the problem, use the following numerical values for water:

the surface tension $\sigma_0 = 0{,}072\,\frac{\mathrm{N}}{\mathrm{m}}$;

the contact angle $\theta = 20^{\circ}$;

the mass density $\rho = 1{,}0\cdot 10^3\,\frac{\mathrm{kg}}{\mathrm{m}^3}$;

the acceleration of gravity $g = 9{,}8\,\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s}^2}$.

**1. Introduction (1.0 points)**

**1** Prove that the change in the surface energy at the liquid-solid interface is found as

$$\Delta U_s = -\sigma_0\cos\theta\ \Delta S, \qquad (1)$$

where $\Delta S$ stands for the change in the area of the contact between the liquid and solid. **[1 т.]**

**2. Water in a vertical cylindrical tube (2.0 points)**

The open tube with the inner radius $R = 1{,}0\,\mathrm{mm}$ is lowered vertically so that its lower end touches the surface of the water.

Let a water level in the tube be at a certain height $h$, which does not necessarily correspond to its equilibrium value.

**2.1** Find the formula for the change in the surface energy of the system $\Delta U_S$ that corresponds to an additional small rise of water level $\Delta h$ in the tube. **[0,5 т.]**

**2.2** Find the formula for the change in the potential energy $\Delta U_G$ of the liquid in the gravitational field that corresponds to an additional small rise of water level $\Delta h$ in the tube. **[0,5 т.]**

**2.3** Using the principle of the minimum potential energy, find the formula for the height of the water in the tube $h_0$ in equilibrium position. Calculate its numerical value from the quantities provided above. **[1 т.]**

**3. Water in a vertical conical tube (4.0 points)**

A long conical tube is vertically lowered into the water so that its lower end touches the surface of the water. The inner radius of the tube at its lower base is equal to $R = 1{,}0\,\mathrm{mm}$, and its inner radius at the upper base is close to zero. The tube walls make an angle $\alpha$ with the vertical.

**Note:** in the following neglect any change in the surface energy at the interface between the liquid and the air.

Let a water level in the tube be at a certain height $h$, which does not necessarily correspond to its equilibrium value.

**3.1** Find the formula for the change in the surface energy of the system $\Delta U_S$ that corresponds to an additional small rise of water level $\Delta h$ in the tube. **[0,5 т.]**

**3.2** Find the formula for the change in the potential energy $\Delta U_G$ of the liquid in the gravitational field that corresponds to an additional small rise of water level $\Delta h$ in the tube. **[0,5 т.]**

**3.3** Find the equation that determines the height of water in the tube in equilibrium position and rewrite it in terms of $\sigma_0, \theta, \alpha$ and the value of $h_0$ found in 2.3. **[1 т.]**

**3.4** Let an angle be $\alpha = 1{,}0 \times 10^{-2}\ \mathrm{rad}$. The tube is partially filled with water up to a certain level $H$. Find the dependence of the ultimate height of the water level in the tube as a function of $H$. **[1 т.]**

**3.5** Specify the range of angles $\alpha$ (providing its numerical values) at which the water completely fills the tube. **[1 т.]**

**4. Outflow of water (3.0 points)**

A bottle is completely filled with water, sealed tightly with the cork and turned upside down. Two identical round holes of radii $R$ are drilled in the cork.

**4.1** At what minimum value of the hole radius the water will pour out of the bottle? **[3 т.]**

**Mathematical tips**

Rather small convex spherically shaped surface can be approximately described by a function

$$z = h\left(1 - \frac{r^2}{R^2}\right),$$

where $R$ denotes the radius of the bulge with $h$ being its height such that $h \ll R$.

Then, up to higher orders the area of the spherical part of the bulge is found as

$$S = \pi\left(R^2 + h^2\right),$$

and its potential energy in the gravitational field is derived as

$$U = \frac{\pi R^2h^2}{6}\rho g.$$

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**1. Introduction (1.0 points)**

1.1 **[1.0 points]** The change in the surface energy at the liquid-solid interface is found as

$$\Delta U_S = -(\sigma_2 - \sigma_1)\Delta S. \qquad (1)$$

Considering the small segment $\Delta l$ of the boundary of the drop one can write the condition of its balance

$$(\sigma_2 - \sigma_1)\Delta l = \sigma_0\Delta l\cos\theta. \qquad (2)$$

It follows from equations (1) and (2) that

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '26.9%', '--fig-max': '172px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/izho-2016-theory-eng/p2-sol-fig1.png" alt="Vertical cylindrical tube of radius R standing in water; the water column of height h and an additional rise Δh are marked." width="539" height="709" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

$$\Delta U_s = -\sigma_0\cos\theta\ \Delta S. \qquad (3)$$

**2. Water in a vertical cylindrical tube (2.0 points)**

2.1 **[0.5 points]** The formula for the change in the surface energy of the system $\Delta U_S$ that corresponds to an additional small rise of water level $\Delta h$ in the tube takes the form

$$\Delta U_S = -\sigma_0\cos\theta\cdot\Delta S = -\sigma_0\cos\theta\cdot 2\pi R\Delta h, \qquad (4)$$

where $\Delta S = 2\pi R\Delta h$ is the change of the contact area between the liquid and the inner surface of the tube.

2.2 **[0.5 points]** The formula for the change in the potential energy $\Delta U_G$ of the liquid in the gravitational field that corresponds to an additional small rise of water level $\Delta h$ in the tube takes the form

$$\Delta U_G = \pi R^2\Delta h\rho gh. \qquad (5)$$

It is taken into account that the liquid of the mass $\Delta m = \pi R^2\Delta h\rho$ has risen to the height $h$.

2.3 **[1.0 points]** If $\left|\Delta U_S\right|$ exceeds $\Delta U_G$, the energy of the system decreases when the liquid has risen, and hence the liquid will continue to rise, otherwise liquid level will go down. At the position of equilibrium, the total change in energy should be equal to zero, and, thus,

$$\sigma_0\cos\theta\cdot 2\pi R\Delta h = \pi R^2\Delta h\rho gh \quad\Rightarrow\quad h_0 = \frac{2\sigma_0\cos\theta}{\rho gR}. \qquad (6)$$

Substitution of the numerical values leads to the following result

$$h_0 = \frac{2\sigma_0\cos\theta}{\rho gR} = \frac{2\cdot 0{,}072\cdot\cos 20^{\circ}}{1{,}0\cdot 10^3\cdot 9{,}8\cdot 1{,}0\cdot 10^{-3}} = 1{,}4\cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m} = 14\,\mathrm{mm}. \qquad (7)$$

**3. Water in a vertical conical tube (4.0 points)**

3.1 **[0.5 points]** The formula for the change in the surface energy of the system $\Delta U_S$ that corresponds to an additional small rise of water level $\Delta h$ in the tube takes the form

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '30%', '--fig-max': '193px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/izho-2016-theory-eng/p2-sol-fig2.png" alt="Conical tube narrowing upwards standing in water; lower radius R, wall angle α to the vertical, water height h and additional rise Δh are marked." width="602" height="657" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

$$\Delta U_S = -\sigma_0\cos\theta\cdot\Delta S = -\sigma_0\cos\theta\cdot 2\pi r\frac{\Delta h}{\cos\alpha}. \qquad (8)$$

Here $r = R - h\,tg\alpha$ is the tube radius at the height $h$.

3.2 **[0.5 points]** The formula for the change in the potential energy $\Delta U_G$ of the liquid in the gravitational field that corresponds to an additional small rise of water level $\Delta h$ in the tube takes the form

$$\Delta U_G = \pi r^2\Delta h\rho gh. \qquad (9)$$

3.3 **[1.0 points]** As above, the equilibrium position corresponds to the equality of the modules for the energy changes written here as

$$\sigma_0\cos\theta\cdot 2\pi r\frac{\Delta h}{\cos\alpha} = \pi r^2\Delta h\rho gh, \qquad (10)$$

Substituting the expression for the radius of the tube at the height $h$, one gets the equation

$$\frac{2\sigma_0\cos\theta}{R - h\,tg\alpha} = \rho gh\cos\alpha, \qquad (11)$$

in which the parameter $h_0$ is easily introduced as

$$\frac{2\sigma_0\cos\theta}{\rho gR\left(1 - \frac{h}{R}tg\alpha\right)} = h\cos\alpha \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{h_0}{1 - \frac{h}{R}tg\alpha} = h\cos\alpha, \qquad (12)$$

3.4 **[1.0 points]** The resulting equation is square with respect to $h$. Therefore it is necessary to analyze its roots, or condition of their absence. Let us rewrite equation (12) in the form

$$h_0 = h\cos\alpha\left(1 - \frac{h}{R}tg\alpha\right). \qquad (13)$$

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '40.6%', '--fig-max': '260px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/izho-2016-theory-eng/p2-sol-fig3.png" alt="Graph of the parabola h cos α (1 − h tgα / R) against h, with zeros at h = 0 and h = R/tgα and maximum at h = R/(2tgα); a horizontal line at the level h0 cuts the parabola at two points." width="812" height="626" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

The quadratic function on the right side of this equation has zeros at $h = 0$ and $h = \frac{R}{tg\alpha}$,, and therefore, it reaches its maximum value of $\frac{R}{4tg\alpha}\cos\alpha$ at $h = \frac{R}{2tg\alpha}$. Consequently, equation (12) has no real roots at $h_0 > \frac{R}{4tg\alpha}\cos\alpha$. Otherwise, there are two roots. At the given parameters of the tube $\frac{R}{4tg\alpha}\cos\alpha = 25\,\mathrm{mm}$, so there are two root corresponding to the two equilibrium positions. It is easy to show that the smaller root gives a stable equilibrium position, and the larger one is unstable and their numerical values are evaluated as

$$h = \frac{\cos\alpha \pm \sqrt{\cos^2\alpha - 4\frac{h_0}{R}\sin\alpha}}{2\frac{\sin\alpha}{R}} \quad\Rightarrow h_1 = 16{,}5\,\mathrm{mm},\ h_2 = 83{,}5\,\mathrm{mm}$$

Thus, when $H < h_2$ the water level in the tube stops at the height $h_1$ and if the initial water level exceeds $h_2$ the water fills up the tube completely.

3.5 **[1.0 points]** The water fills up the tube at any initial value of $H$, if equation (12) has no roots at all. This condition is fulfilled when

$$h_0 > \frac{R}{4tg\alpha}\cos\alpha, \qquad (14)$$

or

$$\sin\alpha > \frac{R}{4h_0} = 0{,}018.$$

**4. Outflow of water (3.0 points)**

4.1 **[3.0 points]** The water will start to pour out through the hole only if the water surface in the openings lose stability. This happens if the decrease of the potential energy in the gravitational field exceeds the increase in the absolute value of the surface energy. This condition is expressed by the inequality

$$2\sigma_0\pi h^2 < 2\frac{\pi R^2h^2}{6}\rho g, \qquad (15)$$

from which it follows that

$$R > \sqrt{\frac{6\sigma_0}{\rho g}} = 6{,}6\,\mathrm{mm} \qquad (16)$$
