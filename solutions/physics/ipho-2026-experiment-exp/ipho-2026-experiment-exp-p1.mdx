In this experimental exam, you will study three physical phenomena: the behavior of an ideal gas, the vapor pressure of water, and heat transfer by conduction. The objective of the exam is to analyze the relationships between pressure $P$, air volume $V$, temperature $T$, and time $t$, present your results in relevant graphs, and estimate associated physical constants and their associated uncertainties.

The apparatus consists of two concentric cylinders made of acrylic (Fig.1a). The inner cylinder (hereafter IC) can contain air and liquids, and the air inside IC is referred to as confined air (hereafter CA). The amount of liquid in the IC can be controlled by connecting the syringe to hose D.

### Part B. Vapor Pressure (8 т.)

In a closed container containing a liquid and its vapor at equilibrium, the vapor pressure depends on the absolute temperature $T$ according to the Clausius–Clapeyron equation:

$$
P_v=P_{v0}e^{-\frac{Q_v}{R}\left(\frac1T-\frac1{T_0}\right)}\qquad (3)
$$

Here, $T_0$ is the reference temperature, taken as 0 °C. $P_{v0}$ is the vapor pressure at $T_0$. $R$ is the universal gas constant, and $Q_v$ is the molar latent heat of vaporization of water.

### Part C. Heat Conduction (8 т.)

In this part of the exam, you will study the heat exchange between the water in the OC and the water in the IC through the wall that separates them. By analyzing the time evolution of the water temperature in the IC, $T_{IC}$, and the water temperature in the OC, $T_{OC}$, the objective of this section is to estimate the effective thermal resistance of the wall, the equilibrium temperature of the system, and the thermal conductivity of acrylic.

Heat conduction through a solid wall follows the relation:

$$
\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta t}=\frac1{R_{Th}}(T_{OC}-T_{IC})\qquad (4)
$$

where $\Delta Q$ is the heat received by the water in the IC through the wall during the time interval $\Delta t$. The effective thermal resistance $R_{Th}$ depends on the material and geometry of the wall separating IC and OC.

### Procedure

1. Set the water level in the OC to $h=15$ cm.
2. Heat the water in the OC to 65 °C. Use the pump to homogenize the water temperature.
3. Set the water level in the IC to $h=10$ cm and start the stopwatch.

**C1.** Record the internal temperature $T_{IC}$ and external temperature $T_{OC}$ as a function of time $t$ **[1 т.]**

**C2.** On a single graph, plot $T_{IC}$ and $T_{OC}$ as functions of time $t$. **[1 т.]**

For questions C3 and C4, ignore the heat capacity of the apparatus. $T_{eq}$ refers to the equilibrium temperature that would be reached by both IC and OC if there were no heat transfer to the environment.

**C3.** Plot $T_{OC}-T_{IC}$ as functions of both $T_{OC}$ and $T_{IC}$ on the same graph with $T_{OC}$ and $T_{IC}$ together on the same temperature axis and $T_{OC}-T_{IC}$ on the other axis. **[0,7 т.]**

**C4.** From the graphs constructed in C3, determine $T_{eq}$. It is not necessary to calculate uncertainty for this question. **[1,1 т.]**

If the variables are measured a finite number of times, Equation (4) can be expressed as

$$
\frac{T_{IC,j}-T_{IC,(j-1)}}{t_j-t_{j-1}}\propto\overline T_{OC}-\overline T_{IC}\qquad (5)
$$

where $j$ represents the measurement number and $\overline T$ are the average temperatures at each cylinder during the interval $t_{j-1}$ to $t_j$

**C5.** Graph the expression on the left hand side of Equation 5 as a function of the expression on the right hand side **[1 т.]**

**C6.** Determine the value of $R_{Th}$ from the graph constructed in C5. **[1,6 т.]**

Fourier’s law for radial heat conduction through a slim cylindrical wall can be written as:

$$
\frac{dQ}{dt}=-\lambda A\frac{dT}{dr}\qquad (6)
$$

where $A$ is the area of the wall, $\lambda$ is the thermal conductivity of the wall material, and $r$ denotes the distance from the axis of the cylinder.

**C7.** Using equations (4) and (6), determine the value $\lambda$ of the thermal conductivity of acrylic (the material separating IC and OC), indicating the formula that you used. **[1,6 т.]**
