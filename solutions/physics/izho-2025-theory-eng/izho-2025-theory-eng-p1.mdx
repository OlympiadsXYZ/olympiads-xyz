**1.1** **Problem 1.1 (3.0 points)**

An interplanetary spacecraft takes off from the Earth's surface with the second cosmic velocity $v_{2E} = 11.2$ km/s. Moving along a ballistic trajectory, the spacecraft, due to calculation errors, is captured by the Moon's gravity and falls onto its surface. Find the possible minimum $v_{min}$ and maximum $v_{max}$ collision velocities of the spacecraft with the Moon. The ratio of the mass $M_E$ of the Earth to the mass $M_M$ of the Moon is $M_E/M_M = 80.0$; the ratio of the radius $R_E$ of the Earth to the radius $R_M$ of the Moon is $R_E/R_M = 3.70$; the distance from the Earth to the Moon is $r = 60.0R_E$. Neglect the influence of the Sun. **[3 т.]**

**1.2** **Problem 1.2 (4.0 points)**

A fully deflated balloon with a maximum volume of $V_0 = 22.4\ l$, made of soft, non-stretchable rubberized fabric, is connected through a valve to a gas holder, which is a very large vessel with nitrogen. Assume that the nitrogen pressure in the gas holder is constant and equal to $P = 5.00$ atm, the ambient air pressure is $P_0 = 1.00$ atm, and the nitrogen temperature in the gas holder is equal to the ambient air temperature $T_0 = 273$ K. The gas holder valve opens and, after the balloon is quickly filled with nitrogen to its maximum volume and the nitrogen pressure in the balloon becomes equal to the pressure in the gas holder, the valve is closed. Find the number of moles of nitrogen $\nu$ in the balloon. Neglect heat exchange with the ambient air during the time the balloon is filled with nitrogen. **[4 т.]**

**1.3** **Problem 1.3 (3.0 points)**

The light sources of flashlight 1 are four identical white LED bulbs located on one line at a distance of $h = 4.90$ mm from each other. The bulbs can be considered point light sources. At a distance of $L = 10.0$ cm, parallel to them, there is a metal grid 2 made of thin wire with a diameter of $d = 0.20$ mm. The grid cells are square, whose side is equal to $a = 1.00$ mm. Calculate at what distances $S_m$ $(m = 1,2\ldots)$) the screen 3 should be located so that the grid image on it is the clearest and sharpest. **[3 т.]**

**Problem 1.1 (3.0 points)**

Let a spacecraft of mass $m$ start from the Earth surface. In order to be able to move away to infinity, it must have the second cosmic velocity at which its total energy becomes zero
$$
E = \frac{mv_{2E}^2}{2} - G\frac{mM_E}{R_E} = 0, \qquad (1)
$$
where $G$ stands for the gravitational constant. From formula (1) we obtain an expression for the second cosmic velocity
$$
v_{2E} = \sqrt{2G\frac{M_E}{R_E}}. \qquad (2)
$$
Now let us find the speed of the Moon's orbital motion $v_M$ in the reference frame associated with the Earth, and to do this we apply Newton's second law in the following form
$$
M_M\frac{v_M^2}{r} = F, \qquad (3)
$$
in which the force of gravitational interaction is determined by Newton's law of gravitation
$$
F = G\frac{M_E M_M}{r^2}. \qquad (4)
$$
From formulas (3) and (4), we find the speed of the Moon sought, which, with the aid of expression (2), takes the form
$$
v_M = \sqrt{G\frac{M_E}{r}} = v_{2E}\sqrt{\frac{R_E}{2r}}. \qquad (5)
$$
Let the apparatus cross the lunar trajectory far from the Moon, where the influence of its gravity can be neglected. The velocity of the apparatus $u$ relative to the Earth at the moment of crossing the Moon's orbit is found from the law of energy conservation as
$$
m\frac{u^2}{2} - G\frac{mM_E}{r} = 0, \qquad (6)
$$
which yields
$$
u = \sqrt{2G\frac{M_E}{r}} = \sqrt{2}v_M. \qquad (7)
$$
The velocity of the spacecraft $V$ in the frame of reference associated with the Moon after its entry into the Moon orbit is determined by the vector difference in the velocities of the spacecraft and the Moon. Its minimum value is equal to
$$
V_{min} = \left(\sqrt{2} - 1\right)v_M, \qquad (8)
$$
and the maximum value is
$$
V_{max} = \left(\sqrt{2} + 1\right)v_M. \qquad (9)
$$
To find the desired quantities, we move to the reference system associated with the Moon and apply the law of energy conservation in the form
$$
\frac{mv_{min}^2}{2} - G\frac{mM_M}{R_M} = \frac{mV_{min}^2}{2}, \qquad (10)
$$
$$
\frac{mv_{max}^2}{2} - G\frac{mM_M}{R_M} = \frac{mV_{max}^2}{2}, \qquad (11)
$$
and introducing the second cosmic velocity $v_{2M}$ for the Moon
$$
v_{2M} = \sqrt{2G\frac{M_M}{R_M}} = v_{2E}\sqrt{\frac{M_M}{M_E}\frac{R_E}{R_M}}. \qquad (12)
$$
we finally obtain the required values of the spacecraft speeds
$$
v_{min} = \sqrt{V_{min}^2 + v_{2M}^2} = 2.45\ \mathrm{km/s}, \qquad (13)
$$
$$
v_{max} = \sqrt{V_{max}^2 + v_{2M}^2} = 3.45\ \mathrm{km/s}. \qquad (14)
$$

**Problem 1.2 (3.0 points)**

Let $V$ be the volume of nitrogen in the gas holder, which is then pumped into the balloon. Imaginarily, this volume can be placed in an elastic shell, then the surrounding nitrogen in the gas holder does work on this gas
$$
A_V = PV. \qquad (1)
$$
When inflating a balloon, the atmosphere also does work on the gas, which is negative and equal to
$$
A_{atm} = -P_0V_0. \qquad (2)
$$
According to the second law of thermodynamics, work on a gas goes towards changing its internal energy
$$
A_V + A_{atm} = U - U_0. \qquad (3)
$$
The internal energy in the gas holder and in the ball are respectively found as
$$
U_0 = \nu C_V T_0, \qquad (4)
$$
$$
U = \nu C_V T, \qquad (5)
$$
where the molar heat capacity of nitrogen at constant volume $C_V$ is expressed through the universal gas constant $R$ in the form
$$
C_V = \frac{5}{2}R. \qquad (6)
$$
Note that immediately after the gas holder valve is closed, the temperature of the nitrogen in the ball differs from the temperature of the nitrogen in the gas holder itself.

Let us write the equation of state of an ideal gas for nitrogen in the gas tank and the balloon
$$
PV = \nu RT_0, \qquad (7)
$$
$$
PV_0 = \nu RT, \qquad (8)
$$
and solving the resulting set of equations together, we find
$$
\nu = \frac{27}{7} = 3.86\ \mathrm{mole}. \qquad (9)
$$
Here the condition is used that a volume of 22.4 liters under normal conditions contains 1 mole of gas.

**Problem 1.3 (3.0 points)**

Since each bulb is a point source of light, the light from each bulb at any position of the screen creates a clear picture of shadows in the form of an enlarged image of the grid. The size of one cell of the image $\Delta x$ is easily expressed through the specified distances
$$
\Delta x = a\frac{L+S}{L}. \qquad (1)
$$
The image of the grid created by the light of another bulb will simply be shifted by some value $\delta x$. This shift is directed parallel to the "shift" when moving from one bulb to another. Therefore, the images of the grid wires parallel to the line of the bulbs will be clear at any position of the screen, the shadows perpendicular to the line of the bulbs will be "blurred".

If the distance between the bulbs is $h$, then the magnitude of the image shift will be equal to
$$
\delta x = h\frac{S}{L}. \qquad (2)
$$
It is easy to understand that the image created by all the bulbs will be clear and sharp if the amount of shift when moving from one bulb to another is an integer number of image cells, that is, when
$$
\delta x = m\Delta x. \qquad (3)
$$
Using the previously obtained formulas (1)-(2), we find
$$
S_m = L\frac{m}{\frac{h}{a} - m}. \qquad (4)
$$
Given the numerical data given in the problem statement, the numerical values of possible screen positions are given in the table below.

| $m$ | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $S_m$, см | 2,56 | 6,90 | 15,8 | 44,4 |

$\int \frac{dx}{x} = \ln|x| + C$, where $C$ stands for an arbitrary constant;

$\int \frac{dx}{(a^2+x^2)^{3/2}} = \frac{x}{a^2\sqrt{a^2+x^2}} + C$, where *a* – constant, $C$ – arbitrary constant;

$(1+x)^{\gamma} \approx 1 + \gamma x + \frac{\gamma(\gamma-1)}{2}x^2$, for $|x| \ll 1$ and any value of $\gamma$;

$\tan x \approx \sin x \approx x$, for $|x| \ll 1$;

$\ln(1+x) \approx x$, for $|x| \ll 1$.
