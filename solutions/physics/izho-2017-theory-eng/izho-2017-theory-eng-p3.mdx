### 1. Mathematical introduction (3 т.)

By definition, it is believed that terms of the numerical sequence obey the recurrence relation if each successive term is expressed through the previous ones. For example, for a well known geometric progression we have

$$
x_k = \lambda x_{k-1}, \qquad (1)
$$

where $k = 1, 2, 3, ...$, $\lambda$ stands for a fixed number and zeroth term of the numerical sequence has some value of $A$, i.e. $x_0 = A$.

**1.1** Obtain an explicit formula for an arbitrary term of the sequence $x_k$, i.e. express it through the successive number $k$, the initial value $A$ and $\lambda$. **[0,2 т.]**

Let us consider the number $\lambda = 2 + \sqrt{3}$. Taking its natural power of $k$, the result can be presented in the following form

$$
\lambda^k = p_k + q_k\sqrt{3}, \qquad (2)
$$

where $p_k, q_k$ denote some integer numbers.

**1.2** Find the recurrence relations, expressing the values of $p_k, q_k$ through the previous values $p_{k-1}, q_{k-1}$. Find also the inverse relations, expressing $p_{k-1}, q_{k-1}$ through $p_k, q_k$. **[0,4 т.]**

**1.3** Calculate the numerical values of the coefficients $p_k, q_k$ for $k = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5$. **[0,7 т.]**

**1.4** Express the number $\lambda^{-k} = \left(2 + \sqrt{3}\right)^{-k}$ in terms of $p_k, q_k$. **[0,2 т.]**

Let the terms of a certain numerical sequence obey the recurrence relation

$$
x_{k+1} = 4x_k - x_{k-1},\quad k = 1, ..., N - 1, \qquad (3)
$$

where it is known that $N$ is some integer number, $x_0 = A$ and $x_N = B$, $A, B$ designate some values.

**1.5** Obtain an explicit formula for an arbitrary term $x_k$ of the sequence (3), i.e. express it in terms of the number $k$ and values $A, B, N$. **[1 т.]**

**1.6** Obtain an explicit formula for an arbitrary term $x_k$ of the sequence (3) through $p_k, q_k$, found in.1.2-1.3. **[0,5 т.]**

**Hint**. The solution to the recurrence relation (3) must be sought in the form $x_k = C\lambda^k$, where $C$ is a constant. Determine at what values of $\lambda$ it is possible and construct an exact solution that satisfies all above stated conditions.

### 1. Mathematical introduction (3 т.)

**1.1 [0.2 points]** From the course of school mathematics it is known that geometrical progression terms are explicitly expressed as

$$
x_k = A\lambda^k. \qquad (1)
$$

**1.2 [0.4 points]** Let us express $\lambda^k$ recurrently in terms of $\lambda^{k-1}$:

$$
\lambda^k = \lambda^{k-1}\cdot\lambda
$$

and transform it as follows

$$
\begin{aligned} \lambda^k &= \left(p_k + q_k\sqrt{3}\right) = \left(p_{k-1} + q_{k-1}\sqrt{3}\right)\cdot\left(2 + \sqrt{3}\right) = 2p_{k-1} + p_{k-1}\sqrt{3} + 2q_{k-1}\sqrt{3} + 3q_{k-1} = \\ &= \left(2p_{k-1} + 3q_{k-1}\right) + \left(p_{k-1} + 2q_{k-1}\right)\sqrt{3}. \end{aligned} \qquad (2)
$$

This equality implies the required recurrence relations in the form

$$
\begin{aligned} p_k &= 2p_{k-1} + 3q_{k-1} \\ q_k &= p_{k-1} + 2q_{k-1}. \end{aligned} \qquad (3)
$$

Inverse relations are obtained analogously

$$
\begin{aligned} \lambda^{k-1} &= p_{k-1} + q_{k-1} = \lambda^k\cdot\lambda^{-1} = \left(p_k + q_k\sqrt{3}\right)\cdot\left(2 - \sqrt{3}\right) = \\ &= \left(2p_k - 3q_k\right) + \left(2q_k - p_k\right)\sqrt{3}, \end{aligned} \qquad (4)
$$

and, thus,

$$
\begin{aligned} p_{k-1} &= 2p_k - 3q_k, \\ q_{k-1} &= 2q_k - p_k. \end{aligned} \qquad (5)
$$

**1.3 [0.7 points]** Calculation of the coefficients is much easier to carry out in series, given that $p_0 = 1,\quad q_0 = 0$. The results are shown in Table 1.

Table 1.

| $k$ | $p_k$ | $q_k$ |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1 | 0 |
| 1 | 2 | 1 |
| 2 | 7 | 4 |
| 3 | 26 | 15 |
| 4 | 97 | 56 |
| 5 | 362 | 209 |

**1.4 [0.2 points]** Note that

$$
\lambda^{-1} = \frac{1}{2 + \sqrt{3}} = 2 - \sqrt{3}, \qquad (6)
$$

therefore,

$$
\lambda^{-k} = \left(2 - \sqrt{3}\right)^k = p_k - q_k\sqrt{3}. \qquad (7)
$$

**1.5 [1.0 points]** Using the hint, we substitute $x_k = C\lambda^k$ into the recurrence relation and obtain the equation to determine $\lambda$ in the form

$$
\lambda^{k+1} = 4\lambda^k - \lambda^{k-1}. \qquad (8)
$$

After reduction the following quadratic equation is derived

$$
\lambda^2 - 4\lambda + 1 = 0, \qquad (9)
$$

which has two solutions

$$
\lambda_{1,2} = 2\pm\sqrt{3}. \qquad (10)
$$

Consequently, the general solution to the recurrence relation (3) is explicitly written by

$$
x_k = C_1\lambda_1^k + C_2\lambda_2^k, \qquad (11)
$$

where $C_1, C_2$ are arbitrary constants that are determined by the boundary conditions:

$$
\begin{aligned} x_0 = A &\quad\Rightarrow\quad C_1 + C_2 = A \\ x_0 = B &\quad\Rightarrow\quad C_1\lambda_1^N + C_2\lambda_2^N = B. \end{aligned} \qquad (12)
$$

Solving the linear set of equation yields

$$
\begin{cases} C_1 + C_2 = A, \\ C_1\lambda_1^N + C_2\lambda_2^N = B \end{cases} \Rightarrow \begin{cases} C_1 = \dfrac{B - A\lambda_2^N}{\lambda_1^N - \lambda_2^N}, \\ C_2 = \dfrac{A\lambda_1^N - B}{\lambda_1^N - \lambda_2^N}. \end{cases} \qquad (13)
$$

Substituting this solution into (11), it is possible to rewrite it in the following symmetrical form

$$
\begin{aligned} x_k &= C_1\lambda_1^k + C_2\lambda_2^k = \frac{B - A\lambda_2^N}{\lambda_1^N - \lambda_2^N}\lambda_1^k + \frac{A\lambda_1^N - B}{\lambda_1^N - \lambda_2^N}\lambda_2^k = \\ &= \frac{A\lambda_1^N\lambda_2^k - B\lambda_2^k + B\lambda_1^k - A\lambda_2^N\lambda_1^k}{\lambda_1^N - \lambda_2^N} = \frac{A\left(\lambda_1^{N-k} - \lambda_2^{N-k}\right) + B\left(\lambda_1^k - \lambda_2^k\right)}{\lambda_1^N - \lambda_2^N}. \end{aligned} \qquad (14)
$$

The derivation of the last relation takes into account that according to the Vieta theorem $\lambda_2 = \lambda_1^{-1}$.

**1.6 [0.5 points]** In view of the above formulas for the $\lambda_{1,2}^k$, we find that

$$
\lambda_1^k - \lambda_2^k = \lambda_1^k - \lambda_1^{-k} = \left(p_k + q_k\sqrt{3}\right) - \left(p_k - q_k\sqrt{3}\right) = 2q_k\sqrt{3}, \qquad (15)
$$

and, finally,

$$
x_k = \frac{A\left(\lambda_1^{N-k} - \lambda_2^{N-k}\right) + B\left(\lambda_1^k - \lambda_2^k\right)}{\lambda_1^N - \lambda_2^N} = \frac{Aq_{N-k} + Bq_k}{q_N}. \qquad (16)
$$

### 2. Wire frame in the shape of a prism (7 т.)

**2.1 [0.8 points]** If the vertices of the cube with the same potentials are connected, then, the following equivalent circuits are obtained

and easily calculated using the standard method as

Ultimately, the cube resistance for the given connection is found as

$$
R = \frac{7}{12}R_0. \qquad (17)
$$

**2.2 [0.2 points]** Visual symmetry of the circuit and of the initial conditions provides obvious relations

$$
y_k = -x_k, \qquad (18)
$$

$$
x_{N-k} = x_k. \qquad (19)
$$

**2.3 [1.0 points]** The algebraic sum of the currents entering a node is equal to zero, thus, using Ohm's law, the following equation is obtained for the node $x_k$

$$
\frac{x_{k-1} - x_k}{R_0} + \frac{x_{k+1} - x_k}{R_0} + \frac{y_k - x_k}{R_0} = 0. \qquad (20)
$$

Since $y_k = -x_k$, the recurrence relation holds

$$
x_{k+1} - 4x_k + x_{k-1} = 0. \qquad (21)
$$

**2.4 [0.2 points]** For an unambiguous determination of all values $x_k$, we need to explicitly specify two boundary conditions. One of those is the initial potential defined as

$$
x_0 = \varphi_0, \qquad (22)
$$

whereas the other follows from the symmetry condition (19), which is valid for any $k$, and, in particular, for $k = 0$ (despite the fact that the node with the number $N$ does not exist in the circuit!)

$$
x_N = x_0. \qquad (23)
$$

**2.5 [0.2 points]** The recurrence relation (21) has been considered in the Mathematical introduction. Therefore, you can use the obtained solution (16) by setting:

$$
x_k = \frac{Aq_{N-k} + Bq_k}{q_N} = \varphi_0\frac{q_{N-k} + q_k}{q_N}. \qquad (24)
$$

**2.6 [0.4 points]** The current in the source circuit is found as the sum of the currents flowing from the node $x_0$:

$$
I = \frac{x_0 - x_1}{R_0} + \frac{x_0 - x_{N-1}}{R_0} + \frac{x_0 - y_0}{R_0} = \frac{4x_0 - 2x_1}{R_0}. \qquad (25)
$$

Here it has been taken into account that $y_0 = -x_0,\quad x_{N-1} = x_1$. Substituting the values for $x_0, x_1$, results in

$$
\begin{aligned} I_0 &= \frac{4x_0 - 2x_1}{R_0} = \frac{2}{R_0}\left(2\phi_0 - \phi_0\frac{q_{N-1} + q_1}{q_N}\right) = \frac{2\phi_0}{R_0}\left(2 - \frac{q_{N-1} + 1}{q_N}\right) = \\ &= \frac{2\phi_0}{R_0}\frac{2q_N - q_{N-1} - 1}{q_N} = \frac{2\phi_0}{R_0}\frac{2q_N - \left(2q_N - p_N\right) - 1}{q_N} = \frac{2\phi_0}{R_0}\frac{p_N - 1}{q_N}. \end{aligned} \qquad (26)
$$

At the last step the relation (5) has been used, $q_{N-1} = 2q_N - p_N$.

**2.7 [0.2 points]** By formulation, the input voltage for the given circuit is

$$
U_0 = 2\varphi_0, \qquad (27)
$$

consequently, the resistance is found in the following elegant form

$$
R_N = \frac{U_0}{I_0} = R_0\frac{q_N}{p_N - 1}. \qquad (28)
$$

**2.8 [1.0 points]** Calculations are easily performed using numerical values in Table 1.

Table 2. Resistances of prisms.

| $N$ | $p_N$ | $q_N$ | $R_N$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | 1 | $R_0$ |
| 2 | 7 | 4 | $R_0\frac{4}{7 - 1} = \frac{2}{3}R_0$ |
| 3 | 26 | 15 | $R_0\frac{15}{26 - 1} = \frac{3}{4}R_0$ |
| 4 | 97 | 56 | $R_0\frac{56}{97 - 1} = \frac{7}{12}R_0$ |
| 5 | 362 | 209 | $R_0\frac{209}{362 - 1} = \frac{11}{19}R_0$ |

Note that for a cubic prism with $N = 4$ the resistance coincides with that previously found in 2.1.

**2.10 [1.0 points]** The limit of the formula (28) can be found in various ways, for example, expressing

$$
p_N = \frac{1}{2}\left(\lambda^N - \lambda^{-N}\right),\quad q_N = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}\left(\lambda^N + \lambda^{-N}\right), \qquad (29)
$$

where $\lambda = 2 + \sqrt{3} > 1$.

Then,

$$
R_\infty = \lim_{N\to\infty}R_N = R_0\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{q_N}{p_N - 1} = R_0\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}\left(\lambda^N + \lambda^{-N}\right)}{\frac{1}{2}\left(\lambda^N - \lambda^{-N}\right) - 1} = \frac{R_0}{\sqrt{3}}. \qquad (30)
$$

**2.11 [1.5 points]** Evaluation gives rise to

$$
\frac{R_\infty}{R_0} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\approx 0.577. \qquad (31)
$$

Then, we carry out the calculation of the relative error of the approximate expression for different values of $N$ listed in Table 2.
