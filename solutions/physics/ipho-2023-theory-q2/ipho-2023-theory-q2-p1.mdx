*Theory — International Physics Olympiad 2023 Tokyo Japan — Q2 — English (Official) · 2,5 т.*

## Условие

We discuss the stability of large nuclei and estimate the mass of neutron stars theoretically and experimentally.

The rest-energy of a nucleus $m(Z,N)c^2$ consisting of $Z$ protons and $N$ neutrons is smaller than the sum of rest-energies of protons and neutrons, hereafter called nucleons, by the binding energy $B(Z,N)$, where $c$ is the speed of light in vacuum. Ignoring minor corrections, we can approximate the binding energy consisting of the volume term with $a_V$, the surface term with $a_S$, the Coulomb energy term with $a_C$, and the symmetry energy term with $a_{\mathrm{sym}}$ in the following way.

$$m(Z,N)c^2 = Am_Nc^2 - B(Z,N), \qquad B(Z,N) = a_V A - a_S A^{2/3} - a_C\frac{Z^2}{A^{1/3}} - a_{\mathrm{sym}}\frac{(N-Z)^2}{A}, \qquad (1)$$

where $A = Z + N$ is the mass number and $m_N$ is the nucleon mass. In the calculation, use $a_V \approx 15.8\ \mathrm{MeV}$, $a_S \approx 17.8\ \mathrm{MeV}$, $a_C \approx 0.711\ \mathrm{MeV}$, and $a_{\mathrm{sym}} \approx 23.7\ \mathrm{MeV}$ ($\mathrm{MeV} = 10^6$ electron volts).

**A.1** Under the condition of $Z = N$, determine $A$ for maximizing the binding energy per nucleon, $B/A$. **[0,9 т.]**

**A.2** Under the condition of fixed $A$, the atomic number of the most stable nucleus $Z^*$ is determined by maximizing $B(Z, A - Z)$. For $A = 197$, calculate $Z^*$ using Eq. (1). **[0,9 т.]**

**A.3** A nucleus having large $A$ breaks up into lighter nuclei through fission in order to minimize the total rest-mass energy. For simplicity, we consider one of multiple ways to break a nucleus with $(Z, N)$ into two equal nuclei, which occurs when the following energy relation holds,

$$m(Z,N)c^2 > 2m(Z/2, N/2)c^2.$$

When this relation is written as

$$Z^2/A > C_{\mathrm{fission}}\frac{a_S}{a_C},$$

obtain $C_{\mathrm{fission}}$ up to two significant digits. **[0,7 т.]**
