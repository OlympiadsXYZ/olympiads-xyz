## Условие

A tricycle of mass $m = 100\ \mathrm{kg}$ is traveling north on a horizontal surface. The geometry of the tricycle is defined as follows:

- Wheelbase (distance from front axle to rear axle): $L = 1.0\ \mathrm{m}$
- Rear track width (distance between rear wheels): $d = 1.0\ \mathrm{m}$
- Center of mass (CM) location: on the longitudinal symmetry axis, a distance $b = 0.4\ \mathrm{m}$ forward of the rear axle
- Radius of gyration about the CM: $k = 0.5\ \mathrm{m}$

(The radius of gyration $k$ is defined by $I_{\mathrm{CM}} = mk^2$, where $I_{\mathrm{CM}}$ is the moment of inertia of the tricycle about a vertical axis through its center of mass.)

Seeing a patch of ice on their right side ahead, the rider panics and slams on the brakes. The front wheel and the left rear wheel slide on dry pavement with a coefficient of kinetic friction $\mu_k = 0.5$, while the right rear wheel slides on frictionless ice ($\mu = 0$).

This asymmetry in friction forces produces a net torque about the center of mass, causing the tricycle to begin rotating. Calculate the initial angular acceleration $\alpha$ of the tricycle.

(A) $3.0\ \mathrm{rad/s^2}$, Counter-Clockwise

(B) $3.0\ \mathrm{rad/s^2}$, Clockwise

(C) $3.3\ \mathrm{rad/s^2}$, Counter-Clockwise

(D) $6.0\ \mathrm{rad/s^2}$, Counter-Clockwise

(E) $3.3\ \mathrm{rad/s^2}$ Clockwise

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

Consider the forces acting on the three wheels while breaking. The two wheels in contact with the rough surface generates a kinetic friction force opposing the motion (pointing South). Using a symmetry argument we can reduce this down to just the left wheel, which is displaced a lateral distance $d/2$ to the left of the central axis.

Now we calculate the torque generated by the left rear wheel about the CM. The position vector $\vec{r}$ points to the left (West), and the force vector $\vec{F}$ points backward (South). Using the cross product we get:

$$
\vec{\tau}_{left} = (r\hat{\imath}) \times (-F\hat{\jmath})
$$

The force pulls backward on the left side of the vehicle. This creates a twisting moment that rotates the vehicle counter-clockwise (Left).

$$
\tau_{net} = F_{fric} \cdot \frac{d}{2} \quad \text{(Counter-Clockwise)}
$$

Since the torque is non-zero and counter-clockwise, the tricycle acquires an initial angular acceleration $\alpha$ in the counter-clockwise direction.

$$
\alpha \propto \tau_{net} > 0 \implies \text{CCW Rotation}
$$

To find the exact value, we first calculate the moment of inertia ($I$) using the radius of gyration $k$:

$$
I = mk^2 = 100(0.5)^2 = 25\ \mathrm{kg} \cdot \mathrm{m}^2
$$

Next, we determine the normal forces on the wheels to find the friction magnitude. Summing moments about the rear axle allows us to find the normal force on the front wheel ($N_f$):

$$
\Sigma\tau_{rear} = 0 \implies N_fL = mgb
$$

Plugging in our values above therefore gives us:

$$
N_f(1.0) = (100)(10)(0.4) \implies N_f = 400\ \mathrm{N}
$$

The remaining weight is supported by the two rear wheels. Since the CM is on the symmetry axis, this load is split equally:

$$
N_{rear\_total} = mg - N_f = 1000 - 400 = 600\ \mathrm{N}
$$

$$
N_{left} = \frac{600}{2} = 300\ \mathrm{N}
$$

Now we calculate the kinetic friction force on the left rear wheel:

$$
F_{friction} = \mu_kN_{left} = 0.5(300) = 150\ \mathrm{N}
$$

As we stated previously, the net torque is generated solely by the left rear wheel acting at a lever arm of $d/2$:

$$
\tau_{net} = F_{friction} \times \frac{d}{2} = 150 \times 0.5 = 75\ \mathrm{N} \cdot \mathrm{m}
$$

Finally, from Newton’s second law for rotation we get:

$$
75 = 25\alpha \implies \alpha = 3.0\ \mathrm{rad/s^2}
$$
