## Условие

A tricycle of mass $m = 100\ \mathrm{kg}$ is traveling north on a horizontal surface. The geometry of the tricycle is defined as follows:

- Wheelbase (distance from front axle to rear axle): $L = 1.0\ \mathrm{m}$
- Rear track width (distance between rear wheels): $d = 1.0\ \mathrm{m}$
- Center of mass (CM) location: on the longitudinal symmetry axis, a distance $b = 0.4\ \mathrm{m}$ forward of the rear axle
- Radius of gyration about the CM: $k = 0.5\ \mathrm{m}$

(The radius of gyration $k$ is defined by $I_{\mathrm{CM}} = mk^2$, where $I_{\mathrm{CM}}$ is the moment of inertia of the tricycle about a vertical axis through its center of mass.)

Seeing a patch of ice on their right side ahead, the rider panics and slams on the brakes. The front wheel and the left rear wheel slide on dry pavement with a coefficient of kinetic friction $\mu_k = 0.5$, while the right rear wheel slides on frictionless ice ($\mu = 0$).

This asymmetry in friction forces produces a net torque about the center of mass, causing the tricycle to begin rotating. Calculate the initial angular acceleration $\alpha$ of the tricycle.

(A) $3.0\ \mathrm{rad/s^2}$, Counter-Clockwise

(B) $3.0\ \mathrm{rad/s^2}$, Clockwise

(C) $3.3\ \mathrm{rad/s^2}$, Counter-Clockwise

(D) $6.0\ \mathrm{rad/s^2}$, Counter-Clockwise

(E) $3.3\ \mathrm{rad/s^2}$ Clockwise
