## Условие

A small hard block of mass $3m$ is suspended from a thread of length $L$. A second block of mass $m$ is located on an incline, originally at rest, a height $y$ above the level of the large mass. When the smaller block is released it slides, without friction, down a ramp, and then collides elastically with the larger block. The large block swings around so that the tension in the string just barely drops to zero at the top of the loop. The small block slides back up the ramp, rising to a maximum vertical height $h$.

**a)** Find $h$ in terms of $y$.

**b)** Find $L$ in terms of $y$.

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

<ProblemSection id="solution-a">

### (a) (13 points)

The speed of the small block just before it collides with the large block, $v_0$, is found from conservation of mechanical energy:

$$
K_f+U_f=K_i+U_i\qquad\text{(2-1)}
$$



$$
\frac12mv_0^2+0=0+mgy\qquad\text{(2-2)}
$$



$$
v_0^2=2gy\qquad\text{(2-3)}
$$

[3 pt]

The speed of the large block just after the collision, $v_2$, is found from conservation of momentum.

$$
3mv_2=mv_0+mv_1\qquad\text{(2-4)}
$$



$$
3v_2=v_0+v_1\qquad\text{(2-5)}
$$

[2 pt]

where $v_1$ is the speed of the small block just after the collision.

This collision is elastic, so kinetic energy is conserved, or

$$
\frac123mv_2^2=\frac12mv_0^2-\frac12mv_1^2\qquad\text{(2-6)}
$$



$$
3v_2^2=v_0^2-v_1^2\qquad\text{(2-7)}
$$

[2 pt]

Dividing Eq. 2-7 by Eq. 2-5 yields

$$
v_2=v_0-v_1\qquad\text{(2-8)}
$$

which can be combined with Eq. 2-5 to give

$$
v_1=v_0/2\qquad\text{(2-9)}
$$



$$
v_2=v_0/2\qquad\text{(2-10)}
$$

[3 pt]

The speed of the small block as it starts back up the path, $v_1$, is related to the height that it rises by conservation of mechanical energy (Eq. 2-1), this can be combined with Eq. 2-9 and Eq. 2-3 to show

$$
K_f+U_f=K_i+U_i\qquad\text{(2-11)}
$$



$$
0+mgh=\frac12mv_1^2+0\qquad\text{(2-12)}
$$



$$
gh=\frac12v_1^2\qquad\text{(2-13)}
$$



$$
=\frac12\left(\frac{v_0}2\right)^2\qquad\text{(2-14)}
$$



$$
=\frac12\frac{2gy}4\qquad\text{(2-15)}
$$



$$
h=y/4\qquad\text{(2-16)}
$$

[3 pt]



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-b">

### (b) (12 points)

Define $v_3$ to be the speed of the large block at the top of the loop. For the large block to just *barely* make it over the top of the loop requires the net force to be equal to the force of gravity:

$$
F_{\mathrm{net}}=3mg=3m\frac{v_3^2}L\qquad\text{(2-17)}
$$

[3 pt]

The last expression comes from the condition that the tangential acceleration of the large block is zero at the top of the loop.

The speed of the large block at the top of the loop is related to the speed at the bottom by conservation of energy (Eq. 2-1).

$$
K_f+U_f=K_i+U_i\qquad\text{(2-18)}
$$



$$
\frac123mv_3^2+(3m)g(2L)=\frac123mv_2^2+0\qquad\text{(2-19)}
$$



$$
v_3^2+4gL=v_2^2\qquad\text{(2-20)}
$$

[3 pt]

Combining the last expression (Eq. 2-20) with the expression for net force (Eq. 2-17) yields

$$
v_3^2+4gL=v_2^2\qquad\text{(2-21)}
$$



$$
gL+4gL=v_2^2\qquad\text{(2-22)}
$$



$$
5gL=v_2^2\qquad\text{(2-23)}
$$

[3 pt]

The combine this result (Eq. 2-23) with the results of part (a), Eq. 2-10. Consequently

$$
v_2=\frac{v_0}2\qquad\text{(2-24)}
$$



$$
v_2^2=\frac{v_0^2}4\qquad\text{(2-25)}
$$



$$
5gL=\frac{2gy}4\qquad\text{(2-26)}
$$



$$
L=y/10\qquad\text{(2-27)}
$$

[3 pt]



</ProblemSection>
