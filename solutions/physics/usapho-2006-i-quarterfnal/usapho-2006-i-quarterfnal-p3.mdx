A uniform thin rod is suspended from the ceiling by two identical ideal vertical springs attached to the ends of the rod. Two experiments are performed:

<ProblemSection id="experiment-1">

### Expt. 1:

From equilibrium, both ends of the rod are pulled down by an equal, small amount. When released, the rod oscillates with period $T_1$.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="experiment-2">

### Expt. 2:

From equilibrium, one end of the rod is pulled down by a small amount, and the other end is pushed up by an equal amount. When released, the rod oscillates with a period $T_2$.

</ProblemSection>

Find the numerical value of the ratio $T_1/T_2$.

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

There are at least two ways to approach this problem. Other solution methods may exist as well.

<ProblemSection id="solution-method-1">

### Method 1

This approach focuses on the differential equations of oscillatory motion.

In general, any system satisfying an equation of the form

$$
\ddot z=-\omega^2z\qquad\text{(3-1)}
$$

will oscillate with angular frequency $\omega$. $\ddot z$ is shorthand for the second time derivative of the variable $z$, or

$$
\ddot z=\frac{d^2z}{dt^2}\qquad\text{(3-2)}
$$

Students don't need to write this down, but they will need to use it if they take this approach.

Let the rod have mass $M$ and length $2r$. Let the springs each have constant $k$.

In experiment 1, let $x$ be the vertical displacement of the rod from equilibrium. Then the equation of motion is

$$
Ma=F_{\mathrm{net}}\qquad\text{(3-3)}
$$



$$
M\ddot x=-2kx\qquad\text{(3-4)}
$$



$$
\ddot x=-\frac{2k}Mx\qquad\text{(3-5)}
$$



$$
\omega_1=\sqrt{\frac{2k}M}\qquad\text{(3-6)}
$$

[6 pt]

where the 2 on the right hand side of Eq. 3-4 arises from the presence of two springs. In the last line we take advantage of Eq. 3-1; it does *not* need to be derived. Note the absence of gravity from this expression. $x$ is the change in length from the equilibrium position, and then net force at the equilibrium position is zero.

In experiment 2, let $\theta$ be the angular displacement of the rod from equilibrium. Note that for small $\theta$, the extension of each spring from equilibrium is given by

$$
x=r\theta\qquad\text{(3-7)}
$$

[2 pt]

The force of the spring on each end of the rod is

$$
F=-kx=-kr\theta\qquad\text{(3-8)}
$$

[2 pt]

so the net torque on the rod about the center of rotation is

$$
\tau_{\mathrm{net}}=-2(kr\theta)r\qquad\text{(3-9)}
$$

[2 pt]

The rotational inertia of the rod of length $2r$ about the center is

$$
I=\frac1{12}M(2r)^2=\frac13Mr^2\qquad\text{(3-10)}
$$

[3 pt]

Then, combining Eq. 3-7 through Eq. 3-10,

$$
I\alpha=\tau\qquad\text{(3-11)}
$$



$$
\frac13Mr^2\ddot\theta=-2kr^2\theta\qquad\text{(3-12)}
$$



$$
\ddot\theta=-\frac{6k}M\theta\qquad\text{(3-13)}
$$



$$
\omega_2=\sqrt{\frac{6k}M}\qquad\text{(3-14)}
$$

[6 pt]

Finally, since

$$
T=\frac{2\pi}\omega\qquad\text{(3-15)}
$$

[2 pt]

we have from Eq. 3-6 and Eq. 3-14

$$
\frac{T_1}{T_2}=\frac{\omega_2}{\omega_1}\qquad\text{(3-16)}
$$



$$
=\sqrt3\qquad\text{(3-17)}
$$

[2 pt]



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-method-2">

### Method 2

This method focuses on the fundamental expression for the period of an oscillating system

The period of oscillation of a mass spring system is given by

$$
T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac mk}\qquad\text{(3-18)}
$$

[3 pt]

where $k$ is the spring constant and $m$ is the mass.

In the first experiment there are two springs, each effectively moving *half* of the total mass $M$ of the thin rod, so

$$
T_1=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{M/2}k}=2\pi\sqrt{\frac M{2k}}\qquad\text{(3-19)}
$$

[2 pt]

The period of oscillation of a rotational system is given by

$$
T_2=2\pi\sqrt{\frac I\kappa}\qquad\text{(3-20)}
$$

[3 pt]

where $\kappa$ is the rotational spring constant and $I$ the rotational inertia. In this case,

$$
\kappa\theta=\tau\qquad\text{(3-21)}
$$

[2 pt]

where $\tau$ is the torque about the center of rotation. That torque is provided for by the force of the spring,

$$
\tau=rF=\frac L2kx\qquad\text{(3-22)}
$$

[4 pt]

where $x$ is the extension of the spring and $L$ the length of the thin rod.

The angle $\theta$ is given by

$$
\theta\simeq\frac x{L/2}\qquad\text{(3-23)}
$$

[2 pt]

for small $x$, so

$$
\kappa=\tau/\theta\qquad\text{(3-24)}
$$



$$
=k\frac{L^2}4\qquad\text{(3-25)}
$$

[2 pt]

for *one* spring.

The rotational inertia for a thin rod about the center is

$$
I_{\mathrm{rod}}=\frac1{12}ML^2\qquad\text{(3-26)}
$$

[1 pt]

but we are interested in only *half*, so

$$
I=\frac1{24}ML^2\qquad\text{(3-27)}
$$

[2 pt]

Combine Eq. 3-20, Eq. 3-25, and Eq. 3-27. The period of oscillation is then

$$
T_2=2\pi\sqrt{\frac I\kappa}\qquad\text{(3-28)}
$$



$$
=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{ML^2/24}{kL^2/4}}\qquad\text{(3-29)}
$$



$$
=2\pi\sqrt{\frac M{6k}}\qquad\text{(3-30)}
$$

[2 pt]

The ratio is found by dividing Eq. 3-19 by Eq. 3-30:

$$
\frac{T_1}{T_2}=\sqrt3\qquad\text{(3-31)}
$$

[2 pt]



</ProblemSection>
