## Условие

A car starts at point P from rest, with zero initial acceleration, but with a constant rate of change of tangential acceleration of 2 m/s$^3$. The car is constrained to travel on a circular track of unknown radius. When the car reaches point Q, the angle between the acceleration and velocity vectors is $30^\circ$. The center of the track is point O. What is the angle (in radians) POQ?

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

There are at least two ways to approach this problem. Other solution methods may exist as well.

<ProblemSection id="solution-method-1">

### Method 1

Here the focus is on angular rotation variables.

Let $kr=2.0$ m/s$^3$. Then the angular acceleration is given by

$$
\alpha_t=kt\qquad\text{(1-1)}
$$

[3 pt]

and the angular velocity is given by

$$
\omega=\int_0^t\alpha_t\,dt=\frac12kt^2\qquad\text{(1-2)}
$$

[3 pt]

and the angular position relative to $P$ is given by

$$
\theta=\int_0^t\omega\,dt=\frac16kt^3\qquad\text{(1-3)}
$$

[3 pt]

The velocity is always tangential, the acceleration has tangential and radial components, so the tangent of the angle $\phi$ between $\vec v$ and $\vec a$ is given by

$$
\tan\phi=a_r/a_t\qquad\text{(1-4)}
$$

[2 pt]

Now

$$
a_r=r\omega^2\qquad\text{(1-5)}
$$

[2 pt]

and

$$
a_t=r\alpha_t\qquad\text{(1-6)}
$$

[2 pt]

so combining Eq. 1-1 through Eq. 1-6.

$$
\tan\phi=\frac{a_r}{a_t}\qquad\text{(1-7)}
$$



$$
=\frac{r\omega^2}{r\alpha_t}\qquad\text{(1-8)}
$$



$$
=\frac{(\frac12kt^2)^2}{kt}\qquad\text{(1-9)}
$$



$$
=\frac14kt^3\qquad\text{(1-10)}
$$



$$
=\frac32\theta\qquad\text{(1-11)}
$$

[6 pt]

Then

$$
\theta=\frac23\tan\phi\qquad\text{(1-12)}
$$



$$
=\frac29\sqrt3\qquad\text{(1-13)}
$$

[4 pt]



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-method-2">

### Method 2

Here the focus is on linear variables.

Let $c=2.0$ m/s$^3$. Then the tangential acceleration is given by

$$
a_t=ct\qquad\text{(1-14)}
$$

[3 pt]

and the tangential velocity is given by

$$
v=\int_0^t a_t\,dt=\frac12ct^2\qquad\text{(1-15)}
$$

[3 pt]

and the arc length $s$ traveled in time $t$ is given by

$$
s=\int_0^t v\,dt=\frac16ct^3\qquad\text{(1-16)}
$$

[3 pt]

The radial acceleration is found from Eq. 1-15,

$$
a_r=\frac{v^2}r=\frac{c^2t^4}{4r}\qquad\text{(1-17)}
$$

[2 pt]

The angle $\phi$ between the velocity vector and the acceleration vector is found from the dot product:

$$
\vec v\cdot\vec a=va\cos\phi\qquad\text{(1-18)}
$$

[2 pt]

where $v$ and $a$ are the magnitudes of the velocity and acceleration. In unit vector notation,

$$
\vec a=a_t\hat\theta+a_r\hat r\qquad\text{(1-19)}
$$



$$
\vec v=v\hat\theta\qquad\text{(1-20)}
$$

so

$$
\vec v\cdot\vec a=va_t\qquad\text{(1-21)}
$$



$$
=vct\qquad\text{(1-22)}
$$

[2 pt]

The magnitude of the acceleration vector found from Eq. 1-14 and Eq. 1-17,

$$
a=\sqrt{a_r^2+a_t^2}\qquad\text{(1-23)}
$$



$$
=\sqrt{\frac{c^4t^8}{16r^2}+c^2t^2}\qquad\text{(1-24)}
$$



$$
=ct\sqrt{\frac{c^2t^6}{16r^2}+1}\qquad\text{(1-25)}
$$

[2 pt]

Combining Eq. 1-18, Eq. 1-22, and Eq. 1-25, and then rearranging,

$$
(\vec v\cdot\vec a)^2=v^2a^2\cos^2\phi\qquad\text{(1-26)}
$$



$$
(vct)^2=v^2(ct)^2\left(\frac{c^2t^6}{16r^2}+1\right)\cos^2\phi\qquad\text{(1-27)}
$$



$$
\frac{c^2t^6}{16r^2}=\frac1{\cos^2\phi}-1\qquad\text{(1-28)}
$$



$$
\frac{9s^2}{4r^2}=\frac1{\cos^2\phi}-1\qquad\text{(1-29)}
$$



$$
\frac sr=\frac23\sqrt{\frac1{\cos^2\phi}-1}\qquad\text{(1-30)}
$$



$$
=\frac23\tan\phi\qquad\text{(1-31)}
$$

[4 pt]

Note that $s/r$ is the angle, in radians, between $P$ and $Q$.

It is stated that $\phi=30^\circ$, so

$$
\frac sr=\frac29\sqrt3\qquad\text{(1-32)}
$$

[4 pt]



</ProblemSection>
