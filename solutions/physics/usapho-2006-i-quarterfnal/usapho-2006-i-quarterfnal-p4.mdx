## Условие

Consider a spherical asteroid of radius $R_a$ and mass $M_a$ in a circular orbit of radius $r\gg R_a$ around a spherical planet of radius $R_p$ and mass $M_p$. The asteroid is "tide-locked;" that is, its rotation is such that the same point on its surface is always facing the planet. Let the gravitational constant be $G$. If the asteroid is too close to the planet, it may be torn apart by tidal forces. Ignore any oblation due to tidal effects.

**a)** Consider a particle of mass $m$ sitting on the surface of the asteroid closest to the planet. Find the normal (contact) force from the surface of the asteroid acting on the particle.

**b)** Approximate your answer to part (a) to first order in $\dfrac{R_a}r$.

**c)** Assume that the asteroid and the planet have the same mass density. What is the smallest value of $r$ such that the particle remains on the surface, in terms of $R_p$ and/or $R_a$ only? This value is known as the "rigid Roche limit."

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

<ProblemSection id="solution-a">

### (a) (13 points)

The magnitude of the net force on an object of mass $m$ in uniform circular motion of radius $r$ is

$$
F_{\mathrm{net}}=m\frac{v^2}r\qquad\text{(4-1)}
$$

[1 pt]

but

$$
v=2\pi r/T\qquad\text{(4-2)}
$$

[1 pt]

where $T$ is the period of revolution of the object. Combining the previous two expressions yields

$$
F_{\mathrm{net}}=m\frac{4\pi^2r}{T^2}\qquad\text{(4-3)}
$$

[1 pt]

Both the particle and the asteroid have the same period of revolution. In the case of the asteroid the net force is equal to the gravitational attraction between the planet and the asteroid, so

$$
F_{\mathrm{net}}=M_a\frac{4\pi^2r}{T^2}=\frac{GM_aM_p}{r^2}\qquad\text{(4-4)}
$$

or

$$
T=\sqrt{4\pi^2\frac{r^3}{GM_p}}\qquad\text{(4-5)}
$$

[2 pt]

The distance from the particle of mass $m$ to the center of rotation is

$$
r_m=r-R_a\qquad\text{(4-6)}
$$

The magnitude of the net force on the particle of mass $m$ is

$$
F_{\mathrm{net}}=m\frac{4\pi^2r_m}{T^2}\qquad\text{(4-7)}
$$

[1 pt]

which can be combined with Eq. 4-5 for the period of the asteroid to yield

$$
F_{\mathrm{net}}=GmM_p\frac{r_m}{r^3}\qquad\text{(4-8)}
$$

[2 pt]

The magnitude of the force of gravity from the planet on the particle of mass $m$ is

$$
F_p=\frac{GmM_p}{r_m^2}\qquad\text{(4-9)}
$$

[1 pt]

The magnitude of the force of gravity from the asteroid on the particle of mass $m$ is

$$
F_a=\frac{GmM_a}{R_a^2}\qquad\text{(4-10)}
$$

[1 pt]

The magnitude of the net force on the particle can also be written as

$$
F_{\mathrm{net}}=F_p-F_N-F_a\qquad\text{(4-11)}
$$

[1 pt]

where $F_N$ is the normal force, so

$$
F_N=F_{\mathrm{net}}+F_a-F_p\qquad\text{(4-12)}
$$



$$
=Gm\left(M_p\frac{r_m}{r^3}+M_a\frac1{R_a^2}-M_p\frac1{r_m^2}\right)\qquad\text{(4-13)}
$$



$$
=Gm\left(M_p\frac{r-R_a}{r^3}+M_a\frac1{R_a^2}-M_p\frac1{(r-R_a)^2}\right)\qquad\text{(4-14)}
$$

[2 pt]



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-b">

### (b) (7 points)

Rewrite the terms, where possible, so that

$$
r-R_a=r(1-x)\qquad\text{(4-15)}
$$

where

$$
x=R_a/r\qquad\text{(4-16)}
$$

is a small quantity. Then Eq. 4-14 can be written as

$$
F_N=Gm\left(M_p\frac{1-x}{r^2}+M_a\frac1{R_a^2}-M_p\frac1{r^2(1-x)^2}\right)\qquad\text{(4-17)}
$$

[2 pt]

Apply a binomial expansion to the last term,

$$
(1-x)^{-2}\simeq1+2x\qquad\text{(4-18)}
$$

[2 pt]

then Eq. 4-14 will be approximated by

$$
F_N\approx Gm\left(M_p\frac{1-x}{r^2}+M_a\frac1{R_a^2}-M_p\frac1{r^2}(1+2x)\right)\qquad\text{(4-19)}
$$



$$
=Gm\left(M_a\frac1{R_a^2}-3M_p\frac1{r^2}x\right)\qquad\text{(4-20)}
$$



$$
=Gm\left(M_a\frac1{R_a^2}-3M_p\frac{R_a}{r^3}\right)\qquad\text{(4-21)}
$$

[3 pt]



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-c">

### (c) (5 points)

Set the normal force equal to zero,

$$
F_N=0\qquad\text{(4-22)}
$$

[1 pt]

and solve Eq. 4-21 for $r$,

$$
r=\sqrt[3]{3\frac{M_p}{M_a}}R_a\qquad\text{(4-23)}
$$

[1 pt]

The mass of the planet is equal to the density $\rho$ times the volume, so

$$
M_p=\frac43\pi\rho R_p^3\qquad\text{(4-24)}
$$

[1 pt]

A similar expression holds true for the asteroid, so the mass ratio is

$$
\frac{M_p}{M_a}=\frac{R_p^3}{R_a^3}\qquad\text{(4-25)}
$$

[1 pt]

Combining Eq. 4-23 and Eq. 4-25,

$$
r=\sqrt[3]3\,R_p\qquad\text{(4-26)}
$$

[1 pt]



</ProblemSection>
