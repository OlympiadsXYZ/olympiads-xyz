## Условие

A total charge $+Q$ is uniformly distributed throughout a sphere of radius $R$. Charge is removed from a spherical hole of radius $R/2$ centered on the $z = R/2$ point of the z-axis and concentrated into a point charge $q$ which is placed at the center of the hole. See Fig. B1-1 above.

**a.** Find the charge $q$ in terms of $Q$. **[5 т.]**

**b.** Find the electric field at all points along the $x$-axis. **[10 т.]**

**c.** The charge $q$ is now removed, leaving the charge distribution seen in Fig. B2-2 above. Find the electric field at all points along the $z$-axis. **[10 т.]**

**d.** Find the electrostatic potential at an arbitrary point $x$, $y$, $z$ outside the sphere of radius $R$ as shown in Fig. B2-2 above. Assume the potential vanishes at an infinite distance from the charges. **[10 т.]**

**e.** Very far from the charge distribution, the electrostatic potential may be written in the following form

$$
V = k\left(\frac{a}{r} + \frac{\vec{b}\cdot\vec{r}}{r^3} + \ldots\right)
$$

where $r$ is the distance from the origin at the center of the large sphere. Using your result from part d) above, determine the constant $a$ and the constant vector $\vec{b}$. ($\vec{b}$ is called the electric dipole moment.) **[10 т.]**

**f.** Interpret $a$ and $\vec{b}$ in terms of a set of discrete charges and their locations. **[5 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a.** $q = \tfrac{1}{8}Q$
- **b.** For |x| > R: E = kQ/R² (as printed); for |x| < R: E = kQ|x|/R³; in a direction out from the origin.
- **c.** |z| > R: E = kQ/z² − kQ/(8(z − R/2)²); −R < z < 0: E = kQ|z|/R³ − kQ/(8(z − R/2)²); 0 < z < R: E = kQ/(2R²); out from the origin.
- **d.** $V = k\frac{Q}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}} - k\frac{Q}{8\sqrt{x^2+y^2+(z-R/2)^2}}$
- **e.** $a = \tfrac{7}{8}Q,\quad \vec{b} = -\frac{QR}{16}\hat{k}$
- **f.** A point charge of 7Q/8 at the origin and a dipole consisting of −Q/8 at (0, 0, R/2) and +Q/8 at the origin.

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

(5) a. If the charge $Q$ is uniformly distributed, the charge density is

$$
\rho = \frac{Q}{\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3}.
$$

The charge in a sphere of radius $R/2$ is

$$
q = \rho\tfrac{4}{3}\pi\left(\frac{R}{2}\right)^3 = \tfrac{1}{8}\rho\tfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3 = \tfrac{1}{8}\frac{Q}{\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3}\tfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3 = \tfrac{1}{8}Q.
$$

(10) b. The $x$-axis is totally outside the cavity. By Gauss’s Law the field outside a spherical symmetric charge distribution of radius $R/2$ centered on $z = R/2$ is the same as that due to point charge at the center with the same total charge. So on the $x$-axis the field is the same as that due to uniform charge distribution of total charge $Q$ and radius $R$.

From Gauss’s Law for a spherically symmetric charge distribution

$$
E = k\frac{Q_{enc}}{R^2}
$$

where $k$ is Coulomb’s constant and $Q_{enc}$ is the total charge enclosed by a Gaussian sphere of radius $R$.

Outside the sphere $\quad Q_{enc} = Q$

For $|x| > R$ $\quad E = k\dfrac{Q}{R^2}$ $\quad$ in a direction out from the origin.

Inside the sphere $\quad Q_{enc} = \rho\tfrac{4}{3}\pi|x|^3 = \dfrac{Q}{\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3}\tfrac{4}{3}\pi|x|^3 = \dfrac{Q|x|^3}{R^3}$

For $|x| < R$ $\quad E = k\dfrac{1}{x^2}\dfrac{Q|x|^3}{R^3} = k\dfrac{Q|x|}{R^3}$ $\quad$ in a direction out from the origin.

(10) c. The total field can be considered the sum of two terms. That due to:

a sphere of radius $R$ centered on the origin with uniformly distributed total charge $+Q$.

a sphere of radius $R/2$ centered on $z = R/2$ with uniformly distributed total charge $-Q/8$.

Outside the large sphere both fields are equal to those due to point charges at their centers.

For $|z| > R$ $\quad E = k\dfrac{Q}{z^2} - k\dfrac{Q}{8(z - R/2)^2}$ $\quad$ out from the origin.

Inside the large sphere but outside the cavity, treat the cavity as a point charge at its center and the sphere as an extended distribution

For $-R < z < 0$ $\quad E = k\dfrac{Q|z|}{R^3} - k\dfrac{Q}{8(z - R/2)^2}$ $\quad$ out from the origin.

Inside the cavity, treat both the sphere and cavities as extended charge distributions.

$0 < z < R/2$ $\quad E = k\dfrac{Qz}{R^3} + k\dfrac{Q(R/2 - z)}{8(R/2)^3} = k\dfrac{Qz}{R^3} + k\dfrac{Q(R/2 - z)}{R^3} = k\dfrac{Q}{2R^2}$ $\quad$ out from the origin.

$R/2 < z < R$ $\quad E = k\dfrac{Qz}{R^3} - k\dfrac{Q(z - R/2)}{8(R/2)^3} = k\dfrac{Qz}{R^3} + k\dfrac{Q(R/2 - z)}{R^3} = k\dfrac{Q}{2R^2}$ $\quad$ out from the origin.

(10) d. Outside the sphere, the electrostatic potential is the same as that due to a point charge of $+Q$ at the origin and a point charge of $-Q/8$ at $x = 0$, $y = 0$, $z = R/2$.

Writing the distance from the origin as $\quad r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}$

and the distance from at $x = 0$, $y = 0$, $z = R/2$. as $\quad r' = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + (z - R/2)^2}$.

The potential is

$$
V = k\frac{Q}{r} - k\frac{q}{r'} = k\frac{Q}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}} - k\frac{Q}{8\sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + (z - R/2)^2}}
$$

(10) e. In order to get the electrostatic potential in the form shown, expand $r'$ in a binomial series.

$$
r' = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + (z - R/2)^2} = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - zR + R^2/4} = \sqrt{r^2 - zR + R^2/4} = r\sqrt{1 - \frac{zR}{r^2} + \frac{R^2}{4r^2}}
$$

$$
V = k\frac{Q}{r} - k\frac{q}{r'} = k\frac{Q}{r} - k\frac{Q}{8r\sqrt{1 - \frac{zR}{r^2} + \frac{R^2}{4r^2}}} \approx k\frac{Q}{r} - k\frac{Q}{8r}\left(1 + \frac{1}{2}\frac{zR}{r^2} - \frac{1}{2}\frac{R^2}{4r^2} + \ldots\right) \approx k\left(\frac{7Q}{8r} - \frac{QzR}{16r^3} + \ldots\right)
$$

Only terms of order $\dfrac{1}{r}$ and of order $\dfrac{1}{\mathrm{r}^2}$ $\left(\text{such as } \dfrac{z}{r^3}\right)$ have been retained.

Comparing to the expression for $V$ given in 2Be $\quad a = \tfrac{7}{8}Q$

And with $z = \vec{r}\cdot\hat{k}$ $\quad \vec{b}\cdot\vec{r} = -\dfrac{QzR}{16} = -\dfrac{Q\left(\vec{r}\cdot\hat{k}\right)R}{16}.$

Which gives $\quad \vec{b} = -\dfrac{QR}{16}\hat{k}$

(5) f. A point charge of $\frac{7}{8}Q$ at the origin and a dipole consisting of $-\frac{1}{8}Q$ at (0, 0, $R/2$) and $+\frac{1}{8}Q$ at the origin.


</Spoiler>
