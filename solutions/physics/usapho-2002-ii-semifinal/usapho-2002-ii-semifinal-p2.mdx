## Условие

A particle decays into two photons which are both observed to have frequency $f$, one moving at an angle $\theta$ above the positive $x$-axis and the other at an angle of $\theta$ below the positive $x$-axis. See the accompanying diagram. The energy and momentum magnitude of a relativistic particle of mass $m$ and speed $v$ are:

$$
E = \frac{mc^2}{\sqrt{1 - (v/c)^2}} \qquad\qquad p = \frac{mv}{\sqrt{1 - (v/c)^2}}
$$

<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-2002-ii-semifinal/p2-fig1.png" alt="Decay diagram: a wavy line comes in from the left along the horizontal x-axis and splits at a point into two wavy photon lines with arrowheads, one going up to the right at angle θ above the axis and one going down to the right at angle θ below it; both photons are labelled f and both angles are labelled θ." />
</figure>

**a.** Find the velocity of the original particle. **[15 т.]**

**b.** Find the rest mass of the original particle. **[5 т.]**

**c.** What are the frequencies of the decay photons in the rest frame of the particle? What are their directions of travel in this rest frame? **[5 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a.** $v = c\cos\theta$
- **b.** $m = \frac{2hf}{c^2}\sin\theta$
- **c.** Both photons have frequency f' = f sin θ; one travels along the +y-axis and the other along the −y-axis.

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

a. The energy and magnitude of the momentum of a photon of frequency $f$ are:

$$
E = hf \qquad\qquad p = \frac{h}{\lambda} = \frac{hf}{c}.
$$

Energy is conserved. Equating the energy of the particle before the decay to the total photon energy after the decay

$$
\frac{mc^2}{\sqrt{1-(v/c)^2}} = hf + hf. \qquad \text{(A2-1)}
$$

Momentum is conserved. Equating the momentum of the particle before the decay to the sum of the x-components of the photon momentum after the decay

$$
\frac{mv}{\sqrt{1-(v/c)^2}} = \frac{hf}{c}\cos\theta + \frac{hf}{c}\cos\theta. \qquad \text{(A2-2)}
$$

Dividing (A2-2) by (A2-1)

$$
\frac{mv}{\sqrt{1-(v/c)^2}}\,\frac{\sqrt{1-(v/c)^2}}{mc^2} = \frac{(2hf/c)\cos\theta}{2hf}
$$

Or

$$
\frac{v}{c^2} = \frac{\cos\theta}{c}
$$

$$
v = c\cos\theta \quad \text{in the positive } x\text{-direction} \qquad \text{(A2-3)}
$$

b. Solving (A2-1) for $mc^2$

$$
mc^2 = 2hf\sqrt{1-(v/c)^2}
$$

and substituting in (A2-3)

$$
mc^2 = 2hf\sqrt{1-(c\cos\theta/c)^2} = 2hf\sqrt{1-(\cos\theta)^2} = 2hf\sin\theta
$$

$$
m = \frac{2hf}{c^2}\sin\theta \qquad \text{(A2-4)}
$$

c. In this frame, the particle has zero momentum, so the momentum of the final photons must be equal and opposite – one along the +$y$-axis, the other along the -$y$-axis. If each photon’s momentum has the same magnitude, the photons must have the same frequency $f'$.

In this frame the particles energy is $mc^2$. Applying energy conservation

$$
mc^2 = 2hf'.
$$

Solving for $f'$ and substituting in (A2-4)

$$
f' = \frac{mc^2}{2h} = \left(\frac{2hf}{c^2}\sin\theta\right)\frac{c^2}{2h} = f\sin\theta
$$


</Spoiler>
