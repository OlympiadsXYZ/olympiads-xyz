## Условие

A hot air balloon with average density 1.20 kg/m$^3$ is in equilibrium at a height of 1.00 km above sea level.  A gust of wind pushes the balloon to a height of 1.10 km above sea level and releases it at rest.

**a.** How long does it take for the balloon to return to its equilibrium position? **[20 т.]**

**b.** How fast is it going when it passes through equilibrium? **[5 т.]**

Assume that:  The atmospheric density is a linear function of height.

The balloon volume and average density are constant.

Variations in the gravitational field with height are negligible.

The density of air at sea level is 1.29 kg/m$^3$.

Drag is negligible.

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a.** 57.9 s
- **b.** 2.71 m/s

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

A2. First find the forces on the balloon. The weight force is

$$
W = mg = \rho_b Vg
$$

where $\rho_b = 1.20\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$ is the density of the balloon, $V$ is its volume, and $g$ is the gravitational field strength. The buoyant force is

$$
B = \rho_a Vg
$$

where $\rho_a$ is the density of air. Since it is assumed to be a linear function of height, $\rho_a = \rho_0 - \alpha h$ where $\rho_0 = 1.29\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$ is the density of air at sea level and $h$ is the height above sea level. The forces are in equilibrium at $h_0 = 1.00\ \mathrm{km} = 1.00 \times 10^3\ \mathrm{m}$.

$$
\rho_b Vg = \rho_a Vg = (\rho_0 - \alpha h_0)Vg. \qquad (\mathrm{A2-1})
$$

Solving this for $\alpha$, $$\alpha = \frac{\rho_0 - \rho_b}{h_0} = \frac{1.29\ \mathrm{kg/m^3} - 1.20\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}}{1000\ \mathrm{m}} = 9 \times 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{kg/m^4}$$

a. After being blown to a height of $h = 1.10$ km, the forces are no longer balanced.

$$
ma = B - W
$$

$$
\rho_b Va = (\rho_0 - \alpha h)Vg - \rho_b Vg.
$$

Substituting (A2 – 1) into the above equation

$$
\rho_b Va = (\rho_0 - \alpha h)Vg - (\rho_0 - \alpha h_0)Vg = -\alpha(h - h_0)Vg = -\alpha\Delta h Vg.
$$

Solving for the acceleration $a$ $$a = -\left(\frac{\alpha g}{\rho_b}\right)\Delta h.$$

The acceleration is proportional to the displacement. The motion is simple harmonic motion with

$$
\omega = \sqrt{\left(\frac{\alpha g}{\rho_b}\right)} = \sqrt{\frac{(9 \times 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{kg/m^4})(9.8\ \mathrm{m/s^2})}{1.20\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}}} = 0.0271\ \mathrm{rad/s}.
$$

The balloon is released at rest at amplitude $A = h - h_0 = 100$ m and first passes through its equilibrium position at a time equal to one fourth its period.

$$
t = \frac{T}{4} = \frac{2\pi}{4\omega} = \frac{\pi}{2(0.0271\ \mathrm{rad/s})} = 57.9\ \mathrm{s}.
$$

b. The balloon passes through its equilibrium position with maximum velocity

$$
v = \omega A = (0.0271\ \mathrm{rad/s})(100\ \mathrm{m}) = 2.71\ \mathrm{m/s}.
$$
