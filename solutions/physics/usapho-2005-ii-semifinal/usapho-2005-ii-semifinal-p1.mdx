Each of the seven resistors in the network above has the same resistance $R$.

**a.** If an ideal battery of emf $\mathcal E$ and no internal resistance is connected across the points labeled A and B, what is the current $I_j$ that flows in the $j^{th}$ resistor $R_j$ (for each of the seven resistors)? **[10 т.]**

**b.** What equivalent resistor could be used to replace the network so that the same current flowed from the battery? **[5 т.]**

**c.** If the resistor labeled $R_4$ was removed and replaced by a capacitor with capacitance $C$, what would the charge be on the capacitor after the circuit had been connected to the battery for a very long time? **[10 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-a">

### a. (10 т.)

Since $R_3$ and $R_6$ are in series, $I_3=I_6$. Since $R_2$ and $R_5$ are in series, $I_2=I_5$. By symmetry, the current through $R_3$ and $R_6$ must be the same as the current through $R_2$ and $R_5$ since the current through A = the current through B and this current splits into two branches — one with a resistance of $2R$ and the other with a resistance of $R$. Therefore,

$$
I_2=I_3=I_5=I_6\qquad (A1-1)
$$

and $I_1=I_7$. (A1-2)

By the junction rule,

$$
I_1=I_2+I_4.\qquad (A1-3)
$$

Applying the loop rule from A to B across the top of the circuit,

$$
\epsilon=I_1R+2I_2R.\qquad (A1-4)
$$

Applying the loop rule from A to B going through $R_1$, $R_4$, and $R_7$ yields

$$
\epsilon=I_1R+I_4R+I_1R.\qquad (A1-5)
$$

Substitute (A1-3) into (A1-4) and (A1-5).

$$
\epsilon=3I_2R+I_4R\qquad (A1-6)
$$

and $\epsilon=2I_2R+3I_4R$. (A1-7)

Subtract (A1-7) from (A1-6) to obtain

$$
I_2=2I_4.\qquad (A1-8)
$$

Substitute into (A1-6) or (A1-7) to find that

$$
I_4=\frac\epsilon{7R}.\qquad (A1-9)
$$

Then, from (A1-8) and (A1-1),

$$
I_2=I_3=I_5=I_6=\frac{2\epsilon}{7R}.\qquad (A1-10)
$$

Using (A1-3) and (A1-2),

$$
I_1=I_7=\frac{3\epsilon}{7R}.\qquad (A1-11)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-b">

### b. (5 т.)

$$
I_{\rm battery}=\frac\epsilon{R_{\rm equiv}}\qquad (A1-12)
$$

and $I_{\rm battery}=I_1+I_2$. (A1-13)

Therefore, by substituting (A1-10) and (A1-11) into (A1-12) and (A1-13),

$$
\frac\epsilon{R_{\rm equiv}}=\frac{5\epsilon}{7R}.\qquad (A1-14)
$$

Thus, $R_{\rm equiv}=7R/5$. (A1-15)

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-c">

### c. (10 т.)

After a very long time, no current will flow through the branch of the circuit with the capacitor. Therefore, $R_1$, $R_2$, and $R_5$ are in series and $R_3$, $R_6$, and $R_7$ are in series. The current through the top branch and the current through the bottom branch will then be

$$
I=\frac\epsilon{3R}.\qquad (A1-16)
$$

The voltage drop across $R_1$ is $IR$ and the voltage drop across $R_3$ and $R_6$ is $2IR$. Thus, the voltage drop across the capacitor is $2IR-IR=IR$.

Using (A1-16),

$$
V_{\rm cap}=\frac\epsilon3.\qquad (A1-17)
$$

The charge on the capacitor is $Q=CV_{\rm cap}=C\epsilon/3$. (A1-18)

</ProblemSection>
