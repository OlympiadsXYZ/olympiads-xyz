A length of wire is formed into a ring of radius $b$ and total charge $Q$ that is distributed uniformly around the ring. The ring is in the $x$-$y$ plane and centered on the origin as shown in the accompanying diagram. $\hat i$, $\hat j$, and $\hat k$ are unit vectors in the $x$, $y$, and $z$ directions respectively. Assume the potential vanishes at infinity.

<ProblemSection id="parts-a-b">

### a.–b.

**a.** Find the field at any point on the $z$ axis. **[7 т.]**

**b.** Find the potential at any point on the $z$ axis. **[5 т.]**

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="parts-c-e">

### c.–e.

A point charge with mass $m$ and charge $-q$ is placed at $z=z_0$ where $z_0\ll b$ and released from rest at $t=0$. Ignore any effects due to radiation.

**c.** Find the potential energy of the system as a function of $z$, the position of the charge. Expand this potential energy function about the origin for $\lvert z\rvert\le z_0$. Keep only terms to lowest non-zero order in $z$. **[10 т.]**

**d.** Find the speed of the charge as it passes through the origin. **[5 т.]**

**e.** Find the velocity of the charge as a function of time. **[8 т.]**

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="parts-f-g">

### f.–g.

The point charge is removed and the ring is set into rotation about the $z$-axis with angular velocity $\vec\omega=\omega\hat k$. Ignore any effects due to radiation.

**f.** Find the magnetic field due to the rotating ring at any point along the $z$-axis. **[10 т.]**

**g.** A second point charge with mass $m$ and charge $-q$ is placed at $z=z_0$ where $z_0\ll b$ and released from rest. What is the magnetic force on the charge as it passes through the origin? **[5 т.]**

</ProblemSection>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-a">

### a. (7 т.)

By symmetry, the $x$ and $y$ components of the electric field from different parts of the ring will cancel:

$$
E_x=E_y=0.
$$

Therefore, we need to add up the $z$-components of the electric field from the various parts of the ring:

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '38.6%', '--fig-max': '248px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-2005-ii-semifinal/p5-fig2-v2.png" alt="Official-key charged-ring geometry with unit vectors." width="774" height="601" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


The electric field due to an infinitesimal charge $dq$ on the ring at a distance $z$ from the origin is

$$
d\vec E=\frac{dq}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^2}\hat r\qquad (B1-1)
$$

where $r=(b^2+z^2)^{1/2}$. (B1-2)

($r$ is the distance from a point on the ring to the point $(0,0,z)$.) $\hat r$ is a unit vector at the point $(0,0,z)$ pointing away from the charge $dq$ on the ring.

The $z$-component is then given by:

$$
dE_z=dE\cos\theta=\frac{z\,dE}{r}\qquad (B1-3)
$$

where $\theta$ is the angle between $\hat r$ and the $z$ axis.

Substituting eq. (B1-1) into (B1-2) yields:

$$
dE_z=\frac{z\,dq}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^3}.\qquad (B1-4)
$$

Integrating (B1-4) around the ring and observing that $r$ is the same for all points on the ring yields

$$
E_z=\int\frac{z\,dq}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^3}=\frac{zQ}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^3}.\qquad (B1-5)
$$

Using the fact that $E_x=E_y=0$ and substituting (B1-2) into (B1-5) gives us the answer.

$$
\vec E=E_z\hat k=\frac{zQ\hat k}{4\pi\epsilon_0(b^2+z^2)^{3/2}}.\qquad (B1-6)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-b">

### b. (5 т.)

The potential a distance $r$ away from an infinitesimal charge $dq$ is given by

$$
dV=\frac{dq}{4\pi\epsilon_0r}.\qquad (B1-7)
$$

Integrating around the ring, using the fact that $r$ is the same from every point on the ring to the point $(0,0,z)$, and substituting (B1-2) for $r$ yields

$$
V=\int\frac{dq}{4\pi\epsilon_0r}=\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0r}=\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0(b^2+z^2)^{1/2}}.\qquad (B1-8)
$$

Note that it is easier to solve part (b) first and then to solve part (a) using

$$
E_z=-\frac{dV}{dz}=-\frac d{dz}\left(\frac Q{4\pi\epsilon_0(b^2+z^2)^{1/2}}\right)=-\frac{(-\frac12)(2zQ)}{4\pi\epsilon_0(b^2+z^2)^{3/2}}=\frac{zQ}{4\pi\epsilon_0(b^2+z^2)^{3/2}}.\qquad (B1-9)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-c">

### c. (10 т.)

The potential energy of the system is given by

$$
U=-qV.\qquad (B1-10)
$$

Using (B1-8),

$$
U=\frac{-qQ}{4\pi\epsilon_0(b^2+z^2)^{1/2}}.\qquad (B1-11)
$$

Factoring out $b^2$,

$$
U=\frac{-qQ}{4\pi\epsilon_0}(b^2+z^2)^{-1/2}=\frac{-qQ}{4\pi\epsilon_0b}\left(1+\frac{z^2}{b^2}\right)^{-1/2}.\qquad (B1-12)
$$

Using the fact that $\lvert z\rvert\ll b$ and using the approximation $(1+x)^n\approx1+nx$ when $x\ll1$, (B1-12) becomes

$$
U\approx\frac{-qQ}{4\pi\epsilon_0b}\left(1-\frac{z^2}{2b^2}\right).\qquad (B1-13)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-d">

### d. (5 т.)

Mechanical energy is conserved, so

$$
U(z_0)+K(z_0)=U(0)+K(0).\qquad (B1-14)
$$

Since the charge is at rest at $z=z_0$ and using (B1-13),

$$
\frac{-qQ}{4\pi\epsilon_0b}\left(1-\frac{z_0^2}{2b^2}\right)+0=\frac{-qQ}{4\pi\epsilon_0b}+\frac12m[v(0)]^2.\qquad (B1-15)
$$

Solving for $v(0)$,

$$
v(0)=\left(\frac{qQz_0^2}{4m\pi\epsilon_0b^3}\right)^{1/2}.\qquad (B1-16)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-e">

### e. (8 т.)

We shall use Newton’s Second Law:

$$
F_{\rm net}=ma=m\frac{d^2z}{dt^2}.\qquad (B1-17)
$$

The force on the charge $-q$ is $-qE$ where $E$ is given by (B1-6). Substituting into (B1-17),

$$
\frac{-qzQ}{4\pi\epsilon_0(b^2+z^2)^{3/2}}=m\frac{d^2z}{dt^2}.\qquad (B1-18)
$$

But, $z\ll b$, so $(b^2+z^2)^{3/2}\approx b^3$ and, after dividing both sides by $m$, (B1-18) becomes

$$
\frac{d^2z}{dt^2}=\frac{-qQz}{4\pi\epsilon_0mb^3}.\qquad (B1-19)
$$

We recognize that the differential equation

$$
\frac{d^2z}{dt^2}=-\omega^2z\qquad (B1-20)
$$

is simple harmonic motion. The solution to (B1-20) is

$$
z(t)=A\cos\omega t.\qquad (B1-21)
$$

Since $z(0)=z_0$, we find that $A=z_0$.

By comparison with (B1-19), we find that

$$
\omega=\left(\frac{qQ}{4\pi\epsilon_0mb^3}\right)^{1/2}.\qquad (B1-22)
$$

We can derive an expression for the velocity as a function of time by differentiating (B1-21):

$$
v(t)=\frac{dz(t)}{dt}=-\omega z_0\sin\omega t\qquad (B1-23)
$$

(where $\omega$ is given by (B1-22).)

Note that an alternative method of solving part (d) is to note that the speed at the origin will be the maximum value of the magnitude of the velocity

$$
v(0)=\omega z_0=\left(\frac{qQz_0^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0mb^3}\right)^{1/2}.
$$

Note that an alternative (easier) method of solving part (e) is to note that the potential energy found in (B1-13) is of the form $U(z)=U(0)+kz^2/2$.

Therefore, since it starts from rest, we know that $z(t)=A\cos\omega t$ and that

$$
v(t)=\frac{dz(t)}{dt}=-\omega z_0\sin\omega t=-v_{\max}\sin\omega t.
$$

Thus, $\omega=v_{\max}/z_0$ and $v_{\max}$ was found in (B1-16).

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-f">

### f. (10 т.)

According to the Biot-Savart law, the magnetic field due to a moving infinitesimal charge $dq$ is given by

$$
d\vec B=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{I\,d\vec s\times\hat r}{r^2}=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{dq\,\vec v\times\hat r}{r^2}.\qquad (B1-24)
$$

Since $d\vec B$ depends on the cross product $\vec v\times\hat r$, $d\vec B$ must be perpendicular to both $\vec v$ and $\hat r$.

By symmetry, when we add the magnetic field vectors due to all points on the rotating ring, the $x$ and $y$ components will cancel. Therefore, we need to find the $z$ components and add them. Let $\phi$ be the angle between $\vec B$ and $\hat z$.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '38.3%', '--fig-max': '245px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-2005-ii-semifinal/p5-fig3-v2.png" alt="Ring elementdQ with radiusb, positionr andz, electric and magnetic contributionsdE,dB and anglephi/theta." width="766" height="612" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


$$
dB_z=dB\cos\phi=\frac{b\,dB}{r}.\qquad (B1-25)
$$

Substituting $v=b\omega$ and (B1-24) into (B1-25):

$$
dB_z=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{b^2\omega\,dq}{r^3}.\qquad (B1-26)
$$

Integrating (B1-26) around the ring and observing that $r$ is the same for all points on the ring yields

$$
B_z=\int\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{b^2\omega\,dq}{r^3}=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{b^2\omega Q}{r^3}.\qquad (B1-27)
$$

Using the fact that $B_x=B_y=0$ and substituting (B1-2) into (B1-27) gives us the answer.

$$
\vec B=B_z\hat k=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{b^2\omega Q\hat k}{(b^2+z^2)^{3/2}}.\qquad (B1-28)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-g">

### g. (5 т.)

A point charge $-q$ moving with velocity $\vec v$ in a magnetic field $\vec B$ experiences a magnetic force

$$
\vec F_{\rm magn}=-q\vec v\times\vec B.\qquad (B1-29)
$$

However, since the charge is moving in the $-\hat k$ direction and the magnetic field is in the $\hat k$ direction, $\vec v\times\vec B=0$, so $\vec F_{\rm magn}=0$.

</ProblemSection>
