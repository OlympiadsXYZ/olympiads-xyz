A plane wave traveling in the positive $z$-direction can be described by the following wavefunction: $\psi(z,t)=A\sin(kz-\omega t+\phi)$, where $A$ (the amplitude) and $\phi$ are constants, $k=2\pi/\lambda$, $\omega=2\pi f$, $f$ is the frequency, and $\lambda$ is the wavelength. The factor $(kz-\omega t+\phi)$ is called the phase of the wave. The following relation may be useful:

$$
\sin\alpha+\sin\beta=2\cos\left[\tfrac12(\alpha-\beta)\right]\sin\left[\tfrac12(\alpha+\beta)\right].
$$

The reflecting surface is replaced by a material that does not reflect but transmits without loss of amplitude. The material has index of refraction $n$. One source is placed a distance $d/2$ above the air-material interface. The other source is embedded in the material a distance $d/2$ below the interface. The two sources are in phase, coherent, emit electromagnetic radiation of the same monochromatic wavelength $\lambda$ in vacuum, and are both located a distance $L$ from a screen, $L\gg d$. Assume that the wave amplitude $A$ due to each source individually is the same at point X and that the wave from the lower source travels entirely in the material.

**b.** What is the resultant wavefunction at point X as a function of time? **[8 т.]**

**c.** Let $I_0$ denote the maximum intensity reaching point X, which occurs when $n=1$. Derive an expression for the time averaged intensity of the electromagnetic radiation at point X as a function of $n$, $I_0$, $L$, and $\lambda$. **[7 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

Source note: (A3-2) repeats the label r1. Equation (A3-5) prints minus pi, followed by the statement n=1 and the final first-maximum expression; these printed forms are mutually inconsistent and are retained. The phase sign also changes between (A3-9) and (A3-11).

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-a">

### a. (10 т.)

We can assume that there is an image of the source S in the perfectly reflecting surface located a distance $d/2$ beneath the surface. The problem then reduces to one of two point sources a distance $d$ apart creating an interference pattern on a screen a located a distance $L\gg d$ away from the sources. We are informed that the midpoint on the screen, $y=0$, is an interference minimum. Since this would be an interference maximum for two point sources which are in phase, then we can conclude that the phase of the reflected wave is shifted by an amount $\pi$, so that the location of interference maxima and interference minima are switched relative to the “normal” presentation. Consequently, the location of the first maximum is $y=\lambda L/2d$.

In the event that you forgot the formula, you will need to derive it.

The distance from the object point source to a point on the screen is

$$
r_1=\sqrt{(y-d/2)^2+L^2}\approx L\left(1+\frac{(y-d/2)^2}{2L^2}\right).\qquad (A3-1)
$$

The distance from the image point source to a point on the screen is

$$
r_1=\sqrt{(y+d/2)^2+L^2}\approx L\left(1+\frac{(y+d/2)^2}{2L^2}\right).\qquad (A3-2)
$$

The path length difference is then

$$
r_2-r_1\approx\frac{yd}{L},\qquad (A3-3)
$$

where the approximation is true for $L\gg y+d/2$.

The relative phase shift due to the path length difference is found by dividing through by the wavelength and multiplying by $2\pi$, so

$$
\Delta\phi_{\rm pld}=2\pi\frac{yd}{\lambda L}.\qquad (A3-4)
$$

There is an additional phase shift of $\pi$ from the reflection, so that the overall phase difference between the waves is

$$
\Delta\phi=2\pi\frac{yd}{\lambda L}-\pi.\qquad (A3-5)
$$

Constructive interference occurs when $\Delta\phi=2n\pi$, where $n$ is an integer; the smallest value for $y$ with constructive interference happens when $n=1$, or

$$
y=\frac{\lambda L}{2d}.\qquad (A3-6)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-b">

### b. (8 т.)

For the wave from the source above the boundary:

$$
\psi_1=A\sin(kz-\omega t+\phi).\qquad (A3-7)
$$

The wave below the boundary travels in a medium that (1) doesn’t affect $\omega$ or $\phi$, but (2) scales $k$ according to $k\to nk$, since the wave velocity is changed but the frequency is unaffected. Consequently the wave equation from the second source is

$$
\psi_2=A\sin(nkz-\omega t+\phi).\qquad (A3-8)
$$

The distance $z$ is the same for both, and is approximately equal to $L$ for $L\gg d$.

Adding the two waves in (A3-7) and in (A3-8) and making use of the given trigonometric identity yields

$$
\psi_1+\psi_2=2A\cos\left[\frac{(n-1)kL}{2}\right]\sin\left[\frac{(n+1)kL}{2}-\omega t+\phi\right].\qquad (A3-9)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-c">

### c. (7 т.)

The intensity is proportional to the wave-function squared, or

$$
I\propto\psi^2.\qquad (A3-10)
$$

The time average is found by integrating the above expression over one period and then dividing by the period, or

$$
\langle I\rangle\propto4A^2\cos^2\left[\frac{(n-1)kL}{2}\right]\frac\omega{2\pi}\int_{\rm period}\sin^2\left[\frac{(n+1)kL}{2}-\omega t-\phi\right]dt.\qquad (A3-11)
$$

Everything inside the sin function can be written compactly as

$$
\sin^2\left[\frac{(n+1)kL}{2}-\omega t-\phi\right]=\sin^2[\alpha-\omega t].\qquad (A3-12)
$$

A change of variables to try would be

$$
u=\alpha-\omega t.\qquad (A3-13)
$$

Then the expression for the time-averaged intensity becomes

$$
\langle I\rangle\propto\cos^2\left[\frac{(n-1)kL}{2}\right]\int_{\rm period}\sin^2u\,du,\qquad (A3-14)
$$

where we have conveniently swallowed all constants into the proportionality. The integral is fairly easy, but it doesn’t matter, since it is also a constant independent of $k$ or $L$. So it can be swallowed as well, leaving

$$
\langle I\rangle\propto\cos^2\left[\frac{(n-1)kL}{2}\right].\qquad (A3-15)
$$

This is true for all values of the argument of the cosine function, including the value that maximizes the cosine function, so

$$
\langle I\rangle=I_0\cos^2\left[\frac{(n-1)kL}{2}\right]\qquad (A3-16)
$$

gives us the required relation for $\langle I\rangle_{\max}$.

</ProblemSection>
