## Условие

A sample of water of mass 0.0360 kg and temperature $100^\circ$C is added to an enclosed container of volume 2.00 m$^3$ that contains air at a pressure of $1.00\times10^5$ Pa and a temperature of 500 K. Assume both air and steam can be treated as ideal gases and that all of the water vaporizes. What is the final temperature and pressure inside the container?

Possibly useful information:

| Molar Heat Capacities | Air | Steam |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $C_V$ (at constant volume) | 20.8 J/(mol·K) | 27.9 J/(mol·K) |
| $C_P$ (at constant pressure) | 29.1 J/(mol·K) | 36.2 J/(mol·K) |

$L_V$ (Latent heat of vaporization) $=2.26\times10^6$ J/kg.

Molar mass of water $=18.0$ g/mol.

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

Source note: the numerical substitution below interchanges the initial air and steam temperatures. The printed equation and the printed final answer are both retained.

Vaporizing the water will require energy:

$$
\Delta U_{\rm vap}=mL_v=(0.0360\text{ kg})(2.26\times10^6\text{ J/kg})=81.4\text{ kJ}.\qquad (A2-1)
$$

We find that we have $\frac{0.0360\text{ kg}}{0.018\text{ kg/mol}}=2.0\text{ mol}$ of water.

Using $pV=nRT$, we find that we have 48.1 mol of air.

Since the volume is constant, the change in internal energy of the air and of the steam are given by

$$
\Delta U=nC_V\Delta T.\qquad (A2-2)
$$

The total change in internal energy of the system must be zero:

$$
\Delta U_{\rm vap}+\Delta U_{\rm steam}+\Delta U_{\rm air}=0.\qquad (A2-3)
$$

Substituting (A2-1) and (A2-2) into (A2-3):

$$
81.4\text{ kJ}+(nC_V\Delta T)_{\rm steam}+(nC_V\Delta T)_{\rm air}=0.\qquad (A2-4)
$$

$$
81.4\text{ kJ}+(2.0\text{ mol})(27.9\text{ J/(mol}\cdot\text{K)})(T_f-500\text{ K})+(48.1\text{ mol})(20.8\text{ J/(mol}\cdot\text{K)})(T_f-373\text{ K})=0.
$$

$$
T_{\rm final}=416\text{ K}.\qquad (A2-5)
$$

There are a total of 2.0 mol + 48.1 mol = 50.1 mol of gas in the container, so from the ideal gas law,

$$
p=\frac{nRT}{V}=\frac{(50.1\text{ mol})(8.31\text{ J/mol}\cdot\text{K})(416\text{ K})}{2.00\text{ m}^3}=86.7\text{ kPa}.\qquad (A2-6)
$$


</Spoiler>
