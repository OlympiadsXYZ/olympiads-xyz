## Условие

Just like electrostatic fields, gravitational fields obey Gauss’s Law. One of the arguments for dark matter in the universe is the observation that the velocity of stars orbiting far from the center of the galaxy is approximately constant. This requires a spherically symmetric mass distribution which the visible stars do not have. Let the mass density of the galaxy be $\rho(r)=Ar^n$ where $A$ is a constant, $r$ is the distance from the center of galaxy, and the power $n$ is as yet undetermined.

**a.** Show that the total mass $M$ enclosed by a sphere of radius $R$ is $M(R)=CR^{n+3}$ and find the constant $C$ in terms of given variables and known constants. **[5 т.]**

**b.** You may use $M(R)=CR^{n+3}$, with $C$ unspecified, for the remaining parts of this problem.

Find the magnitude of the gravitational force on a star of mass $m$ which has a circular orbit of radius $R$ about the center of this galaxy. **[5 т.]**

**c.** Find the orbital velocity $v$ of this star. **[5 т.]**

**d.** What power $n$ is required by the observation that $v$ is constant? **[5 т.]**

**e.** If the mass density were $\rho(r)\approx0$ for $r>R$, a reasonable assumption based on the observed visible star distribution, what would be the orbital velocity of a star with $r>R$? Let $M_0=$ the total mass of the galaxy. **[5 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-a">

### a. (5 т.)

The mass of a spherical shell of radius $r$ and thickness $dr$ is

$$
\rho(r)4\pi r^2dr=Ar^n4\pi r^2dr=Ar^{n+2}4\pi dr.\qquad (A4-1)
$$

To find the total mass enclosed by a sphere of radius $R$, we add up the mass of all of the spherical shells:

$$
M(R)=\int_0^RAr^{n+2}4\pi dr=\frac{4\pi AR^{n+3}}{n+3}=CR^{n+3}\qquad (A4-2)
$$

where $C=\frac{4\pi A}{n+3}$. (A4-3)

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-b">

### b. (5 т.)

Gauss’s Law applied to a spherical shell of radius $R$ gives us

$$
\oint\vec g\cdot d\vec A=-4\pi GM_{\rm enclosed}.\qquad (A4-4)
$$

By symmetry, $g$ is constant over the surface of the spherical shell. $g$ is radially inward, so

$$
\oint\vec g\cdot d\vec A=-g4\pi R^2.\qquad (A4-5)
$$

When we substitute (A4-5) into (A4-4) and substitute (A4-2) for $M_{\rm enclosed}$, we obtain

$$
-g4\pi R^2=-4\pi GCR^{n+3}.\qquad (A4-6)
$$

Solving for $g$,

$$
g=GCR^{n+1}.\qquad (A4-7)
$$

The gravitational force on a star of mass $m$ is then

$$
F_{\rm grav}=mg=mGCR^{n+1}.\qquad (A4-8)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-c">

### c. (5 т.)

Applying Newton’s second law to the circular motion of the star, we find that

$$
F_{\rm grav}=\frac{mv^2}{R}.\qquad (A4-9)
$$

Substituting (A4-8) into (A4-9) yields

$$
mGCR^{n+1}=\frac{mv^2}{R}.\qquad (A4-10)
$$

$$
v=\sqrt{GCR^{n+2}}.\qquad (A4-11)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-d">

### d. (5 т.)

If $v$ is constant, then $R^{n+2}=R^0$, so $n=-2$. (A4-12)

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-e">

### e. (5 т.)

Apply Gauss’s Law to a spherical shell of radius $r$, where $r>R$. Here, the mass enclosed is $M_0$.

$$
-g4\pi r^2=-4\pi GM_0.\qquad (A4-13)
$$

$$
g=\frac{GM_0}{r^2}.\qquad (A4-14)
$$

Substituting into (A4-9)

$$
\frac{mGM_0}{r^2}=\frac{mv^2}{r}.\qquad (A4-15)
$$

$$
v=\sqrt{\frac{GM_0}{r}}.\qquad (A4-16)
$$

</ProblemSection>


</Spoiler>
