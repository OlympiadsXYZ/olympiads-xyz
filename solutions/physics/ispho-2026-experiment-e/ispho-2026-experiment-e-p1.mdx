An optical fiber is a dielectric waveguide structure made of an optically transparent material in the form of a thin filament. It is designed for the controlled transmission of electromagnetic signals in the visible and infrared spectral ranges. Today optical fibers designed for an operating wavelength of $1550\ \mathrm{nm}$ are widely used in long-haul communication links and telecommunication networks.

In this problem we consider a single-mode fiber (SMF) shown in figure 1. It consists of a thin core with refractive index $n_c$ and a cladding with refractive index $n_s<n_c$. Due to the phenomenon of total internal reflection, the optical radiation remains confined within the core and propagates along its axis. The refractive-index difference between the core and the cladding provides the fiber with its waveguiding properties, allowing efficient light confinement even when the fiber is subjected to slight bends.

During the fiber manufacturing process, various optical structures can be embedded within the fiber, allowing them to be readily integrated into measurement and sensing systems. We consider a structure known as a Fiber Bragg Grating (FBG). In this configuration the fiber core contains defects with a period $\Lambda$ (shown in gray in figure 2), which are regions with a slightly increased refractive index.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '81.1%', '--fig-max': '519px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/ispho-2026-experiment-e/p1-fig2-v6.png" alt="Fig.2: Structure of an FBG" width="1623" height="769" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

*[Fig.2: Structure of an FBG]*

A Fiber Bragg Grating exhibits the properties of a narrowband dielectric mirror: it reflects light whose vacuum wavelength is close to

$$
\lambda_B=2n_c\Lambda.
$$

Figure 2 shows the transmission spectrum of an FBG, i.e., the dependence of the energy transmission coefficient $T$ on the optical wavelength $\lambda$. The sharp minimum corresponds to the Bragg resonance at $\lambda_B$.

### Part B. Coupled mode theory (2,9 т.)

The behavior of light inside the SMF can be approximately described using Coupled Mode Theory (CMT). Consider the propagation of radiation with the wavelength $\lambda_B$ through the fiber core.

The total electric field inside the SMF can be expressed as the sum of two waves traveling in different directions:

$$
E(z)=a(z)e^{+ikz}+b(z)e^{-ikz},
$$

where $a(z)$ is the complex amplitude of the wave traveling in the positive direction of the $z$ fiber axis, and $b(z)$ is the complex amplitude of the wave traveling in the negative direction of the $z$ fiber axis, $k=2\pi n_c/\lambda_B$ is the magnitude of the wave vector.

When the radiation propagates in the SMF without any refractive index modulation, the amplitudes of both components remain constant:

$$
a=\mathrm{const},\qquad b=\mathrm{const}.
$$

However, when the radiation enters the grating, it begins to interact with its periodic structure. As a result, the amplitudes of the components change:

$$
\begin{cases}a'(z)=i\varkappa b(z),\\ b'(z)=-i\varkappa a(z).\end{cases}
$$

Here $\varkappa$ is the interaction constant, which is called the grating strength, $a'(z)$ is the derivative of the complex amplitude $a(z)$ with respect to coordinate $z$, $b'(z)$ is the derivative of the complex amplitude $b(z)$ with respect to coordinate $z$.

### Part C. Calibration of an FBG temperature sensor (2,9 т.)

When the SMF is heated with the written FBG, the wavelength of the Bragg resonance changes. This is caused by the thermal expansion increasing the distance between adjacent defects and by the temperature-induced change in the refractive index. The wavelength shift of the Bragg resonance can be written as

$$
\Delta\lambda_B=\lambda_B\cdot A\cdot\Delta t,
$$

where $\Delta t$ is the temperature change, $A$ is a constant with the units of $^\circ\mathrm C^{-1}$.

Elongation of a sample with a length $l$ due to the thermal expansion is given by the equation $\Delta l=l\alpha\Delta t$, where $\alpha$ is the temperature coefficient of expansion.

The temperature dependence of the refractive index is described by the equation $\Delta n_c=\frac{dn_c}{dt}\Delta t$, where $\Delta n_c$ is the refractive index change.

### Part E. FBG dynamometer (3,9 т.)

When the SMF is stretched with the written FBG, the wavelength of the Bragg resonance changes. This is caused by the increase in spacing between adjacent defects and by the change in the refractive index of the sample due to elastic deformations of the medium. The change in the Bragg resonance wavelength induced by an applied force $F$ can be expressed as:

$$
\Delta\lambda_B=\lambda_B\cdot B\cdot F,
$$

where $B$ is a constant with the units of $\mathrm N^{-1}$. Fiber deformation is described by Hooke's law $\sigma=E\varepsilon$, where $\sigma$ is the mechanical stress (measured in Pa), $E$ is the Young's modulus of the fiber material, $\varepsilon$ is the strain ($\varepsilon=\Delta l/l$).

The dependence of the refractive index of medium on stretching is described by the equation $\Delta n_c=\frac{dn_c}{d\varepsilon}\varepsilon$, where $\Delta n_c$ is the refractive index change. Let $S$ denote the cross-sectional area of the fiber.

### Part F. Birefringence in FBG (2,9 т.)

When an FBG is written using a femtosecond laser (pulse duration $1\ \mathrm{fs}=10^{-15}\ \mathrm s$), elongated defects are formed in it, which leads to effective birefringence. The strength of the grating and the wavelength of the Bragg resonance depend on the polarization of the light. For radiation with polarization directed along defects, we introduce $\varkappa_{\parallel}$ and $\lambda_{B,\parallel}$, and for radiation with perpendicular polarization — $\varkappa_{\perp}$ and $\lambda_{B,\perp}$ (grating strength and wavelength, respectively). It is known that $\varkappa_{\parallel}<\varkappa_{\perp}$.

If an FBG is illuminated with polarized light, the observed transmission spectrum will depend on the angle $\theta-\theta_0$ between the polarization of the light and the orientation of the grating defects.
