### A.

Consider a very thin elastic rod, prevented from bending. The rod has length $L$, density ρ, and Young elasticity modulus $E$. The cross section of the rod is to be taken as constant. The rod is slightly stretched from both ends and then let to oscillate freely.

**a.** Write the kinetic energy of the rod at some moment in terms of its mass $m$, length $L$, and change rate of the tensile strain ε. **[1 т.]**

**b.** Write the potential elastic energy of the rod at some moment in terms of $m$, ρ, $E$, and ε. **[0,5 т.]**

**c.** Show that the conservation of mechanical energy for the rod leads to the characteristic differential equation for an undamped harmonic oscillation. Resorting to the analogy with a point mass $m$ acted upon by the force σ$S$ (σ being the tensile stress and $S$ being the cross section), specify the quantity that plays here the role of coordinate. **[0,5 т.]**

**d.** Write the expression for the period of small longitudinal oscillations of the rod in terms of $L$, ρ, and $E$. **[0,5 т.]**

</ProblemSection>

### B.

**e.** Write the mechanical energy of the sphere at some moment in terms of $m$, $R$, ρ, $E$, ε and the change rate of ε. **[0,5 т.]**

**f.** Write down the expression for the period of small radial oscillations of the sphere in terms of $R$, ρ, and $E$. **[0,5 т.]**

### C.

Consider a very thin rectangular elastic plate, prevented from bending. The plate has the linear dimensions $L$ and $l$ respectively. The thickness of the plate is to be taken as constant.

The effects of the tensile stresses $\sigma_x$ and $\sigma_y$ acting on the plate are **NOT** independent, in the sense that a stretching on one of the directions leads to a shrinking on the other direction. In the limits of Hooke's Law, this can be written as:

$$
\begin{aligned}\varepsilon_x&=-\mu\frac{\sigma_y}{E}\ ,\\ \varepsilon_y&=-\mu\frac{\sigma_x}{E}\end{aligned}
$$

where the dimensionless factor μ (Poisson's ratio) is somewhere in the range of 0.3.

**g.** Express $\sigma_x$ and $\sigma_y$ in terms of $\varepsilon_x$, $\varepsilon_y$, $E$, and μ. **[0,5 т.]**

**h.** Write the system of differential equations for the movement of a point mass $m$ on two orthogonal directions, using as coordinates the equivalent found at point **c**. **[0,5 т.]**

**i.** Find the possible values of ω for which the solutions of the above system are simple undamped harmonic oscillations (modes):

$$
\begin{aligned}\varepsilon_x&=A\sin\omega t\\ \varepsilon_y&=B\sin\omega t\end{aligned}\ .
$$

Express the results in terms of $L$, $l$, ρ, $E$, and μ. **[1,5 т.]**

**j.** In general, the solution of the above system of equations is a superposition of the two modes found. For a square plate ($L = l$) and a weak Poisson ratio ($\mu^2 \ll 1$), express the beats period in terms of μ and the period $T_{\mathrm{long}}$ of longitudinal oscillations of a rod with the same length. **[0,5 т.]**

</ProblemSection>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи решението от архива">

The official key prints the marks below. In i., the final frequency formula includes an extra factor $\mu$ before the inner square root compared with the preceding line; j. continues with $\mu^2$ and $T_{\mathrm{beats}}=T_{\mathrm{long}}/\mu^2$. These source expressions are retained.

<ProblemSection id="solution-sol-a">

### a. (1 т.)

Consider an element d$x$ of the rod, placed at distance $x$ from the center of the rod. Its mass is $\mathrm{d}m = m\,\mathrm{d}x/L$ each. Let $2l$ be the length of the rod at some moment, and let $y$ be the corresponding length of the region $x$.

Since the object is homogenous at all times,

$$
\frac{y}{x}=\frac{l}{L/2}
$$

Let $v$ be the velocity of the two ends of the rod at some moment.

$$
v=\frac{\mathrm{d}l}{\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\left(2l-L\right)}{2\,\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\left(\frac{2l-L}{L}\right)}{\frac{2\,\mathrm{d}t}{L}}=\frac{L\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}{2\,\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{L}{2}\dot{\varepsilon}
$$

The velocity of the element considered is

$$
v(x)=\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\left(\frac{lx}{L/2}\right)}{\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{x}{L/2}\frac{dl}{dt}=\frac{xv}{L/2}=\frac{2x}{L}\frac{L\dot{\varepsilon}}{2}=x\dot{\varepsilon}
$$

The kinetic energy of the rod is

$$
E_{\mathrm{kin}}=2\int_0^{L/2}\frac{dmv^2(x)}{2}=\int_0^{L/2}x^2\dot{\varepsilon}^2\frac{mdx}{L}=\frac{m\dot{\varepsilon}^2}{L}\left.\frac{x^3}{3}\right|_0^{L/2}=\frac{mL^2\dot{\varepsilon}^2}{24}
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-sol-b">

### b. (0,5 т.)

Let $S$ be the cross section of the rod, and $V$ its volume. The elementary work done by the tensile force σ$S$ equals the increase in elastic potential energy.

$$
\mathrm{d}E_{\mathrm{pot}}=\mathrm{d}W=F\,\mathrm{d}(2l)=\sigma S\,\mathrm{d}(2l-L)=E\varepsilon\frac{V}{L}\mathrm{d}(2l-L)=\frac{mE}{\rho}\varepsilon\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon=\mathrm{d}\left(\frac{mE}{2\rho}\varepsilon^2\right)\Rightarrow
$$

$$
E_{\mathrm{pot}}=\frac{mE\varepsilon^2}{2\rho}
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-sol-c">

### c. (0,5 т.)

$$
E_{\mathrm{mech}}=E_{\mathrm{kin}}+E_{\mathrm{pot}}=\frac{mL^2\dot{\varepsilon}^2}{24}+\frac{mE\varepsilon^2}{2\rho}=\mathrm{constant}\Rightarrow\dot{E}_{\mathrm{mech}}=\frac{mL^2\dot{\varepsilon}\ddot{\varepsilon}}{12}+\frac{mE\varepsilon\dot{\varepsilon}}{\rho}=0\Rightarrow
$$

$$
\frac{mL^2\ddot{\varepsilon}}{12}+V\sigma=0
$$

Dividing by $L$ we get:

$$
m\left(\frac{L\varepsilon}{12}\right)^{\cdot\cdot}=-\sigma S\Rightarrow\ddot{x}_{\mathrm{equivalent}}=\left(\frac{L\varepsilon}{12}\right)^{\cdot\cdot}
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-sol-d">

### d. (0,5 т.)

Dividing also by $m$ we get:

$$
\frac{L^2}{12}\ddot{\varepsilon}+\frac{E}{\rho}\varepsilon=0\Rightarrow\omega^2=\frac{12E}{\rho L^2}\Rightarrow T_{\mathrm{long}}=\pi L\sqrt{\frac{\rho}{3E}}
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-sol-e">

### e. (0,5 т.)

Consider very thin spherical layers of radius $x$ and thickness d$x$. Their masses are:

$$
\mathrm{d}m=m\frac{4\pi x^2\mathrm{d}x}{\frac{4\pi R^3}{3}}=\frac{3m}{R^3}x^2\,\mathrm{d}x
$$

Let $r$ be the radius of the sphere and $v$ the velocity of its surface at some moment. The argument goes similarly as in section A.

$$
v=R\dot{\varepsilon}\Rightarrow v(x)=x\dot{\varepsilon}\Rightarrow\mathrm{d}E_{\mathrm{kin}}=\frac{\mathrm{d}mv^2(x)}{2}=\frac{3mx^2\,\mathrm{d}x}{R^3}\frac{x^2\dot{\varepsilon}^2}{2}\Rightarrow E_{\mathrm{kin}}=\frac{3mR^2\dot{\varepsilon}^2}{10}
$$

$$
\mathrm{d}E_{\mathrm{pot}}=\mathrm{d}W=\sigma S\,\mathrm{d}r=\varepsilon E4\pi R^2R\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon=\frac{3EV\,\mathrm{d}\left(\varepsilon^2\right)}{2}=\mathrm{d}\left(\frac{3mE}{2\rho}\varepsilon^2\right)
$$

$$
E_{\mathrm{mech}}=\frac{3m}{2}\left(\frac{R^2\dot{\varepsilon}^2}{5}+\frac{E\varepsilon^2}{\rho}\right)=\mathrm{constant}
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-sol-f">

### f. (0,5 т.)

$$
\dot{E}=0\Rightarrow\frac{R^2\dot{\varepsilon}\ddot{\varepsilon}}{5}+\frac{E\varepsilon\dot{\varepsilon}}{\rho}=0\Rightarrow\omega^2=\frac{5E}{\rho R^2}\Rightarrow T_{\mathrm{radial}}=2\pi R\sqrt{\frac{\rho}{5E}}
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-sol-g">

### g. (0,5 т.)

$$

</ProblemSection>

### h. (0,5 т.)

$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-sol-i">

### i. (1,5 т.)

By replacing the sought solutions into the system of equations we get

$$
\begin{cases}-\dfrac{\omega^2AL^2}{12}+\dfrac{E\left(A+\mu B\right)}{\rho\left(1-\mu^2\right)}=0\\ -\dfrac{\omega^2Bl^2}{12}+\dfrac{E\left(B+\mu A\right)}{\rho\left(1-\mu^2\right)}=0\end{cases}
$$

By dividing the two equations term by term we get a simpler one:

$$
\frac{AL^2}{Bl^2}=\frac{A+\mu B}{B+\mu A}
$$

Let us denote the ratio of the two amplitudes by $r$.

$$
r\frac{L^2}{l^2}=\frac{r+\mu}{1+r\mu}\Rightarrow\mu L^2r^2+\left(L^2-l^2\right)r-\mu l^2=0\Rightarrow
$$

$$
r_{1;2}=\frac{-\left(L^2-l^2\right)\pm\sqrt{\left(L^2-l^2\right)^2+4\mu^2L^2l^2}}{2\mu L^2}
$$

Returning $r$ in the second equation we get:

$$
\omega^2=\frac{12E}{\rho l^2\left(1-\mu^2\right)}\left[1+\mu\frac{-\left(L^2-l^2\right)\pm\sqrt{\left(L^2-l^2\right)^2+4\mu^2L^2l^2}}{2\mu L^2}\right]\Rightarrow
$$

$$
\omega_{1;2}=\sqrt{\frac{6E\left[L^2+l^2\pm\mu\sqrt{\left(L^2-l^2\right)^2+\left(2\mu Ll\right)^2}\right]}{\rho L^2l^2\left(1-\mu^2\right)}}
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-sol-j">

### j. (0,5 т.)

$$
L=l\Rightarrow\omega_{1;2}=\sqrt{\frac{6E\left(2L^2\pm2\mu^2L^2\right)}{\rho L^4\left(1-\mu^2\right)}}=\sqrt{\frac{12E\left(1\pm\mu^2\right)}{\rho L^2\left(1-\mu^2\right)}}
$$

$$
\Delta\omega=\sqrt{\frac{12E}{\rho L^2}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1+\mu^2}{1-\mu^2}}-1\right)\approx\mu^2\sqrt{\frac{12E}{\rho L^2}}\Rightarrow T_{\mathrm{beats}}=\frac{T_{\mathrm{long}}}{\mu^2}
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-sol-k">

### k. (1,5 т.)

Let $d$ be the thickness of the plate. The shear force $\tau ld$ can be decomposed into a stretching component along $L$ ($x$-axis) and a shrinking component along $l$ ($y$-axis).

$$
\sigma_x=\frac{\tau ld\sin\gamma}{ld}\ ;\ \sigma_y=\frac{\tau ld\cos\gamma}{\left(L/\cos\gamma\right)d}\Rightarrow
$$

$$
\varepsilon_x=\frac{\tau\sin\gamma}{E}-\mu\left(-\frac{\tau l\cos^2\gamma}{LE}\right)
$$

$$
\varepsilon_y=-\frac{\tau l\cos^2\gamma}{LE}-\mu\frac{\tau\sin\gamma}{E}
$$

But

$$
\varepsilon_x=\frac{\frac{L}{\cos\gamma}-L}{L}=\frac{1-\cos\gamma}{\cos\gamma}\ ;\ \varepsilon_y=\frac{l\cos\gamma-l}{l}=-\left(1-\cos\gamma\right)\Rightarrow
$$

$$
\begin{cases}\dfrac{E}{\tau}\dfrac{1-\cos\gamma}{\cos\gamma}=\sin\gamma+\mu\dfrac{l}{L}\cos^2\gamma\\ \dfrac{E}{\tau}\left(1-\cos\gamma\right)=\dfrac{l}{L}\cos^2\gamma+\mu\sin\gamma\end{cases}
$$

Multiplying the second equation by μ and subtracting it from the first one we get:

$$
\frac{E}{\tau}\left(1-\cos\gamma\right)\left(\frac{1}{\cos\gamma}-\mu\right)=\sin\gamma\left(1-\mu^2\right)\Rightarrow\frac{E\gamma^2}{2\tau}\left(1-\mu\right)\approx\gamma\left(1-\mu^2\right)\Rightarrow\gamma=\frac{2\tau\left(1+\mu\right)}{E}
$$

$$
G=\frac{E}{2\left(1+\mu\right)}
$$

### n. (1 т.)

Let α be a very small angle with witch one cap of the cylinder rotates with respect to the other. Then the slanting angle of a cylindrical layer is:

$$
x\alpha=L\gamma\Rightarrow\gamma=\frac{x}{L}\alpha
$$

The corresponding shear stress is

$$
\tau=G\frac{x}{L}\alpha
$$

The elementary shear force acting on the cap is

$$
\mathrm{d}F=\tau\,\mathrm{d}S=G\frac{x}{L}\alpha2\pi x\,\mathrm{d}x
$$

The corresponding elementary torque is

$$
\mathrm{d}M=\mathrm{d}F\cdot x=\frac{2\pi G\alpha x^3\mathrm{d}x}{L}
$$

$$
M=\frac{2\pi G\alpha}{L}\int_0^R x^3\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{2\pi GR^4\alpha}{4L}\Rightarrow C=\frac{\pi GR^4}{2L}=\frac{\pi ER^4}{4L\left(1+\mu\right)}
$$

</ProblemSection>

\omega^2=\frac{12E}{\rho l^2(1-\mu^2)}\left[1+\mu\frac{-(L^2-l^2)\pm\sqrt{(L^2-l^2)^2+4\mu^2L^2l^2}}{2\mu L^2}\right]
$$





$$
\Rightarrow\quad\omega_{1;2}=\sqrt{\frac{6E\left[L^2+l^2\pm\mu\sqrt{(L^2-l^2)^2+(2\mu Ll)^2}\right]}{\rho L^2l^2(1-\mu^2)}}.
$$



Reader note: the μ immediately before the inner radical in the last expression is actually printed. It has not been cancelled or repaired here. **[1,5 т.]**

**j.** 

$$
L=l\quad\Rightarrow\quad\omega_{1;2}=\sqrt{\frac{6E(2L^2\pm2\mu^2L^2)}{\rho L^4(1-\mu^2)}}=\sqrt{\frac{12E(1\pm\mu^2)}{\rho L^2(1-\mu^2)}}.
$$





$$
\Delta\omega=\sqrt{\frac{12E}{\rho L^2}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1+\mu^2}{1-\mu^2}}-1\right)\approx\mu^2\sqrt{\frac{12E}{\rho L^2}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad T_{\mathrm{beats}}=\frac{T_{\mathrm{long}}}{\mu^2}.
$$



These μ² terms and the beats expression are retained as printed, not replaced with a different derivation. **[0,5 т.]**

**k.** Let d be the thickness of the plate. The shear force τld can be decomposed into a stretching component along L (x-axis) and a shrinking component along l (y-axis).



$$
\sigma_x=\frac{\tau ld\sin\gamma}{ld};\qquad\sigma_y=\frac{\tau ld\cos\gamma}{(L/\cos\gamma)d}.
$$





$$
\varepsilon_x=\frac{\tau\sin\gamma}{E}-\mu\left(-\frac{\tau l\cos^2\gamma}{LE}\right),\qquad\varepsilon_y=-\frac{\tau l\cos^2\gamma}{LE}-\mu\frac{\tau\sin\gamma}{E}.
$$



But



$$
\varepsilon_x=\frac{L/\cos\gamma-L}{L}=\frac{1-\cos\gamma}{\cos\gamma};\qquad\varepsilon_y=\frac{l\cos\gamma-l}{l}=-(1-\cos\gamma),
$$





$$
\begin{cases}\dfrac E\tau\dfrac{1-\cos\gamma}{\cos\gamma}=\sin\gamma+\mu\dfrac lL\cos^2\gamma,\\\dfrac E\tau(1-\cos\gamma)=\dfrac lL\cos^2\gamma+\mu\sin\gamma.\end{cases}
$$



Multiplying the second equation by μ and subtracting it from the first one we get:



$$
\frac E\tau(1-\cos\gamma)\left(\frac1{\cos\gamma}-\mu\right)=\sin\gamma(1-\mu^2)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac{E\gamma^2}{2\tau}(1-\mu)\approx\gamma(1-\mu^2)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\gamma=\frac{2\tau(1+\mu)}E.
$$





$$
G=\frac E{2(1+\mu)}.
$$

**l.** The quantities involved in the shear deformation are absolutely analogous to those describing the longitudinal deformation.



$$
T_{\mathrm{slant}}=\pi L\sqrt{\frac\rho{3G}}=T_{\mathrm{long}}\sqrt{2(1+\mu)}.
$$

**m.** Consider very thin cylindrical layers of radius x and thickness dx. When the cylinder is twisting, each one of them is subject to a very small shear.



$$
T_{\mathrm{twist}}=\pi L\sqrt{\frac\rho{3G}}.
$$

 **[0,5 т.]**

**n.** Let α be a very small angle with witch one cap of the cylinder rotates with respect to the other. Then the slanting angle of a cylindrical layer is:



$$
x\alpha=L\gamma\quad\Rightarrow\quad\gamma=\frac xL\alpha.
$$



The corresponding shear stress is



$$
\tau=G\frac xL\alpha.
$$



The elementary shear force acting on the cap is



$$
dF=\tau\,dS=G\frac xL\alpha\,2\pi x\,dx.
$$



The corresponding elementary torque is



$$
dM=dF\cdot x=\frac{2\pi G\alpha x^3\,dx}{L}.
$$





$$
M=\frac{2\pi G\alpha}{L}\int_0^R x^3\,dx=\frac{2\pi GR^4\alpha}{4L}\quad\Rightarrow\quad C=\frac{\pi GR^4}{2L}=\frac{\pi ER^4}{4L(1+\mu)}.
$$
