## Условие

A volume $V_f$ of fluid with uniform charge density $\rho$ is sprayed into a room, forming spherical drops. As they float around the room, the drops may break apart into smaller drops or coalesce into larger ones. Suppose that all of the drops have radius $R$. Ignore inter-drop forces and assume that $V_f \gg R^3$.

**a.** Calculate the electrostatic potential energy of a single drop. (Hint: suppose the sphere has radius $r$. How much work is required to increase the radius by $dr$?). **[10 т.]**

**b.** What is the total electrostatic energy of the drops? **[4 т.]**

Your answer to (b) should indicate that the total energy increases with $R$. In the absence of surface tension, then, the fluid would break apart into infinitesimally small drops. Suppose, however, that the fluid has a surface tension $\gamma$. (This value is the potential energy per unit surface area, and is positive.)

**c.** What is the total energy of the drops due to surface tension? **[4 т.]**

**d.** What is the equilibrium radius of the drops? **[7 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a.** $U = \frac{4\pi R^5\rho^2}{15\epsilon_0}$
- **b.** $U_{e,tot} = \frac{R^2\rho^2}{5\epsilon_0}V_f$
- **c.** $U_{s,tot} = \frac{3\gamma}{R}V_f$
- **d.** $R = \left(\frac{15\gamma\epsilon_0}{2\rho^2}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}$

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

a. If the sphere has radius $r$, it has charge

$$
q = \frac{4}{3}\pi\rho r^3 \qquad (A3-1)
$$

and thus its surface is at electrostatic potential

$$
V = \frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r} = \frac{\rho r^2}{3\epsilon_0} \qquad (A3-2)
$$

To increase the radius by $dr$, an additional charge $dq = 4\pi r^2 dr$ must be brought in from infinity, requiring work

$$
dU = V\,dq\ = \frac{4\pi r^4\rho^2}{3\epsilon_0}dr \qquad (A3-3)
$$

Thus to grow the sphere from $r = 0$ to $r = R$ requires

$$
U = \int_0^R \frac{4\pi r^4\rho^2}{3\epsilon_0}dr = \frac{4\pi R^5\rho^2}{15\epsilon_0} \qquad (A3-4)
$$

b. Each drop has volume $V_d = \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3$, so the number of drops is

$$
n = \frac{V_f}{V_d} = \frac{V_f}{\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3} \qquad (A3-5)
$$

Since we are ignoring inter-drop forces, the total energy of the drops is simply the sum of the energies of each individual drop:

$$
U_{e,tot} = nU = \frac{V_f}{\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3}\frac{4\pi R^5\rho^2}{15\epsilon_0} = \frac{R^2\rho^2}{5\epsilon_0}V_f \qquad (A3-6)
$$

c. Each drop has surface area $4\pi R^2$ and thus surface tension energy $4\pi R^2\gamma$. As before, the total energy due to surface tension is just the sum of the energies of the individual drops:

$$
U_{s,tot} = 4\pi R^2\gamma n = 4\pi R^2\gamma\frac{V_f}{\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3} = \frac{3\gamma}{R}V_f \qquad (A3-7)
$$

d. The total potential energy from both sources is

$$
U_{tot} = \left(\frac{R^2\rho^2}{5\epsilon_0} + \frac{3\gamma}{R}\right)V_f \qquad (A3-8)
$$

Equilibrium is reached when the total energy is a minimum; since $U \to \infty$ at both $R \to 0$ and $R \to \infty$, it must have an interior minimum.

$$
\frac{d}{dR}U_{tot} = \left(\frac{2R\rho^2}{5\epsilon_0} - \frac{3\gamma}{R^2}\right)V_f \qquad (A3-9)
$$

Setting this equal to zero,

$$
\frac{2R\rho^2}{5\epsilon_0} = \frac{3\gamma}{R^2} \qquad (A3-10)
$$

$$
R^3 = \frac{15\gamma\epsilon_0}{2\rho^2} \qquad (A3-11)
$$

$$
R = \left(\frac{15\gamma\epsilon_0}{2\rho^2}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}} \qquad (A3-12)
$$


</Spoiler>
