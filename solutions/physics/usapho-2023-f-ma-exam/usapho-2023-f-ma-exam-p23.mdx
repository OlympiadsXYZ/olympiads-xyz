## Условие

An astronomer on Earth, which is a distance $L_\odot$ from the Sun, observes a star and galaxy. The star is a distance $L_s \gg L_\odot$ away, and the galaxy is much further away than the star. Throughout the year, the angular distance between the star and galaxy appears to vary, reaching a minimum of $2$ arcseconds on January 1st and a maximum of $2.5$ arcseconds on July 1st. (One degree is equal to $3600$ arcseconds.) Assume the Sun, star, and galaxy do not move relative to each other, and that the Earth’s orbit lies within their plane. What is the ratio $L_s/L_\odot$?

- (A) $1.4 \times 10^4$
- (B) $7 \times 10^4$
- (C) $4 \times 10^5$
- (D) $8 \times 10^5$
- (E) $4 \times 10^6$

- D

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

The change in apparent position of the star is due to parallax. Using the small angle approximation,

$$
\Delta\theta = \frac{2L_\odot}{L_s}
$$

where $\Delta\theta = 0.5$ arcseconds $= 1^{\circ}/7200 = 2.4 \times 10^{-6}$ rad, and there is a factor of 2 since the change in position of the Earth across half a orbit is $2L_s$. Solving for the ratio gives $L_s/L_\odot = 8 \times 10^5$.


</Spoiler>
