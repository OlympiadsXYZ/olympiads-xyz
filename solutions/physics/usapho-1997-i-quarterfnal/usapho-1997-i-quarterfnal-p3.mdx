## Условие

A basketball of mass $M$, radius $R$, and moment of inertia $I$ about its center of mass (CM) is set spinning with angular velocity $\omega_o$ about a horizontal axis through its CM.  The CM is originally at rest and located at the height $h$ above the floor.  The basketball is dropped while spinning, and subsequently collides with the floor. Neglect air resistance.

<figure>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-1997-i-quarterfnal/p3-fig1.png" alt="A circle (the basketball) with a curved arrow labelled ω0 showing its clockwise spin; a dotted horizontal line from its centre and a vertical arrow h down to the floor line; a dotted vertical line from the centre to the floor marked x = 0." />
</figure>

**(a)** Let $K_1$ be the basketball’s kinetic energy just before it collides with the floor.  Write $K_1$ in terms of the given parameters and any needed constants. **[3 т.]**

**(b)** Immediately after the first bounce, the basketball is no longer spinning, and its kinetic energy is $\beta K_1$, where $\beta \lt 1$ is a known factor.  What are the horizontal and vertical components of the basketball's velocity immediately after the first bounce? **[17 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **(a)** $K_1 = \tfrac{1}{2}I\omega_o^2 + mg(h - R)$
- **(b)** $v_x = I\omega_o/mR,\quad v_y = [\,2\beta K_1/m - v_x^2\,]^{1/2}$

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**(a, 3)**  
By conservation of energy,  
$\tfrac{1}{2}I\omega_o^2 + mgh = K_1 + mgR$  
so that  
$K_1 = \tfrac{1}{2}I\omega_o^2 + mg(h - R)$ (3)

**(b, 17)**

Let $(v_x, v_y)$ = velocity of the ball immediately *after* the collision is over.  
Given:  
$\beta K_1 = \tfrac{1}{2}m(v_x^2 + v_y^2)$ (2)  
Since $\beta$ and $K_1$ are known, if either of $v_x$ or $v_y$ is known then so is the other.  Let us find $v_x$.  
(Note that linear momentum is *not* conserved.)

In the collision, the angular frequency goes from $\omega_o$ to $0$.  
Let $f$ be the average force of friction that acts horizontally on the ball during the collision.  Then from torque = $\Delta L/\Delta t$,  
$fR = I\omega_o/\Delta t$  
where $\Delta t$ is the collision time. (5)

Now apply $F = m\,\Delta v/\Delta t$ in the $x$-direction.  
The $x$-component of velocity changes from $0$ to $v_x$ during time $\Delta t$, and the horizontal force is $f$:  
$f = m\,v_x/\Delta t$. (5)

From the torque equation we have $f\Delta t = I\omega_o/R$, so combining the torque and force equations, (2)  
$I\omega_o/R = m\,v_x$  
and solving for $v_x$,  
$v_x = I\omega_o / mR$ (2)

Now $v_x$ is known.  Thus, so is $v_y$.  
$v_y = [\,2\beta K_1/m - v_x^2\,]^{1/2}$ (1)


</Spoiler>
