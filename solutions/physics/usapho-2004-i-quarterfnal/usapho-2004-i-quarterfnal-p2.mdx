## Условие

One means of attempting to detect planets orbiting other stars is to look for stellar wobble. This wobble indicates motion of the star about the star-planet center of mass. Assume the mass of the star is $M$, the mass of the planet $\alpha M$ ($\alpha \ll 1$), and the distance from the center of the star to the center of the planet is $R$. See accompanying diagram.

**a.** Derive an equation for the magnitude of the star’s velocity $v$ about the star-planet center of mass in terms of $\alpha$, $G$, $M$, and $R$. Your answer should be given to lowest order in $\alpha$. **[20 т.]**

Scientists think they can detect stars with speeds $v$ as small as $3.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$.

**b.** What is the maximum distance a planet with Earth’s mass could be from a star of solar mass and still be detected by stellar wobble? Express your answer in astronomical units (A.U.). **[5 т.]**

Possibly useful information:

| | |
|---|---|
| Mass of sun | $2.0\times10^{30}\ \mathrm{kg}$ |
| $\alpha$ for Earth | $3.0\times10^{-6}$ |
| $R$ for Earth | $1.5\times10^{11}\ \mathrm{m}$ (= 1 A.U.) |

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a.** $v \approx \alpha\sqrt{\frac{GM}{R}}$
- **b.** 0.00089 A.U.

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**2. a.** To find the center of mass of the star planet system, divide the distance $R$ into $R - x$ and $x$ as shown in the diagram. Placing the origin at the center of mass, the general center of mass equation $M_{tot}x_{cm} = m_1x_1 + m_2x_2$ becomes $0 = Mx - \alpha M(R - x)$.

Solving for $x$, the center of star to center of mass distance. $x = \frac{\alpha R}{1 + \alpha}$. [5]

Using Newton’s Second Law to describe the star’s motion $\sum F = Ma$ [2]

The net force is the universal gravitational force $F = G\frac{M\alpha M}{R^2}$ [3]

*(Maximum of [2] if $x$ is used in place of $R$.)*

For uniform motion in a circle of radius $x$ about the center of mass, $a = \frac{v^2}{x}$. [3]

*(Maximum of [2] if $R$ is used in place of $x$.)*

Substituting for $x$, this becomes $a = \frac{(1 + \alpha)v^2}{\alpha R}$ [1]

Combining these equations $G\frac{M\alpha M}{R^2} = M\left(\frac{(1 + \alpha)v^2}{\alpha R}\right)$ [2]

Canceling common factors $G\frac{\alpha M}{R} = \frac{(1 + \alpha)v^2}{\alpha}$. [1]

Solving for $v$ $v = \sqrt{\frac{G\alpha^2M}{R(1 + \alpha)}}$. [1]

To lowest order in $\alpha$ this is $v \approx \alpha\sqrt{\frac{GM}{R}}$ [2] *(Exact answer required.)*

**b.** Solving for $R$ $R \approx \frac{\alpha^2GM}{v^2}$ [2]

Inserting the values given with the problem

$$
R \approx \frac{(3\times10^{-6})^2(6.67\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2})(2.0\times10^{30}\ \mathrm{kg})}{(3.0\ \mathrm{m/s})^2} = 1.33\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{m}
$$
[2]

Converting to A.U. $R \approx 8.9\times10^{-4}$ A.U. [1]
