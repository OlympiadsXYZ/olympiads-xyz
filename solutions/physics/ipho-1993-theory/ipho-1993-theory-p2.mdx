## Условие

By means of refraction a strong laser beam can exert appreciable forces on small transparent objects. To see that this is so, consider a small glass triangular prism with an apex angle $A=\pi-2\alpha$, a base of length $2h$ and a width $w$. The prism has an index of refraction $n$ and a mass density $\rho$.

Suppose that this prism is placed in a laser beam travelling horizontally in the $x$ direction. (Throughout this problem assume that the prism does not rotate, i.e., its apex always points opposite to the direction of the laser beam, its triangular faces are parallel to the $xy$ plane, and its base is parallel to the $yz$ plane, as shown in Fig. 1.) Take the index of refraction of the surrounding air to be $n_{\mathrm{air}}=1$. Assume that the faces of the prism are coated with an anti-reflection coating so that no reflection occurs.

The laser beam has an intensity that is uniform across its width in the $z$ direction but falls off linearly with distance $y$ from the $x$ axis such that it has a maximum value of $I_0$ at $y=0$ and falls to zero at $y=\pm4h$ (Fig. 2). [Intensity is power per unit area, e.g. expressed in $\mathrm{W\ m^{-2}}$.]

**[1,5 т.]**

**2)** Express, in terms of $I_0$, $\theta$, $h$, $w$ and $y_0$, the $x$ and $y$ components of the net force exerted on the prism by the laser light when the apex of the prism is displaced a distance $y_0$ from the $x$ axis where $|y_0|\leq3h$.

Plot graphs of the values of the horizontal and vertical components of force as functions of vertical displacement $y_0$. **[5 т.]**

**3)** Suppose that the laser beam is 1 mm wide in the $z$ direction and 80 μm thick (in the $y$ direction). The prism has $\alpha=30^\circ$, $h=10\ \mathrm{\mu m}$, $n=1.5$, $w=1\ \mathrm{mm}$ and $\rho=2.5\ \mathrm{g\ cm^{-3}}$. How many watts of laser power would be required to balance this prism against the pull of gravity (in the $-y$ direction) when the apex of the prism is at a distance $y_0=-h/2$ (= −5 μm) below the axis of the laser beam? **[1,5 т.]**

**4)** Suppose that this experiment is done in the absence of gravity with the same prism and a laser beam with the same dimensions as in (3), but with $I_0=10^8\ \mathrm{W\ m^{-2}}$. What would be the period of oscillations that occur when the prism is displaced and released a distance $y=h/20$ from the center line of the laser beam? **[2 т.]**

### 1) Refraction angle

This is a simple problem in geometry and Snell's Law.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '49.5%', '--fig-max': '317px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/ipho-1993-theory/p2-sol-fig1.png" alt="Complete nativeFig1 refraction throughwedge withalpha/beta/theta,a1/a2/a3,ray/normal arrows andcaption." width="991" height="763" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


The angle of incidence $a_3=\alpha$ because $a_1=\alpha$ and $a_1+a_2=a_2+a_3=90^\circ$. The angle $\beta$ is found from Snell's law $\sin\alpha=n\sin\beta$. The angle of incidence on the base is

$$
\frac\pi2-\left(\pi-\alpha-\left(\frac\pi2-\beta\right)\right)=\alpha-\beta,
$$

from which it follows that

$$
\sin\theta=n\sin(\alpha-\beta),
$$

implying that

$$
\theta=\sin^{-1}\left[n\sin\left(\alpha-\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{\sin\alpha}n\right)\right)\right].
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part2">

### 2) Forces in both displacement ranges

The force on the prism is equal and opposite to the rate of change of momentum of the laser light passing through it. To analyze this, consider the momentum changes of the laser light incident on the upper half of the prism.

Think of the laser beam as delivering to the upper half of the prism $r_u$ photons per second parallel to the $x$ axis. If the energy of a photon is $E$, then its momentum is $\mathbf p_i=(E/c)\hat{\mathbf i}$, and a photon leaving the prism at an angle $\theta$ to the $x$ axis will differ in momentum from the incident photon by

$$
\delta\mathbf p=\frac Ec(\cos\theta-1)\hat{\mathbf i}-\frac Ec\sin\theta\hat{\mathbf j}.
$$

The rate of charge of momentum of these photons will then be

$$
\mathbf F_{\mathrm{up}}=r_u\delta\mathbf p=\frac{r_uE}c[(\cos\theta-1)\hat{\mathbf i}-\sin\theta\hat{\mathbf j}].
$$

The phrase “rate of charge of momentum” is printed in the native key.

The quantity $r_uE$ is the power $P_u$ delivered to the upper face, and the recoil force $\mathbf F_u$ produced by light refracting through the upper half of the prism will be

$$
\mathbf F_u=\frac{P_u}c[(1-\cos\theta)\hat{\mathbf i}+\sin\theta\hat{\mathbf j}].
$$

A similar argument gives the force on the lower half as

$$
\mathbf F_l=\frac{P_l}c[(1-\cos\theta)\hat{\mathbf i}-\sin\theta\hat{\mathbf j}].
$$

From these two results we see that the net force on the prism will be

$$
\mathbf F=\frac1c[(P_u+P_l)(1-\cos\theta)]\hat{\mathbf i}+\frac1c[(P_u-P_l)\sin\theta]\hat{\mathbf j}.
$$

The angle $\theta$ can be expressed in terms of $\alpha$ (see answer to part 1). To find the values of $P_u$ and $P_l$ calculate the average intensities, $\bar I_u$ and $\bar I_l$, incident on each half of the prism, and multiply by $hw$, the area of each half of the prism projected perpendicular to the laser beam. Because the intensity distribution $I(y)$ is a linear function of $y$, the average intensities are easily determined.

The problem states that

$$
I(y)=I_0\left(1-\frac y{4h}\right)\quad\text{for }0<y<+4h,
$$

$$
I(y)=I_0\left(1+\frac y{4h}\right)\quad\text{for }-4h<y<0.
$$

Now suppose that the prism is lifted a distance $y_0$ from the $x$ axis ($y_0>0$). There are two distinct cases:

**(a) When $h\leq y_0\leq3h$,** the whole prism is entirely in the upper half of the beam. As Fig. 2 shows, for this case the average is equal to the intensity at the center of each face which is at $y_0+h/2$ for the upper face and at $y_0-h/2$ for the lower one. This gives

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '54.1%', '--fig-max': '347px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/ipho-1993-theory/p2-sol-fig2.png" alt="Complete nativeFig2 average-intensity graph fory0>=h,fullaxes,I0,l/u shadedareas,0,y0-h/y0/y0+h andcaption." width="1084" height="969" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


$$
\bar I_u=I_0\left(1-\frac{y_0+h/2}{4h}\right)=I_0\left(\frac78-\frac{y_0}{4h}\right),
$$

$$
\bar I_l=I_0\left(1-\frac{y_0-h/2}{4h}\right)=I_0\left(\frac98-\frac{y_0}{4h}\right).
$$

From these it follows that

$$
F_x=\frac{2hwI_0}c\left(1-\frac{y_0}{4h}\right)(1-\cos\theta),\qquad F_y=-\frac{hwI_0}{4c}\sin\theta.
$$

**(b) When $0<y_0<h$,** the lower half of the prism is partly in the lower half of the laser beam as shown in Fig. 3. Then the part of the lower half of the prism between 0 and $y_0$ has a fraction $y_0/h$ of the area of the lower half of the prism and sees an average intensity

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '47.7%', '--fig-max': '306px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/ipho-1993-theory/p2-sol-fig3.png" alt="Complete nativeFig3 average-intensity graph for0＜y0＜h,fullaxes,shadedl/u,I0,y0-h,0,y0,y0+h andcaption." width="955" height="1064" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


$$
\bar I_{l_1}=I(y_0/2)=I_0\left(1-\frac{y_0}{8h}\right).
$$

The part between 0 and $y_0-h$ has a fraction $1-y_0/h$ of the area and sees an average intensity of

$$
\bar I_{l_2}=I\left(\frac{h-y_0}2\right)=I_0\left(\frac78+\frac{y_0}{8h}\right).
$$

Putting these together we get

$$
P_l=hw\frac{y_0}h\bar I_{l_1}+hw\left(1-\frac{y_0}h\right)\bar I_{l_2}=hwI_0\left(\frac78+\frac{y_0}{4h}-\frac{y_0^2}{4h^2}\right).
$$

The average intensity on the upper face has the same functional dependence on $y_0$ as in the first case. Therefore, $P_u=hwI_0(7/8-y_0/4h)$ as before.

Putting these together gives

$$
P_u+P_l=hwI_0\left(\frac74-\frac{y_0^2}{4h^2}\right),\qquad P_u-P_l=-hwI_0\frac{y_0}{2h}\left(1-\frac{y_0}{2h}\right),
$$

from which it follows that

$$
F_x=\frac{hwI_0}c\left(\frac74-\frac{y_0^2}{4h^2}\right)(1-\cos\theta),
$$

$$
F_y=-\frac{hwI_0}c\frac{y_0}{2h}\left(1-\frac{y_0}{2h}\right)\sin\theta.
$$

Because the intensity distribution is symmetric about the axis of the laser beam, the solutions for $y_0<0$ will mirror the solutions for $y_0>0$. Graphs of $F_x$ and $F_y$ as functions of $y_0$ are shown in Fig. 4.

### 3) Laser power against gravity

Both the equation and the graph of $F_y$ show that to have $F_y>0$ and opposite the force of gravity, $y_0$ must be $<0$. Then to find the force necessary to support the prism against gravity, find the prism's mass, and equate the expression for the vertical component of force from the laser beam to the weight of the prism, and find $I_0$ for the parameters given. Use that result to find the total power in the laser beam. This can be done by finding the average value $\bar I$ over the specified cross sectional area of the laser beam.

To find the mass of the prism first find its volume $=\tan\alpha\,h^2w$ then

$$
m=\frac1{\sqrt3}\times(10^{-3})^2\times.1\times2.5=1.44\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm g=1.44\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm{kg};\qquad mg=1.42\times10^{-9}\ \mathrm N.
$$

The solution to (2) assumed a displacement in the $y>0$ direction, but the problem is symmetric so we can use that solution. We want the value of $I_0$ that satisfies

$$
\frac{I_0hw}c\frac{y_0}{2h}\left(1-\frac{y_0}{2h}\right)\sin\theta=mg=1.42\times10^{-9}
$$

when $\theta=15.9^\circ$, $y_0=h/2$, $h=10\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm m$, $w=10^{-3}\ \mathrm m$.

$$
I_0=\frac{3\times10^8\times1.42\times10^{-9}}{10^{-5}\times10^{-3}\times.274\times\frac3{16}}=8.30\times10^8\ \mathrm{W/m^2},
$$

since the power $P$ is given by $P=\bar I\times\text{area of laser beam}$ where $\bar I=I_0/2$. This yields

$$
P=\frac12\times8.30\times10^8\times10^{-3}\times80\times10^{-6}=33.2\ \mathrm W.
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part4">

### 4) Oscillation period

A displacement of $h/20$ corresponds to $y_0/h=.05\ll1$ so that the vertical force component is well approximated by

$$
F_y=-\frac{I_0w\sin\theta}{2c}y.
$$

This is the equation of a harmonic oscillator with angular frequency

$$
\omega=\sqrt{\frac{I_0w\sin\theta}{2mc}}=\sqrt{\frac{I_0\sin\theta}{2c\rho h^2\tan\alpha}}.
$$

Putting numbers into this gives

$$
T=\frac{2\pi}\omega=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{2\times3\times10^8\times2.5\times10^3\times10^{-10}\times1/\sqrt3}{10^8\times.274}}=11.2\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm s.
$$

</ProblemSection>
