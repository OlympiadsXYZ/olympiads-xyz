### Part 1. Characteristics of the galvanic cell "Camelion".

**1.1 – 1.3** Table 1 shows the results of measuring resistances $R_{0k}$ and voltages $U_k$, as well as the results of calculating currents $I_k$

Table 1

| $k$ | $R_{0k}$, Ohm | $U_k$, V | $I_k$, A |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1,0 | 1,300 | 1,300 |
| 2 | 2,0 | 1,405 | 0,703 |
| 3 | 3,0 | 1,445 | 0,482 |
| 4 | 4,0 | 1,470 | 0,368 |
| 5 | 5,0 | 1,480 | 0,296 |

Current strengths are calculated according to Ohm's law

$$
I_k=\frac{U_k}{R_k},\qquad (1)
$$

and the graph of the obtained dependence is shown in Figure 1.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '71.8%', '--fig-max': '460px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/izho-2025-experiment-exp-eng/p1-sol-fig1-v2.png" alt="Fig. 1: Camelion voltage versus current with fitted line." width="1436" height="943" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


**1.4** The theoretical dependence of the voltage in the external circuit on the current strength follows from Ohm's law:

$$
U=\varepsilon-IR.\qquad (2)
$$

Using the least squares method, we find the parameters of the linear dependence $U=aI+b$. As follows from formula (2), the characteristics of the galvanic element are equal to:

$$
\varepsilon=b=(1{,}53\pm0{,}04)\ \mathrm{V},\qquad (3)
$$

$$
r=-a=(0{,}18\pm0{,}02)\ \mathrm{Ohm}.\qquad (4)
$$
