*Theoretical Competition-Problem No. 2. Optical fiber*

## Условие

An optical fiber consists of a cylindrical core of radius $a$, made of a transparent material with refraction index varying gradually from the value $n=n_1$ on the axis to $n = n_2$ (with $1<n_2<n_1$) at a distance $a$ from the axis, according to the formula

$$
n = n(x) = n_1\sqrt{1-\alpha^2x^2}
$$

where $x$ is the distance from the core axis and $\alpha$ is a constant. The core is surrounded by a cladding made of a material with constant refraction index $n_2$. Outside the fiber is air, of refractive index $n_0$.

Let O$z$ be the axis of the fiber, with O - the center of the fiber end.

Given $n_0=1.000$; $n_1=1.500$; $n_2= 1.460$, $a = 25\ \mu\mathrm{m}$.

1. A monochromatic light ray enters the fiber at point O under an incident angle $\theta_\mathrm{i}$, the incident plane being the plane $x$O$z$.

**1.a.** Show that at each point on the trajectory of the light in the fiber, the refractive index $n$ and the angle $\theta$ between the light ray and the O$z$ axis satisfy the relationship $n\cos\theta = C$ where $C$ is a constant. Find the expression for $C$ in terms of $n_1$ and $\theta_\mathrm{i}$. ***[1.0 points]*** **[1 т.]**

**1.b.** Use the result found in 1.a. and the trigonometric relation $\cos\theta = \left(1+\tan^2\theta\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}$, where $\tan\theta = \dfrac{dx}{dz} = x'$ is the slope of the tangent to the trajectory at point ($x$, $z$), derive an equation for $x'$. Find the full expression for $\alpha$ in terms of $n_1$, $n_2$ and $a$. By differentiating the two sides of this equation versus $z$, find the equation for the second derivative $x''$. ***[1.0 points]*** **[1 т.]**

**1.c.** Find the expression of $x$ as a function of $z$, that is $x = f(z)$, which satisfies the above equation. This is the equation of the trajectory of light in the fiber. ***[1.0 points]*** **[1 т.]**

**1.d.** Sketch one full period of the trajectories of the light rays entering the fiber under two different incident angles $\theta_\mathrm{i}$. ***[1.0 points]*** **[1 т.]**

2. Light propagates in the optical fiber.

**2.a.** Find the maximum incident angle $\theta_\mathrm{iM}$, under which the light ray still can propagate inside the core of the fiber. ***[1.5 points]*** **[1,5 т.]**

**2.b.** Determine the expression for coordinate $z$ of the crossing points of a light ray with O$z$ axis for $\theta_\mathrm{i} \neq 0$. ***[1.5 points]*** **[1,5 т.]**

3. The light is used to transmit signals in the form of very short light pulses (of negligible pulse width).

**3.a.** Determine the time $\tau$ it takes the light to travel from point O to the first crossing point with O$z$ for incident angle $\theta_\mathrm{i} \neq 0$ and $\theta_\mathrm{i} \le \theta_\mathrm{iM}$.

The ratio of the coordinate $z$ of the first crossing point and $\tau$ is called the propagation speed of the light signal along the fiber. Assume that this speed varies monotonously with $\theta_i$.

Find this speed (called $v_\mathrm{M}$) for $\theta_\mathrm{i} = \theta_\mathrm{iM}$.

Find also the propagation speed (called $v_0$) of the light along the axis Oz.

Compare the two speeds. ***[3.25 points]*** **[3,25 т.]**

**3.b.** The light beam bearing the signals is a converging beam entering the fiber at O under different incident angles $\theta_i$ with $0 \le \theta_i \le \theta_\mathrm{iM}$. Calculate the highest repetition frequency $f$ of the signal pulses, so that at a distance $z = 1000\ \mathrm{m}$ two consecutive pulses are still separated (that is, the pulses do not overlap). ***[1.75 points]*** **[1,75 т.]**

***Attention***

1. The wave properties of the light are not considered in this problem.
2. Neglect any chromatic dispersion in the fiber.
3. The speed of light in vacuum is c = $2.998\times 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m/s}$
4. You may use the following formulae:

- The length of a small arc element $ds$ in the $x$O$z$ plane is

$$
ds = dz\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{dx}{dz}\right)^2}
$$

$$
\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2x^2}} = \frac{1}{b}\,\mathrm{Arc}\sin\frac{bx}{a}
$$

$$
\int\frac{x^2dx}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2x^2}} = -\frac{x\sqrt{a^2-b^2x^2}}{2b^2} + \frac{a^2\,\mathrm{Arc}\sin\dfrac{bx}{a}}{2b^3}
$$

- $\mathrm{Arc}\sin x$ is the inverse function of the sine function. Its value equals the less angle the sine of which is $x$. In other words, if $y = \mathrm{Arc}\sin x$ then $\sin y = x$.

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **1.a.** $n\cos\theta = C = \sqrt{n_1^2-\sin^2\theta_\mathrm{i}}$
- **1.b.** $\alpha = \frac{\sqrt{n_1^2-n_2^2}}{a n_1},\quad x'' + \frac{n_1^2-n_2^2}{a^2(n_1^2-\sin^2\theta_\mathrm{i})}\,x = 0$
- **1.c.** $x = \frac{a\sin\theta_\mathrm{i}}{\sqrt{n_1^2-n_2^2}}\sin\left(\sqrt{\frac{n_1^2-n_2^2}{n_1^2-\sin^2\theta_\mathrm{i}}}\,\frac{z}{a}\right)$
- **2.a.** 20.13 °
- **2.b.** $z = \frac{k\pi}{p} = k\pi a\sqrt{\frac{n_1^2-\sin^2\theta_\mathrm{i}}{n_1^2-n_2^2}}$
- **3.a.** $\tau = \frac{\pi a n_1^2}{c\sqrt{n_1^2-n_2^2}}\left(1-\frac{\sin^2\theta_\mathrm{i}}{2n_1^2}\right),\quad v_\mathrm{M} = \frac{2cn_2}{n_1^2+n_2^2} = 1.998\times 10^8\ \mathrm{m/s},\quad v_0 = \frac{c}{n_1} = 1.999\times 10^8\ \mathrm{m/s}$
- **3.b.** 547.1 MHz

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**Solutions of Problem No. 1**

**Optical fiber**

1.a. At both sides of the point O (outside and inside the fiber), according to Snell law, we have:

$$
n_0\sin\theta_\mathrm{i} = n_1\sin\theta_1 \qquad (1)
$$

where $\theta_1$ is the value of angle $\theta$ at point O *inside* the fiber.

The light trajectory lays in the $xOz$ plane. Because the refraction index $n$ varies along $x$ direction, we divide $Ox$ axis into small elements $dx$, so that in each of these elements $n$ can be considered as constant. We have, then:

$$
n\sin i = (n+dn).\sin(i+di) \qquad (2)
$$

where $i$ is the angle between the light trajectory and $x$ direction. Because $\theta + i = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, then

$$
n\cos\theta = (n+dn).\cos(\theta+d\theta) \qquad (3)
$$

Thus, at each point of coordinate $x$ on the light trajectory, we have:

$$
n\cos\theta = n_1\sqrt{1-\alpha^2x^2}\,\cos\theta = n_1\cos\theta_1 \qquad (4)
$$

Because

$$
\cos\theta_1 = \sqrt{1-\sin^2\theta_1} = \sqrt{1-\frac{\sin^2\theta_\mathrm{i}}{n_1^2}} \qquad (5)
$$

we have

$$
n\cos\theta = n_1\cos\theta_1 = n_1\sqrt{1-\frac{\sin^2\theta_\mathrm{i}}{n_1^2}} = \sqrt{n_1^2-\sin^2\theta_\mathrm{i}}
$$

Then

$$
n\cos\theta = C = \sqrt{n_1^2-\sin^2\theta_\mathrm{i}} \qquad (6)
$$

1.2. Because $\dfrac{dx}{dz} = x' = \tan\theta$, from (6) we have:

$$
n_1\sqrt{1-\alpha^2x^2}\,\cos\theta = n_1\sqrt{1-\alpha^2x^2}\left(1+\tan^2\theta\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}} = C \qquad (7)
$$

Squaring the two sides, we obtain:

$$
\left(1-\alpha^2x^2\right)\left(1+\tan^2\theta\right)^{-1} = \frac{C^2}{n_1^2}
$$

and

$$
1+x'^2 = (1-\alpha^2x^2)\frac{n_1^2}{C^2} \qquad (8)
$$

After derivating the two sides of (8) versus $z$, we get:

$$
x'' + \frac{\alpha^2n_1^2}{C^2}x = 0 \qquad (9)
$$

Because $n = n_1\sqrt{1-\alpha^2x^2}$ and

- $n = n_1$ at $x$=0
- $n = n_2$ at $x$=$a$

we get

$$
\alpha = \frac{\sqrt{n_1^2-n_2^2}}{a.n_1}
$$

Finally, we get the equation for $x''$

$$
x'' + \frac{n_1^2-n_2^2}{a^2(n_1^2-\sin^2\theta_i)}\cdot x = 0 \qquad (10)
$$

1.c. The equation for the light trajectory is obtained by solving (10). This is an equation similar to that for an harmonic oscillation, which solution can be written right away

$$
x = x_0\sin(pz+q) \qquad (11)
$$

with

$$
p = \frac{1}{a}\sqrt{\frac{n_1^2-n_2^2}{n_1^2-\sin^2\theta_\mathrm{i}}}
$$

The parameters $p$ and $q$ are determined from the boundary conditions:

- at $z$=0, $x$=0, hence $q$=0
- at $z$=0 inside the fiber, $x' = \dfrac{dx}{dz} = \tan\theta_1$, then

$$
x_0 = \frac{\tan\theta_1}{p} = \frac{a.\sin\theta_\mathrm{i}}{\sqrt{n_1^2-n_2^2}} \qquad (12)
$$

The equation for the trajectory of the light inside the fiber is:

$$
x = \frac{a\sin\theta_\mathrm{i}}{\sqrt{n_1^2-n_2^2}}.\sin\left(\sqrt{\frac{n_1^2-n_2^2}{n_1^2-\sin^2\theta_\mathrm{i}}}.\frac{z}{a}\right) \qquad (13)
$$

1.d. Here is a sketch of the trajectories of two rays entering the fiber at O, under different incident angles.

2.a. The condition for the light to propagate along the fiber is that $x_0 \le a$. This means that:

$$
\frac{a\sin\theta_\mathrm{i}}{\sqrt{n_1^2-n_2^2}} \le a
$$

or:

$$
\sin\theta_\mathrm{i} \le \sqrt{n_1^2-n_2^2} \qquad (14)
$$

Thus the incident angle $\theta_\mathrm{i}$ must not exceed $\theta_\mathrm{iM}$, with

$$
\sin\theta_\mathrm{iM} = \sqrt{n_1^2-n_2^2} = 0.344 \qquad (14a)
$$

or:

$$
\theta_\mathrm{i} \le \theta_\mathrm{iM} = \mathrm{Arc}\sin\left(\sqrt{n_1^2-n_2^2}\right) = \mathrm{Arc}\sin 0.344 = 0.351\,\mathrm{rad} = 20.13^{\circ}
$$

2.b. The crossing points of the light beam with O$z$ axis must satisfy the condition $pz = k\pi$, with $k$ - an integer. The $z$ coordinates of these points are:

$$
z = \frac{k\pi}{p} = k\pi a\sqrt{\frac{n_1^2-\sin^2\theta_\mathrm{i}}{n_1^2-n_2^2}} \qquad (15)
$$

except for $\theta_\mathrm{i}$=0.

3.a. The rays entering the fiber at different incident angles have different trajectories. As a consequence, the propagation speeds of the rays along the fiber should be different.

The light trajectories are sinusoidal as given in (13). Let us calculate the time $\tau$ it takes the light to propagate from point O to its first crossing point with O$z$ axis. This is twice the time it takes the light to propagate from point O to its position most distant from O$z$ axis.

The time required for the light to travel a small segment $ds$ along its trajectory is

$$
dt = \frac{n}{c}ds = \frac{n}{c}\sqrt{dx^2+dz^2} = \frac{n}{c}\sqrt{1+\frac{dz^2}{dx^2}}.dx
$$

$$
= \frac{n}{c}\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{1}{\tan\theta}\right)^2}.dx = \frac{n}{c}\frac{dx}{\sin\theta}
$$

From (6), we have

$$
dt = \frac{n_1^2\left(1-\alpha^2x^2\right)}{c.\sqrt{\sin^2\theta_\mathrm{i}-n_1^2\alpha^2x^2}}.dx
$$

and

$$
\frac{\tau}{2} = \int_0^{x_0}dt = \frac{n_1^2}{c}\left[\int_0^{x_0}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{\sin^2\theta_\mathrm{i}-n_1^2\alpha^2x^2}} - \alpha^2\int_0^{x_0}\frac{x^2dx}{\sqrt{\sin^2\theta_\mathrm{i}-n_1^2\alpha^2x^2}}\right] \qquad (16)
$$

$$
= \frac{n_1^2}{c}\left[I_1-\alpha^2I_2\right]
$$

where

$$
I_1 = \frac{1}{n_1\alpha}\,\mathrm{Arc}\sin\left.\frac{n_1\alpha x}{\sin\theta_\mathrm{i}}\right|_0^{x_0} = \frac{\pi a}{2\sqrt{n_1^2-n_2^2}} \qquad (17)
$$

$$
I_2 = \left.\frac{-x\sqrt{\sin^2\theta_\mathrm{i}-n_1^2\alpha^2x^2}}{2n_1^2\alpha^2}\right|_0^{x_0} + \left.\frac{\sin^2\theta_\mathrm{i}.\mathrm{Arc}\sin\dfrac{n_1\alpha x}{\sin\theta_\mathrm{i}}}{2n_1^3\alpha^3}\right|_0^{x_0} = \frac{\pi\sin^2\theta_\mathrm{i}}{4n_1^3\alpha^3} \qquad (18)
$$

Using (16), (17), (18), we obtain

$$
\tau = \frac{\pi a.n_1^2}{c\sqrt{n_1^2-n_2^2}}\left(1-\frac{\sin^2\theta_\mathrm{i}}{2n_1^2}\right) \qquad (19)
$$

The propagation speed along the fiber is $v = \dfrac{z}{\tau}$, where $z$ is the coordinate of the first crossing point, which is determined by (15) for $k = 1$. Because $z$ and $\tau$ depend on the incident angle $\theta_\mathrm{i}$, $v$ also depends on $\theta_\mathrm{i}$.

For $\theta_\mathrm{i} = \theta_\mathrm{iM}$, from (14a), we get

$$
v_\mathrm{M} = \frac{\pi a n_2}{\sqrt{n_1^2-n_2^2}}.\frac{2c\sqrt{n_1^2-n_2^2}}{\pi a n_1^2}\left(1+\frac{n_2^2}{n_1^2}\right)^{-1} = \frac{2cn_2}{n_1^2+n_2^2} \qquad (20)
$$

and

$$
v_\mathrm{M} = \frac{2\times 2.998\times 10^8\times 1.460}{1.500^2+1.460^2} = 1.998\times 10^8\ \mathrm{m/s} \qquad (20a)
$$

The propagation speed of the light along the O$z$ axis is

$$
v = \frac{c}{n_1} \qquad (21)
$$

because the refraction index is n$_1$ on the axis of the fiber.

The numerical value is

$$
v_0 = \frac{2.998\times 10^8}{1.5} = 1.999\times 10^8\ \mathrm{m/s} \qquad (21a)
$$

3.b. If the beam of the light pulses is formed by rays converging at O, then the rays with different incident angles has different propagation speeds. The two rays of incident angles $\theta_i = 0$ and $\theta_i = \theta_\mathrm{iM}$ arrive to the plane $z$ with a time delay

$$
\Delta t = \frac{z}{v_\mathrm{M}} - \frac{z}{v_0} = \frac{z}{c}.\frac{(n_1-n_2)^2}{2n_2} \qquad (22)
$$

This means that a very short light pulse becomes a pulse of finite width $\Delta t$ given by (22) at the plane $z$. If two consecutive pulses enter the fiber with a delay greater than $\Delta t$, then at the plane $z$, they are separated. Hence the repetition frequency of the pulses must not exceed the maximal value:

$$
f_\mathrm{M} = (\Delta t)^{-1} = \frac{2.c.n_2}{z.(n_1-n_2)^2} \qquad (23)
$$

If $z = 1000\ \mathrm{m}$, then

$$
f_\mathrm{M} = \frac{2\times 2.998\times 10^8\times 1.460}{1000\times(1.500-1.460)^2} = 547.1\mathrm{MHz}
$$
