## Условие

The purpose is to estimate the lifetime of a (hemispherical) drop of a liquid sitting on top of a very thin layer of vapour which is thermally insulating the drop from the very hot plate below.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '72.5%', '--fig-max': '465px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/apho-2009-theory-th/p3-fig1.png" alt="Perspective sketch of a hemispherical drop on a flat plate; labels: drop, very hot surface, thin layer of vapour (a thin layer between the drop and the plate)." width="1452" height="408" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Figure 1</figcaption>
</figure>

It will be assumed here that the flow of vapour underneath the drop is streamline and behaves as a Newtonian fluid of viscosity coefficient $\eta$ and of thermal conductivity $\mathcal{K}$. The specific latent heat of vaporization of the liquid is $\ell$. And for a Newtonian fluid we have the shear stress $\dfrac{F}{A} = \eta \times$ the rate of shear $\dfrac{dv}{dz}$ where $v$ is the flow velocity and $z$ is the perpendicular distance to the direction of flow, and the direction of $F$ is tangential to the surface area $A$.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '75.9%', '--fig-max': '486px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/apho-2009-theory-th/p3-fig2.png" alt="Cross-section of a hemispherical drop of radius R at distance b above the hot surface, with vertical axis Z through centre O, gravity arrow g, a dotted mid-plane of the vapour layer, the velocity v at height z above the mid-plane, and radial distances r and δr marked along the hot surface." width="1519" height="724" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
<figcaption>Figure 2</figcaption>
</figure>

$v$ is the velocity of vapour in the radial direction at the height $z$ above the mid-plane. The pressure $P$ inside the vapour must be higher towards the centre O. This will result in the out-flowing of vapour and in force that holds the drop against the pull of gravity. The thickness of vapour layer under thermal and mechanical equilibria is $b$.

For a Newtonian flow of vapour we can approximate that

$$
\frac{d}{dz}v = \frac{z}{\eta}\frac{d}{dr}P
$$

**3.1)** Show that

$$
v(z) = \frac{z^2}{2\eta}\frac{d}{dr}P + C
$$

where $C$ is an arbitrary constant of integration. **[0,5 т.]**

**3.2)** Refer to figure 2, find the value of $C$ in terms of $\eta, \dfrac{d}{dr}P,$ and $b$ using the boundary condition $v = 0$ for $z = \pm\dfrac{b}{2}$. **[0,5 т.]**

**3.3)** Calculate the volume rate of flow of vapour through the cylindrical surface defined by $r$. (Hint: the cylinder is of radius $r$ and of height $b$ underneath the drop). **[1 т.]**

**3.4)** By assuming that the rate of production of vapour of density $\rho_{\mathrm{V}}$ is due to heat flow from the hot surface to the drop, find the expression for the pressure $P(r)$. Use $P_{\mathrm{a}}$ to represent the atmospheric pressure, and use $\Delta T$ for the temperature difference between those of the hot surface and of the drop. Assume that the system has reached the steady state. **[2 т.]**

**3.5)** Calculate the value of $b$ by equating the weight of the drop to the net force due to pressure difference between the bottom and the top of the drop. The density of the drop is $\rho_0$. **[2 т.]**

**3.6)** Now, what is the total rate of vaporization? **[2 т.]**

**3.7)** Assume that the drop maintains a hemispherical shape, what is the life-time of the drop? **[2 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи решението от архива">

### The Leidenfrost Phenomenon — archive solution original

**3.1)** As given in the problem

$$
\frac{dv}{dz}=\left(\frac1\eta\frac{dP}{dr}\right)\cdot z.\qquad\text{(i)}
$$

Integrating (i) with respect to $z$, we get

$$
v(z)=\left(\frac1{2\eta}\frac{dP}{dr}\right)\cdot z^2+C.\qquad\text{(ii)}\quad\text{(0.5 point)}
$$

**3.2)**

$$
v\left(\frac b2\right)=0=\left(\frac1{2\eta}\frac{dP}{dr}\right)\cdot\left(\frac b2\right)^2+C.\qquad\text{(iii)}
$$

Therefore

$$
C=-\frac{b^2}{8\eta}\frac{dP}{dr}.\quad\text{(0.5 point)}
$$

Note that $C$ is not a real constant; its value depends on $\frac{dP}{dr}$ which is a function of $r$.

**3.3)** Let $Q$ be the volume rate of flow of the vapour through the cylindrical surface of $2\pi rb$.

$$
\delta Q=v(z)\cdot2\pi r\delta z\quad\text{(0.3 point)}
$$

where from (ii) and (iii):

$$
v(z)=\left(\frac1{2\eta}\frac{dP}{dr}\right)\cdot\left[z^2-\frac{b^2}{4}\right].\qquad\text{(iv)}
$$

Therefore

$$
Q=2\int_{z=0}^{b/2}v(z)\cdot2\pi r\,dz=\left(\frac{2\pi r}{\eta}\frac{dP}{dr}\right)\int_{z=0}^{b/2}\left[z^2-\frac{b^2}{4}\right]dz,
$$

$$
Q=-\frac{\pi rb^3}{6\eta}\frac{dP}{dr}.\qquad\text{(v)}\quad\text{(0.7 point)}
$$

**3.4)** The total rate of heat flow from the area $\pi r^2$ of the hot surface to the drop is $\frac{\pi r^2\mathcal K\Delta T}{b}$. We assume that this heat goes into vaporizing the drop.

Hence

$$
\rho Q\ell=\frac{\pi r^2\mathcal K\Delta T}{b},
$$

and using (v) we get

$$
\frac{dP}{dr}=-\left(\frac{6\eta\mathcal K\Delta T}{\rho_{\mathrm V}\ell b^4}\right)\cdot r.\qquad\text{(vi)}\quad\text{(0.4 point)}
$$

This gives

$$
P(r)=-\left(\frac{3\eta\mathcal K\Delta T}{\rho_{\mathrm V}\ell b^4}\right)\cdot r^2+B.\quad\text{(0.4 point)}
$$

where $B$ is an arbitrary constant whose value can be found by applying the boundary condition $P(R)=P_{\mathrm a}$, the atmospheric pressure. Hence

$$
B=P_{\mathrm a}+\left(\frac{3\eta\mathcal K\Delta T}{\rho_{\mathrm V}\ell b^4}\right)\cdot R^2,\qquad\text{(vii)}\quad\text{(0.4 point)}
$$

and

$$
P(r)=P_{\mathrm a}+\left(\frac{3\eta\mathcal K\Delta T}{\rho_{\mathrm V}\ell b^4}\right)\cdot(R^2-r^2).\qquad\text{(viii)}\quad\text{(0.8 point)}
$$

**3.5)** The net force due to pressure is in the upward direction and of magnitude

$$
f=\int_{r=0}^R[P(r)-P_{\mathrm a}]2\pi r\,dr=\frac{3\pi\eta\mathcal K\Delta TR^4}{2\rho_{\mathrm V}\ell b^4}.\qquad\text{(ix)}\quad\text{(1.0 point)}
$$

The weight of the drop is $\frac23\pi R^3\rho_0g$, where $\rho_0$ is density of liquid. Therefore

$$
\frac23\pi R^3\rho_0g=\frac{3\pi\eta\mathcal K\Delta TR^4}{2\rho_{\mathrm V}\ell b^4},
$$

$$
b=\left(\frac{9\eta\mathcal KR\Delta T}{4\rho_0\rho_{\mathrm V}\ell g}\right)^{1/4}.\qquad\text{(x)}
$$

Note that

$$
\frac{3\eta\mathcal K\Delta T}{\rho_{\mathrm V}\ell b^4}=\frac43\frac{\rho_0g}{R}.\qquad\text{(xi)}\quad\text{(1.0 point)}
$$

**3.6)** Use equations (xi) and (viii) to obtain

$$
P(r)=P_{\mathrm a}+\left(\frac43\frac{\rho_0g}{R}\right)\cdot(R^2-r^2),\qquad\text{(xii)}
$$

$$
\frac{dP(r)}{dr}=-\left(\frac83\frac{\rho_0g}{R}\right)\cdot r.\qquad\text{(xiii)}\quad\text{(0.8 point)}
$$

Then use (v) to calculate the total mass-rate of vaporization $Q\rho_{\mathrm V}$ at $r=R$:

$$
Q\rho_{\mathrm V}=\left(\frac{2\pi b^3R}{12\eta}\right)\left(\frac83\frac{\rho_0g}{R}\right)R\rho_{\mathrm V}=\left(\frac{4\pi\rho_{\mathrm V}\rho_0gR}{9\eta}\right)b^3
$$

$$
=\left(\frac{4\pi\rho_{\mathrm V}\rho_0gR}{9\eta}\right)\left(\frac{9\eta\mathcal KR\Delta T}{4\rho_0\rho_{\mathrm V}\ell g}\right)^{3/4}
$$

$$
=\left(\frac{4\pi^4\mathcal K^3\rho_{\mathrm V}\rho_0g(\Delta T)^3}{9\eta\ell^3}\right)^{1/4}\cdot R^{7/4}=\beta R^{7/4}.\qquad\text{(xiv)}\quad\text{(1.2 points)}
$$

**3.7)** The life-time $(\tau)$ of the drop is to be found from

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac23\pi R^3\rho_0\right)=-Q\rho_{\mathrm V}=-\beta R^{7/4},
$$

$$
R^{1/4}\frac{dR}{dt}=-\frac\beta{2\pi\rho_0},
$$

$$
\int_R^0R^{1/4}\,dR=-\int_0^\tau\frac\beta{2\pi\rho_0}\,dt,\quad\text{(1.0 point)}
$$

$$
\tau=\frac{8\pi\rho_0}{5\beta}R^{5/4}=\frac85\left(\frac{9\eta\rho_0^3\ell^3}{4\mathcal K^3\rho_{\mathrm V}g(\Delta T)^3}\right)^{1/4}\cdot R^{5/4}.\quad\text{(1.0 point)}
$$
