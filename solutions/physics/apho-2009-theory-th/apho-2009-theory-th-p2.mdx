## Условие

A metallic disc of radius $a$ mounted on a slender axle is rotating with a constant angular velocity $\omega$ inside a long solenoid of inductance $L$ whose two ends are connected to the rotating disc by two brush contacts as shown. The total resistance of the whole circuit is $R$. A small magnetic disturbance can initiate the growth of an induced electromotive force across the terminals P, Q.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '57%', '--fig-max': '365px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/apho-2009-theory-th/p2-fig1.png" alt="A vertical axle rotating with angular velocity ω passes through a helical solenoid; a disc of radius a is mounted on the axle inside the solenoid. Labels: ω, a, P, Q, brush, long uniform solenoid of N turns, length ℓ." width="1142" height="1307" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

**2.1)** Write down the differential equation for $i(t)$, the current through the circuit. Express your answer in terms of $L, R,$ and the induced e.m.f. $(\mathcal{E})$ across the terminals P and Q. **[1 т.]**

**2.2)** What is the value of the magnetic flux density $(B)$ in terms of $i, N, \ell,$ and the permeability of free space $\mu_0$? Ignore the magnetic field generated by the disc and the axle. **[1,5 т.]**

**2.3)** What is the expression for the induced e.m.f. $(\mathcal{E})$ in terms of $\mu_0, N, a, \ell, i,$ and the angular velocity $\omega$? **[2 т.]**

**2.4)** Solve the equation in question 2.1 for current at any time $t$ in terms of the initial current $i(0)$, and other parameters. **[1,5 т.]**

**2.5)** What is the minimum value of the angular velocity that will permit the current to grow? Give your answers in terms of $R, \mu_0, N, a,$ and $\ell$. **[2 т.]**

**2.6)** In order to maintain a certain steady angular velocity $\omega$, what must be the value of torque applied to the axle at the instant $t$? **[2 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи решението от архива">

### A Self-excited Magnetic Dynamo — archive solution original

**2.1)**

$$
L\frac{di}{dt}+Ri=\mathcal E.\qquad\text{(i)}\quad\text{(1.0 point)}
$$

**2.2)** If the length $\ell$ of solenoid is much greater than its diameter, then the magnetic flux density in the inside mid-section of the solenoid is given by

$$
B=\frac{\mu_0Ni}{\ell}.\qquad\text{(ii)}\quad\text{(1.5 points)}
$$

**2.3)**

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '72.5%', '--fig-max': '464px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/apho-2009-theory-th/p2-sol-fig1.png" alt="Original2.3 rotating disc electric-field derivation diagram: disc/brush/O/P,a/r,omega/omega r arrows, B directed out of plane of paper." width="1451" height="625" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


The electric field intensity $(E)$ at distance $r$ from the centre of the axle is $E=B\omega r$ pointing towards the rim of the disc. The induced e.m.f. $(\mathcal E)$ between terminals P and Q is given by

$$
\mathcal E=\int_{r=0}^{a}B\omega r\,dr=\frac12B\omega a^2=\frac{\mu_0Ni\omega a^2}{2\ell}.\qquad\text{(iii)}\quad\text{(2.0 points)}
$$

**2.4)** By combining the results in 2.1) and 2.3) we get

$$
L\frac{di}{dt}+Ri=\left(\frac{\mu_0Na^2\omega}{2\ell}\right)i,
$$

$$
\frac{di}{dt}=+\frac1L\left(\frac{\mu_0Na^2\omega}{2\ell}-R\right)i\equiv+\gamma i.\quad\text{(0.5 point)}
$$

Therefore

$$
i(t)=i(0)e^{\gamma t},\qquad\text{(iv)}\quad\text{(1.0 point)}
$$

where

$$
\gamma\equiv\frac1L\left(\frac{\mu_0Na^2\omega}{2\ell}-R\right).
$$

**2.5)** In order that the current $i(t)$ will grow, the value of $\gamma$ must be positive otherwise the current will gradually decay.

$$
\frac{\mu_0Na^2\omega}{2\ell}-R\ge0,\quad\text{(1.5 points)}
$$

$$
\omega_{\min}=\frac{2\ell R}{\mu_0Na^2}.\qquad\text{(v)}\quad\text{(0.5 point)}
$$

**2.6)**

#### Method 1

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '67.9%', '--fig-max': '435px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/apho-2009-theory-th/p2-sol-fig2.png" alt="Original2.6 Method1 rotating disc torque diagram: sliding contact/O/M,a/r,i delta r/delta f arrows and B directed out of plane of paper." width="1359" height="634" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


At the instant $t$ the current is given in 4) as $i(t)=i(0)e^{\gamma t}$.

The magnetic force $\delta f$ on the current element $i\delta r$ is $\delta f=Bi\delta r$. (1.0 point)

The torque $\delta\tau=r\delta f=Bir\delta r$ opposes the rotation of the disc. The total torque

$$
\tau=\int_{r=0}^{r=a}Bir\,dr=\frac12Bia^2,
$$

$$
\tau=\frac{\mu_0Na^2}{2\ell}i^2=\frac{\mu_0Na^2}{2\ell}i^2(0)e^{+2\gamma t}.\qquad\text{(vi)}\quad\text{(1.0 point)}
$$

In order to maintain the angular velocity of the disc at a steady value we must apply a turning torque of equal magnitude and of opposite direction to that of (vi).

#### Method 2

$$
\tau\omega=i^2R+\frac{d}{dt}(\text{magnetic energy in solenoid})\quad\text{(1.0 point)}
$$

$$
=i^2R+L\frac{di}{dt}i,
$$

$$
\tau=\frac{\mu_0Na^2}{2\ell}i^2=\frac{\mu_0Na^2}{2\ell}i^2(0)e^{+2\gamma t}.\quad\text{(1.0 point)}
$$
