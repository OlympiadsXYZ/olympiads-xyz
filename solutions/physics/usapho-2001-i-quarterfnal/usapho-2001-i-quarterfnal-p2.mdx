## Условие

Two twin asteroids of identical mass $M$ are located a distance $r$ apart. A probe of mass $m$ (with $m\ll M$) is released from rest a distance $A$ (with $A\ll r$) from the center of mass of the two-body asteroid system. The probe is an equal distance from both asteroids. Assume that the probe does not affect the separation distance $r$ and that all other objects in the universe are too far away to exert a significant gravitational force. The asteroids must be orbiting their common center of mass in order to maintain a constant distance, but ignore any effects of orbiting in this problem. For both parts, clearly justify any approximations that you are making and give your answers in terms of $r$, $A$, $G$, $M$, and $m$.

**a** How long will it take for the probe to reach the center of mass? **[17 т.]**

**b** What will be the speed of the probe as it passes through the center of mass of the asteroid system? **[8 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a** $t=\frac{\pi}{8}\sqrt{\frac{r^3}{GM}}$
- **b** $v_{max}=4A\sqrt{\frac{GM}{r^3}}$

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

(If a student's only error is using $r$ instead of $r/2$ as the asteroid to center of mass distance, deduct only one point for part a) and one point for part b).)

**a.** Let $x$ = the distance of the probe from the center of mass of the asteroid system. The diagram below is not to scale. The distance $x$ has been greatly exaggerated. The probe is released from $A=x_{max}$. Diagram [3]

*[Probe displaced from the midpoint between two asteroids and the two gravitational forces]*

The magnitude of the force $F$ that each asteroid exerts on the probe is

$$
F=\frac{GMm}{(r/2)^2+x^2}.\qquad\text{Eq. 2-1}
$$

[2]

The total force on the probe is in the negative $x$-direction and equal to [1]

$$
F_x=-2F\sin\theta\qquad\text{Eq. 2-2}
$$

[1]

with

$$
\sin\theta=\frac{x}{\sqrt{(r/2)^2+x^2}}\qquad\text{Eq. 2-3}
$$

[1]

combining Eqs. 2-1, 2-2, 2-3

$$
F_x=-2G\frac{Mm}{(r/2)^2+x^2}\frac{x}{\sqrt{(r/2)^2+x^2}}=-\frac{2GMmx}{\left((r/2)^2+x^2\right)^{3/2}}
$$

[1]

Since $x\le A\ll r$,

$$
\left((r/2)^2+x^2\right)^{3/2}=\left((r/2)^2\right)^{3/2}\left(1+(2x/r)^2\right)^{3/2}\approx(r/2)^3.
$$

[1]

With this approximation, $F_x$ becomes

$$
F_x\approx-\frac{16GMm}{r^3}x
$$

[1]

Comparing this with the defining equation of simple harmonic motion $F=-kx$ [1]

(Students should be given equivalent credit for a valid method using calculus.)

The motion is approximately simple harmonic with

$$
k=\frac{16GMm}{r^3}\qquad\text{Eq. 2-4}
$$

[1]

The period of simple harmonic motion is

$$
T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}\qquad\text{Eq. 2-5}
$$

[1]

Combining Eqs. 2-4 and 2-5

$$
T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{(16GMm/r^3)}}=\frac{2\pi}{4}\sqrt{\frac{r^3}{GM}}
$$

[1]

The time $t$ for the probe to reach the center of mass of the asteroid system is

$$
t=\frac{T}{4}
$$

[1]

$$
t=\frac{2\pi}{16}\sqrt{\frac{r^3}{GM}}=\frac{\pi}{8}\sqrt{\frac{r^3}{GM}}
$$

[1]

**b.** The center of mass of the asteroid system is the equilibrium position for the simple harmonic motion. The probe's velocity as it passes through the equilibrium position is

$$
v_{max}=\omega x_{max}=\omega A.
$$

[2]

$$
\omega=\frac{2\pi}{T}
$$

[2]

$$
\omega=\frac{2\pi}{(2\pi/4)\sqrt{r^3/GM}}=4\sqrt{\frac{GM}{r^3}}.
$$

[2] Then

$$
v_{max}=4A\sqrt{\frac{GM}{r^3}}.
$$

[2]
