## Условие

In the lower part of the earth’s atmosphere, the temperature decreases with increase of height. Choose the origin of the coordinate system at the ground level with the $y$-axis vertically upward and the $x$-axis horizontal. We assume a linear decrease of temperature such that the temperature at a height $y$ from the ground level is

$$
T(y)=T_0(1-by)
$$

where $T_0$ is the temperature at the ground level. The constant $b=0.023\ \mathrm{km^{-1}}$. We consider the propagation of sound in the $x$-$y$ plane. Ignore any attenuation, reflection, and diffraction of sound.

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи решението от архива">

### (a)

Speed of sound is

$$
v=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma RT}{m}}\qquad (1.1)
$$

where $m$ and $\gamma$ are the molar mass and adiabatic index of the gas respectively.

$$
v(y)=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma RT(y)}m}=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma RT_0}m}\sqrt{1-by}=v_0\sqrt{1-by}\qquad (1.2)
$$

Here $v_0=\sqrt{\gamma RT_0/m}$ is the speed of the sound at ground level.

### (b)

Consider the propogation of sound ‘ray’ from one medium to the other.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '33.9%', '--fig-max': '217px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/inpho-2019-question/p1-key-ray.png" alt="Entire native sound-ray refraction sketch with interface, dashed normal, incident/transmitted arrows, i1/i2 and theta1/theta2." width="678" height="685" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


From Snell’s law

$$
\frac{\sin i_2}{\sin i_1}=\frac{v_2}{v_1}\qquad (1.3)
$$

$$
\frac{\sin(\pi/2-\theta_2)}{\sin(\pi/2-\theta_1)}=\frac{\cos\theta_2}{\cos\theta_1}=\frac{v_2}{v_1}\qquad (1.4)
$$

$$
\frac{\cos\theta}{\cos\theta_0}=\frac{v(y)}{v_0}=\sqrt{1-by}\qquad (1.5)
$$

$$
\theta=\cos^{-1}\left[\cos\theta_0\sqrt{1-by}\right]\qquad (1.6)
$$

### (c)

$$
y=\frac1b\left(1-\frac{\cos^2\theta}{\cos^2\theta_0}\right)\qquad (1.7)
$$

$$
\frac{dy}{dx}=\tan\theta\qquad (1.8)
$$

$$
dx=\frac{2\cos\theta\sin\theta}{\tan\theta\,b\cos^2\theta_0}\,d\theta\qquad (1.9)
$$

$$
x=\int_0^x dx=\frac1{2b\cos^2\theta_0}\left[2(\theta-\theta_0)+\sin2\theta-\sin2\theta_0\right]\qquad (1.10)
$$

### (d)

For $y\le100\ \mathrm m$ and $\theta_0=0$, Eq. (1.6) gives $\theta\approx3^\circ$ (very small).

$$
y\approx\frac1b\left[1-\left(1-\frac{\theta^2}{2}\right)^2\right]=\frac1b\theta^2\qquad (1.11)
$$

$$
x\approx\frac1{2b}(2\theta+2\theta)=\frac{2\theta}b\qquad (1.12)
$$

$$
x^2=\frac{4y}b\qquad (1.13)
$$

$$
x(y=2)\approx590\ \mathrm m\qquad (1.14)
$$

Accepted range: $585$–$600\ \mathrm m$.
