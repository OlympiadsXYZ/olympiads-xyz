## Условие

Consider a particle of mass $m$ confined to a one dimensional box of length $L$. The particle moves in the box with momentum $p$ colliding elastically with the walls. We consider the quantum mechanics of this system. As far as possible, express your answers in terms of $\alpha=h^2/8m$.

**a)** At each energy state, the particle may be represented by a standing wave given by the de Broglie hypothesis. Express its wavelengths $\lambda_{\mathrm{dB}}$ in terms of $L$ in the $n$th energy state.

**Summary answer box:** $\lambda_{\mathrm{dB}}=$ [blank]. **[1 т.]**

**b)** Write the energy of the $n$th energy state, $E_n$.

**Summary answer box:** $E_n=$ [blank]. **[1 т.]**

**c)** Let there be $N$ (mass $m$) electrons in this box where $N$ is an even number. Obtain the expression for the lowest possible total energy $U_0$ of the system (e.g., the ground state energy of this $N$-particle system). Neglect coulombic interaction between the electrons.

**Summary answer box:** $U_0=$ [blank]. **[2 т.]**

**d)** Express the total energy $U_1$ in terms of $U_0$ and relevant quantities when the system is in the first excited state. Also express the total energy $U_2$ in terms of $U_0$ and relevant quantities when the system is in the second excited state.

**Summary answer boxes:** $U_1=$ [blank]; $U_2=$ [blank]. **[3,5 т.]**

**e)** When the system is in the ground state, let the length of the box change slowly from $L$ to $L-\Delta L$. Obtain the magnitude of the force $F$ on each wall in terms of $U_0$, when $\Delta L\ll L$.

**Summary answer box:** $F=$ [blank]. **[1 т.]**

**f)** Assuming $N$ is large ($N\gg1$) obtain the ratio $r$ of $dU_0/dN$ to the energy level of the highest occupied ground state.

**Summary answer box:** $r=$ [blank]. **[1 т.]**

**g)** We assume once again that $N$ is large. Consider the possibility of the electrons forming a uniform continuum of length $L$ with constant linear density. Using dimensional analysis, calculate the gravitational energy of this system $U_G$ assuming that it depends on its total mass, universal gravitational constant $G$ and $L$. Equate this (attractive) energy to the (repulsive) energy $U_0(N)$. Obtain $L$ in terms of $N$ and related quantities.

**Summary answer boxes:** $U_G=$ [blank]; $L=$ [blank].

Detailed answers can be found on page numbers: [blank]. **[1,5 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи решението от архива">

### (a)

$$
\lambda_{\mathrm{dB}}=\frac{2L}{n}
$$

### (b)

$$
E_n=\frac{p^2}{2m}=\frac{n^2h^2}{8mL^2}=\frac{\alpha n^2}{L^2}\qquad (2.1)
$$

where $p=h/\lambda_{\mathrm{dB}}$.

### (c)

Highest occupied energy level is $n_{\max}=N/2$ as each energy level is occupied by two electrons.

$$
U_0=\sum_{n=1}^{N/2}E_n=\frac{2\alpha}{L^2}\sum_{n=1}^{N/2}n^2=\frac{\alpha N(N+1)(N+2)}{12L^2}\qquad (2.2)
$$

**Source note:** The first sum in printed Eq. (2.2) has no factor2 even though the adjacent text gives two electrons per level and the following sum has $2\alpha/L^2$. The complete printed equality is retained.

### (d)

1st excited state configuration is

$$
U_1=U_0-\frac\alpha{L^2}\left(\frac N2\right)^2+\frac\alpha{L^2}\left(\frac N2+1\right)^2\qquad (2.3)
$$

$$
=U_0+\frac\alpha{L^2}(N+1)\qquad (2.4)
$$

2nd excited state configuration is

$$
U_2=U_0-\frac\alpha{L^2}\left(\frac N2-1\right)^2+\frac\alpha{L^2}\left(\frac N2+1\right)^2\qquad (2.5)
$$

$$
=U_0+\frac\alpha{L^2}2N\qquad (2.6)
$$

### (e)

From the work-energy theorem,

$$
F\Delta L=U_{\mathrm{final}}-U_{\mathrm{initial}}\qquad (2.7)
$$

$$
=\frac{\alpha N(N+1)(N+2)}{12}\left[\frac1{(L-\Delta L)^2}-\frac1{L^2}\right]\qquad (2.8)
$$

$$
F\approx\frac{\alpha N(N+1)(N+2)2}{12L^3}=\frac{2U_0}{L}\qquad (2.9)
$$

### (f)

$$
\frac{dU_0}{dN}=\frac\alpha{12L^2}\frac d{dN}(N^3+3N^2+2N)\qquad (2.10)
$$

$$
\approx\frac\alpha{12L^2}3N^2\qquad (2.11)
$$

Highest occupied ground state corresponds to $N/2$, for which the energy level is $\alpha N^2/4L^2$. Thus

$$
r=1\qquad (2.12)
$$

### (g)

$$
[U_G]=\frac{GM^2}L=\frac{GN^2m^2}L\quad\text{or}\quad-\frac{GN^2m^2}L
$$

For large $N$, $U_0\approx\alpha N^3/12L^2$.

$$
\frac{GN^2m^2}L=\frac{\alpha N^3}{12L^2}\qquad (2.13)
$$

$$
L=\frac{\alpha N^3}{12GM^2}=\frac{Nh^2}{96Gm^3}\qquad (2.14)
$$
