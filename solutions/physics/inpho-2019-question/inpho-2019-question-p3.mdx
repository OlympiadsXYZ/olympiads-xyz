## Условие

A chain of length $l$ and linear density $\lambda$ hangs from a horizontal support with both ends A and B fixed to a horizontal support as shown. The two fixed ends are close to each other. At time $t=0$ the end A is released. All vertical distances ($x$) are measured with respect to the horizontal support with the downward direction taken as positive (A and B are initially at $x=0$).

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи решението от архива">

### (a)

Let total mass of the chain to be $M=\lambda l$.

The mass of the left side of the chain $=\lambda\left(\frac{l+x}{2}\right)$.

CM of the left side of the chain $=\left(\frac{l+x}{4}\right)$.

The mass of the right side of the chain $=\lambda\left(\frac{l-x}{2}\right)$.

CM of the right side of the chain $=\left(x+\frac{l-x}{4}\right)$.

$$
x_{\mathrm{CM}}=\frac{\lambda\left(\frac{l+x}2\right)\left(\frac{l+x}4\right)+\lambda\left(\frac{l-x}2\right)\left(x+\frac{l-x}4\right)}{\lambda l}\qquad (3.1)
$$

$$
\lambda l x_{\mathrm{CM}}=\frac{\lambda l^2}4+\frac{\lambda lx}2-\frac{\lambda x^2}4\qquad (3.2)
$$

$$
P=\lambda l\dot x_{\mathrm{CM}}=\lambda\left(\frac{l-x}2\right)\dot x\qquad (3.3)
$$

### (b)

$$
\ddot x=g\qquad (3.4)
$$

$$
\dot x=gt=\sqrt{2gx}\qquad (3.5)
$$

$$
\dot P=Mg-T\qquad (3.6)
$$

From Eq. (3.3)

$$
\dot P=\frac\lambda2\left[\ddot x(l-x)-\dot x^2\right]\qquad (3.7)
$$

$$
\frac\lambda2[gl-3gx]=Mg-T\qquad (3.8)
$$

$$
T(x)=\frac{Mg}2\left(1+\frac{3x}l\right)=\frac{\lambda lg}2\left(1+\frac{3x}l\right)\qquad (3.9)
$$

$$
T(x=l)=2\lambda lg\qquad (3.10)
$$

Experimentally, the value of tension is found to be different from the above result. We adopt an alternative approach assuming conservation of mechanical energy.

### (c)

In general, $U=-\lambda lgx_{\mathrm{CM}}$. Using Eq. (3.2)

$$
U=\frac{-\lambda g}4(l^2+2lx-x^2)\qquad (3.11)
$$

### (d)

From Eq. (3.3), kinetic energy of the chain

$$
K(x)=\frac12\lambda\left(\frac{l-x}2\right)\dot x^2\qquad (3.12)
$$

As the total energy is conserved,

$$
U(x)+K(x)=U(t=0)\qquad (3.13)
$$

$$
\dot x^2=\left[\frac{g(2lx-x^2)}{(l-x)}\right]^{1/2}\qquad (3.14)
$$

$$
\dot x=\left[\frac{g(2lx-x^2)}{(l-x)}\right]^{1/2}\qquad (3.15)
$$

**Source note:** Printed Eq. (3.14) has $\dot x^2$ on the left and a square-root exponent on the right; the next Eq. (3.15) repeats that right-hand side with $\dot x$ on the left. Both original equations are retained.

### (e)

$$
\frac d{dt}\dot x^2=2\dot x\ddot x
$$

Substituing Eq. (3.15) in the above equation yields

$$
\ddot x=g+\frac{g(2lx-x^2)}{2(l-x)^2}\qquad (3.16)
$$

Combining Eqs. (3.6), (3.7) and (3.16)

$$
T=\lambda lg-\frac\lambda2\left[\ddot x(l-x)-\dot x^2\right]\qquad (3.17)
$$

$$
=\frac{\lambda g}{4(l-x)}[2l^2+2lx-3x^2]\qquad (3.18)
$$
