## Условие

A pair of long parallel metallic rails of negligible resistance and separation $w$ are placed horizontally. A horizontal metal rod (dark thick line) of mass $M$ and resistance $R$ is placed perpendicularly onto the rails at one end (as shown). A uniform magnetic field $B$ exists perpendicular to the plane of the paper (pointing into the page). One end of the rail track is connected to a key ($K$) and a capacitor of capacitance $C$ charged to voltage $V_0$. At $t=0$ the key is closed. Neglect friction and self inductance of the loop.

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи решението от архива">

### (a)

At $t=0$, $Q_0=CV_0$. At any instant, $\dot Q=-I$, where $Q$ is the charge on capacitor and $I$ is the current in RC circuit. From Newton’s second law and Lorentz force,

$$
m\frac{dv}{dt}=IwB\qquad (5.1)
$$

$$
=-\dot QwB\qquad (5.2)
$$

Induced emf in the circuit is $Bwv$. From KVL,

$$
\frac QC=IR+Bwv\qquad (5.3)
$$

$$
=-\dot QR+Bwv\qquad (5.4)
$$

Differentiating Eq. (5.4) and using Eq. (5.2)

$$
\ddot Q=-\frac1{RC}\dot Q-\frac{B^2w^2}{mR}\dot Q\qquad (5.5)
$$

$$
=-\dot Q\left[\frac1{RC}+\frac{B^2w^2}{mR}\right]\qquad (5.6)
$$

$$
\ddot Q=-\frac{\dot Q}\tau\quad\text{where}\quad\frac1\tau=\frac1{RC}+\frac{B^2w^2}{mR}\qquad (5.7)
$$

$$
\log\left(\frac{\dot Q}{\dot Q_0}\right)=-\frac t\tau\quad\text{where}\quad\dot Q_0=I(t=0)=-\frac{V_0}R\qquad (5.8)
$$

$$
\dot Q=-\frac{V_0}R e^{-t/\tau}\qquad (5.9)
$$

Combining Eqs. (5.2) and (5.9)

$$
m\frac{dv}{dt}=\frac{V_oBw}R e^{-t/\tau}\qquad (5.10)
$$

$$
\int_0^{v_{\mathrm{final}}}m\,dv=\int_0^\infty\frac{V_0Bw}R e^{-t/\tau}\,dt\qquad (5.11)
$$

$$
mv_{\mathrm{final}}=\left.\frac{BwV_0}R(-\tau)e^{-t/\tau}\right|_0^\infty\qquad (5.12)
$$

$$
=\frac{BwV_0\tau}R\qquad (5.13)
$$

$$
v_{\mathrm{final}}=\frac{BwCV_0}{m+B^2w^2c}\qquad (5.14)
$$

**Source note:** The native question uses rod mass $M$; the native derivation uses $m$. Printed Eq. (5.8) identifies $\dot Q_0$ with $I(t=0)$ although the preceding text states $\dot Q=-I$. Eq. (5.10) prints $V_o$ with letter $o$, and Eq. (5.14) prints lowercase $c$ in its denominator while the capacitance elsewhere is $C$. All original notation and equalities are retained.

### (b)

$$
r=\frac{mv_{\max}^2/2}{CV_0^2/2}\qquad (5.15)
$$

$$
=\frac{x}{(1+x)^2}\quad\text{where}\quad x=\frac{B^2w^2C}m\qquad (5.16)
$$

$r$ has a maximum value of $\frac14$ at $x=1$.

$$
r_{\max}=1/4\qquad (5.17)
$$

### (c)

For $r_{\max}$,

$$
\frac{B^2w^2C}m=1\Rightarrow B=31.6\ \mathrm T\qquad (5.18)
$$

$$
v_{\max}=1.58\times10^3\ \mathrm{m/s}\qquad (5.19)
$$
