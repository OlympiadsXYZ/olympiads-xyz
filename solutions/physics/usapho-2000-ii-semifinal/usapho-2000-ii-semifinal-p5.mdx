A hollow spherical shell of radius $b$ and negligible thickness is constructed out of insulating material with total charge $-Q$. A second similar shell has radius $c$ where $c>2b$ and total charge $+Q$. Each shell has its charge distributed uniformly over its surface. Assume the electrostatic potential is zero at infinity. As shown at the right, the symbols I, II, and III may be used to denote the regions inside the smaller shell, between the shells, and outside the larger shell, respectively. Express your answers to the following in terms of given quantities and known constants. You may use either spherical or Cartesian coordinates.

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-a">

### a. (10 т.)

Because the charge distribution is uniform, there is spherical symmetry and we can use Gauss’s Law to calculate the electrostatic field. This yields $\vec E=kq_{enc}\hat e_R/R^2$, where $R=\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}$ $q_{enc}$ is the charge enclosed by a Gaussian surface of radius R, and $\hat e_R$ is a unit vector in the $R$ direction.

In region I, $R<b$ and $q_{enc}=0$, so $\vec E_I=0$.

In region II, $c>R>b$ and $q_{enc}=-Q$, so

$$
\vec E_{II}=-\frac{kQ}{R^2}\hat e_R,\quad\text{or }E_{II}=\frac{kQ}{R^2}\text{ directed radially inward}.\qquad (B1.1)
$$

In region III, $R>c$ and $q_{enc}=-Q+Q=0$, so $\vec E_{III}=0$.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-b">

### b. (10 т.)

Region III: Since the field is zero, the potential is a constant throughout the region. Its value at any point in III is the same as its value at infinity $V_{III}=0$.

In region II,

$$
V_{II}(R)-V_{II}(c)=-\int_c^R\vec E\cdot d\vec l.
$$

Using (B1.1), $d\vec l=(-\hat e_R)(-dR)=\hat e_RdR$ and $V_{II}(c)=V_{III}(c)=0$,

$$
V_{II}(R)=\int_c^R\frac{kQ}{r^2}\,dr=\left.-\frac{kQ}{r}\right|_{r=c}^{R}=\frac{kQ}{c}-\frac{kQ}{R}=kQ\left(\frac1c-\frac1R\right).
$$

In region I the field is zero, so the potential is constant and equals the potential at $R=b$:

$$
V_I=kQ\left(\frac1c-\frac1b\right).
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-c">

### c. (10 т.)

In each region the total field can be obtained by superposition, adding vectorially the fields due to the individual spheres. $\vec E=\vec E_{+Q}+\vec E_{-Q}$.

Inside each uniformly charged sphere the field due to that sphere is zero.

Region I: This region is inside both spheres so both contributions are zero and $E_I=0$.

Region II: The field due to the $+Q$ sphere is zero in this region. The field due to the $-Q$ is equivalent to that due to a $-Q$ point charge at $x=0$, $y=0$, $z=b$. Letting $\vec R=x\hat i+y\hat j+(z-b)\hat k$ represent the vector from this point charge to the arbitrary field point, we have

$$
\vec E_{II}=-\frac{kQ\vec R}{R^3}=\frac{kQ(x\hat i+y\hat j+(z-b)\hat k)}{[x^2+y^2+(z-b)^2]^{3/2}}.
$$

Region III: In this region both spheres contribute to the field. Adding to the previous result the contribution equivalent to a $+Q$ point charge at the origin, yields

$$
\vec E_{III}=-\frac{kQ(x\hat i+y\hat j+(z-b)\hat k)}{[x^2+y^2+(z-b)^2]^{3/2}}+\frac{kQ(x\hat i+y\hat j+z\hat k)}{[x^2+y^2+z^2]^{3/2}}.
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-d">

### d. (10 т.)

Region III: Since the field here is the equivalent to that due to two point charges, so is the potential.

$$
V_{III}=kQ\left(\frac1{\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}}-\frac1{\sqrt{x^2+y^2+(z-b)^2}}\right).
$$

Region II: In this region, the contribution due to the inner sphere is equivalent to that due to a point charge. The contribution due to the outer sphere is constant. To find this value, equate across the boundary with Region III.

$$
V_{II}=kQ\left(\frac1c-\frac1{\sqrt{x^2+y^2+(z-b)^2}}\right).
$$

Region I: Since the field is zero, the potential is constant. To find this value, equate across the boundary with Region II.

$$
V_I=kQ\left(\frac1c-\frac1b\right).
$$

</ProblemSection>
