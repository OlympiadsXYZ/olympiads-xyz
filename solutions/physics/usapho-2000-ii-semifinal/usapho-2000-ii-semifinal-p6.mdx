## Условие

<nav className="problem-sections-toc" aria-label="Части на задачата">

**В тази задача**

- [h–i](#parts-h-i)

</nav>

We shall model a car as a uniform rectangular block of length $L$ and height $h$ with mass $M$ that has struts of length $R$ that rest on the frictionless axles of two wheels of radius $R$, one front wheel and one rear wheel. Each wheel has a mass $m$ and a uniform mass density. The driver can exert a friction force at the top of the front wheel to slow down the car. See the diagram below.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '81%', '--fig-max': '518px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-2000-ii-semifinal/p6-fig1.png" alt="Car block M of length L and height h, wheels m and radius R, velocity and front-wheel friction brake." width="1620" height="763" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


Assume that the two identical wheels have a coefficient of static friction $\mu$ with the ground. The driver applies a braking force that causes the vehicle to decelerate with a deceleration of magnitude $a$.

<ProblemSection id="parts-a-g">

### a–g

Express your answers to the following in terms of given quantities and known constants.

**a.** Find $f_R$ the frictional force of the ground on the rear wheel. **[4 т.]**

**b.** Find $F_{CR}$ the horizontal component of the force exerted by the car on the axle of the rear wheel. **[4 т.]**

**c.** Find $f_F$ the frictional force of the ground on the front wheel. **[4 т.]**

**d.** Find $f_B$ the force of the brake that is applied to the front wheel. **[4 т.]**

**e.** Find $F_{CF}$ the horizontal component of the force exerted by the car on the axle of the front wheel. **[4 т.]**

**f.** Find $n_F$ and $n_R$ the normal forces exerted by the ground on the front and rear wheels. **[8 т.]**

**g.** For each wheel, find the maximum possible deceleration of the car before that wheel starts to slip. **[4 т.]**

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="parts-h-i">

### h–i

If $L=2.50$ m, $h=1.50$ m, $R=0.300$ m, $M=6000$ kg, $m=200$ kg, $\mu=0.600$,

**h.** Numerically evaluate the magnitude of the maximum possible deceleration of the car in terms of the acceleration of gravity, $g$. **[4 т.]**

**i.** Numerically evaluate the ratio of the normal force of the ground on the front wheel to that on the rear wheel at this maximum deceleration. **[4 т.]**

</ProblemSection>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-a">

### a. (4 т.)

For the wheel $I=\tfrac12mR^2$ and $a=\alpha R$ since it is rolling without slipping. The wheel is turning clockwise. It’s decelerating so the force producing the torque must be towards the front of the car. For the rotational motion of the rear wheel about its axis

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '19.9%', '--fig-max': '128px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-2000-ii-semifinal/p6-fig2.png" alt="Rear-wheel rotation with angular velocity, angular deceleration and ground friction." width="399" height="367" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


$$
I\alpha=f_RR
$$

$$
\tfrac12mR^2\frac aR=f_RR
$$

Therefore

$$
f_R=\tfrac12ma\qquad\text{toward the front.}
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-b">

### b. (4 т.)

Now consider the translational deceleration of the rear wheel

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '20.9%', '--fig-max': '134px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-2000-ii-semifinal/p6-fig3.png" alt="Rear-wheel translation with axle force, friction and acceleration." width="419" height="262" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


$$
ma=F_{CR}-f_R
$$

where we have chosen the acceleration direction to be positive. Therefore

$$
F_{CR}=ma+f_R=ma+\tfrac12ma=\tfrac32ma\qquad\text{towards the rear.}
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-c">

### c. (4 т.)

Consider the total external force acting on the car-wheel combination which has total mass $M+2m$

$$
f_F-f_R=(M+2m)a
$$

$$
f_F=f_R+(M+2m)a=\tfrac12ma+(M+2m)a=\left(M+\tfrac52m\right)a\qquad\text{towards rear.}
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-d">

### d. (4 т.)

Considering the rotational motion of the front wheel about its axis

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '22.4%', '--fig-max': '144px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-2000-ii-semifinal/p6-fig4.png" alt="Front-wheel rotation showing brake and ground friction." width="450" height="347" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


$$
I\alpha=(f_B-f_F)R
$$

$$
\tfrac12mR^2\frac aR=(f_B-f_F)R
$$

Solving for $f_B$

$$
f_B=\tfrac12ma+f_F=\tfrac12ma+\left(M+\tfrac52m\right)a=(M+3m)a
$$

towards the rear.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-e">

### e. (4 т.)

Consider the total external force acting on the front wheel

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '21.9%', '--fig-max': '140px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-2000-ii-semifinal/p6-fig5.png" alt="Front-wheel translation showing brake, ground friction, axle force and acceleration." width="439" height="291" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


$$
f_F+f_B-F_{CF}=ma
$$

$$
F_{CF}=f_F+f_B-ma=\left(M+\tfrac52m\right)a+(M+3m)a-ma=\left(2M+\tfrac92m\right)a
$$

towards the front.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-f">

### f. (8 т.)

We will first calculate the normal forces exerted by the axles on the carriage part of the car. Call these normal forces $N_R$ and $N_F$. Using Newton’s third law to find the horizontal forces exerted on the carriage by the axles and the brake, we have the force diagram at the right.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '59.8%', '--fig-max': '383px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-2000-ii-semifinal/p6-fig6.png" alt="Carriage free-body diagram with length, heights, weight, normal and horizontal forces." width="1197" height="667" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


Consider the vertical forces acting on the block part of the car. There is no acceleration in the vertical direction, so these must sum to zero. Or

$$
N_F+N_R=Mg.\qquad (B2.1)
$$

Consider the torques acting on the block part of the car. It is not rotating, so the total torque about the center of mass must equal zero.

$$
F_{CR}\left(\frac h2+R\right)+f_B\frac h2+N_F\frac L2-F_{CF}\left(\frac h2+R\right)-N_R\frac L2=0
$$

Rearranging terms

$$
N_F-N_R=\frac2L\left[(F_{CF}-F_{CR})\left(\frac h2+R\right)-f_B\frac h2\right]
$$

Substituting the known $F_{CF}$, $F_{CR}$, and $f_B$

$$
N_F-N_R=\frac2L\left[\left(2M+\frac92m-\frac32m\right)a\left(\frac h2+R\right)-(M+3m)a\frac h2\right]
$$

$$
N_F-N_R=\frac aL[(2M+3m)(h+2R)-(M+3m)h]=\frac aL[hM+2R(2M+3m)]\qquad (B2.2)
$$

Adding (B2.1) and (B2.2)

$$
2N_F=\frac aL[hM+2R(2M+3m)]+Mg
$$

$$
N_F=\frac12Mg+\frac{ah}{2L}M+\frac{aR}{L}(2M+3m)
$$

And for the rear axle

$$
N_R=Mg-N_F=\frac12Mg-\frac{ah}{2L}M-\frac{aR}{L}(2M+3m)
$$

These are the upward forces exerted by the axles on the carriage. Consider now the vertical forces acting on the wheel, shown in the diagram at the right. Since there is no vertical acceleration these must sum to zero.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '13.8%', '--fig-max': '89px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-2000-ii-semifinal/p6-fig7.png" alt="Front-wheel vertical forces nF, NF and mg." width="277" height="354" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


$$
n_F-N_F-mg=0
$$

Finally

$$
n_F=N_F+mg=\frac12(M+2m)g+\frac{ah}{2L}M+\frac{aR}{L}(2M+3m)\qquad (B2.3)
$$

Similarly for the rear wheel

$$
n_R=\frac12(M+2m)g-\frac{ah}{2L}M-\frac{aR}{L}(2M+3m)\qquad (B2.4)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-g">

### g. (4 т.)

For each wheel the maximum frictional force corresponding to the maximum deceleration is $\mu n$. For the rear wheel $f_R=\mu n_R$.

Using the results of Part a and Part f.

$$
\frac12ma=\mu\left[\frac12(M+2m)g-\frac{ah}{2L}M-\frac{aR}{L}(2M+3m)\right]
$$

Solving for a

$$
a_{R\max}=\frac{\mu(M+2m)gL}{mL+\mu[Mh+(2M+3m)2R]}\qquad (B2.5)
$$

For the front wheel $f_F=\mu n_F$

$$
\left(M+\frac52m\right)a=\mu\left[\frac12(M+2m)g+\frac{ah}{2L}M+\frac{aR}{L}(2M+3m)\right]
$$

$$
a_{F\max}=\frac{\mu(M+2m)gL}{(2M+5m)L-\mu[Mh+(2M+3m)2R]}\qquad (B2.6)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-h">

### h. (4 т.)

Putting the given values into (B2.5) and (B2.6), we have

$$
a_{R\max}=\frac{0.6(6000\text{ kg}+2(200\text{ kg}))g(2.5\text{ m})}{(200\text{ kg})(2.5\text{ m})+0.6[(6000\text{ kg})(1.5\text{ m})+(2(6000\text{ kg})+3(200\text{ kg}))2(0.3\text{ m})]}
$$

$$
a_{R\max}=0.920g
$$

$$
a_{F\max}=\frac{0.6(6000\text{ kg}+2(200\text{ kg}))g(2.5\text{ m})}{(2(6000\text{ kg})+5(200\text{ kg}))(2.5\text{ m})-0.6[(6000\text{ kg})(1.5\text{ m})+(2(6000\text{ kg})+3(200\text{ kg}))2(0.3\text{ m})]}
$$

$$
a_{F\max}=0.425g
$$

therefore the maximum deceleration the car can have is $a_{\text{car}\max}=0.425g$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-i">

### i. (4 т.)

Forming the ratio and substituting in the given values

$$
\frac{n_F}{n_R}=\frac{\frac12(M+2m)g+\frac{ah}{2L}M+\frac{aR}{L}(2M+3m)}{\frac12(M+2m)g-\frac{ah}{2L}M-\frac{aR}{L}(2M+3m)}=\frac{(M+2m)gL+ahM+2aR(2M+3m)}{(M+2m)gL-ahM-2aR(2M+3m)}
$$

$$
\frac{n_F}{n_R}=\frac{(6000\text{ kg}+2(200\text{ kg}))g(2.5\text{ m})+(0.425g)(1.5\text{ m})(6000\text{ kg})+2(0.425g)(0.3\text{ m})(2(6000\text{ kg})+3(200\text{ kg}))}{(6000\text{ kg}+2(200\text{ kg}))g(2.5\text{ m})-(0.425g)(1.5\text{ m})(6000\text{ kg})-2(0.425g)(0.3\text{ m})(2(6000\text{ kg})+3(200\text{ kg}))}
$$

$$
\frac{n_F}{n_R}=\frac{(6400\text{ kg})(2.5\text{ m})+(0.425)(1.5\text{ m})(6000\text{ kg})+2(0.425)(0.3\text{ m})(12600\text{ kg})}{(6400\text{ kg})(2.5\text{ m})-(0.425)(1.5\text{ m})(6000\text{ kg})-2(0.425)(0.3\text{ m})(12600\text{ kg})}
$$

$$
\frac{n_F}{n_R}=2.57
$$

</ProblemSection>


</Spoiler>
