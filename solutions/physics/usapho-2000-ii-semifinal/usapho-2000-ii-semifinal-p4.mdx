## Условие

Two movable wedges of mass $M$ meet the horizontal plane smoothly. They are free to slide without friction on the plane. See accompanying diagram. A coin of mass $m$ is released from rest at the height $h$ and slides down the left hand wedge. Assume that the coin always slides without friction. Express your answers to the following in terms of given quantities and known constants.

**a.** What is the maximum height the coin will rise on the right-hand wedge? **[10 т.]**

**b.** For what minimum mass $M$ will the coin again contact the left-hand wedge? **[10 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

Since friction is assumed non-existent, all collisions are elastic. Both momentum and mechanical energy is conserved.

Let $v$ be the speed of the coin as it leaves the left wedge, $V_L$ the speed of the left wedge, $H$ the maximum height of the coin on the right wedge, and $V$ the speed of the right wedge and coin at the highest point, when the coin is at rest relative to the wedge. In all of the following, velocities to the right will be positive and velocities to the left will be negative. Equating the energy and momentum of the initial condition (coin at height h) and final condition (coin has completely left left-hand wedge). Momentum conservation

$$
0=-MV_L+mv,\qquad V_L=\frac mM v.\qquad (A4.1)
$$

Conservation of energy gives

$$
mgh=\frac12MV_L^2+\frac12mv^2.
$$

Substituting A4.1,

$$
2mgh=M\left(\frac mMv\right)^2+mv^2=m\left(\frac mM+1\right)v^2.
$$

$$
v^2=\frac{2Mgh}{m-M}.\qquad (A4.2)
$$

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-a">

### a. (10 т.)

Equating the energy and momentum of the initial condition (coin with velocity v, right-hand wedge at rest) and final condition (coin at rest with respect to right-hand wedge). Momentum conservation and energy conservation

$$
mv=(m+M)V\qquad (A4.3)
$$

$$
\frac12mv^2=\frac12(m+M)V^2+mgH.\qquad (A4.4)
$$

Combining (a4.3) and (A4.4),

$$
mv^2=(m+M)\left(\frac m{m+M}v\right)^2+2mgH=\frac{m^2}{m+M}v^2+2mgH.
$$

$$
H=\frac1{2g}\left(1-\frac m{m+M}\right)v^2=\frac1{2g}\left(\frac M{m+M}\right)v^2.
$$

Substituting (A4.2),

$$
H=\frac1{2g}\left(\frac M{m+M}\right)\left(\frac{2Mgh}{m+M}\right)=\left(\frac M{m+M}\right)^2h.
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-b">

### b. (10 т.)

Let $v_F$ be the speed of the coin after leaving the right wedge and $V_R$ the speed of the right wedge after the coin has left it. For the coin to catch the left wedge, $v_F>V_L$. Conservation of momentum gives

$$
mv=MV_R-mv_F,\qquad V_R=\frac mM(v+v_F).
$$

Conservation of energy gives

$$
\frac12mv^2=\frac12MV_R^2+\frac12mv_F^2.
$$

Combining the last two equations and dividing by $m$ and multiplying by 2.

$$
v^2=\frac mM(v+v_F)^2+v_F^2,
$$

$$
Mv^2=m(v^2+2vv_F+v_F^2)+Mv_F^2,
$$

$$
0=(M+m)v_F^2+(2mv)v_F-(M-m)v^2.
$$

Solving the quadratic,

$$
v_F=\frac{-2mv\pm\sqrt{(2mv)^2+4(M+m)(M-m)v^2}}{2(M+m)}=\frac{-2mv\pm\sqrt{4M^2v^2}}{2(M+m)}=\frac{-mv\pm Mv}{M+m}.
$$

Only the plus sign gives a positive speed and a velocity in the correct direction.

$$
v_F=\frac{M-m}{M+m}v.
$$

For the coin to catch the left wedge,

$$
v_F>V_L,\qquad\frac{M-m}{M+m}v>\frac mMv.
$$

$$
(M-m)M>(M+m)m,\qquad M^2-2Mm-m^2>0.
$$

Solving the quadratic gives $M>\tfrac12(2m\pm\sqrt{4m^2+4m^2})$. Since M must be positive this becomes

$$
M>m(1+\sqrt2).
$$

</ProblemSection>
