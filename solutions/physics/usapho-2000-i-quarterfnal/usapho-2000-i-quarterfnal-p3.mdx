## Условие

A uniform thin rod of length $L$ and mass $M$ is free to swing in a vertical plane about a frictionless horizontal axis through its upper end. Express your answers to Parts (a) and (b) in terms of $M,L$, and $g$. Assume air resistance is negligible.

**a.** The rod is released from rest in a horizontal position. Find its angular velocity at the moment it reaches a vertical orientation. **[10 т.]**

**b.** Suppose at this moment its bottom end collides totally inelastically with a stationary point mass $M$ (the same mass as that of the rod). The point mass sticks to the rod. What is the angular velocity just after the collision? **[8 т.]**

**c.** Numerically evaluate the maximum angle (with respect to the vertical) the rod attains after the collision. **[7 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a.** $\omega=\sqrt{3g/L}$
- **b.** $\omega_F=\frac14\sqrt{3g/L}$
- **c.** 0.41 rad

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**a.** The rotational inertia of a uniform thin rod about its end is found using the parallel axis theorem:

$I=I_{cm}+M(L/2)^2=(\frac1{12})ML^2+(\frac14)ML^2=(\frac13)ML^2$ **[2]**

Using energy conservation: $K_0+U_0=K_F+U_F$ **[2]**

Let the lowest position of the rod’s center of mass ($L/2$ below the axis) be the zero of gravitational potential energy. Then $U_0=Mg(L/2)$ and $U_F=0$ **[1+1]**

For kinetic energy: $K_0=0$, $K_F=(1/2)I\omega^2$ **[1+1]**

Substitution into the energy conservation equation yields

$Mg(L/2)=(\frac12)(\frac13)ML^2\omega^2$. **[1]**

Dividing by $M,L$, and $1/2$

$g=(\frac13)L\omega^2$

or $\omega=\sqrt{\frac{3g}{L}}$ **[1]**

**b.** The collision is inelastic. Using angular momentum conservation

$I_0\omega_0=I_F\omega_F$ **[2]**

Only the rod is moving initially, so $I_0=(\frac13)ML^2$ and $\omega_0=\sqrt{\frac{3g}{L}}$ **[1+1]**

The final rotational inertia is the sum of the rod’s and point mass’s

$I_F=(\frac13)ML^2+ML^2=(\frac43)ML^2$ **[2]**

Therefore:

$$
\omega_F=\omega_0\frac{I_0}{I_F}=\sqrt{\frac{3g}{L}}\left(\frac{(\frac13)ML^2}{(\frac43)ML^2}\right)=\left(\frac14\right)\sqrt{\frac{3g}{L}}
$$

**[2]**

**c.** Once the collision is over, mechanical energy is again conserved. Equating total mechanical energy immediately after the collision and at the highest point

$K_0+U_0=K_F+U_F$

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '38.9%', '--fig-max': '249px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-2000-i-quarterfnal/p3-sol-fig1-astra-r6-20260925-v2.png" alt="Rod and attached point mass at angle theta, with vertical rises h and h-prime." width="778" height="492" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


At the highest point $K_F=0$ **[1]**

$$
K_0=(\tfrac12)I\omega^2=(\tfrac12)(\tfrac43)ML^2\left((\tfrac14)\sqrt{\frac{3g}{L}}\right)^2=(\tfrac18)MgL
$$

**[1]**

Choosing the lowest point as the zero of gravitational potential energy, $U_0=0$.

In swinging to an angle $\theta$, the center of mass of the rod (marked by an x in the diagram) has moved up a distance $h'=(L/2)(1-\cos\theta)$, **[1]**

While the point mass at the end has moved up $h=L(1-\cos\theta)$. **[1]**

Therefore the final potential energy is

$$
U_F=Mgh'+Mgh=Mg(L/2)(1-\cos\theta)+MgL(1-\cos\theta)=(3/2)MgL(1-\cos\theta).
$$

**[1]**

Substituting into the energy conservation equation

$(\frac18)MgL=(\frac32)MgL(1-\cos\theta)$ **[1]**

$(\frac1{12})=1-\cos\theta$

$\theta=\cos^{-1}(\frac{11}{12})=0.41\ rad$ **[1]**
