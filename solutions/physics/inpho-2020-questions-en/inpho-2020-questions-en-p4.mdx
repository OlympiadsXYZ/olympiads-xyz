## Условие

A sound source S is performing uniform circular motion with time period $T$. It is continuously emitting sound of a fixed frequency $f_0$. Two detectors 1 and 2 are placed somewhere in the same plane as the circular trajectory of the source. The frequency $f$, of the sound received by the two detectors is plotted as a function of time $t$ as shown below (the clocks of the two detectors are synchronized).

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи решението от архива">

### (a)

Note the time at the peak frequencies of any of the detectors. For detector 1, first peak is at $t=16\ \mathrm s$ and the second peak is at $t=40\ \mathrm s$. Hence the time period of source $T=24\ \mathrm s$.

### (b)

Couple of things can be easily seen from the graphs:

Time period of the source $T=24\ \mathrm s$.

Time difference between the maximum and minimum frequencies detected by detector 1 $=9\ \mathrm s$.

Time difference between the maximum and minimum frequencies detected by detector 2 $=9\ \mathrm s$.

Time difference between the maximum frequencies detected by detector 1 and 2 $=4\ \mathrm s$.

Maximum frequency detected by 1 $=1300\ \mathrm{Hz}$.

Minimum frequency detected by 1 $=800\ \mathrm{Hz}$.

Resolution of the graph is not enough to give the maximum and minimum frequencies detected by the detector 2. Let the source $S$ is moving in the circle of radius $R$. There are four possibilities:

1. Both detectors are outside the circle.
2. Both detectors are inside the circle.
3. One detector is inside and other is outside the circle.
4. One detector is either inside or outside the circle, and other detector is at $R$.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '24.2%', '--fig-max': '155px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/inpho-2020-questions-en/p4-key-angle.png" alt="Native source-detector angle sketch with S,D,phi and velocity arrow." width="484" height="215" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


When source is approaching the stationary detector $D$ is at an angle $\phi$, the frequency detected by $D$ is

$$
f=\frac{f_0}{1-\frac vc\cos\phi}
$$

Here $f_0$ is the frequency emitted by the source and $c$ is the speed of the sound. If we consider fourth possibility, we should have observed a sharp change in the plot when source is crossing the detector. Hence, we rule out this case.

When the detector $D_{\mathrm{out}}$ is outside the circle (see figure below):

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '67.6%', '--fig-max': '433px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/inpho-2020-questions-en/p4-key-outside.png" alt="Entire native outside-detector circle geometry: O,S,R,Do,Dout,phi_o,v,tangent/right angle and clockwise arrow." width="1352" height="643" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


$$
f^{\mathrm{out}}=\frac{f_0}{1-\frac vc\cos\phi_o}
$$

For maximum and minimum frequency, $\cos\phi=\pm1$.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '67.1%', '--fig-max': '430px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/inpho-2020-questions-en/p4-key-outside-extrema.png" alt="Native outside-detector extrema geometry including both Ao/Ao-prime,alpha_o,R,Do,Dout,O and dashed tangents." width="1343" height="720" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


$$
\text{at }\mathrm A_o,\quad f_{\max}^{\mathrm{out}}=\frac{f_0}{1-\frac vc}=\frac{f_0}{1-\frac{\omega R}c}\qquad (4.1)
$$

$$
\text{at }\mathrm A'_o,\quad f_{\min}^{\mathrm{out}}=\frac{f_0}{1+\frac vc}=\frac{f_0}{1+\frac{\omega R}c}\qquad (4.2)
$$

If both the detectors are outside, maximum and minimum frequencies detected by them are same which is not the case if you observe the graph given in the question. Hence, we rule out the first possibility.

When detector $D_{\mathrm{in}}$ is inside the circle (see figure below):

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '48.4%', '--fig-max': '310px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/inpho-2020-questions-en/p4-key-inside.png" alt="Entire native inside-detector geometry: O,S,R,Di,Din,beta_i,gamma_i,phi_i,v and right angle." width="968" height="769" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


$$
f^{\mathrm{in}}=\frac{f_0}{1-\frac vc\cos\phi_i}\qquad (4.3)
$$

$$
|\cos\phi_i|=|\sin\beta_i|\qquad (4.4)
$$

$$
\frac{\sin\gamma_i}R=\frac{\sin\beta_i}{D_i}\qquad (4.5)
$$

$$
\cos\phi_i=\frac{D_i}R\sin\gamma_i\qquad (4.6)
$$

$$
f^{\mathrm{in}}=\frac{f_0}{1-\frac vc\frac{D_i}R\sin\gamma_i}\qquad (4.7)
$$

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '47.9%', '--fig-max': '307px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/inpho-2020-questions-en/p4-key-inside-extrema.png" alt="Native inside-detector extrema geometry: Ai/Ai-prime,alpha_i,beta_i,gamma_i,phi_i,R,Di,Din,O and tangent/right angles." width="959" height="762" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


$f^{\mathrm{in}}$ is maximum (minimum) when $\sin\gamma_i$ is $1$ ($-1$).

$$
\text{at }\mathrm A_i,\quad f_{\max}^{\mathrm{in}}=\frac{f_0}{1-\frac{\omega D_i}c}<f_{\max}^{\mathrm{out}}\qquad (4.8)
$$

$$
\text{at }\mathrm A'_i,\quad f_{\min}^{\mathrm{in}}=\frac{f_0}{1+\frac{\omega D_i}c}>f_{\min}^{\mathrm{out}}\qquad (4.9)
$$

If both the detectors are inside, again there are two possibilities: they are at the same distances or at the different distances from the center. In the former case, their observed peak frequencies will be same which is clearly not evident from the given graph. In the latter case, their maximum frequencies will be different but the time difference between the maximum and minimum frequencies will also be different. However from the graph, the time difference between maximum and minimum frequencies for detectors 1 and 2 is

$$
t(f_{\min})-t(f_{\max})=9\ \mathrm s.
$$

This is possible only if one detector is inside and other detector is outside. Also, detector may not be colinear with the center.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '44.6%', '--fig-max': '286px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/inpho-2020-questions-en/p4-key-location-answer.png" alt="Native qualitative answer diagram: detector1 outside,detector2 inside,source S,centre O and clockwise arrow." width="894" height="534" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


In this part, marking (anywhere) 1 outside and 2 inside the circle with a correct justification will be given full credit.

### (c)

From equations (4.1) and (4.2),

$$
\frac{f_0}{1-\frac vc}=1300\ \mathrm{Hz}\qquad (4.10)
$$

$$
\frac{f_0}{1+\frac vc}=800\ \mathrm{Hz}\qquad (4.11)
$$

$$
f_0\approx991\ \mathrm{Hz}\qquad (4.12)
$$

### (d)

We have to find out $D_i$, $D_o$ and angle $\theta$ between the detectors. From equations (4.10) and (4.11),

$$
v=78.6\ \mathrm{m/s}\qquad (4.13)
$$

$$
v=R\omega=R\frac{2\pi}{24}\qquad (4.14)
$$

$$
R\approx300\ \mathrm m\qquad (4.15)
$$

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '64%', '--fig-max': '410px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/inpho-2020-questions-en/p4-key-joint-geometry.png" alt="Entire native joint detector distance geometry with all four extrema,centre,theta,alpha_i/alpha_o,Di/Do,Din/Dout and dashed paths." width="1281" height="773" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


By symmetry, $\mathrm A_o$ and $\mathrm A'_o$ are equidistant from the detector 1. Similarly, $\mathrm A_i$ and $\mathrm A'_i$ are equidistant from the detector 2. It takes $9\ \mathrm s$ from $\mathrm A_i$ to $\mathrm A'_i$ ($\mathrm A_o$ and $\mathrm A'_o$). Hence

$$
2\alpha_o=2\alpha_i=\frac{3\pi}4\qquad (4.16)
$$

$$
D_i=R\cos\alpha_i\approx115\ \mathrm m\qquad (4.17)
$$

$$
D_o=\frac R{\cos\alpha_o}\approx784\ \mathrm m\qquad (4.18)
$$

There will be signal delay due to finite $D_o$ and $D_i$.

Let $\theta$ be the angular separation between the detectors. Time difference between the peak frequencies of detectors 1 and 2 is 4 seconds.

$$
4=\frac\theta\omega+\frac{\mathrm A_iD_{\mathrm{in}}}c-\frac{\mathrm A_oD_{\mathrm{out}}}c\qquad (4.19)
$$

$$
4=\frac\theta\omega+\frac{R\sin\alpha_i}c-\frac{D_o\sin\alpha_o}c\qquad (4.20)
$$

$$
\Rightarrow\theta=\frac\pi3-\frac{\omega R\sin\alpha_i}c+\frac{\omega D_o\sin\alpha_o}c\qquad (4.21)
$$

$$
=1.4\ \mathrm{rad}\qquad (4.22)
$$

The distance between the detectors $=\sqrt{\mathrm{OD}_{\mathrm{in}}^2+\mathrm{OD}_{\mathrm{out}}^2-2\mathrm{OD}_{\mathrm{in}}\cdot\mathrm{OD}_{\mathrm{out}}\cos(1.4)}\approx773\ \mathrm m$.
