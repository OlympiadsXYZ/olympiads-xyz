A massless inextensible string of length $D$ lies with one end fixed, while the other is attached to one end of a uniform rod of length $L$. The system is initially at rest with the rod aligned along the $x$-axis and the string stretched to its natural length at an angle with the negative $y$-axis $\theta$ ($\cos\theta=1/3$). At a certain instant, a bullet of the same mass $m$ as the rod and negligible dimensions is fired horizontally along the positive $y$-direction. The bullet hits the rod at its right end with velocity $v_o$ and gets lodged in it, the impact being nearly instantaneous. What is the tension ($T$) in the string immediately after the impact? Assume the string doesn’t break.

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи решението от архива">

Geometry of the problem dictates that the string becomes taut during the impact and hence exerts an impulse. We conclude:

1. Angular momentum is conserved only about $P$.
2. The velocity of $P$ is perpendicular to the string right after the impact (refer to the figure).

We have two unknowns – $\omega$ and $u$.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '42.7%', '--fig-max': '273px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/inpho-2020-questions-en/p5-key-motion.png" alt="Full native immediately-after-impact geometry: fixedstring/theta,P,CM,u,omega arc and separatexyaxes." width="854" height="604" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


We first note that the momentum of CM is preserved perpendicular to the string since the string applies force only along its length.

$$
(P_i)_\perp=(P_f)_\perp
$$

$$
mv\sin\theta=2m(v_f)_\perp
$$

where $(v_f)_\perp$ is the velocity of the CM perpendicular to the string. CM is located at a distance $3L/4$ from $P$. So, $(v_f)_\perp=\frac{3L\omega}4\sin\theta-u$

$$
\Rightarrow\frac{3L\omega}4\sin\theta-u=\frac{v_o\sin\theta}2\qquad (5.1)
$$

Applying conservation of angular momentum about $P$,

$$
L_i=L_f
$$

$$
mv_oL=2m\left(\frac{3L\omega}4-u\sin\theta\right)\frac{3L}4+I_{\mathrm{cm}}\omega\qquad (5.2)
$$

Solving equations 5.1 and 5.2, we get

$$
\omega=\frac{v_o}L\qquad (5.3)
$$

$$
u=\frac{v_o}{3\sqrt2}\qquad (5.4)
$$

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '42.7%', '--fig-max': '273px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/inpho-2020-questions-en/p5-key-tension.png" alt="Full native tension geometry including T along string,thetaa,P,CM,u,omega arc and separatexyaxes." width="854" height="618" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


Designate the tension in the string immediately after the impact as $T$. This gives a clockwise angular acceleration of the rod equal to

$$
\alpha=\frac{3L}4\frac{T\cos\theta}{I_{\mathrm{cm}}}=\frac{18T\cos\theta}{5mL}\qquad (5.5)
$$

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '59.4%', '--fig-max': '380px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/inpho-2020-questions-en/p5-key-acceleration.png" alt="Native acceleration component diagram at P,all three arrows,T/2m,3Lalpha/4,omega-squared3L/4 and theta." width="1189" height="450" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


We now calculate the acceleration of $P$ on the rod in the direction of the string, $(a_P)_\parallel$ (in the ground frame) and set it equal to $u^2/D$.

To this end, note that the acceleration of the CM in the direction of the string is equal to $T/2m$. Also, point $P$ is instantaneously rotating about the CM with angular velocity $\omega$ in a circle of radius $3L/4$ – this gives a centripetal acceleration of $\omega^2\times3L/4$ directed towards the CM. Furthermore, the tangential acceleration of $P$ along its trajectory is given by $3L\alpha/4$. Accounting for all these contributions and taking appropriate components, we can write the acceleration of point $P$ in the direction of the string as

$$
(a_P)_\parallel=\frac T{2m}+\frac{27T}{10m}\cos^2\theta-\frac{3\omega^2L}4\sin\theta
$$

Now we know

$$
(a_P)_\parallel=\frac T{2m}+\frac{27T}{10m}\cos^2\theta-\frac{3\omega^2L}4\sin\theta=\frac{u^2}D\qquad (5.6)
$$

Hence we get

$$
\boxed{T=\frac{5mv_o^2}4\left[\frac1{18D}+\frac1{L\sqrt2}\right]}
$$

### Comment [not for grading purposes]

We assumed above that the string is taut. This is consistent with the solution above, as we found a non-zero answer for the tension. However, if you do not picture that the geometry of the problem requires tension in the string, you can convince yourselves that this is the correct assumption, by assuming that the tension is zero and arriving at a contradiction.

Assume that the string becomes slack. Let the angular velocity of the rod be $\omega$ counter-clockwise, as seen from top, immediately after the impact. Then, noting that velocity of CM remains preserved (because the string is assumed to go slack during the impact) and applying conservation of angular momentum about the point of impact, one gets

$$
L_i=L_f
$$

$$
0=2m\frac{v_o}2\frac L4-I_{\mathrm{cm}}\omega
$$

$$
\omega=\frac{mv_oL}{4I_{\mathrm{cm}}}
$$

where $I_{\mathrm{cm}}$ is the moment of inertia of the rod and the bullet system about its center of mass. Now, $I_{\mathrm{cm}}=\frac{mL^2}{12}+\frac{mL^2}8+\frac{mL^2}8=\frac{5mL^2}{24}$, thereby yielding

$$
\omega=\frac{6v_o}{5L}
$$

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '42.7%', '--fig-max': '273px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/inpho-2020-questions-en/p5-key-slack-contradiction.png" alt="Native slack-string contradiction diagram with v_o/2 at CM,omega arc,theta,fixedstring and xyaxes." width="854" height="611" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


The velocity of the end of the string tied to the rod ($P$) immediately after the impact, therefore, would be given by $-\left(\frac{3L}4\times\frac{6v_o}{5L}-\frac{v_o}2\right)\hat j=-\frac{2v_o}5\hat j$. But this would mean the string would get elongated. Contradiction!
