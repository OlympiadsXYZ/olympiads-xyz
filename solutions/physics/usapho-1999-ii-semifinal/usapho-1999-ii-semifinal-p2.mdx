Assume the electric potential to be zero at infinity. A charge $q$ is located on the x-axis at $x = b$. One additional charge is placed elsewhere on the x-axis so that all points a distance $c$ from the origin have zero total electric potential. What is that additional charge and where is it located? Express your answer in terms of $q$, $c$, and $b$.

*(contributed by Mary Mogge)*

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- $Q_2 = -\frac{c}{b}q \text{ at } x = d = \frac{c^2}{b}$

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

Assume a charge of $Q_2$ at $x = d$ along the x-axis. The total potential a distance c from the origin is

$$
V = k\frac{Q_2}{R_2} + k\frac{q}{R_1} \qquad (1)
$$

where k is Coulomb's constant and $R_1$ and $R_2$ can be found using the law of cosines.

$$
R_1 = \sqrt{b^2 - 2bc\cos\theta + c^2}
$$

and

$$
R_2 = \sqrt{d^2 - 2dc\cos\theta + c^2}.
$$

Since V = 0,

$$
0 = k\frac{Q_2}{R_2} + k\frac{q}{R_1}
$$

Therefore

$$
\frac{Q_2}{R_2} = -\frac{q}{R_1}.
$$

This must be true for all points a distance c from the origin. In particular at θ = 0 or x = +c we have

$$
\frac{Q_2}{\left(c - d\right)} = -\frac{q}{\left(b - c\right)}. \qquad (2)
$$

At θ = 180° = π or x = -c we have

$$
\frac{Q_2}{\left(c + d\right)} = -\frac{q}{\left(b + c\right)}. \qquad (3)
$$

Solving equations (2) and (3) simultaneously for $Q_2$ and $d$, we have

$$
Q_2 = -\frac{c}{b}q \qquad \text{at} \qquad d = \frac{c^2}{b}.
$$

Substituting $d$ into $R_2$, we have

$$
R_2 = \sqrt{\left(c^2/b\right)^2 - 2\left(c^2/b\right)c\cos\theta + c^2} = \left(c/b\right)\sqrt{c^2 - 2bc\cos\theta + b^2} = \left(c/b\right)R_1.
$$

for any angle $\theta$, i.e. any point a distance c from the origin. Substituting $Q_2$ and $R_2$ into (1),

$$
V = k\frac{\left(-c/b\right)q}{\left(c/b\right)R_1} + k\frac{q}{R_1} = 0
$$
