*1999 Semi-Final Exam*

## Условие

A small crate of weight 5.0 N is released from rest at a height 2.0 m up a smooth inclined plane. The crate slides down the plane and across a smooth 1.50-m floor to a rough plane where a spring is located. The bottom of the spring is located at a height of 0.50 m. The spring constant is 20 N/m. The coefficient of kinetic friction between the rough plane and crate is $1/\sqrt{3}$ and the coefficient of static friction is $1/\sqrt{2}$.

**a.** What is the maximum height the crate reaches on the right-hand inclined plane? **[10 т.]**

**b.** Where does the crate permanently come to rest? How many times does the crate travel up the rough plane? **[10 т.]**

*(contributed by Mary Mogge)*

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a.** 0.75 m
- **b.** At a height 0.25 m up the right-hand (rough) plane; it travels up the rough plane twice.

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

a. Let $Mg = 5.0\ \mathrm{N}$ = weight of the crate

$h = 2.0\ \mathrm{m}$ = the initial height of the crate

$h'$ = maximum height the crate reaches on the right hand side

$s$ = distance traveled along the rough plane to highest point.

$x$ = maximum compression of the spring

$$h' = s\sin 30^{\circ} = s\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right) \qquad s = 2h'$$

$$x = s - \left(\frac{0.5m}{\sin 30^{\circ}}\right) = s - 1m = 2h' - 1m$$

To find the final height h', apply the work energy theorem

$$U_G' + U_{sp}' = U_G + W_f$$

$$Mgh' + \tfrac{1}{2}kx^2 = Mgh - \mu_k F_N s$$

$$F_N = Mg\cos 30^{\circ} = Mg\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$$

$$Mgh' + \tfrac{1}{2}k\left(2h' - 1\right)^2 = Mgh - \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right)\left(Mg\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)\left(2h'\right)$$

$$Mgh' + \tfrac{1}{2}k\left(2h' - 1\right)^2 = Mgh - Mgh'$$

$$2Mgh' + \tfrac{1}{2}k\left(2h' - 1\right)^2 = Mgh$$

$$2(5N)h' + \tfrac{1}{2}\left(20N/m\right)\left(2h' - 1\right)^2 = \left(5N\right)\left(2m\right)$$

$$h' + \left(2h' - 1\right)^2 = 1 \qquad \text{where } h' \text{ is in meters}$$

$$4h'^2 - 3h' = 0$$

Since $h'$ can't be zero

$$h' = \left(\tfrac{3}{4}\right)m$$

b. Initially it has $Mgh = (5N)(2m) = 10J$ of total mechanical energy. Sliding up to the highest point on the right hand side, the crate loses total mechanical energy

$$\mu_k F_N s = \mu_k\left(Mg\cos\theta\right)\left(2h'\right) = \left(1/\sqrt{3}\right)Mg\left(\sqrt{3}/2\right)\left(2h'\right)$$

$$= Mgh' = (5N)(0.75m) = 3.75J$$

(Note: On the first trip up the plane, the crate reached the spring which provided an additional force of kx down the plane. At maximum extension this force is 10 N, sufficient to overcome static friction.)

Sliding back down it loses another 3.75 J of total mechanical energy. Therefore it has

$$10J - 2\left(3.75J\right) = 2.5J$$

at the bottom of the right hand plane. The total mechanical energy remains unchanged until the crate returns to the right hand plane and begins to slide up it. Using our prior result that as the crate slides up the plane

$$\mu_k F_N s = Mgh'$$

That is, as the crate rises half the kinetic energy becomes potential and half is dissipated by friction, it will rise to a height given by

$$\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\left(2.5J\right) = Mgh' = \left(5N\right)h'$$

or

$$h' = 0.25m$$

It stops before it reaches the spring. At this point, the only two forces acting on the crate are the gravitational force and the frictional force. The component of the weight parallel to the plane is

$$F_{G|} = Mg\sin 30^{\circ} = (5N)\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right) = 2.5N$$

down the plane. the maximum force of static friction is

$$f_{s\max} = \mu_s Mg\cos 30^{\circ} = \left(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)(5N)\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) = 3.1N$$

$$F_{G|} < f_{s\max}$$

The crate comes to rest a height 0.25m up the right hand plane. It has slid down the smooth plane, up the rough plane, temporarily compressing the spring, back down the rough plane, across the horizontal surface, up and down the smooth plane, back across the horizontal surface, and up the rough plane a second time before it finally stops.


</Spoiler>
