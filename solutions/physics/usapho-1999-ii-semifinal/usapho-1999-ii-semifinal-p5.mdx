*1999 Semi-Final Exam*

## Условие

Consider two uniform rods each of mass $M$ and length $L$, hinged together to form an upside-down “vee” shape whose angle can vary. The rods are released from rest when their angle θ with the horizontal is 45°. All hinges are frictionless and have negligible mass.

Case 1: The left end is also hinged at a fixed point at the bottom. The right-hand end of the configuration slides on the horizontal surface without friction.

Case 2: Both ends can slide frictionlessly along the surface.

**(The sub-parts of this problem may be worked in any order.)**

<figure>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-1999-ii-semifinal/p5-fig1.png" alt="Two diagrams of an upside-down V made of two rods of length L, each at angle θ to the horizontal. Case 1: the left end is hinged to a vertical wall/fixed point at the bottom and the right end rests on the horizontal surface. Case 2: both ends rest on the horizontal surface." />
</figure>

**a.** Find the upward force $F_N$ that the horizontal surface exerts on the right hand side of the Case 1 rods just after release, when θ = 45°. **[10 т.]**

**b.** Repeat Part (a) for Case 2. **[10 т.]**

**c.** Find the angular velocity of the Case 1 rods as a function of θ. $0 < \theta < 45^{\circ}$ **[10 т.]**

**d.** Repeat Part (c) for the configuration of Case 2. **[10 т.]**

*(contributed by Leaf Turner)*

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a.** $F_N = \frac{7}{10}Mg$
- **b.** $F_N = \frac{5}{8}Mg$
- **c.** $\omega^2 = \frac{3g}{L}\frac{\left(1/\sqrt{2} - \sin\theta\right)}{\left(1 + 3\sin^2\theta\right)}$
- **d.** $\omega^2 = \frac{3g}{L}\left(1/\sqrt{2} - \sin\theta\right)$

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

B1a. For θ = 45°, both $\cos\theta = 1/\sqrt{2}$ and $\sin\theta = 1/\sqrt{2}$. The relation between the magnitude of the angular acceleration α and the magnitude of the linear acceleration a is $a = \alpha L/2$ with $a_x = a_y = \alpha L/\left(2\sqrt{2}\right)$. Using Iα = Στ for the rotation about the hinge at the lower end of the left rod

$$
\frac{ML^2\alpha}{3} = \frac{MgL}{2\sqrt{2}} - \frac{VL}{\sqrt{2}} - \frac{HL}{\sqrt{2}} \qquad (a.1)
$$

Applying Newton's second law to the right rod

$$
Ma_x = \sum F_x \qquad \frac{M3\alpha L}{2\sqrt{2}} = H \qquad (a.2)
$$

$$
Ma_y = \sum F_y \qquad \frac{M\alpha L}{2\sqrt{2}} = Mg + V - F_N \qquad (a.3)
$$

The net torque about the center of mass of the right rod is

$$
I\alpha = \sum\tau \qquad \frac{ML^2\alpha}{12} = \frac{F_N L}{2\sqrt{2}} + \frac{VL}{2\sqrt{2}} - \frac{HL}{2\sqrt{2}} \qquad (a.4)
$$

Equations (a.1), (a.2), (a.3), and (a.4) form a set of four simultaneous equations in the four unknowns $H$, $V$, $\alpha$, and $F_N$. Solving them simultaneously for $F_N$, yields $F_N = \frac{7}{10}Mg$

b. By symmetry V = 0. Applying Newton's second law to the right rod

$$
Ma_x = \sum F_x \qquad \frac{M\alpha L}{2\sqrt{2}} = H \qquad (b.1)
$$

$$
Ma_y = \sum F_y \qquad \frac{M\alpha L}{2\sqrt{2}} = Mg - F_N \qquad (b.2)
$$

The net torque about the center of mass

$$
I\alpha = \sum\tau \qquad \frac{ML^2\alpha}{12} = \frac{F_N L}{2\sqrt{2}} - \frac{HL}{2\sqrt{2}} \qquad (b.3)
$$

Solving these three equations in unknowns $H$, $\alpha$ and $F_N$. Solving them simultaneously for $F_N$, yields

$$
F_N = \frac{5}{8}Mg
$$

c. Considering both rods as the system so we don't need to include the work done by the forces H and V,

$$
PE_{Li} + PE_{Ri} = PE_{Lf} + PE_{Rf} + KE_{Lf} + KE_{Rf} \qquad (c.1)
$$

Initially the center of mass of each rod is at a height $(L/2)\sin\theta_i$ where $\theta_i = 45^{\circ}$

$$
PE_{Li} + PE_{Ri} = Mg\frac{L}{2}\sin\theta_i + Mg\frac{L}{2}\sin\theta_i = MgL\sin\theta_i
$$

Finally,

$$
PE_{Lf} + PE_{Rf} = Mg\frac{L}{2}\sin\theta + Mg\frac{L}{2}\sin\theta = MgL\sin\theta
$$

For each side

$$
KE_f = \frac{1}{2}Mv^2 + \frac{1}{2}I\omega^2 \qquad \text{with} \qquad I = \frac{1}{12}ML^2.
$$

For the left rod $v_L = \omega L/2$, so

$$
KE_{Lf} = \frac{1}{2}M\left(\frac{\omega L}{2}\right)^2 + \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{12}ML^2\right)\omega^2 = \frac{1}{6}ML^2\omega^2.
$$

For the right rod $v_{Ry} = \omega(L/2)\cos\theta$, but $v_{Rx} = 3\,\omega(L/2)\sin\theta$, so

$$
KE_{Rf} = \frac{1}{2}M\left(\frac{\omega L}{2}\right)^2\left(\cos^2\theta + 9\sin^2\theta\right) + \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{12}ML^2\right)\omega^2 = ML^2\omega^2\left(\frac{1}{6} + \sin^2\theta\right)
$$

Substituting all of the above into (c.1):

$$
MgL\sin\theta_i = MgL\sin\theta + ML^2\omega^2\left(\frac{2}{6} + \sin^2\theta\right)
$$

Or

$$
\omega^2 = \frac{3g}{L}\frac{\left(\sin\theta_i - \sin\theta\right)}{\left(1 + 3\sin^2\theta\right)} = \frac{3g}{L}\frac{\left(1/\sqrt{2} - \sin\theta\right)}{\left(1 + 3\sin^2\theta\right)}.
$$

d. By symmetry, V = 0. The top of the “vee” doesn't move horizontally so H does no work. $v = \omega L/2$ for both rods. Applying energy conservation to either rod

$$
PE_i = PE_f + KE_f \qquad (d.1)
$$

with

$$
PE_i = Mg\frac{L}{2}\sin\theta_i \qquad \text{and} \qquad PE_f = Mg\frac{L}{2}\sin\theta \qquad (d.2)
$$

For either side

$$
KE_f = \frac{1}{2}Mv^2 + \frac{1}{2}I\omega^2 \qquad \text{with} \qquad I = \frac{1}{12}ML^2.
$$

With $v_L = \omega L/2$, so

$$
KE_f = \frac{1}{2}M\left(\frac{\omega L}{2}\right)^2 + \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{12}ML^2\right)\omega^2 = \frac{1}{6}ML^2\omega^2. \qquad (d.3)
$$

Combining equations (d.1), (d.2), and (d.3), yields

$$
MgL\sin\theta_i = MgL\sin\theta + \frac{2}{6}ML^2\omega^2
$$

Or

$$
\omega^2 = \frac{3g}{L}\left(\sin\theta_i - \sin\theta\right) = \frac{3g}{L}\left(1/\sqrt{2} - \sin\theta\right)
$$
