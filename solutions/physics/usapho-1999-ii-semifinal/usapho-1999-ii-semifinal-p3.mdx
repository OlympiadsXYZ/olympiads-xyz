Coherent light of wavelength $\lambda$ passes through three infinitesimally narrow slits with spacing $d$. It produces an interference pattern on screen a large distance $D \gg d$ away. (The figure above has been distorted to fit the page.) Assume the light through all three slits is in phase at the location of the slits. Further assume that a wave through any slit has amplitude $A$ at any point $P$ on the screen.

**a.** What is the phase shift between adjacent waves arriving at point $P$ as a function of θ? **[3 т.]**

**b.** Write a wave function for each of the three waves arriving at point P. Specify which wave is which by using the subscripts “t”, “m”, and “b” to denote the wave through the top, middle, and bottom slit, respectively. Define any quantities you introduce. **[3 т.]**

**c.** What is the amplitude of the resultant wave at point P? **[7 т.]**

**d.** Write an expression for the intensity on the screen as a function of angle θ. Let $I_o$ be the intensity at θ = 0. **[3 т.]**

**e.** What is the position of the first minimum, that is, what is the smallest angle at which the intensity is zero? **[4 т.]**

*(contributed by Mary Mogge)*

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a.** $\delta = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}d\sin\theta$
- **c.** $A_{res} = A(1 + 2\cos\delta)$
- **d.** $I(\theta) = \frac{I_o}{9}(1 + 2\cos\delta)^2$
- **e.** $\theta = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{\lambda}{3d}\right)$

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

a. The path difference $\Delta L$ between wave through adjacent slits is

$$
\Delta L = d\sin\theta.
$$

The phase difference δ is

$$
\delta = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\Delta L = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}d\sin\theta
$$

b.

$$
\Psi_t = A\sin\left(\omega t + \phi - \delta\right)
$$

where ω = 2πf, f is the frequency of the wave and ϕ is a constant that is independent of the wave (may be set to zero).

$$
\Psi_m = A\sin\left(\omega t + \phi\right)
$$

$$
\Psi_b = A\sin\left(\omega t + \phi + \delta\right)
$$

(or any equivalent set with phase shifts differing by δ).

c.

$$
\Psi = \Psi_t + \Psi_m + \Psi_b
$$

$$
\Psi = A\sin\left(\left(\omega t + \phi\right) - \delta\right) + A\sin\left(\omega t + \phi\right) + A\sin\left(\left(\omega t + \phi\right) + \delta\right)
$$

$$
\Psi = A\left[\sin\left(\omega t + \phi\right)\cos\delta - \sin\delta\cos\left(\omega t + \phi\right)\right] + A\sin\left(\omega t + \phi\right)
$$

$$
+ A\left[\sin\left(\omega t + \phi\right)\cos\delta + \sin\delta\cos\left(\omega t + \phi\right)\right]
$$

$$
\Psi = A\left(1 + 2\cos\delta\right)\sin\left(\omega t + \phi\right) = A_{res}\sin\left(\omega t + \phi\right)
$$

With the resultant amplitude

$$
A_{res} = A\left(1 + 2\cos\delta\right)
$$

d. The intensity is proportional to the amplitude squared. Calling the constant of proportionality c, we have

$$
I(\theta) = cA^2\left(1 + 2\cos\delta\right)^2.
$$

At $\theta = 0$, $\delta = 0$, $\cos\delta = 1$

$$
I_o = cA^2\left(1 + 2\right)^2 = 9cA^2.
$$

Eliminating c from the $I(\theta)$ equation

$$
I(\theta) = \frac{I_o}{9}\left(1 + 2\cos\delta\right)^2.
$$

e. In order for $I(\theta) = 0$

$$
1 + 2\cos\delta = 0
$$

or

$$
\cos\delta = -\tfrac{1}{2} \qquad \text{or} \qquad \delta = 120^{\circ} = 2\pi/3
$$

$$
\frac{2\pi}{3} = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}d\sin\theta
$$

or

$$
\theta = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{\lambda}{3d}\right)
$$
