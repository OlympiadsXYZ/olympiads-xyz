A robot is equipped with an eye containing an adjustable thin lens mounted at the front. A small flat retina-like sensor is mounted on the optical axis at the back of the eye. The distance from the lens to the sensor is

$$
s' = 10.0\ \mathrm{cm}.
$$

The circular aperture of the lens (the pupil) has diameter

$$
d = 5.00\ \mathrm{mm}.
$$

By adjusting its internal lens, the robot can focus sharply on objects whose distances lie between

$$
1.00\ \mathrm{m} \le s \le 10.0\ \mathrm{m}.
$$

In this problem, treat all lenses as thin.

<ProblemSection id="a1-a">

### (a)

Find both the range of focal lengths $f$ and the range of optical powers $P$ through which the internal lens is adjustable.

The robot is to be adapted for two new functions:

- observing stars (objects at infinity),
- reading printed material at a distance of 25.0 cm.

To do this, a thin corrective lens is placed immediately in front of the eye. Neglect the separation between this corrective lens and the eye lens.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="a1-b">

### (b)

Find the range of optical powers $P$ of the corrective lens that allows the robot to observe stars.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="a1-c">

### (c)

Can a single corrective lens allow the robot to both observe stars and read at 25.0 cm? Explain your answer.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="a1-d">

### (d)

Estimate the diffraction-limited maximum print resolution (in dots per inch, dpi) when viewing a page at 25.0 cm. Use light of wavelength

$$
\lambda = 550\ \mathrm{nm}.
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="a1-e">

### (e)

When viewing a stellar object, the eye lens is adjusted so that the image forms on the sensor. The total optical power collected by the pupil is $P_0$. Due to diffraction at the pupil, the image is a central bright spot (Airy disk).

**(i)** Estimate the intensity at the sensor in terms of $P_0$, $\lambda$, and the parameters of the eye.

**(ii)** By what factor does the intensity change if, while observing the same object, the pupil diameter is reduced from 5.0 mm to 2.0 mm?

</ProblemSection>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

(a) The image distance is fixed at $s' = 0.10$ m. The thin lens equation gives $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{s} + \frac{1}{s'}$. Since optical power is $P = \frac{1}{f}$, we have $P = \frac{1}{s} + \frac{1}{s'}$. For $s = 10.0$ m, $P = \frac{1}{10.0} + \frac{1}{0.10} = 0.10 + 10.0 = 10.1$ D. For $s = 1.00$ m, $P = \frac{1}{1.00} + \frac{1}{0.10} = 1.0 + 10.0 = 11.0$ D. Thus, $P_{\mathrm{eye}} \in [10.1,\ 11.0]$ D. Since $f = \frac{1}{P}$, the focal-length range is $f \in [9.09,\ 9.90]$ cm.

(b) For stars, $s = \infty$, so the corrective lens must form a virtual image at a distance $L$ that lies within the robot's normal focusing range: $1.00\ \mathrm{m} \le L \le 10.0\ \mathrm{m}$. For the corrective lens, $P = \frac{1}{s} - \frac{1}{L}$. Since $1/s = 0$, $P = -\frac{1}{L}$. Thus, $P_{\mathrm{corr}} \in [-1.0,\ -0.10]$ D.

(c) To read at 25.0 cm, the total optical power required is $P_{\mathrm{total}} = \frac{1}{0.25} + \frac{1}{0.10} = 4 + 10 = 14$ D. The internal lens can provide powers in the range $P_{\mathrm{eye}} \in [10.1,\ 11.0]$ D. Thus, the corrective lens must provide $P_{\mathrm{corr}} = P_{\mathrm{total}} - P_{\mathrm{eye}}$, which gives $P_{\mathrm{corr}} \in [14-11.0,\ 14-10.1] = [3.0,\ 3.9]$ D. From part (b), observing stars requires $P_{\mathrm{corr}} \in [-1.0,\ -0.10]$ D. These intervals do not overlap, so no single corrective lens can perform both functions.

(d) The diffraction-limited angular resolution is approximately $\theta \approx 1.22\frac{\lambda}{d}$. Using $\lambda = 5.5\times10^{-7}$ m, $d = 5.0\times10^{-3}$ m, we get $\theta \approx 1.22 \cdot \frac{5.5\times10^{-7}}{5.0\times10^{-3}} \approx 1.34\times10^{-4}$ rad. At a viewing distance $L = 0.25$ m, the smallest resolvable spacing is $\delta x = L\theta = 0.25 \cdot 1.34\times10^{-4} \approx 3.35\times10^{-5}$ m. Thus, $\mathrm{dpi} = \frac{0.0254}{\delta x} \approx 760$.

(e) (i) The diffraction-limited spot has characteristic size $r \sim \frac{\lambda s'}{d}$. Thus the area scales as $A \sim \frac{\lambda^2 s'^2}{d^2}$. Hence $I \sim \frac{P_0}{A} \sim \frac{P_0 d^2}{\lambda^2 s'^2}$.

(ii) Since $P_0 \propto d^2$, we have $I \propto d^4$. Thus, $\frac{I_{\mathrm{new}}}{I_{\mathrm{old}}} = \left(\frac{2}{5}\right)^4 = \frac{16}{625} \approx 0.026$, so the intensity decreases by a factor of about 39.


</Spoiler>
