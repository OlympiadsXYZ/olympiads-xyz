## Условие

A small block of weight $mg$ is initially at rest at the starting point $s = 0$ shown above.  It slides down an increasingly rough incline at an angle of $\theta$ to the horizontal. The coefficient of kinetic friction is

$$\mu = \alpha s,$$

where $\alpha$ is a constant and $s$ is measured down along the incline from the starting point $s = 0$. The magnitude of the work done by a non-constant force can be found from the area under a force versus displacement graph.

**a.** Draw a diagram which shows all the forces acting on the block (free body diagram).  Clearly label each force.  Write down the magnitude of each force in terms of only the quantities given above. **[9 т.]**

**b.** Sketch a graph of the magnitude of the frictional force versus displacement. **[3 т.]**

**c.** Find the area under the graph and use it to determine the magnitude of the work done by the frictional force as a function of $s$. **[3 т.]**

**d.** How far down the plane does the block slide before coming to rest? **[10 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a.** $N = mg\cos\theta,\quad f = \alpha s mg\cos\theta,\quad mg$
- **b.** A straight line through the origin: f = αsmg cosθ, linear in s.
- **c.** $|W| = \tfrac{1}{2}(\alpha mg\cos\theta)s^2$
- **d.** $s = \frac{2}{\alpha}\tan\theta$

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**1. a.**

Drawing and labeling the $mg$ vector in the downward direction or correctly drawing and labeling the components parallel to the plane $(mg\sin\theta)$ and perpendicular to the plane $(mg\cos\theta)$. **[3]**

Drawing and labeling the normal force (any alternate symbol for $N$ may be used).

$$N = mg\cos\theta.$$

**[3]**

Drawing and labeling the friction force (any alternate symbol for $f$ may be used).

$$f = \mu mg\cos\theta$$

or

$$f = \alpha s mg\cos\theta.$$

**[3]**

**b.** Sketch of force versus displacement graph showing a linear function of $s$. **[3]**

**c.** Finding the area under the graph by any method and equating it to $|W|$. For example,

$$|W| = \mathit{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2}bh = \tfrac{1}{2}(s)(\alpha s mg\cos\theta)$$

$$|W| = \tfrac{1}{2}(\alpha mg\cos\theta)s^2.$$

**[3]**

*(The absolute value signs around $W$ are not required.)*

**d.** Using the work-energy theorem

$$\mathrm{PE_i + KE_i} + W = \mathrm{PE_f + KE_f}$$

**[1]**

“i” refers to the starting point and “f” refers to the stopping point, a distance $s$ down the plane. At both points the kinetic energy is zero

$$\mathrm{KE_i} = 0 \qquad \mathrm{KE_f} = 0.$$

**[2]**

Setting the potential energy at the stopping point equal to zero, the potential energies are

$$\mathrm{PE_i} = mgh \qquad \mathrm{PE_f} = 0.$$

**[2]**

where $h$ is the vertical displacement of the starting point above the stopping point

$$h = s\sin\theta.$$

**[1]**

The frictional force does negative work

$$W = -\tfrac{1}{2}(\alpha mg\cos\theta)s^2.$$

**[1]**

Combining terms:

$$mgs\sin\theta - \tfrac{1}{2}(\alpha mg\cos\theta)s^2 = 0$$

**[1]**

The $s = 0$ solution represents the starting point and can be eliminated.

Dividing by $mgs$,

$$\sin\theta - \tfrac{1}{2}(\alpha\cos\theta)s = 0$$

**[1]**

Therefore

$$s = \frac{2\sin\theta}{\alpha\cos\theta} = \frac{2}{\alpha}\tan\theta$$

**[1]**
