*16th Asian Physics Olympiad 2015, 3rd-11th May, Hangzhou, China — Question 3 · 3 май 2015 г.*

## Условие

Figure 1 shows a Fabry-Perot (F-P) etalon, in which air pressure is tunable. The F-P etalon consists of two glass plates with high-reflectivity inner surfaces. The two plates form a cavity in which light can be reflected back and forth. The outer surfaces of the plates are generally not parallel to the inner ones and do not affect the back-and-forth reflection. The air density in the etalon can be controlled. Light from a Sodium lamp is collimated by the lens L1 and then passes through the F-P etalon. The transmitivity of the etalon is given by $T = \dfrac{1}{1 + F\sin^2(\delta/2)}$, where $F = \dfrac{4R}{(1-R)^2}$, R is the reflectivity of the inner surfaces, $\delta = \dfrac{4\pi nt\cos\theta}{\lambda}$ is the phase shift of two neighboring rays, n is the refractive index of the gas, t is the spacing of inner surfaces, θ is the incident angle, and λ is the light wavelength.

The Sodium lamp emits D1 ($\lambda = 589.6\mathrm{nm}$) and D2 ($589\mathrm{nm}$) spectral lines and is located in a tunable uniform magnetic field. For simplicity, an optical filter F1 is assumed to only allow the D1 line to pass through. The D1 line is then collimated to the F-P etalon by the lens L1. A microscope is used to observe the fringes. We take the reflectivity R= 90% and the inner-surface spacing t=1cm.

**(d)** Energy levels splitting of Sodium atoms occurs when they are placed in a magnetic field. This is called as the Zeeman effect. We have $J = \dfrac{1}{2}$ for both $^{2}\mathrm{P}_{1/2}$ and $^{2}\mathrm{S}_{1/2}$. Therefore, in the magnetic field, each energy level will be split into two levels. We define the energy gap of two splitting levels as $\Delta E_1$ for $^{2}\mathrm{P}_{1/2}$ and $\Delta E_2$ for $^{2}\mathrm{S}_{1/2}$ respectively ($\Delta E_1 \lt \Delta E_2$). As a result, the D1 line is split into 4 spectral lines (a, b, c, and d), as showed in Fig. 3. Please write down the expression of the frequency ($\nu$) of four lines a, b, c, and d. **[2 т.]**

**(a) (3points)** The D1 line ($\lambda = 589.6\mathrm{nm}$) is collimated to the F-P etalon. For the vacuum case (n=1.0), please calculate (i) interference orders $m_i$, (ii) incidence angle $\theta_i$ and (iii) diameter $D_i$ for the first three (i=1, 2, 3) fringes from the center of the ring patterns on the focal plane.

Solution:

The transmittivity of the F-P etalon is given by:

$$
T = \frac{1}{1 + F\sin^2\frac{\delta}{2}}
$$

For bright fringes, we have

$$
T = 1 \quad \text{i.e.} \quad \sin^2\frac{\delta}{2} = 0
$$

$$
\frac{\delta}{2} = m\pi
$$

$$
2nt\cos\theta = m\lambda
$$

For n=1.0, t=1cm, $\lambda = 589.6\mathrm{nm}$, thus:

$$
\cos\theta_i = \frac{m_i}{2nt/\lambda} = \frac{m_i}{33921.3}
$$

(a1) **(1 point if Eqs. (a2-a3) are not correct.)**

Because of $\cos\theta \le 1$, so the orders of the first three fringes are:

$$
m_1 = 33921, m_2 = 33920, m_3 = 33919
$$

**(a2) (1 point)**

The incident angles of the first three fringes are:

$$
\theta_1 = 0.241^{0}, \theta_2 = 0.502^{0}, \theta_3 = 0.667^{0}
$$

**(a3) (1 point)**

The fringe diameter is given by:

$$
D_i = 2f\tan\theta_i \approx 2f\theta_i
$$

(a4) **(0.5 point if Eq(a5) is not correct.)**

For the focal length f=30cm, thus:

$$
D_1 = 2.52\mathrm{mm}, D_2 = 5.26\mathrm{mm}, D_3 = 6.99\mathrm{mm}
$$

\Delta E = \Delta E_1 + \Delta E_2 = (g_{k1} + g_{k2})\mu_B B.
