## Условие

A rectangular conducting loop of mass $m$, width $w$, length $h$, and self inductance $L$ is held in the vertical $x$-$y$ plane with its bottom edge along the $y$-axis (see figure on the left below). In this problem take the resistance of the loop to be zero. A uniform magnetic field $\vec B$ is applied horizontally as shown in the figure such that

$$
\begin{aligned}\vec B&=B\hat k&&\text{for }x\le0\\&=0&&\text{for }x>0\end{aligned}
$$

The loop is released from rest at time $t=0$ and descends under gravity (see the figure to the right below). The acceleration due to gravity $g$ is in $+x$ direction.

**a)** Obtain $x(t)$, the position of the bottom edge of the loop at time $t$, in terms of relevant variables. **[5 т.]**

**b)** Imagine different possible scenarios for the nature of motion of the loop and plot $x(t)$ for each. **[6 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**(a) Solution:**

$$
m\ddot x=mg-BIw\qquad (4.1)
$$

$$
\phi=Bw(h-x)+LI\qquad (4.2)
$$

$$
-IR=\dot\phi=-Bw\dot x+L\dot I\qquad (4.3)
$$

Since $R=0$ Implies

$$
\dot I=\frac{Bw\dot x}L\qquad (4.4)
$$

Differentiating equation of motion with respect to $t$

$$
m\ddot v=-Bw\dot I\qquad (4.5)
$$

$$
\ddot v=\frac{-B^2w^2\dot x}{mL}\qquad (4.6)
$$

$$
=-\omega_0^2v\qquad (4.7)
$$

where

$$
\omega_0^2=\frac{B^2w^2}{mL}\qquad (4.8)
$$

The solution to $v$ is

$$
v=A\cos\omega_0t+D\sin\omega_0t\qquad (4.9)
$$

$$
\dot v=-A\omega_0\sin\omega_0t+D\omega_0\cos\omega_0t\qquad (4.10)
$$

$$
\ddot v=-A\omega_0^2\cos\omega_0t+D\omega_0^2\sin\omega_0t\qquad (4.11)
$$

Applying boundary conditions at $t=0$, $\dot v=g$, $v=0$ which implies that

$$
D=\frac g{\omega_0}\qquad (4.12)
$$

$$
A=0\qquad (4.13)
$$

Hence,

$$
v=\frac g{\omega_0}\sin\omega_0t\qquad (4.14)
$$

$$
x=-\frac g{\omega_0^2}\cos\omega_0t+C\qquad (4.15)
$$

at $t=0$, $x=0$, which implies that $C=\frac g{\omega_0^2}$.

Hence,

$$
x=\frac g{\omega_0^2}(1-\cos\omega_0t)\qquad (4.16)
$$

**(b) Solution:** We found that

$$
x=\frac g{\omega_0^2}(1-\cos\omega_0t)\qquad (4.17)
$$

The frequency of the oscillation is inversely proportional to $m$, and the amplitude increases with $m$. When the loop oscillates, the amplitude is

$$
x_m=2\frac g{\omega_0^2}=2\frac{gmL}{B^2w^2}\qquad (4.18)
$$

We can take three limiting cases, $m>m_0$, $m=m_0$, and $m<m_0$, where $m_0=hB^2w^2/2gL$.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '72.4%', '--fig-max': '464px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/inpho-2024-question/p4-key-cases.png" alt="Original colored x(t) graph with all three lower, equal and higher mass cases, h, time labels and pi/omega0." width="1450" height="826" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


For $m<m_0$, the loop oscillates. For $m=m_0$, the amplitude of the oscillation is $h$, and the loop comes out of the magnetic field region and falls under gravity. For $m>m_0$, the loop will come out of the magnetic field quicker it falls under gravity.
