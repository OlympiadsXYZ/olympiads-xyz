## Условие

Two masses, $m_1$ and $m_2$, attached to equal length massless strings, are hanging side-by-side just in contact with each other. Mass $m_1$ is swung out to the side to a point having a vertical displacement $0.20\ \mathrm{m}$ above mass $m_2$. It is then released from rest and collides elastically with the stationary hanging mass $m_2$. Each of the masses is observed to rise to the same height following the collision.

**a.** Find the numerical value of this height. **[20 т.]**

**b.** The masses swing back down and undergo a second elastic collision. Describe what happens following the second collision. (A proof is not required.) **[5 т.]**

(contributed by Leaf Turner)

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a.** 0.05 m
- **b.** After the second collision m₂ stops and m₁ reaches the maximum height hᵢ = 0.20 m.

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

3. Let $h_i = 0.20\ \mathrm{m}$ the initial height of $m_1$.

$h_f$ = the final height of both masses.

$v_{1i}$ = the speed of $m_1$ just before the collision.

$v_{1f}$ = the speed of $m_1$ just after the collision.

$v_{2f}$ = the speed of $m_2$ just after the collision.

a. Before the collision, for $m_1$ $\quad m_1gh_i = \tfrac{1}{2}m_1v_{1i}^2$ **[3]**

After the collision, for $m_1$ $\quad m_1gh_f = \tfrac{1}{2}m_1v_{1f}^2$

After the collision, for $m_2$ $\quad m_2gh_f = \tfrac{1}{2}m_2v_{2f}^2$

Since both masses rise to the same height after the collision, they must have equal speeds after the collision $\quad v_{1f} = v_{2f} = v_f$ **[3]**

Since the collision is elastic, the total kinetic energy is unchanged **[2]**

$\tfrac{1}{2}m_1v_{1i}^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}m_1v_f^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}m_2v_f^2$ **[2]**

or $\quad m_1gh_i = m_1gh_f + m_2gh_f$

$m_1h_i = (m_1 + m_2)h_f \qquad \text{Eq. 1}$ **[2]**

Using momentum conservation **[2]**

$m_1v_{1i} = m_2v_f - m_1v_f$ **[2]**

or $\quad m_1\sqrt{2gh_i} = (m_2 - m_1)\sqrt{2gh_f}$

$m_1\sqrt{h_i} = (m_2 - m_1)\sqrt{h_f}$

Squaring both sides $\quad m_1^2h_i = \left(m_2^2 - 2m_1m_2 + m_1^2\right)h_f$

Substituting in Eq. 1 $\quad m_1(m_1 + m_2)h_f = \left(m_2^2 - 2m_1m_2 + m_1^2\right)h_f$

Solving for the relation between the masses $\quad 0 = m_2^2 - 3m_2m_1$

Or assuming $m_2 \neq 0$ $\quad m_2 = 3m_1$ **[2]**

Solving Eq. 1 for $h_f$ $\quad h_f = \dfrac{m_1}{m_1 + m_2}h_i = \dfrac{m_1}{m_1 + 3m_1}h_i = \dfrac{1}{4}0.20\ \mathrm{m} = 0.050\ \mathrm{m}$ **[2]**

b. After the second collision $m_2$ stops **[3]**

and $m_1$ reaches maximum height $h_i$ **[2]**


</Spoiler>
