A mass $M$ with weight $Mg=10.00\ N$ is placed at the end of a massless spring with spring constant $100.0\ N/m$ as shown in the above diagram. The coefficients of static and kinetic friction between the mass and the rough horizontal surface are $0.400$ and $0.200$, respectively. The spring is compressed a distance $A_0=0.180\ m$ from its equilibrium position and released from rest. Find the total distance the mass travels and where it comes to rest with respect to the equilibrium position.

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- 0.800 m total distance; stops 0.020 m to the left of the equilibrium position.

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

In order for the mass to continue to oscillate back and forth, the spring force at maximum extension must exceed the maximum force of static friction. Letting $A_i$ equal the maximum displacement after the $i$th half cycle.

The mass stops oscillating when $F|_{x=A_i}<f_{s,max}$. [2] The spring force has magnitude $F=kx$. [1] The maximum force of static friction is $f_{s,max}=\mu_sN=\mu_sMg$. [2] The oscillation stops if $kA_i<\mu_sMg$. [1] Or numerically,

$$
A_i<\frac{\mu_sMg}{k}=\frac{(0.400)(10.00\ N)}{100.0\ N/m}=0.0400\ m.\qquad (3-1)
$$

[1] Applying the work energy theorem to a single half oscillation,

$$
W_{nc}+K_i+U_i=K_{i+1}+U_{i+1},\qquad (3-2)
$$

[2] where $W_{nc}$ is the work done by non-conservative forces, friction in this case. At the extreme displacements $A_i$ and $A_{i+1}$, the kinetic energy is zero: at $x=A_i$ and $x=A_{i+1}$, $K_i=0$ and $K_{i+1}=0$. [1]

The spring potential energy at $A_i$ and $A_{i+1}$ is $U_i=\frac12kA_i^2$ and $U_{i+1}=\frac12kA_{i+1}^2$. [1] The work done by kinetic friction is $W_{nc}=-f_kd=-\mu_kMgd$, [2] where $d$ is the total distance traveled, or $d=A_i+A_{i+1}$. [1]

$$
W_{nc}=-\mu_kMg(A_i+A_{i+1}).
$$

[1] Substituting into (3-2),

$$
-\mu_kMg(A_i+A_{i+1})+\frac12kA_i^2=\frac12kA_{i+1}^2.
$$

[1]

$$
\mu_kMg(A_i+A_{i+1})=\frac12k(A_i^2-A_{i+1}^2)=\frac12k(A_i+A_{i+1})(A_i-A_{i+1}).
$$

Dividing by $(A_i+A_{i+1})$, $\mu_kMg=\frac12k(A_i-A_{i+1})$. Solving for $A_{i+1}$,

$$
A_{i+1}=A_i-\frac{2\mu_kMg}{k}.
$$

[2] Numerically,

$$
A_{i+1}=A_i-\frac{2(0.200)(10.00\ N)}{100.0\ N/m}=A_i-0.0400\ m.
$$

Therefore each half cycle the amplitude decreases by $0.040\ m=4.0\ cm$. [1]

Successive amplitudes (starting with the initial displacement) are $0.180\ m$, $0.140\ m$, $0.100\ m$, $0.060\ m$, $0.020\ m$. [2] At $0.020\ m$ the amplitude satisfies condition (3-1) and the mass remains at rest. Since $A_0$ is to the left of the equilibrium position and they alternate, the stopping point is $0.020\ m$ to the left of the equilibrium position. [2]

The total distance traveled is $D=A_0+2A_1+2A_2+2A_3+A_4$.

$$
D=(0.180\ m)+2(0.140\ m)+2(0.100\ m)+2(0.060\ m)+(0.020\ m)=0.800\ m.
$$

[2]


</Spoiler>
