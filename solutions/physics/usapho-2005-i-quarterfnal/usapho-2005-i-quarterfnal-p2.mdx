## Условие

A space probe is sent toward a distant planet which has mass $M$ and radius $R$. When the probe is very, very far from the planet, the probe has speed $v$ and is headed in a direction such that, if undeflected, it would pass a distance $b>R$ from the planet's center. See the accompanying diagram.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '45%', '--fig-max': '288px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-2005-i-quarterfnal/p2-fig1-astra-r7-20260925.png" alt="Incoming probe with speed v and impact parameter b relative to a planet of mass M and radius R" width="901" height="463" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

**a** Assuming that the probe hits the planet, what is the probe's speed just before it hits? **[10 т.]**

**b** What is the maximum initial speed the probe can have and still hit the planet? **[15 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **a** $v_p=\sqrt{v^2+2GM/R}$
- **b** $v=\sqrt{\frac{2GMR}{b^2-R^2}}$

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**a.** Using energy conservation, the total mechanical energy when the probe is an infinite distance from the planet equals the total mechanical energy just before the probe hits:

$$
K_\infty+U_\infty=K_p+U_p.
$$

[2] Setting $U_\infty=0$, [1] we have $U_p=-GMm/R$, [2] where $m$ is the mass of the probe. The probe's kinetic energy is $K_\infty=\frac12mv^2$ and $K_p=\frac12mv_p^2$, [2] where $v_p$ is the probe's speed just before it hits the surface. Combining,

$$
\frac12mv^2=\frac12mv_p^2-\frac{GMm}{R}.\qquad (2-1)
$$

[1] Solving for $v_p$, we have $v_p=\sqrt{v^2+2GM/R}$. [2]

**b.** A central force, such as the universal gravitational force, causes no torque. Therefore angular momentum is conserved: $L_\infty=L_p$. [2] Angular momentum is given by

$$
\vec L=\vec r\times\vec p=\vec r\times m\vec v.\qquad (2-2)
$$

[2] Conservation gives $\vec r_\infty\times m\vec v=\vec r_p\times m\vec v_p$. [1] When the probe is very far away,

$$
|\vec r_\infty\times m\vec v|=mvr_\infty\sin\theta=mvb.\qquad (2-3)
$$

[2] When the probe has the largest speed it can have and still hit the planet, it will come in tangential to the surface, or perpendicular to the radius.

$$
|\vec r_p\times m\vec v_p|=mv_pR.\qquad (2-4)
$$

[2] Combining (2-2), (2-3), and (2-4), $mvb=mv_pR$. [1] Solving for $v_p$,

$$
v_p=v\frac bR.\qquad (2-5)
$$

[1] Substituting (2-5) into (2-1),

$$
\frac12mv^2=\frac12m\left(v\frac bR\right)^2-\frac{GMm}{R}.
$$

[2]

$$
v^2\left[\left(\frac bR\right)^2-1\right]=\frac{2GM}{R},
$$

$$
v=\sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R[(b/R)^2-1]}}=\sqrt{\frac{2GMR}{b^2-R^2}}.
$$

[2]


</Spoiler>
