## Условие

Super conducting magnets are widely used in laboratories. The most common form of super conducting magnets is a solenoid made of super conducting wire. The wonderful thing about a superconducting magnet is that it produces high magnetic fields without any energy dissipation due to Joule heating, since the electrical resistance of the superconducting wire becomes zero when the magnet is immersed in liquid helium at a temperature of $4.2\ \mathrm{K}$. Usually, the magnet is provided with a specially designed superconducting switch, as shown in Fig. 1. The resistance $r$ of the switch can be controlled: either $r=0$ in the superconducting state, or $r=r_n$ in the normal state. When the persistent mode, with a current circulating through the magnet and superconducting switch indefinitely. The persistent mode allows a steady magnetic field to be maintained for long periods with the external source cut off.

The details of the superconducting switch are not given in Fig. 1. It is usually a small length of superconducting wire wrapped with a heater wire and suitably thermally insulated from the liquid helium bath. On being heated, the temperature of the superconducting wire increases and it reverts to the resistive normal state. The typical value of $r_n$ is a few ohms. Here we assume it to be $5\ \Omega$. The inductance of a superconducting magnet depends on its size; assume it be $10\ \mathrm{H}$ for the magnet in Fig. 1. The total current $I$ can be changed by adjusting the resistance $R$.

## Решение

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-1">

### 1)

For $t=t_1$ to $t_3$:

Since $r=0$, the voltage across the magnet $V_M=L\,dI_1/dt=0$, therefore,

$$
I_1=I_1(t_1)=\frac12I_0;\qquad I_2=I-I_1=I-\frac12I_0.
$$

For $t=t_3$ to $t_4$:

Since $I_2=0$ at $t=t_3$, and $I$ is kept at $\tfrac12I_0$ after $t=t_3$, $V_M=I_2r_n=0$, therefore, $I_1$ and $I_2$ will not change.

$$
I_1=\frac12I_0;\qquad I_2=0.
$$

These results are shown in Fig. 6.

### 2)

For $t=0$ to $t=1\ \mathrm{min}$:

Since $r=0$, $V_M=L\,dI_1/dt=0$,

$$
I_1=I_1(0)=0,\qquad I_2=I-I_1=0.5\ \mathrm{A}.
$$

At $t=1\ \mathrm{min}$, $r$ suddenly jumps from $0$ to $r_n$, $I$ will drop from $E/R$ to $E/(R+r_n)$ instantaneously, because $I_1$ can not change abruptly due to the inductance of the magnet coil. For $E/R=0.5\ \mathrm{A}$, $R=7.5\ \Omega$ and $R_n=5\ \Omega$, $I$ will drop to $0.3\ \mathrm{A}$.

For $t=1\ \mathrm{min}$ to $2\ \mathrm{min}$:

$I$, $I_1$ and $I_2$ gradually approach their steady values:

$$
I=\frac ER=0.5\ \mathrm{A},\qquad I_1=I=0.5\ \mathrm{A},\qquad I_2=0.
$$

The time constant

$$
\tau=\frac{L(R+r_n)}{Rr_n}.
$$

When $L=10\ \mathrm{H}$, $R=7.5\ \Omega$ and $r_n=5\ \Omega$, $\tau=3\ \mathrm{sec}$.

For $t=2\ \mathrm{min}$ to $3\ \mathrm{min}$:

Since $r=0$, $I_1$ and $I_2$ will not change, that is

$$
I_1=0.5\ \mathrm{A}\quad\text{and}\quad I_2=0.
$$

### 3)

The operation steps are:

**First step**

Turn on power switch $K$, and increase the total current $I$ to $20\ \mathrm{A}$, i.e. equal to $I_1$.

Since the superconducting switch is in the state $r=0$, so that $V_M=L\,dI_1/dt=0$, that is, $I_1$ can not change and $I_2$ increases by $20\ \mathrm{A}$, in other words, $I_2$ changes from $-20\ \mathrm{A}$ to zero.

**Second step**

Switch $r$ from $0$ to $r_n$.

**Third step**

Gradually reduce $I$ to zero while keeping $I_2<0.5\ \mathrm{A}$; since $I_2=V_M/r_n$ and $V_m=L\,dI_1/dt$, when $L=10\ \mathrm{H}$, $r_n=5\ \Omega$, the requirement $I_2<0.5\ \mathrm{A}$ corresponds to $dI_1/dt<0.25\ \mathrm{A/sec}$, that is, a drop of $<15\ \mathrm{A}$ in $1\ \mathrm{min}$. In Fig. 8 $dI/dt\sim0.1\ \mathrm{A/sec}$ and $dI_1/dt$ is around this value too, so the requirement has been fulfilled.

**Final step**

Switch $r$ to zero when $V_M=0$ and turn off the power switch $K$. These results are shown in Fig. 8.

### 4)

**First step and second step** are the same as that in part 3, resulting in $I_2=0$.

**Third step** Increase $I$ by $10\ \mathrm{A}$ to $30\ \mathrm{A}$ with a rate subject to the requirement $I_2<0.5\ \mathrm{A}$.

**Fourth step** Switch $r$ to zero when $V_M=0$.

**Fifth step** Reduce $I$ to zero, $I_1=30\ \mathrm{A}$ will not change because $V_M$ is zero. $I_2=I-I_1$ will change to $-30\ \mathrm{A}$. The current flowing through the magnet is thus closed by the superconducting switch.

**Final step** Turn off the power switch $K$. The magnet is operating in the persistent mode.

These results are shown in Fig. 9.
