## Условие

(Cylindrical coordinates are used in this problem and $\hat u_r$, $\hat u_\theta$, and $\hat u_z$, represent unit vectors in the $r$, $\theta$ and $z$ directions, respectively. The coordinates form a right-handed orthogonal system, e.g., $\hat u_r\times\hat u_\theta=\hat u_z$.) A particle with charge $q$ and mass $m$ is constrained to move in the xy-plane with velocity that can be expressed as $\vec v=v_r\hat u_r+r\omega\hat u_\theta$. The particle is initially at rest and a distance $r_o$ from the origin. Two forces act on the particle. One is due to the presence of a uniform magnetic field given by $\vec B=B_o\hat u_z$. The other force and its corresponding potential energy are:

$$
\vec F=\frac{-2k}{r^3}\hat u_r\qquad\text{and}\qquad U(r)=-\frac k{r^2},
$$

where $k$ is a constant and $r$ is the distance from the origin. The particle executes a trajectory that periodically varies between its initial radius $r_o$ and a minimum radius, $r_{\min}$. Express your answers in terms of $q$, $m$, $v_r$, $r$, $\omega$, $B_o$, $k$, $r_o$, $r_{\min}$, and the unit vectors.

**a.** What is the magnetic force acting on the particle? **[5 т.]**

**b.** How much work is done by this magnetic force? **[5 т.]**

**c.** Use the work-energy theorem to derive an equation relating the variables $v_r$, $r$, and $\omega$ to the initial parameters **[8 т.]**

**d.** What is the net torque acting on the particle? **[7 т.]**

**e.** What is the angular momentum of the particle in terms $q$, $B_o$, $r$, and $r_o$ up to an arbitrary constant vector? **[5 т.]**

**f.** What is the particle's angular velocity $\omega$ in terms of $m$, $q$, $B_o$, $r$, and $r_o$? **[5 т.]**

**g.** Using your results from (c) and (f), find $r_{\min}$, the particle's closest approach to the origin. **[5 т.]**

**h.** Find the range of $B_o$ for which the particle does not reach the origin. **[5 т.]**

**i.** Find $\omega$ for large magnetic fields $B_o$ at each of the limiting radii $r=r_o$ and $r=r_{\min}$. Write your answer to lowest nonvanishing order in $\dfrac1{B_o}$. **[5 т.]**

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-a">

### a. (5 т.)

The magnetic force is $\vec F=q\vec v\times\vec B$.

Inserting the expressions for the velocity and magnetic field and taking the cross product,

$$
\vec F=q(v_r\hat u_r+r\omega\hat u_\theta)\times B_o\hat u_z=qB_o(v_r\hat u_r\times\hat u_z+r\omega\hat u_\theta\times\hat u_z)
$$

$$
\vec F=qB_o(r\omega\hat u_r-v_r\hat u_\theta)
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-b">

### b. (5 т.)

At any instant the displacement is in the direction of the velocity while the force is perpendicular to it. Therefore $W=0$.

Or

$$
dW=\vec F\cdot d\vec l=\vec F\cdot\vec v\,dt=q(\vec v\times\vec B)\cdot\vec v\,dt=0
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-c">

### c. (8 т.)

The magnetic force does no work. The other force has an associated potential energy. Using the work-energy theorem which reduces to conservation of mechanical energy,

$$
U_o+K_o=U+K
$$

Since the particle is initially at rest, its initial kinetic energy is zero. $K_o=0$

Substituting the energy terms

$$
-\frac k{r_o^2}=\frac12mv^2-\frac k{r^2}.
$$

Where

$$
v^2=(v_r\hat u_r+r\omega\hat u_\theta)\cdot(v_r\hat u_r+r\omega\hat u_\theta)=v_r^2+r^2\omega^2
$$

Combining the last two equations

$$
-\frac k{r_o^2}=-\frac k{r^2}+\frac12m(v_r^2+r^2\omega^2)\qquad\text{(B2-1)}
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-d">

### d. (7 т.)

The torque about the z-axis is $\vec\tau=\vec r\times\vec F=r\hat u_r\times\vec F$. The other force $\vec F=-\dfrac{2k}{r^3}\hat u_r$ contributes no torque, since $\hat u_r\times\hat u_r=0$. Therefore the net torque is due to the magnetic force

$$
\vec\tau=r\hat u_r\times qB_o(r\omega\hat u_r-v_r\hat u_\theta)=-qB_orv_r\hat u_z.
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-e">

### e. (5 т.)

The net torque is equal to the time rate of change of angular momentum. The radial component of the velocity $v_r$ is equal to the time rate of change of $r$.

$$
\frac{d\vec L}{dt}=\vec\tau=-qB_orv_r\hat u_z=-qB_or\frac{dr}{dt}\hat u_z=-qB_o\frac12\frac{dr^2}{dt}\hat u_z
$$

$$
\frac d{dt}\left(\vec L+qB_o\frac12r^2\hat u_z\right)=0
$$

Or

$$
\vec L+qB_o\frac12r^2\hat u_z=\vec C,
$$

where $\vec C$ is a constant vector.

$$
\vec L=\vec C-qB_o\frac12r^2\hat u_z.
$$

Dropping the vector notation since all motion occurs in the xy-plane,

$$
L=C-qB_o\frac12r^2
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-f">

### f. (5 т.)

For a particle the magnitude of the angular momentum about the z-axis can be written

$$
L=mr^2\omega.
$$

Combining the last two equations

$$
mr^2\omega=C-qB_o\frac12r^2
$$

Or

$$
mr^2\omega+qB_o\frac12r^2=C.
$$

The constant $C$ can be evaluated in terms of the initial conditions

$$
mr^2\omega+qB_o\frac12r^2=mr_o^2\omega_o+qB_o\frac12r_o^2
$$

since the particle is initially at rest $\omega_o=0$ and

$$
mr^2\omega+qB_o\frac12r^2=qB_o\frac12r_o^2
$$

Solving for $\omega$, we have

$$
mr^2\omega=qB_o\frac12(r_o^2-r^2)
$$

$$
\omega=\frac{qB_o}{2m}\left(\frac{r_o^2}{r^2}-1\right)\qquad\text{(B2-2)}
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-g">

### g. (5 т.)

At $r_{\min}$, $v_r=0$, Equation (B2-1) becomes

$$
-\frac k{r_o^2}=-\frac k{r_{\min}^2}+\frac12mr_{\min}^2\omega^2
$$

Bring the other $k$ term to the left and substituting equation (B2-2) at $r_{\min}$

$$
\frac k{r_{\min}^2}-\frac k{r_o^2}=\frac12mr_{\min}^2\left[\frac{qB_o}{2m}\left(\frac{r_o^2}{r_{\min}^2}-1\right)\right]^2
$$

or multiplying by $r_{\min}^2r_o^2$,

$$
k(r_o^2-r_{\min}^2)=\frac{r_o^2q^2B_o^2}{8m}(r_o^2-r_{\min}^2)^2.
$$

Canceling the common factor

$$
k=\frac{r_o^2q^2B_o^2}{8m}(r_o^2-r_{\min}^2)
$$

and solving for

$$
r_{\min}^2=r_o^2-\frac{8km}{q^2B_o^2r_o^2}\qquad\text{(B2-3)}
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-h">

### h. (5 т.)

The particle will not reach the origin if $r_{\min}^2>0$. From the preceding expression, this occurs when

$$
r_o^2>\frac{8km}{q^2B_o^2r_o^2}
$$

or solving for

$$
B_o^2>\frac{8km}{q^2r_o^4}.
$$

Thus

$$
|B_o|>\frac1{r_o^2}\sqrt{\frac{8km}{q^2}}
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-part-i">

### i. (5 т.)

Evaluating equation (B2-2)

at $r=r_o$

$$
\omega_o=\frac{qB_o}{2m}\left(\frac{r_o^2}{r_o^2}-1\right)=0
$$

at $r=r_{\min}$

$$
\omega=\frac{qB_o}{2m}\left(\frac{r_o^2}{r_{\min}^2}-1\right).
$$

combining with equation (B2-3)

$$
\omega=\frac{qB_o}{2m}\left(\frac{r_o^2}{r_o^2-\frac{8km}{q^2B_o^2r_o^2}}-1\right)=\frac{qB_o}{2m}\left(\frac1{1-\frac{8km}{q^2B_o^2r_o^4}}-1\right).
$$

For large $B_o$, $\dfrac{8km}{q^2B_o^2r_o^4}$ is small. Using the binomial expansion

$$
\omega=\frac{qB_o}{2m}\left(1+\frac{8km}{q^2B_o^2r_o^4}-1\right)=\frac{4k}{qB_or_o^4}
$$

</ProblemSection>
