## Условие

An unmanned space probe approaches the surface of a planet whose atmosphere is pure CO$_2$ (molecular weight $M = 44$ gm/mol). Upon entering atmosphere, the probe descends straight down at a constant speed $v_o$, recording information about the atmospheric pressure, which is shown on the attached graph. Unfortunately, a technician forgot to calibrate the pressure-measuring instrument, so that the $P$-axis on the pressure vs. time graph has no units! (The technician has been reprimanded, but fortunately, as leader of the data analysis team, you have figured out how to use the data anyway.) Upon reaching the planet’s surface, the probe reports the surface temperature to be 400 K, and the gravitational field there to be 9.9 N/kg. The radius of the planet is $5.0\times10^{6}$ m. Model the atmosphere locally as an ideal gas.

**(a)** Apply Newton’s Second Law to a small slab of the atmosphere of thickness $\Delta y$ that is in static equilibrium, to show that the change in pressure $\Delta P$ between the top and bottom sides of the slab is given by $\Delta P = \pm\rho g\Delta y$, where $\rho$ the atmosphere’s density and $g$ is the local gravitational field (the plus or minus sign is determined by the choice for the positive direction of the vertical $y$-axis; work out the sign in terms of your choice). **[5 т.]**

**(b)** Estimate the velocity $v_o$ with which the probe descends to the surface. **[15 т.]**

**(c)** Estimate the temperature of the atmosphere 15 km above the surface. Neglect the variation of the planet’s gravitational field between the surface and this height. **[15 т.]**

**(d)** Justify the neglect of the variation of the planet’s gravitational field between the surface and 15 km above the surface. Suggestion: Calculate the fractional difference between the gravitational field’s magnitude on the surface and its value 15 km above the surface, and assess whether that difference is significant compared to the accuracy of the estimate made in part (c). **[5 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **(a)** With y positive upwards, ΔP = −ρgΔy.
- **(b)** 6.4 m/s
- **(c)** 367 K
- **(d)** 0.006

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**(a)** Apply $F = ma$ to a slab of atmosphere of thickness $\Delta y$, cross-sectional area $A$, density $\rho$. Take $y$ positive upwards.

Forces: $P(y+\Delta y)A$ (down), $P(y)A$ (up), $mg$ (down).

$$
F = ma \quad (\text{which} = 0)
$$

$$
P(y)A - P(y+\Delta y)A - (\rho A\Delta y)g = 0
$$

$$
\underbrace{P(y+\Delta y) - P(y)}_{\Delta P} = -\rho g\Delta y
$$

$$
\Delta P = -\rho g\Delta y
$$

[note $\rho = \rho(y)$, $g = g(y)$]

**(b)** Since $v_o = \dfrac{-\Delta y}{\Delta t}$ (probe going down, $y$ points up)

$\Delta P = -\rho g\Delta y$ gives

$$
\frac{\Delta P}{\Delta t} = \rho g v_o \qquad \ldots(1)
$$

Need $\rho$: From ideal gas eq. of state $PV = nRT$,

$$
P = \frac{n}{V}RT \qquad \text{where } \frac{n}{V} = \frac{\#\,\text{moles}}{\text{vol.}} = \frac{\#\,\text{moles}}{\text{mass}}\,\frac{\text{mass}}{\text{vol.}}
$$

$$
\frac{n}{V} = \frac{1}{M}\rho
$$

$$
\Rightarrow\ P = \frac{\rho}{M}RT
$$

$$
\rho = \frac{PM}{RT} \qquad \ldots(2)
$$

Using $\rho$ from (2) in (1),

$$
\frac{\Delta P}{\Delta t} = \frac{PM}{RT}\,g v_o
$$

$$
v_o = \left(\frac{\Delta P}{\Delta t}\right)\frac{RT}{PMg} \qquad \ldots(3)
$$

From graph, probe lands on surface at $t = 4000$ s

$$
P = 60\ \text{units}, \quad g = 9.9\ \mathrm{N/kg}, \quad T = 400\ \mathrm{K}
$$

[From graph: at surface

$$
\frac{\Delta P}{\Delta t} = \frac{(60 - 55)\ \text{units}}{(4000 - 3900)\ \mathrm{s}} = .05\ \text{units/s}
$$

[line from (3900, 55) to (4000, 60) is straight]]

From Eq. (3),

$$
v_o = \frac{(.05\ \text{units/s})(8.3\ \mathrm{J/K\text{-}mol})(400\ \mathrm{K})}{(60\ \text{units})(44\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{kg/mol})(9.9\ \mathrm{N/kg})} \cong 6.4\ \mathrm{m/s}
$$

**(c)** Probe was 15 km above surface

$$
\Delta t = \frac{15\ \mathrm{km}}{6.4\ \mathrm{m/s}} \cong 2344\ \mathrm{s} \text{ before touchdown; ie., at}
$$

$$
t = (4000 - 2344)\ \mathrm{s} = 1656\ \mathrm{s}
$$

at $t \approx 1650$ s, $P = 11$ units

slope at $t = 1650$ s is about the same as slope of line from (1500 s, 9 units) to (2000 s, 15 units)

$$
\text{slope} \cong \frac{(15 - 9)\ \text{units}}{(2000 - 1500)\ \mathrm{s}} = .012\ \text{units/s}
$$

$\approx .01$ to two sig. figures (like slope found above)

From Eq. (3),

$$
T = \frac{PMg v_o}{R\left(\dfrac{\Delta P}{\Delta t}\right)}
$$

$$
\Rightarrow\ \frac{T_2}{T_1} = \frac{P_2}{P_1}\,\frac{(\Delta P/\Delta t)_1}{(\Delta P/\Delta t)_2} \qquad \begin{matrix}\text{Point 1: surface} \\ \text{Point 2: 15 km above surface}\end{matrix}
$$

$$
T_2 = \frac{11}{60}\left(\frac{.05}{.01}\right)400\ \mathrm{K} \cong 367\ \mathrm{K}
$$

**(d)**

$$
\begin{aligned} g(h) &= \frac{GM}{(R+h)^2} \\ &= \frac{GM}{R^2}\left(1 + \frac{h}{R}\right)^{-2} \\ &\cong g_o\left(1 - \frac{2h}{R}\right) \\ &\equiv g_o - \Delta g \end{aligned}
$$

$$
g_o = \frac{GM}{R^2}
$$

where

$$
\Delta g = \frac{2h}{R}g_o
$$

$$
\frac{\Delta g}{g_o} = \frac{2h}{R} = \frac{2(15\times10^{3}\ \mathrm{m})}{5\times10^{6}\ \mathrm{m}} = 6\times10^{-3}
$$

In part (c), the estimate of $P$ and $\Delta P/\Delta t$ at 15 km above the surface was much cruder than 6 parts out of a thousand.
