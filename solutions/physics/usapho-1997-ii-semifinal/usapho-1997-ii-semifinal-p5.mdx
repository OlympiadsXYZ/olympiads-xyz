## Условие

Consider an infinitely long, cylindrical cloud of electrons. The cloud has radius $R$, the number density of electrons has the uniform value $n_o$, the charge of each electron is $-e$ (where $e > 0$), and the mass of each electron is $m$. The cloud is surrounded by vacuum. There is a constant, uniform magnetic field along the cylinder's axis (the $z$-axis), so that $\mathbf{B} = B_o\mathbf{k}$, with $B_o > 0$. The cloud of electrons is rotating about the $z$-axis with angular velocity $\omega$ as shown in the figure. The speeds are non-relativistic. [A gas of charged particles is called a "plasma."]

</figure>

**(a)** Find the electric field at the radius $r < R$ within the cloud. **[6 т.]**

**(b)** Find the total force on an electron located at the radius $r$ within the cloud, neglecting any magnetic field induced by the cloud's rotation. (This neglect will be justified in part (f) below.) **[6 т.]**

**(c)** Using Newton's second law, show that there are two values of $\omega$ possible for the electrons to steadily orbit the cylinder's axis. Write these values of $\omega$ in terms of two other frequencies relevant to such problems: the cyclotron frequency $\omega_c = eB_o/m$, and the "plasma frequency" $\omega_p = [\,n_oe^2 / \epsilon_o m\,]^{1/2}$, where $\epsilon_o$ is the permittivity of empty space. **[8 т.]**

**(d)** Find the largest electron number density $(n_o)_{max}$ that can be confined by the applied magnetic field $B_o\mathbf{k}$. Write your answer in terms of the magnetic field energy density $B^2/2\mu_o$ and the electron's mass energy $mc^2$. **[6 т.]**

[This theoretical limit is called the *Brillouin limit*, and plays a key role in the confinement of plasmas consisting of particles all of like charge.]

**(e)** At a point located at radius $r$ within the cylindrical cloud, calculate the magnetic field induced by the cloud's rotation. Assume that the field induced on the axis of symmetry is zero. **[8 т.]**

**(f)** Determine the force on a charge in the cloud, due to the induced magnetic field of part (e). Show that the magnitude of this force divided by the magnitude of the electric force is $v^2/c^2$, where $v$ is the speed of the charge and $c$ is the speed of light. [This shows that the effect of the induced field is ignorable when the rotation speeds are non-relativistic.] **[6 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **(a)** $\vec{E} = \frac{n_o e}{2\epsilon_o}\,r\,(-\hat{r})$
- **(b)** $\vec{F} = \left(\frac{n_o e^2 r}{2\epsilon_o} - r\omega B_o e\right)\hat{r}$
- **(c)** $\omega = \frac{\omega_c}{2}\left(1 \pm \sqrt{1 - 2\left(\frac{\omega_p}{\omega_c}\right)^2}\right)$
- **(d)** $(n_o)_{max} = \frac{B_o^2\epsilon_o}{2m} = \frac{B_o^2/2\mu_o}{mc^2}$
- **(e)** $B' = \mu_o n_o e\omega\frac{r^2}{2}$
- **(f)** $\vec{F}' = \frac{\mu_o n_o e^2\omega^2 r^3}{2}(-\hat{r}),\quad \frac{|\vec{F}'|}{|\vec{F}_{el.}|} = \frac{v^2}{c^2}$

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**(a)** Use Gauss' law:

$$
\text{flux of } \vec{E} = \frac{1}{\epsilon_o}(\text{charge enclosed})
$$

$$
E\,2\pi rh = \frac{1}{\epsilon_o}(n_oe)(\pi r^2h)
$$

$$
E = \left(\frac{n_oe}{2\epsilon_o}\right)r
$$

$$
\vec{E} = \frac{n_oe}{2\epsilon_o}\,r\,(-\hat{r}) \qquad \text{radially } \underline{\text{in}}
$$

[gaussian surface: cylinder, $r \le R$, height $h$; no flux through end caps ($\vec{E}\cdot\hat{n} = 0$ there)]

**(b)**

$$
\begin{aligned} \vec{F} &= q(\vec{E} + \vec{v}\times\vec{B}) \\ &= -e\left[-\frac{n_oer}{2\epsilon_o}\hat{r} + r\omega B_o\hat{r}\right] \end{aligned}
$$

[$\vec{B} = B_o\hat{k}$, $\vec{v} = r\omega\hat{\theta}$, $\hat{\theta}\times\hat{k} = \hat{r}$]

$$
\vec{F} = \left(\frac{n_oe^2r}{2\epsilon_o} - r\omega B_oe\right)\hat{r}
$$

**(c)**

$$
\vec{F} = m\vec{a}
$$

$$
\left(\frac{n_oe^2r}{2\epsilon_o} - r\omega B_oe\right)\hat{r} = m\frac{(r\omega)^2}{r}(-\hat{r})
$$

$$
\Rightarrow\ \frac{n_oe^2}{2\epsilon_o} - \omega B_oe + m\omega^2 = 0
$$

$$
\omega^2 - \left(\frac{Be}{m}\right)\omega + \frac{n_oe^2}{2\epsilon_om} = 0
$$

$$
\omega^2 - \omega_c\omega + \tfrac{1}{2}\omega_p^2 = 0
$$

[$\omega_c \equiv \dfrac{B_oe}{m}$, $\omega_p^2 \equiv \dfrac{n_oe^2}{\epsilon_om}$]

$$
\omega = \frac{\omega_c}{2} \pm \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\omega_c^2 - 2\omega_p^2}
$$

$$
\omega = \frac{\omega_c}{2}\left(1 \pm \sqrt{1 - 2\left(\frac{\omega_p}{\omega_c}\right)^2}\right)
$$

**(d)** Since $\omega$ is real, this requires

$$
1 - 2\left(\frac{\omega_p}{\omega_c}\right)^2 \ge 0
$$

$$
\omega_p^2 \le \frac{1}{2}\omega_c^2
$$

$$
\frac{n_oe^2}{\epsilon_om} \le \frac{1}{2}\frac{e^2B_o^2}{m^2}
$$

$$
n_o \le \frac{B_o^2\epsilon_o}{2m} \qquad \text{so} \qquad (n_o)_{max} = \frac{B_o^2\epsilon_o}{2m}
$$

**(e)**

$$
(n_o)_{max} = \frac{B_o^2\epsilon_o}{2m} \qquad \text{use } \mu_o\epsilon_o = \frac{1}{c^2}
$$

$$
= \frac{B_o^2}{2m(\mu_oc^2)}
$$

$$
= \frac{B_o^2/2\mu_o}{mc^2} = \frac{\text{magnetic field energy density}}{\text{electron mass energy}}
$$

**(e)** Apply Ampere's law,

$$
\oint_C \vec{B}\cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_oI_S \qquad \text{where } C = \text{boundary of } S
$$

$$
B'h = \mu_o\int_S j\,da
$$

where

$$
\begin{aligned} j &= \text{current density} \\ &= (n_oe)v \\ &= n_oer'\omega \end{aligned}
$$

(or $\Sigma\, r\Delta r$)

$$
B'h = \mu_o(n_oe\omega)\int_0^r r'(dr'\,h)
$$

$$
B'h = \mu_on_oe\omega h\int_0^r r'\,dr'
$$

$$
B' = \mu_on_oe\omega\frac{r^2}{2}
$$

[$\vec{B}'$ = induced field $= B'\hat{k}$; given $\vec{B}' = 0$ on axis; note $\vec{B}'\cdot d\vec{l} = 0$ horizontally]

**(f)** Force due to induced magnetic field is $\vec{F}'$ where

$$
\begin{aligned} \vec{F}' &= -e\,\vec{v}\times\vec{B}' = -e(r\omega\hat{\theta})\times\left(\mu_on_oe\omega\frac{r^2}{2}\hat{k}\right) \\ &= \frac{\mu_on_oe^2\omega^2r^3}{2}(-\hat{r}) \end{aligned}
$$

[$\hat{\theta}\times\hat{k} = \hat{r}$; $v = r\omega$]

$$
|\vec{F}'| = \tfrac{1}{2}\mu_on_oe^2v^2r
$$

$$
\therefore\ \frac{|\vec{F}'|}{|\vec{F}_{el.}|} = \frac{\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_on_oe^2v^2r}{\tfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{n_oe^2r}{\epsilon_o}} = \mu_o\epsilon_ov^2 = \frac{v^2}{c^2}
$$

(the denominator is $|e\vec{E}|$ where $|\vec{E}|$ is from part (a))
