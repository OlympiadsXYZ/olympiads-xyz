## Условие

A child of mass $m$ holds onto the end of a massless rope of length $\ell$, which is attached to a pivot a height $H$ above the ground. The child is released from rest when the rope is straight and horizontal.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '40.3%', '--fig-max': '258px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/usapho-2024-f-ma-exam/p16-fig1-v3.png" alt="A vertical pole of height H with a horizontal rope of length ℓ at its top ending in the child m; a dashed path swings down along a circular arc, then continues as a projectile arc to the left, landing on the ground a horizontal distance d from the pole." width="807" height="456" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

At some point, the child lets go of the rope, flies through the air, and lands on the ground a horizontal distance $d$ from the pivot. On Earth, the maximum possible value of $d$ is $d_E$. If the setup is moved to the Moon, which has $1/6$ the gravitational acceleration, what is the new maximum possible value of $d$?

(A) $d_E/6$

(B) $d_E/\sqrt{6}$

(C) $d_E$

(D) $\sqrt{6}\,d_E$

(E) $6\,d_E$

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- d<sub>E</sub>

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

This is most easily solved by dimensional analysis: $d$ has to be some function of $l$, $H$, $m$, and $g$. Of these, $g$ is the only quantity with seconds in it; since $d$ doesn’t have any seconds in it, the distance cannot depend on the value of $g$.


</Spoiler>
