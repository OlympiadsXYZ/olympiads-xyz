## Условие

Consider a fixed infinite vertical thin rod (shown by the red color in the figure below) of linear charge density $\lambda$ along the $z$-axis at the origin (see figure below). A uniformly charged ring of total charge $Q$, mass $M$, and radius $a$ is placed with its center at the origin in the $x$-$y$ plane. Point P is an arbitrary point on the ring. The projection of point P on $x$-$y$ plane makes an angle $\theta$ with respect to the $x$-axis in the anticlockwise direction as seen from the top.

The ring is now given an initial angular velocity $\omega_0$ about the $x$-axis. We define the angle $\alpha$ which the plane of the ring makes with the $x$-$y$ plane. This is illustrated by drawing line segment AB in the plane of the ring. Initially $\alpha = 0$. Ignore gravity.

You may find the following differentiation useful

$$
D = \frac{d}{d\theta}[\tan^{-1}(q\tan\theta)] = \frac{1}{1 + (q\tan\theta)^2}[q(\sec^2\theta)] \qquad (4.1)
$$

**(a)** **[1 marks]** State an expression for the electric field ($\vec{E}_0$) due to the infinite rod at a point on the ring when $\alpha=0$ in terms of $x, y$ and $\theta$, and related quantities. **[1 т.]**

**(b)** **[2 marks]** At some instant the ring makes an angle $\alpha$. Derive an expression for the electric field $\vec{E}$ due to the infinite rod at a point on the ring in terms of $\theta$, and $\alpha$. **[2 т.]**

**(c)** **[1 marks]** Find the net force $\vec{F}$ acting on the ring. **[1 т.]**

**(d)** **[5 marks]** Find the net torque $\vec{\tau}$ acting on the ring in terms of $\alpha$ and the constants only. Qualitatively plot torque as a function of $\alpha$. **[5 т.]**

**(e)** **[2 marks]** Let the ring is in equilibrium with respect to $\alpha = 0$. Derive an expression for the time period $T$ of small oscillations of the ring in terms of $\lambda$, and $Q$. Take $\lambda = 0.1\mu\,\mathrm{C/m}$, $Q = 2.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$, $M = 50.0\mathrm{g}$, radius $a = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, and $\omega_0 = 1.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. Calculate $T$. **[2 т.]**

**(f)** **[2.5 marks]** Find an expression for the potential energy $U$ of the ring in terms of $\alpha$. Qualitatively plot $U$ as a function of $\alpha$. Take the zero of potential energy to be at $\alpha = 0$. **[2,5 т.]**

**(g)** **[2.5 marks]** Obtain the expression of maximum value of $\alpha$ ($\alpha_{\max}$) in terms of $\omega_0$. Calculate $\alpha_{\max}$. **[2,5 т.]**

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- **(a)** $\vec{E}_0 = \frac{\lambda(\cos\theta\hat{x} + \sin\theta\hat{y})}{2\pi\epsilon_0 a}$
- **(b)** $\vec{E} = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0 a}\sqrt{(1 + \sin^2\theta\tan^2\alpha)}(\cos\theta\hat{x} + \sin\theta\hat{y})$
- **(c)** The total force acting on the ring is zero.
- **(d)** $\tau_x = -\frac{\lambda Q}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\tan(\alpha/2)$
- **(e)** $T = 2\pi a\sqrt{\frac{2M\pi\epsilon_0}{Q\lambda}}$ ≈ $1.17$ s
- **(f)** $U(\alpha) = -\frac{\lambda Q}{\pi\epsilon_0}\log(\cos(\alpha/2))$
- **(g)** $\alpha_{\max} = 2\left[\cos^{-1}\left(\exp\left(-\frac{\omega_0^2 T^2}{32\pi^2}\right)\right)\right]$ ≈ $10.66$ °

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**(a)** **Solution:** The electric field on the ring due to the infinite rod is given by

$$
\vec{E}_0 = \frac{\lambda(x\hat{x} + y\hat{y})}{2\pi\epsilon_0(x^2 + y^2)} \qquad (4.2)
$$

Since the rod is infinite, the electric field will not depend on $z$.

$$
\vec{E}_0 = \frac{\lambda(a\cos\theta\hat{x} + a\sin\theta\hat{y})}{2\pi\epsilon_0(a^2\cos^2\theta + a^2\sin^2\theta)} \qquad (4.3)
$$

The above expression simplifies to

$$
\vec{E}_0 = \frac{\lambda(\cos\theta\hat{x} + \sin\theta\hat{y})}{2\pi\epsilon_0 a} \qquad (4.4)
$$

**(b)** **Solution:** The new coordinates of the ring are $x = \dfrac{a\cos\theta}{\sqrt{1 + \sin^2\theta\tan^2\alpha}}$, $y = \dfrac{a\sin\theta}{\sqrt{1 + \sin^2\theta\tan^2\alpha}}$ and $z = \dfrac{a\sin\theta\tan\alpha}{\sqrt{1 + \sin^2\theta\tan^2\alpha}}$.

$$
\vec{E} = \frac{\lambda(x\hat{x} + y\hat{y})}{2\pi\epsilon_0(x^2 + y^2)} \qquad (4.5)
$$

$$
= \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0 a}\sqrt{(1 + \sin^2\theta\tan^2\alpha)}(\cos\theta\hat{x} + \sin\theta\hat{y}) \qquad (4.6)
$$

**(c)** **Solution:** Charge $dQ$ in elementary length is $\dfrac{Q}{2\pi\cos\alpha}\dfrac{1}{(1 + \sin^2\theta\tan^2\alpha)}d\theta$. Force on small element $ds$ is

$$
d\vec{F} = \vec{E}dQ = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0 a}\sqrt{(1 + \sin^2\theta\tan^2\alpha)}(\cos\theta\hat{x} + \sin\theta\hat{y})\frac{Q}{2\pi\cos\alpha}\frac{1}{(1 + \sin^2\theta\tan^2\alpha)}d\theta \qquad (4.7)
$$

which simplifies to

$$
d\vec{F} = \frac{\lambda Q}{4\pi^2\epsilon_0 a\cos\alpha}\frac{\cos\theta\hat{x} + \sin\theta\hat{y}}{\sqrt{(1 + \sin^2\theta\tan^2\alpha)}} \qquad (4.8)
$$

where $C = \lambda Q/4\pi^2\epsilon_o a$. Consider

$$
dF_x = \frac{C}{\cos\alpha}\frac{\cos\theta}{\sqrt{(1 + \sin^2\theta\tan^2\alpha)}}d\theta \qquad (4.9)
$$

$$
F_x = \frac{C}{\cos\alpha}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\cos\theta}{\sqrt{(1 + \sin^2\theta\tan^2\alpha)}}d\theta \qquad (4.10)
$$

The integrand is an even function, hence

$$
F_x = \frac{2C}{\cos\alpha}\int_0^{\pi}\frac{\cos\theta}{\sqrt{(1 + \sin^2\theta\tan^2\alpha)}}d\theta \qquad (4.12)
$$

using $\int_0^{2a} f(x)dx = \int_0^a f(x)dx + \int_0^a f(2a - x)dx$

$$
F_x = \frac{2C}{\cos\alpha}\left[\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\cos\theta}{(1 + \sin^2\theta\tan^2\alpha)} + \int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\cos(\pi - \theta)}{(1 + \sin^2(\pi - \theta)\tan^2\alpha)}\right] = 0 \qquad (4.14)
$$

Similarly

$$
F_y = 0 \qquad (4.15)
$$

The total force acting on the ring is zero. Answers based on symmetric arguments will be also given credit.

**(d)** **Solution:**

$$
d\tau = \vec{r} \times d\vec{F}(\theta) \qquad (4.16)
$$

Consider

$$
d\tau_z = xdF_y - ydF_x \qquad (4.17)
$$

$$
= 0 \qquad (4.18)
$$

$$
\Longrightarrow \tau_z = 0 \qquad (4.19)
$$

$$
\tau_y = \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} zdF_x\tau_y = \frac{aC\tan\alpha}{2\cos\alpha}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\sin 2\theta}{(1 + \sin^2\theta\tan^2\alpha)} \qquad (4.20)
$$

Integrand is odd function, hence

$$
\tau_y = 0 \qquad (4.21)
$$

$$
\tau_x = \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} -zdF_y \qquad (4.22)
$$

$$
\tau_x = -\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{a\sin\theta\tan\alpha}{\sqrt{1 + \sin^2\theta\tan^2\alpha}}\frac{C\sin\theta}{\cos\alpha(1 + \sin^2\theta\tan^2\theta)} \qquad (4.23)
$$

which simplifies to

$$
\tau_x = -\,aC\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\tan^2\theta\sin\alpha}{(\cos^2\alpha + \tan^2\theta)}d\theta \qquad (4.24)
$$

The integrand is an even function, hence

$$
\tau_x = -2aC\sin\alpha\int_0^{\pi}\frac{\tan^2\theta}{(\cos^2\alpha + \tan^2\theta)}d\theta \qquad (4.25)
$$

using $\int_0^{2a} f(x)dx = \int_0^a f(x)dx + \int_0^a f(2a - x)dx$

$$
\tau_x = -4aC\sin\alpha\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\tan^2\theta}{(\cos^2\alpha + \tan^2\theta)}d\theta \qquad (4.26)
$$

$$
\tau_x = -4aC\sin\alpha\sec^2\alpha\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\tan^2\theta}{(1 + \sec^2\alpha\tan^2\theta)}d\theta \qquad (4.27)
$$

Substituting $\sec\alpha = u$, in above equation, we get

$$
\tau_x = -4aC\sin\alpha u^2\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\tan^2\theta}{(1 + u^2\tan^2\theta)}d\theta \qquad (4.28)
$$

It is given that

$$
D = \frac{d}{d\theta}(\tan^{-1}(u\tan\theta)) = \frac{u\sec^2\theta}{1 + u^2\tan^2\theta} \qquad (4.29)
$$

$$
= \frac{u(1 + \tan^2\theta)}{1 + u^2\tan^2\theta} \qquad (4.30)
$$

$$
D = \frac{u}{1 + u^2\tan^2\theta} + \frac{u\tan^2\theta}{1 + u^2\tan^2\theta} + u - u\frac{1 + u^2\tan^2\theta}{1 + u^2\tan^2\theta} \qquad (4.31)
$$

Solving above equation, we get

$$
\frac{\tan^2\theta}{1 + u^2\tan^2\theta} = \frac{D}{u - u^3} - \frac{1}{1 - u^2} \qquad (4.32)
$$

Hence Substituting above equation in Eq.(4.28) , we get

$$
\tau_x = -4Ca\sin\alpha u^2\int_0^{\pi/2}\left[\frac{D}{u - u^3} - \frac{1}{1 - u^2}\right]d\theta \qquad (4.33)
$$

$$
\tau_x = -4Ca\sin\alpha u^2\int_0^{\pi/2}\left[\frac{D}{u - u^3} - \frac{1}{1 - u^2}\right]d\theta \qquad (4.34)
$$

Substituting value of D, we get

$$
\tau_x = -4Ca\sin\alpha u^2\int_0^{\pi/2}\left[\frac{\frac{d}{d\theta}(\tan^{-1}(u\tan\theta))}{u - u^3} - \frac{1}{1 - u^2}\right]d\theta \qquad (4.35)
$$

$$
\tau_x = -4Ca\sin\alpha u^2\left[\frac{\tan^{-1}(u\tan\theta)}{u - u^3} - \frac{\theta}{1 - u^2}\right]_0^{\pi/2} \qquad (4.36)
$$

Applying limits and solving further, we get

$$
\tau_x = -\frac{\lambda Q}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\tan(\alpha/2) \qquad (4.38)
$$

Working of $\tau_y, \tau_z$ is not required.

**(e)** **Solution:** Under small angle approximation of $\alpha$, $\tau_x$ becomes

$$
\tau_x = \frac{-\lambda Q\alpha}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \qquad (4.39)
$$

$$
I\frac{d^2\alpha}{dt^2} = \frac{-\lambda Q\alpha}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \qquad (4.40)
$$

$$
\frac{Ma^2}{2}\frac{d^2\alpha}{dt^2} = \frac{-\lambda Q\alpha}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \qquad (4.41)
$$

$$
\frac{d^2\alpha}{dt^2} = -\frac{2\lambda Q}{4Ma^2\pi\epsilon_0}\alpha \qquad (4.42)
$$

This is a differential equation of SHM, hence

$$
T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{\frac{2\lambda Q}{4Ma^2\pi\epsilon_0}} \qquad (4.43)
$$

$$
\Longrightarrow T = 2\pi a\sqrt{\frac{2M\pi\epsilon_0}{Q\lambda}} \qquad (4.44)
$$

$$
T = 1.17\mathrm{s} \qquad (4.45)
$$

This can be used as an electrostatic clock!

**(f)** **Solution:** We know that $\tau = -\dfrac{dU}{d\alpha}$

$$
\Longrightarrow U = -\int \tau d\alpha \qquad (4.46)
$$

$$
= \frac{\lambda Q}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\int \tan(\alpha/2)d\alpha \qquad (4.47)
$$

$$
U = -\frac{\lambda Q}{2\pi\epsilon_0}2\log(\cos(\alpha/2)) + c \qquad (4.48)
$$

where $c$ is the constant of integration. At $\alpha = 0$, $U = 0$, which implies that $c = 0$.

$$
\Longrightarrow U(\alpha) = -\frac{\lambda Q}{\pi\epsilon_0}\log(\cos(\alpha/2)) \qquad (4.49)
$$

**(g)** **Solution:** Consider

$$
I\frac{d^2\alpha}{dt^2} = \tau_x \qquad (4.50)
$$

$$
\frac{Ma^2}{2}\frac{d^2\alpha}{dt^2} = \frac{-\lambda Q}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\tan(\alpha/2) \qquad (4.51)
$$

Multiplying both sides by $\frac{d\alpha}{dt}$

$$
\frac{Ma^2}{2}\frac{d^2\alpha}{dt^2}\frac{d\alpha}{dt} = \frac{-\lambda Q}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\tan(\alpha/2)\frac{d\alpha}{dt} \qquad (4.52)
$$

Integrating on both sides

$$
\frac{Ma^2}{4}\left(\frac{d\alpha}{dt}\right)^2 = \frac{\lambda Q}{\pi\epsilon_0}\log(\cos(\alpha/2)) + c' \qquad (4.53)
$$

$$
\left(\frac{d\alpha}{dt}\right)^2 = \frac{32\pi^2}{T^2}\log(\cos(\alpha/2)) + c' \qquad (4.54)
$$

where $c'$ is the constant of integration. At $t = 0$, $\frac{d\alpha}{dt} = \omega_0$, which implies that $c = \omega_0^2$.

$$
\left(\frac{d\alpha}{dt}\right)^2 = \frac{32\pi^2}{T^2}\log(\cos(\alpha/2)) + \omega_0^2 \qquad (4.55)
$$

$$
\frac{d\alpha}{dt} = \sqrt{\frac{32\pi^2}{T^2}\log(\cos(\alpha/2)) + \omega_0^2} \qquad (4.56)
$$

For $\alpha = \alpha_{\max}$, $\frac{d\alpha}{at} = 0$, hence solving above equation, we get

$$
\alpha_{\max} = 2\left[\cos^{-1}\left(\exp\left(-\frac{\omega_0^2 T^2}{32\pi^2}\right)\right)\right] \qquad (4.57)
$$

$$
\alpha_{\max} = 10.66^{\circ} \qquad (4.58)
$$
