## Условие

One mole of an ideal monoatomic gas goes through a linear process from A to B as shown in the pressure-volume ($P$-$V$) diagram. The temperature at A is $T_{\mathrm{A}} = 227^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$. The process is such that, the temperature decreases and the heat is continuously supplied to the gas. The ratio of the specific heat at the constant pressure to that at the constant volume is 5/3. Obtain the expression for the maximum work ($W_{\max}$) the gas can perform in such a process. Calculate $W_{\max}$.

## Отговори

<Spoiler title="Покажи отговорите">

- $W_{\max} = \frac{7}{128}\frac{b^2}{a} \approx 909\,\mathrm{J}$

</Spoiler>

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**Solution:** The variation of $P$ is linear with respect to $V$, hence it can be written as

$$
P = -aV + b \qquad (3.1)
$$

where $a$ and $b$ are positive constants. At A,

$$
P_A V_A = R T_A \qquad (3.2)
$$

$$
(-aV_A + b)V_A = R \times 500 \qquad (3.3)
$$

where $T_A = 500\,\mathrm{K}$. Using the ideal gas equation $PV = RT$

$$
T = \frac{PV}{R} = \frac{-aV^2 + bV}{R} \qquad (3.4)
$$

$$
\frac{dT}{dV} = \frac{-2aV + b}{R} \qquad (3.5)
$$

In this process, $V$ is increasing but the temperature is decreasing, hence

$$
\frac{dT}{dV} \leq 0 \qquad (3.6)
$$

Using Eq. (3.5)

$$
\frac{-2aV + b}{R} \leq 0 \qquad (3.7)
$$

$$
\Longrightarrow V \geq \frac{b}{2a} \qquad (3.8)
$$

This is the lower bound on the volume. This means if we want work done to be maximum

$$
V_{\min} = V_A = \frac{b}{2a} \qquad (3.9)
$$

Using the first law of thermodynamics $dQ = dU + PdV$,

$$
dQ = \frac{R}{\gamma - 1} dT + PdV \qquad (3.10)
$$

where we use $dU = C_V dT$ and Eq.(3.2), Eq. (3.5), and $\gamma = 5/3$ in the above equation yields

$$
dQ = \frac{3R(-2aV + b)dV}{2R} + (-aV + b)dV \qquad (3.11)
$$

$$
\frac{dQ}{dV} = \left(-4aV + \frac{5b}{2}\right) \qquad (3.12)
$$

In the process, heat is taken and volume is also increasing. hence

$$
\frac{dQ}{dV} \geq 0 \qquad (3.13)
$$

$$
-4aV + \frac{5b}{2} \geq 0 \qquad (3.14)
$$

$$
\Longrightarrow V \leq \frac{5b}{8a} \qquad (3.15)
$$

This is the upper bound on the volume. This means if we want work done to be maximum

$$
V_{\max} = V_{\mathrm{B}} = \frac{5b}{8a} \qquad (3.16)
$$

To get maximum work, The gas must expand from $V_A$ to $V_B$

$$
W_{\max} = \int_{V_A}^{V_B} PdV = \int_{V_{\mathrm{A}}}^{V_{\mathrm{B}}} (-aV + b)dV \qquad (3.17)
$$

$$
= \left[\frac{-aV^2}{2} + bV\right]_{b/2a}^{5b/8a} \qquad (3.18)
$$

Substituting the limits, we get,

$$
W_{\max} = \frac{7}{128} \frac{b^2}{a} \qquad (3.19)
$$

Solving $(-aV_A + b)V_A = 500R$, we get $\frac{b^2}{a} = 500 \times 4R$. Substituting this in the above equation, we get,

$$
W_{\max} \approx 909\,\mathrm{J} \qquad (3.20)
$$
