## Условие

A metasurface is a two-dimensional, ultra-thin optical structure consisting of an array of nanospaced optical nano-elements (also known as meta-atoms) on a flat surface (typically an ultra-thin glass plate). The primary function of the nano-elements is to locally introduce a phase shift $\phi(\vec r)$ to the wave, incident at position $\vec r$. This function, $\phi(\vec r)$, is called the phase profile of the metasurface.

Metalens has a circular metasurface with a circularly symmetric phase profile function, $\phi(r)$, which depends on the distance $r$ of the point from the center of the metalens (see figure below). This type of metalens can be used for focusing incoming parallel rays to a point. Unlike normal lenses, the metalens will look just like an ultrathin circular disc.

[[figure:p5-metalens]]

Consider two homogeneous media of refractive indices $n_1$ and $n_2$ separated by a metalens as shown in the figure below. Suppose a beam of plane wave is incident at point $Q$ (at distance $r$ from the pole $P$) on the metasurface at an angle of $\theta_1$ from medium 1. Assume that the rays falling at $Q$ are in the plane containing $PQ$ and the axis of the lens ($x$-$z$ plane). These rays will be refracted at an angle $\theta_2$ in the medium 2. The angle of refraction $\theta_2$ depends on $r$. Thus, the modified law of refraction for the metasurface can be written as

$$
n_1\sin\theta_1-n_2\sin\theta_2=f(r)
$$

Similarly, the rays falling at $Q'$, at a distance $r'$, will be refracted by an angle $\theta'_2$.

**a)** Find $f(r)$ in terms of $\phi(r)$ and $k_o$, the wave number of the incoming wave in a vacuum. To determine $f(r)$, assume two rays in $x$-$z$ plane incident at an angle $\theta_1$ at two infinitesimally close points, $r$ and $r+\Delta r$, are refracted by the same angle $\theta_2$. You don’t need to derive the exact functional form of $\phi(r)$ for this part. **[8 т.]**

**b)** Derive an expression for the phase profile $\phi(r)$, to convert a plane wavefront to spherical wavefront, with light being focused to a point $F$ on the axis (see figure below), which is at a distance $f$ from the pole P.

[[figure:p5-focus]] **[4 т.]**

**c)** Consider a metalens whose phase profile is obtained in part (b). For the paraxial approximation, derive an expression for the lens equation, having object distance $u$ and image distance being $v$, with focal length $f$ (see figure below).

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи официалното решение">

**(a) Solution:**

[[figure:p5-key-wavefronts]]

Consider two rays, which are close to each other, incident locally on metalens. The ray $OA$ is incident at an angle $\theta_1$ at $r$ and exits in medium 2 at an angle $\theta_2$. Similarly the ray $BB'$ exits in medium 2 at an angle of $\theta_2$ from $r+\Delta r$. The rays being very close to each other have a plane wavefront. Let wavefront AB propagate to A’B’ in time $t$. Let $\psi_A,\psi_B,\psi_{A'}$ and $\psi_{B'}$ be phases at points $A,B,A'$ and $B'$ respectively. Then

$$
\psi_{A'}-\psi_A=k_2\Delta r\sin\theta_2-\omega t+\phi(r)
$$

$$
\psi_{B'}-\psi_B=k_1\Delta r\sin\theta_1-\omega t+\phi(r+\Delta r)
$$

Since these two phase differences must be equal, we get

$$
k_2\Delta r\sin\theta_2-k_1\Delta r\sin\theta_1=\phi(r+\Delta r)-\phi(r)
$$

$$
n_2\sin\theta_2-n_1\sin\theta_1=\frac1{k_0}\frac{\phi(r+\Delta r)-\phi(r)}{\Delta r}\ \to\ \boxed{\frac1{k_0}\frac{d\phi(r)}{dr}}
$$

**Alternate solution:**

Time interval for the wavefront to travel from $A$ to $A'$ is equal to the time taken by the wavefront to travel from $B$ to $B'$.

$$
\tau_{AA'}+\tau_{A'}=\tau_{BB'}+\tau_{B'}\qquad(5.1)
$$

where $\tau_{BB'}$ is the time it takes for point B on the wavefront (traveling at speed $v_1$) to reach point B’, and $\tau_{AA'}$ is the time it takes the transmitted portion of that same wavefront (traveling at speed $v_2$) to reach point A’ from point A. The time delays due to the metasurface at points A and B’ are $\tau_A$ and $\tau_B$, respectively. From figure

$$
\tau_{AA'}=\frac1c\Delta r n_2\sin\theta_2
$$

$$
\tau_{BB'}=\frac1c\Delta r n_1\sin\theta_1
$$

$$
\tau_A=\frac{\phi_A}{ck_o}=\frac{\phi(r)}{ck_o}
$$

$$
\tau'_B=\frac{\phi_A}{ck_o}=\frac{\phi(r+\Delta r)}{ck_o}
$$

Substituting above terms in Eq.5.1, we get

$$
\Delta r\sin\theta_2+\frac{\Phi(r)}{k_o}=\Delta r\sin\theta_1+\frac{\Phi(r+\Delta r)}{k_o}
$$

Simplifying:

$$
\frac{\Phi(r+\Delta r)-\Phi(r)}{\Delta r}=\frac1{k_0}\frac{d\Phi}{dr}
$$

This gives the relation:

$$
\boxed{\frac1{k_0}\frac{d\Phi}{dr}=n_2\sin\theta_2-n_1\sin\theta_1}
$$

**(b) Solution:** From the previous part

$$
n_2\sin\theta_2-n_1\sin\theta_1=\frac1{k_o}\frac{d\phi}{dr}
$$

From the above figure, the ray arriving at $r$ (with $\theta_1=0$) must bend by an angle $\theta_2=\sin^{-1}\left(\frac r{\sqrt{f^2+r^2}}\right)$. Thus,

$$
\frac{n_2r}{\sqrt{(f^2+r^2)}}-0=\frac1{k_o}\frac{d\phi}{dr}
$$

Simplifying above equation, we get

$$
\boxed{\phi(r)=\pm k_2\left(\sqrt{(r^2+f^2)}-f\right)}
$$

where $k_2=k_0n_2$.

**(c) Solution:** We know that

$$
n_2\sin\theta_2-n_1\sin\theta_1=\frac1{k_o}\frac{d\phi}{dr}
$$

For paraxial rays, we can use small angle approximation, $\sin\theta_1=r/u$ and then above equation becomes

$$
n_2\theta_2-n_1\theta_1=\frac1{k_o}\frac{d\phi}{dr}
$$

We know that

$$
\frac1{k_0}\frac{d\phi}{dr}=\frac{n_2r}{\sqrt{(r^2+f^2)}}
$$

For paraxial approximation, we get

$$
\frac1{k_0}\frac{d\phi}{dr}=\frac{n_2r}{\sqrt{(r^2+f^2)}}\approx n_2\frac rf
$$

Using these approximations, we get

$$
\frac{n_1}{u}+\frac{n_2}{v}=\frac{n_2}{f}
$$

If $n_1=n_2$, then the above equation becomes

$$
\boxed{\frac1u+\frac1v=\frac1f}\qquad(5.2)
$$

**Source note:** The question defines $f(r)$ with $n_1\sin\theta_1-n_2\sin\theta_2$, while the key derives the reverse order. The alternate solution prints $\tau_{A'},\tau_{B'}$ in (5.1), $\tau_A,\tau_B$ in its prose, and $\tau'_B=\phi_A/(ck_o)$ in its formulas. It changes lowercase $\phi$ to uppercase $\Phi$, drops $n_1,n_2$ in the substitution line, and prints an additional $1/k_0$ on the finite-difference simplification. The source varies $k_o$ and $k_0$ and gives the phase profile with $\pm$. All these native expressions, both alternative derivations and the complete paraxial key are retained literally.
