Preface  
This publication contains the competition problems (Volume 2) from the 21st – 40th  
International Chemistry Olympiads (IChO) organized in the years 1989 – 2008  and is a
continuation of the publication that appeared last year as Volume 1 and contained
competition problems from the first twenty IChOs.  The whole review of the competition
tasks set in the IChO in its fourty-year history is a contribution of the IChO International  
Information Centre in Bratislava (Slovakia) to the development of this world known
international competition. This Volume 2 contains 154 theoretical and 46 practical
competition problems from the mentioned years. The review as a whole presents
altogether 279 theoretical and 96 practical problems.  
In the elaboration of this collection the editor had to face certain difficulties because
the aim was not only to make use of past recordings but also to give them such a form
that they may be used in practice and further chemical education. Consequently, it was
necessary to make some corrections in order to unify the form of the problems. However,
they did not concern the contents and language of the problems.  
Unfortunately, the authors of the particular competition problems are not known and
due to the procedure of the creation of the IChO competition problems, it is impossible to
assign any author's name to a particular problem. As the editor I would appreciate many  
times some discussion with the authors about any critical places that occurred in the text.  
On the other hand, any additional amendments to the text would be not correct from the  
historical point of view. Therefore, responsibility for the scientific content and language of
the problems lies exclusively with the organizers of the particular International Chemistry  
Olympiads.    

Some parts of texts, especially those gained as scanned materials, could not be
used directly and thus, several texts, schemes and pictures had to be re-written or created
again. Some solutions were often available in a brief form and necessary extent only, just
for the needs of members of the International Jury.  

Recalculations of the solutions were made in some special cases only when the
numeric results in the original solutions showed to be obviously not correct. Although the
numbers of significant figures in the results of several solutions do not obey the criteria
generally accepted, they were left without change.      



In this publication SI quantities and units are used and a more modern method of
chemical calculations is introduced. Only some exceptions have been made when, in an
effort to preserve the original text, the quantities and units have been used that are not SI.  
There were some problems with the presentation of the solutions of practical tasks,
because most of the relatively simple calculations were based on the experimental results
of contestants. Moreover, the practical problems are accompanied with answer sheets in
the last years and several additional questions and tasks have appeared in them that were
not a part of the text of the original experimental problems. Naturally, answer sheets could
not be included in this publication and can only be preserved as archive materials.
  
When reading the texts of the IChO problems one must admire and appreciate the
work of those many known and unknown people – teachers, authors, pupils, and
organizers – who contributed so much to development and success of this important
international competition.

I am sure about the usefulness of the this review of the IChO problems. It may serve
not only as archive material but, in particular, this review should serve to both competitors
and their teachers as a source of further inspiration in their preparation for this challenging
competition.




Bratislava, July 2009  






  

                                    Anton Sirota, editor

## Условие

**В тази задача**

</nav>


After heavy spring rain, fresh-water rivers entering Chesapeake Bay reduce the salinity where oysters grow. Normal oyster growth needs a minimum chloride concentration of $8\ \mathrm{ppm}$ ($8\ \mathrm{mg\,dm^{-3}}$).

After one week of heavy rain, a $50.00\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ bay-water sample receives a few drops of $\mathrm{K_2CrO_4}$ solution and is titrated with $16.16\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ of $0.00164\ \mathrm M$ $\mathrm{AgNO_3}$. A bright red-orange precipitate forms.

<ProblemSection id="native-section-1">

### Questions




**7.1**  
Find the molar chloride concentration in the sample.



**7.2**  
Is there enough chloride for normal oyster growth? Show the calculation.



**7.3**  
Write a balanced analyte–titrant reaction equation.



**7.4**  
Write the balanced net-ionic endpoint reaction. Which compound gives the brick-red colour?



**7.5**  
At the endpoint $[\mathrm{CrO_4^{2-}}]=0.020\ \mathrm M$. Calculate the chloride concentration when the red precipitate forms.



**7.6**  
The titration works best in neutral or slightly basic solution. Write the balanced competing reaction in acidic medium that affects the endpoint.

The sample has pH $5.10$, too acidic for accurate analysis. A buffer is normally added.



**7.7**  
Select a buffer giving pH $7.20$ and show the calculations leading to the choice:

| System | Composition | Weak-acid $K_a$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | $0.10\ \mathrm M$ lactic acid / $0.10\ \mathrm M$ sodium lactate | $1.4\times10^{-4}$ |
| 2 | $0.10\ \mathrm M$ acetic acid / $0.10\ \mathrm M$ sodium acetate | $1.8\times10^{-5}$ |
| 3 | $0.10\ \mathrm M$ sodium dihydrogen phosphate / $0.10\ \mathrm M$ sodium hydrogen phosphate | $6.2\times10^{-8}$ |
| 4 | $0.10\ \mathrm M$ ammonium chloride / $0.10\ \mathrm M$ ammonia | $5.6\times10^{-10}$ |



**7.8**  
For the selected system, calculate the masses of weak acid and conjugate base needed to make $500\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ of stock solution buffered at pH $7.2$.



**7.9**  
The chloride in another $50.00\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ sample is determined by the Volhard method. Excess $\mathrm{AgNO_3}$ is added, and excess $\mathrm{Ag^+}$ is titrated with standardized KSCN to precipitate AgSCN. Once $\mathrm{Ag^+}$ is depleted, reddish-brown $\mathrm{FeSCN^{2+}}$ signals the endpoint.

The excess silver from $50.00\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ of $0.00129\ \mathrm M$ $\mathrm{AgNO_3}$ requires $27.46\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ of $1.41\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm M$ KSCN. Calculate the sample's chloride concentration.

In water with much more chloride, gravimetric determination precipitates AgCl. AgCl undergoes photodecomposition:

$$
\mathrm{AgCl(s)}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Ag(s)}+\frac12\mathrm{Cl_2(g)}.
$$

With excess silver ions, a further reaction occurs:

$$
3\mathrm{Cl_2(g)}+3\mathrm{H_2O}+5\mathrm{Ag^+}
\longrightarrow5\mathrm{AgCl}+\mathrm{ClO_3^-}+6\mathrm{H^+}.
$$



**7.10**  
If $0.010\ \mathrm g$ of a $3.000\ \mathrm g$ AgCl sample contaminated with excess silver ions undergoes these reactions, is the apparent AgCl weight too high or too low? Explain and calculate the mass difference.

Data: $K_{\mathrm{sp}}(\mathrm{AgCl})=1.78\times10^{-10}$; $K_{\mathrm{sp}}(\mathrm{Ag_2CrO_4})=1.00\times10^{-12}$.



</ProblemSection>

### Source notes

The key's $4x^3$ expression in 7.5 is retained alongside its stated $0.020\ \mathrm M$ chromate. The alternative hydrogen-chromate equation in 7.6 retains the printed water term. In 7.9 the question gives $0.00141\ \mathrm M$ KSCN, while the key substitution prints $0.0141$ but states $3.87\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{mol}$; these disagreements are visible and unchanged. All ten connected requests, buffer choices and gravimetric extension remain one whole problem.

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи решението от архива">




<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-1">

### 7.1


$n(\mathrm{Ag^+})=n(\mathrm{Cl^-})$:

$$
c(\mathrm{Cl^-})
=\frac{0.01616\ \mathrm{dm^3}\times0.00164\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}}
{0.050\ \mathrm{dm^3}}
=5.30\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}.
$$



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-2">

### 7.2


$$
5.30\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}\times35.5\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}
=0.0188\ \mathrm{g\,dm^{-3}}
=18.8\ \mathrm{mg\,dm^{-3}}.
$$

This is sufficient for normal oyster growth.



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-3">

### 7.3


$$
\mathrm{Ag^+(aq)}+\mathrm{Cl^-(aq)}\longrightarrow\mathrm{AgCl(s)}\downarrow.
$$



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-4">

### 7.4


$$
2\mathrm{Ag^+(aq)}+\mathrm{CrO_4^{2-}(aq)}
\longrightarrow\mathrm{Ag_2CrO_4(s)}\downarrow
$$

Silver chromate gives the brick-red colour.



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-5">

### 7.5


The key prints

$$
K_{\mathrm{sp}}(\mathrm{Ag_2CrO_4})=[\mathrm{Ag^+}]^2[\mathrm{CrO_4^{2-}}]=4x^3,\quad x=[\mathrm{Ag^+}],
$$

then gives $[\mathrm{Ag^+}]=7.07\times10^{-6}$ and $[\mathrm{CrO_4^{2-}}]=2\times10^{-2}$.

$$
[\mathrm{Cl^-}]
=\frac{K_{\mathrm{sp}}(\mathrm{AgCl})}{[\mathrm{Ag^+}]}
=\frac{1.78\times10^{-10}}{7.07\times10^{-6}}
=2.5\times10^{-5}.
$$



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-6">

### 7.6


$$
2\mathrm{CrO_4^{2-}}+2\mathrm{H^+}
\longrightarrow\mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-}}+\mathrm{H_2O},
$$

or, as printed,

$$
\mathrm{CrO_4^{2-}}+\mathrm{H^+}\longrightarrow\mathrm{HCrO_4^-}+\mathrm{H_2O}.
$$



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-7">

### 7.7


Maximum buffer capacity occurs when $\mathrm{pH}=\mathrm pK_a$. System 3 is best, since $\mathrm pK_a=7.2$.



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-8">

### 7.8


$$
m(\mathrm{NaH_2PO_4})
=0.10\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}\times0.500\ \mathrm{dm^3}
\times119.98\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}=6.0\ \mathrm g,
$$

$$
m(\mathrm{Na_2HPO_4})
=0.10\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}\times0.500\ \mathrm{dm^3}
\times141.96\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}=7.1\ \mathrm g.
$$



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-9">

### 7.9


$$
n(\mathrm{Ag^+})_{\mathrm{added}}
=0.05\ \mathrm{dm^3}\times0.00129\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}
=6.45\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{mol}.
$$

The key's next substitution prints $0.0141\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}$:

$$
n(\mathrm{Ag^+})_{\mathrm{left}}
=0.02746\ \mathrm{dm^3}\times0.0141\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}
=3.87\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{mol}.
$$

$$
n(\mathrm{Cl^-})=(6.45-3.87)\times10^{-5}
=2.58\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{mol},
$$

$$
[\mathrm{Cl^-}]=\frac{2.58\times10^{-5}}{0.050}=5.16\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}.
$$



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-10">

### 7.10


$$
n(\mathrm{AgCl})_{\mathrm{lost}}
=\frac{0.010\ \mathrm g}{143.35\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}}
=6.98\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{mol}.
$$

$n(\mathrm{Cl_2})=\frac12(6.98\times10^{-5})=3.49\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{mol}$.

New AgCl is $\frac53(3.49\times10^{-5})=5.82\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{mol}$, equivalent to $8.34\ \mathrm{mg}$.

The silver amount equals the amount of AgCl lost:

$$
[\mathrm{Ag}]_{\mathrm{formed}}
=6.98\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{mol}\times107.9\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}
=7.53\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm g.
$$

The sample mass is $3.0-0.010+0.00834+0.00753=3.006\ \mathrm g$. Thus the total solid mass (AgCl + Ag) is too high by $6\ \mathrm{mg}$.



</ProblemSection>
