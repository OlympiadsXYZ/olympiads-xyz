This publication contains the competition problems (Volume 2) from the 21st – 40th  
International Chemistry Olympiads (IChO) organized in the years 1989 – 2008  and is a
continuation of the publication that appeared last year as Volume 1 and contained
competition problems from the first twenty IChOs.  The whole review of the competition
tasks set in the IChO in its fourty-year history is a contribution of the IChO International  
Information Centre in Bratislava (Slovakia) to the development of this world known
international competition. This Volume 2 contains 154 theoretical and 46 practical
competition problems from the mentioned years. The review as a whole presents
altogether 279 theoretical and 96 practical problems.  
In the elaboration of this collection the editor had to face certain difficulties because
the aim was not only to make use of past recordings but also to give them such a form
that they may be used in practice and further chemical education. Consequently, it was
necessary to make some corrections in order to unify the form of the problems. However,
they did not concern the contents and language of the problems.  
Unfortunately, the authors of the particular competition problems are not known and
due to the procedure of the creation of the IChO competition problems, it is impossible to
assign any author's name to a particular problem. As the editor I would appreciate many  
times some discussion with the authors about any critical places that occurred in the text.  
On the other hand, any additional amendments to the text would be not correct from the  
historical point of view. Therefore, responsibility for the scientific content and language of
the problems lies exclusively with the organizers of the particular International Chemistry  
Olympiads.    

Some parts of texts, especially those gained as scanned materials, could not be
used directly and thus, several texts, schemes and pictures had to be re-written or created
again. Some solutions were often available in a brief form and necessary extent only, just
for the needs of members of the International Jury.  

Recalculations of the solutions were made in some special cases only when the
numeric results in the original solutions showed to be obviously not correct. Although the
numbers of significant figures in the results of several solutions do not obey the criteria
generally accepted, they were left without change.      



In this publication SI quantities and units are used and a more modern method of
chemical calculations is introduced. Only some exceptions have been made when, in an
effort to preserve the original text, the quantities and units have been used that are not SI.  
There were some problems with the presentation of the solutions of practical tasks,
because most of the relatively simple calculations were based on the experimental results
of contestants. Moreover, the practical problems are accompanied with answer sheets in
the last years and several additional questions and tasks have appeared in them that were
not a part of the text of the original experimental problems. Naturally, answer sheets could
not be included in this publication and can only be preserved as archive materials.
  
When reading the texts of the IChO problems one must admire and appreciate the
work of those many known and unknown people – teachers, authors, pupils, and
organizers – who contributed so much to development and success of this important
international competition.

I am sure about the usefulness of the this review of the IChO problems. It may serve
not only as archive material but, in particular, this review should serve to both competitors
and their teachers as a source of further inspiration in their preparation for this challenging
competition.




Bratislava, July 2009

Laboratory $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_5NO_2]Cl_2}$ often contains a considerable $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_6]Cl_3}$ by-product. Determine the amount of the first complex in a sample containing only the second as impurity, by cation exchange. The resin is strongly acidic cross-linked polystyrene containing $\mathrm{-SO_3H}$ groups. One mole $\mathrm{M^{n+}}$ reacting with it releases $n$ moles $\mathrm{H^+}$. The ion-exchanged mixture solution titrates standardized NaOH.

<ProblemSection id="native-section-1">

### Preparation of the cation exchange resin


About 10 g wet resin in H⁺ form is supplied. Remove all free acid as follows.



**1**  
Transfer to a 250 cm³ beaker, washing from its container with about 50 cm³ water. Allow a few minutes to settle.



**2**  
Carefully decant as much acidic solution as possible into a waste beaker, minimizing resin loss. Wash with approximately 20 cm³ distilled-water portions. Use a glass rod and pH paper to test a drop of wash until excess acid is completely removed, at about pH 5. No more than 200 cm³ water should be needed.



**3**  
Drain until the resin is just covered. Put all acidic washings into the “acid waste” bottle, not down the sink. Do not let the resin dry.



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="native-section-2">

### Preparation and standardization of approximately 0.0125 M NaOH




**4**  
Prepare 250.0 cm³ approximately 0.0125 M NaOH by accurately diluting the supplied approximately 0.125 M solution with distilled water in a volumetric flask.



**5**  
Using the supplied standard 0.01253 M HCl, titrate 25.00 cm³ diluted NaOH aliquots with phenolphthalein indicator.



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="native-section-3">

### Analysis procedure


Approximately 40 cm³ of 0.00500 M HCl containing 0.2000 g mixture in 40.00 cm³ is supplied.



**6**  
Pipette a 25.00 cm³ aliquot of cobalt complex solution into a 250 cm³ beaker (No. 1); add about 25 cm³ water.



**7**  
With a plastic spoon add about half the wet resin, approximately 5 g. Stand at least five minutes for exchange and H⁺ release. Occasionally swirl gently to hasten exchange.



**8**  
Carefully wash the acidic solution into a second 250 cm³ beaker (No. 2) with about 20 cm³ distilled water, leaving as much resin behind as possible. Its lighter colour indicates most, but not all, cobalt complex is bound to resin. Remove the last cobalt(III) traces, releasing more acid, using a second resin batch.



**9**  
Add most remaining resin, about 4 g, to beaker No. 2. Stand at least five minutes for further exchange. The solution should then be colourless. If not, perhaps insufficient exchange time was allowed; repeat exchange and washing with the final approximately 1 g resin.



**10**  
Filter both resin samples through carefully washed filter paper. Collect the acidic filtrate in a 100 cm³ volumetric flask. Carefully wash resin with small water portions into the flask; make up to the mark.



**11**  
Use this acid solution to titrate 25.00 cm³ standardized NaOH aliquots.

Calculate the moles H⁺ liberated by the 25 cm³ mixture aliquot and report the percentage of $\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_5NO_2]Cl_2}$ in the sample. Molar masses / g mol⁻¹: Co 58.93, N 14.01, H 1.01, Cl 35.45, O 16.00.



</ProblemSection>

**Model solution.** Standard HCl in the bottle: $0.01253\ldots\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}$.

| NaOH standardization titration | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NaOH aliquot / cm³ | 25.00 | 25.00 | 25.00 |
| Initial burette / cm³ | 13.60 | 17.40 | 10.35 |
| Final burette / cm³ | 37.75 | 41.50 | 34.45 |
| Volume of standard KMnO₄ / cm³ — native label | 24.15 | 24.10 | 24.10 |

Average titre 24.12 cm³. The native displayed calculation is

$$
c(\mathrm{NaOH})=0.01250\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}\times0.02412\ \mathrm{dm^3}=0.01206\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}.
$$

The cobalt-solution aliquot used for exchange is 25 cm³.

| Acid titration | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Initial burette / cm³ | 26.25 | 16.10 | 3.80 |
| Final burette / cm³ | 48.50 | 38.40 | 26.20 |
| Volume of standard KMnO₄ / cm³ — native label | 22.25 | 22.30 | 22.40 |

Average titre 22.32 cm³. Acid collected in 100 cm³ flask:

$$
c(\mathrm{H^+})=0.01206\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}\frac{25.00\ \mathrm{cm^3}}{22.32\ \mathrm{cm^3}}=0.01351\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}.
$$

Total collected H⁺ is $0.01351(0.100)=1.351\times10^{-3}$ mol. Acid initially put on the column is $0.00500(0.02500)=1.250\times10^{-4}$ mol. The complexes therefore release $1.351\times10^{-3}-1.250\times10^{-4}=1.226\times10^{-3}$ mol.

Let $y$ be the mass of nitropentaammine complex in the aliquot. Total mixture mass is $(25.00/40)(0.2000)=0.1250$ g; hexaammine mass is $(0.1250-y)$ g. The key prints

$$
n(\mathrm{H^+})_{\text{nitro}}=\frac{2y}{261.00\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}}=0.007663x\ \mathrm{mol},
$$

$$
n(\mathrm{H^+})_{\text{hexa}}=\frac{3(0.1250-y)\ \mathrm{g}}{267.50\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}}=(0.001402-0.011214y)\ \mathrm{mol}.
$$

Thus $0.007663y+(0.001402-0.011214y)=0.001226$, giving $y=0.04956$ g and

$$
\%\mathrm{[Co(NH_3)_5NO_2]Cl_2}=100\frac{0.04956}{0.1250}=39.6\%.
$$




</Spoiler>
