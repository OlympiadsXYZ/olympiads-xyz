Purification of water is one of the most important problems of a big city. In Moscow, water is quite clean; one can drink cold tap water without any additional filtrations or other procedures. Such purity is achieved by the integrated use of mechanical, physico-chemical and chemical methods in the urban water purification system.

**1** Chlorination is the most common method of water purification. Indicate what other methods are suitable for use in urban water treatment systems.

1) UV irradiation

2) Ozonation

3) Binding of heavy metal ions by concentrated ammonia solutions

4) Oxidation by silver and gold ions

5) Adsorption on carbon filters

6) Precipitation of heavy metal ions by hydrogen sulfide

Numbers of the correct answers: ______ **[3 т.]**

For many years in Moscow, pure chlorine was used for chlorination of water. Chlorine was stored as a liquid at elevated pressure. However, since 2012 urban services use another reagent – sodium hypochlorite, which is safe for humans, but effectively kills most types of microorganisms. Being dissolved in water, both chlorinating reagents form (reversibly) the same substance.

### Solutions

**1** Numbers of the correct answers:1,2,5.

1 point for each correct answer,

−1 point for each wrong answer **[3 т.]**

**2** $\mathrm{H{-}O{-}Cl}$ — 2 points

(1 point without structure) **[2 т.]**

**3a** Calculation

$\mathrm{OCl^-+H_2O=HOCl+OH^-}$ — 1 point

$$
K=\frac{[\mathrm{HOCl}][\mathrm{OH^-}]}{[\mathrm{OCl^-}]}=\frac{[\mathrm{OH^-}]^2}{C-[\mathrm{OH^-}]}=\frac{K_\mathrm w}{K_\mathrm a}=10^{-6.46}
$$

— 1 point

At $\mathrm{pH}=9$, $[\mathrm{OH^-}]=10^{-5}\ \mathrm M$.

$C(\mathrm{OCl^-})=[\mathrm{OH^-}]^2/K+[\mathrm{OH^-}]=2.98\cdot10^{-4}\ \mathrm M$ — 0.5 points

$m(\mathrm{NaOCl})=2.98\cdot10^{-4}\cdot74.5\cdot10^3=22\ \mathrm{mg}$ — 0.5 points **[3 т.]**

**3b** Calculation

$$
\mathrm{HOCl=H^++OCl^-}
$$

$$
K_\mathrm a=\frac{[\mathrm{H^+}][\mathrm{OCl^-}]}{[\mathrm{HOCl}]}
$$

— 1 point

If $[\mathrm{OCl^-}]=[\mathrm{HOCl}]$, then $[\mathrm{H^+}]=K_\mathrm a$, $\mathrm{pH}=pK_\mathrm a$.

pH=7.54 — 1 point **[2 т.]**

**4a** Answer (yes, no): Yes

Reaction equation or arguments:

$$
\mathrm{NaClO_2+4HCl=NaCl+2Cl_2+2H_2O},
$$

$m(\mathrm{Cl_2})/m(\mathrm{NaClO_2})>1$

2 points **[2 т.]**

**4b** Calculation

$\mathrm{NaOCl+2HCl=Cl_2+NaCl+H_2O}$ — 1 point

Take100 g of the solution. $m(\mathrm{NaOCl})=15\ \mathrm g$, $n(\mathrm{NaOCl})=15/74.5=0.20\ \mathrm{mol}=n(\mathrm{Cl_2})$.

$m(\mathrm{Cl_2})=0.2\cdot71=14.2\ \mathrm g$, $m(\mathrm{Cl_2})/m(\text{solution})=14.2\%$ — 1 point

% of “active chlorine”=14.2% **[2 т.]**

**5** Reagent: NaCl — 1 point

Cathode process: $\mathrm{2H_2O+2e\rightarrow H_2\uparrow+2OH^-}$ — 1 point

Anode process: $\mathrm{Cl^-+2OH^--2e\rightarrow OCl^-+H_2O}$ — 1 point

Overall equation: $\mathrm{Cl^-+H_2O\rightarrow OCl^-+H_2\uparrow}$

or $\mathrm{NaCl+H_2O\rightarrow NaOCl+H_2\uparrow}$ — 1 point **[4 т.]**

**6** Calculation:

Total volume of water: $141\cdot14.2\cdot10^6=2.0\cdot10^9\ \mathrm L$ — 0.5 points

Total mass of active chlorine: $2.0\cdot10^9\ \mathrm L\cdot0.3\cdot10^{-3}\ \mathrm{g/L}=6.0\cdot10^5\ \mathrm g=600\ \mathrm{kg}$ — 0.5 points

$m(\text{solution})=600/0.142=4200\ \mathrm{kg}=4.2\ \mathrm t$ — 1 point **[2 т.]**
