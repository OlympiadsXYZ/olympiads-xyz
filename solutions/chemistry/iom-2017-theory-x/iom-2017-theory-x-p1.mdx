The white substance **X₁** can be obtained by burning of powdered metal **X** in oxygen (*reaction 1*). The treatment of a mixture of **X₁** and carbon with chlorine gives a colorless liquid **X₂** containing 25.24 wt.% of element **X** (*reaction 2*). The vapor of **X₂** reacts with ammonia at $1000^\circ\mathrm C$ giving a brown powder **X₃**, containing from 77.37 to 85.07 wt.% of **X** (*reaction 3*). The compound **X₃** is responsible for golden color of domes of several Moscow churches and cathedrals.

The compound **X₃** can be synthesized directly from **X** or **X₁** (*reactions 4 and 5*).

### Solutions

**1** Calculations

$\mathrm{X_1}$ is an oxide, hence $\mathrm{X_2}$ is a chloride of metal X. Denote chloride as $\mathrm{XCl_n}$, then

$$
\omega(\mathrm X)=\frac{M(\mathrm X)}{M(\mathrm X)+35.45n}=0.2524
$$

$$
M(\mathrm X)=11.87n.
$$

At $n=4$, we get $M(\mathrm X)=47.87\ \mathrm{g/mol}$, X – titanium. X₁ is titanium dioxide, TiO₂.

Reaction between titanium chloride and ammonia gives titanium nitride – the substance with a non-stoichiometric composition. Let us determine the range of its compositions:

$$
\mathrm{TiN_x}:\quad\omega(\mathrm{Ti})=\frac{47.87}{47.87+14.01x}=0.7737,\quad x=1.
$$

$$
\mathrm{TiN_y}:\quad\omega(\mathrm{Ti})=\frac{47.87}{47.87+14.01y}=0.8507,\quad y=0.6.
$$

X – titanium, Ti — 1 points

X₁ – TiO₂ — 1.5 points

X₂ – TiCl₄ — 1.5 points

X₃ – TiN₀.₆–TiN₁.₀ — 3 points **[7 т.]**

**2** Reaction 1: $\mathrm{Ti+O_2=TiO_2}$ — 1 point

Reaction 2: $\mathrm{TiO_2+2C+2Cl_2=TiCl_4+2CO}$ — 2 points

Reaction 3: $\mathrm{2TiCl_4+2NH_3=2TiN+6HCl+Cl_2}$ — 2 points

Reaction 4: $\mathrm{2Ti+N_2=2TiN}$ — 2 points

Reaction 5: $\mathrm{2TiO_2+4C+N_2=2TiN+4CO}$ — 2 points **[9 т.]**

**3** Structural type – sodium chloride, NaCl (39.3% of Na) — 2 points **[2 т.]**

**4** Coordination polyhedron of:

cation – octahedron — 1 point

anion – octahedron — 1 point **[2 т.]**
