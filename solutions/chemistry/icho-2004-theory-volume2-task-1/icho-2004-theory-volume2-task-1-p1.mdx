Preface  
This publication contains the competition problems (Volume 2) from the 21st – 40th  
International Chemistry Olympiads (IChO) organized in the years 1989 – 2008  and is a
continuation of the publication that appeared last year as Volume 1 and contained
competition problems from the first twenty IChOs.  The whole review of the competition
tasks set in the IChO in its fourty-year history is a contribution of the IChO International  
Information Centre in Bratislava (Slovakia) to the development of this world known
international competition. This Volume 2 contains 154 theoretical and 46 practical
competition problems from the mentioned years. The review as a whole presents
altogether 279 theoretical and 96 practical problems.  
In the elaboration of this collection the editor had to face certain difficulties because
the aim was not only to make use of past recordings but also to give them such a form
that they may be used in practice and further chemical education. Consequently, it was
necessary to make some corrections in order to unify the form of the problems. However,
they did not concern the contents and language of the problems.  
Unfortunately, the authors of the particular competition problems are not known and
due to the procedure of the creation of the IChO competition problems, it is impossible to
assign any author's name to a particular problem. As the editor I would appreciate many  
times some discussion with the authors about any critical places that occurred in the text.  
On the other hand, any additional amendments to the text would be not correct from the  
historical point of view. Therefore, responsibility for the scientific content and language of
the problems lies exclusively with the organizers of the particular International Chemistry  
Olympiads.    

Some parts of texts, especially those gained as scanned materials, could not be
used directly and thus, several texts, schemes and pictures had to be re-written or created
again. Some solutions were often available in a brief form and necessary extent only, just
for the needs of members of the International Jury.  

Recalculations of the solutions were made in some special cases only when the
numeric results in the original solutions showed to be obviously not correct. Although the
numbers of significant figures in the results of several solutions do not obey the criteria
generally accepted, they were left without change.      



In this publication SI quantities and units are used and a more modern method of
chemical calculations is introduced. Only some exceptions have been made when, in an
effort to preserve the original text, the quantities and units have been used that are not SI.  
There were some problems with the presentation of the solutions of practical tasks,
because most of the relatively simple calculations were based on the experimental results
of contestants. Moreover, the practical problems are accompanied with answer sheets in
the last years and several additional questions and tasks have appeared in them that were
not a part of the text of the original experimental problems. Naturally, answer sheets could
not be included in this publication and can only be preserved as archive materials.
  
When reading the texts of the IChO problems one must admire and appreciate the
work of those many known and unknown people – teachers, authors, pupils, and
organizers – who contributed so much to development and success of this important
international competition.

I am sure about the usefulness of the this review of the IChO problems. It may serve
not only as archive material but, in particular, this review should serve to both competitors
and their teachers as a source of further inspiration in their preparation for this challenging
competition.




Bratislava, July 2009  






  

                                    Anton Sirota, editor

For his 18th birthday party in February Peter plans to turn a hut in the garden of his parents into a swimming pool with an artificial beach. In order to estimate the costs for heating the water and the house, Peter obtains the data for the natural gas composition and its price.

### 2004 — Theoretical 1: Thermodynamics

**1.1**  
Write down the chemical equations for the complete combustion of the main components of natural gas, methane and ethane, given in Table 1. Assume that nitrogen is inert under the chosen conditions.

Calculate the reaction enthalpy, the reaction entropy, and the Gibbs energy under standard conditions ($1.013\cdot10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}$, $25.0{}^\circ\mathrm C$) for the combustion of methane and ethane according to the equations above assuming that all products are gaseous.

The thermodynamic properties and the composition of natural gas can be found in Table 1.



**1.2**  
The density of natural gas is $0.740\ \mathrm{g\,dm^{-3}}$ ($1.013\times10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}$, $25.0{}^\circ\mathrm C$) specified by PUC, the public utility company.

#### a)

Calculate the amount of methane and ethane (in moles) in $1.00\ \mathrm{m^3}$ of natural gas (natural gas, methane, and ethane are not ideal gases!).

#### b)

Calculate the combustion energy which is released as thermal energy during the burning of $1.00\ \mathrm{m^3}$ of natural gas under standard conditions assuming that all products are gaseous. (If you do not have the amount from 1.2a) assume that $1.00\ \mathrm{m^3}$ natural gas corresponds to $40.00\ \mathrm{mol}$ natural gas.)

According to the PUC the combustion energy will be $9.981\ \mathrm{kWh}$ per $\mathrm{m^3}$ of natural gas if all products are gaseous. How large is the deviation (in percent) from the value you obtained in b)

The swimming pool inside the house is $3.00\ \mathrm m$ wide, $5.00\ \mathrm m$ long and $1.50\ \mathrm m$ deep (below the floor). The tap water temperature is $8.00{}^\circ\mathrm C$ and the air temperature in the house (dimensions given in the figure below) is $10.0{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Assume a water density of $\rho=1.00\ \mathrm{kg\,dm^{-3}}$ and air behaving like an ideal gas.

**1.3**  
Calculate the energy (in MJ) which is required to heat the water in the pool to $22.0{}^\circ\mathrm C$ and the energy which is required to heat the initial amount of air (21.0% of $\mathrm{O_2}$, 79.0% of $\mathrm{N_2}$) to $30.0{}^\circ\mathrm C$ at a pressure of $1.013\times10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}$.

In February, the outside temperature is about $5{}^\circ\mathrm C$ in Northern Germany. Since the concrete walls and the roof of the house are relatively thin ($20.0\ \mathrm{cm}$) there will be a loss of energy. This energy is released to the surroundings (heat loss released to water and/or ground should be neglected). The heat conductivity of the wall and roof is $1.00\ \mathrm{W\,K^{-1}\,m^{-1}}$.



**1.4**  
Calculate the energy (in MJ) which is needed to maintain the temperature inside the house at $30.0{}^\circ\mathrm C$ during the party (12 hours).

$1.00\ \mathrm{m^3}$ of natural gas as delivered by PUC costs $0.40\ \mathrm{€}$ and $1.00\ \mathrm{kWh}$ of electricity costs $0.137\ \mathrm{€}$. The rent for the equipment for gas heating will cost him about $150.00\ \mathrm{€}$ while the corresponding electrical heaters will only cost $100.00\ \mathrm{€}$.



**1.5**  
What is the total energy (in MJ) needed for Peter’s “winter swimming pool” calculated in 1.3 and 1.4? How much natural gas will he need, if the gas heater has an efficiency of 90.0%? What are the different costs for the use of either natural gas or electricity? Use the values given by PUC for your calculations and assume 100% efficiency for the electric heater.

## Решение

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-1">

### 1.1


Chemical equations:

a) methane: $\mathrm{CH_4+2O_2\rightarrow CO_2+2H_2O}$.

b) ethane: $\mathrm{2C_2H_6+7O_2\rightarrow4CO_2+6H_2O}$.

Thermodynamic data for the equations:

$$
\Delta H^0=[2\times(-241.8)-393.5-(-74.6)]\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}=-802.5\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}},
$$
$$
\Delta S^0=[2\times188.8+213.8-186.3-2\times205.2]\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}=-5.3\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}},
$$
$$
\Delta G^0=-802.5\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}-298.15\ \mathrm K\times(-5.3\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}})=-800.9\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}.
$$

Methane: $\Delta H^0=-802.5\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$; $\Delta S^0=-5.3\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$; $\Delta G^0=-800.9\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.

$$
\Delta H^0=[6\times(-241.8)-4\times393.5-2\times(-84.0)]\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}=-2856.8\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}},
$$
$$
\Delta S^0=[6\times188.8+4\times213.8-2\times229.2-7\times205.2]\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}=+93.2\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}},
$$
$$
\Delta G^0=-2856.8\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}-298.15\ \mathrm K\times(93.2\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}})=-2884.6\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}.
$$

Ethane: $\Delta H^0=-2856.8\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$; $\Delta S^0=+93.2\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$; $\Delta G^0=-2884.6\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$.



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-2">

### 1.2


#### a)

Amount of methane and ethane in $1\ \mathrm{m^3}$ natural gas:

The source's density symbol in the first line is printed as a right-angle glyph:

$$
m=\text{〉}\times V=0.740\ \mathrm{g\,dm^{-3}}\times1000\ \mathrm{dm^3}=740\ \mathrm g,
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_{\mathrm{av}}&=\sum_i x(i)M(i)\\
&=(0.0024\times44.01\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}})+(0.0134\times28.02\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}})\\
&\quad+(0.9732\times16.05\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}})+(0.011\times30.08\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}})\\
&=16.43\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}},
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
n_{\mathrm{tot}}=m(M_{\mathrm{av}})^{-1}=740\ \mathrm g\times(16.43\ \mathrm{g/mol})^{-1}=45.04\ \mathrm{mol},
$$
$$
n(i)=x(i)\cdot n_{\mathrm{tot}},
$$
$$
n(\mathrm{CH_4})=x(\mathrm{CH_4})\times n_{\mathrm{tot}}=0.9732\times45.04\ \mathrm{mol}=43.83\ \mathrm{mol},
$$
$$
n(\mathrm{C_2H_6})=x(\mathrm{C_2H_6})\times n_{\mathrm{tot}}=0.0110\times45.04\ \mathrm{mol}=0.495\ \mathrm{mol}.
$$

#### b)

Energy of combustion, deviation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{\mathrm{comb.}}(\mathrm{H_2O(g)})&=\sum_i n(i)\Delta_cH^\circ(i)\\
&=43.83\ \mathrm{mol}\times(-802.5\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}})\\
&\quad+0.495\ \mathrm{mol}\times0.5\times(-2856.8\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}})\\
&=-35881\ \mathrm{kJ}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$E_{\mathrm{comb.}}(\mathrm{H_2O(g)})=-35881\ \mathrm{kJ}$.

Deviation from PUC:

$$
E_{\mathrm{PUC}}(\mathrm{H_2O(g)})=9.981\ \mathrm{kWh\,m^{-3}}\times1\ \mathrm{m^3}\times3600\ \mathrm{kJ\,(kWh)^{-1}}=35932\ \mathrm{kJ}.
$$

The printed symbolic deviation line has an unmatched opening parenthesis:

$$
\Delta E=(E_{\mathrm{comb.}}(\mathrm{H_2O(g)})-E_{\mathrm{PUC}}(\mathrm{H_2O(g)})\times100\%\times[E_{\mathrm{comb.}}(\mathrm{H_2O(g)})]^{-1}
$$
$$
=(35881\ \mathrm{kJ}-35932\ \mathrm{kJ})\times100\%\times(35881\ \mathrm{kJ})^{-1}=-0.14\%.
$$



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-3">

### 1.3


Energy for heating the water:

Volume of water: $V_{\mathrm{water}}=22.5\ \mathrm{m^3}$.

$$
n_{\mathrm{water}}=V_{\mathrm{water}}\rho_{\mathrm{water}}(M_{\mathrm{water}})^{-1}
=22.5\ \mathrm{m^3}\times10^6\ \mathrm{g\,m^{-3}}\times(18.02\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}})^{-1}=1.249\times10^6\ \mathrm{mol},
$$
$$
E_{\mathrm{water}}=n_{\mathrm{water}}\times C_p\times\Delta T
=1.249\times10^6\ \mathrm{mol}\times75.30\ \mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}\times14\ \mathrm K=1316\ \mathrm{MJ}.
$$

Energy for heating the air:

Volume of the house is:

$$
V_{\mathrm{air}}=(15\ \mathrm m\times8\ \mathrm m\times3\ \mathrm m)+0.5\times(15\ \mathrm m\times8\ \mathrm m\times2\ \mathrm m)=480\ \mathrm{m^3},
$$
$$
n_{\mathrm{air}}=pV(RT)^{-1}=1.013\times10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}\times480\ \mathrm{m^3}\times(8.314\ \mathrm{J\,(K\,mol)^{-1}}\times283.15\ \mathrm K)^{-1}=2.065\times10^4\ \mathrm{mol},
$$
$$
C_p(\mathrm{air})=0.21\times29.4\ \mathrm{J\,(K\,mol)^{-1}}+0.79\times29.1\ \mathrm{J\,(K\,mol)^{-1}}=29.16\ \mathrm{J\,(K\,mol)^{-1}},
$$
$$
E_{\mathrm{air}}=n_{\mathrm{air}}\times C_p(\mathrm{air})\times\Delta T
=2.065\times10^4\ \mathrm{mol}\times29.17\ \mathrm{J\,(K\,mol)^{-1}}\times20\ \mathrm K=12.05\ \mathrm{MJ}.
$$



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-4">

### 1.4


Energy for maintaining the temperature:

Surface area of the house:

$$
A_{\mathrm{house}}=3\ \mathrm m\times46\ \mathrm m+8\ \mathrm m\times2\ \mathrm m+\sqrt{(2\ \mathrm m)^2+(4\ \mathrm m)^2}\times2\times15\ \mathrm m=288.16\ \mathrm{m^2}.
$$

Heat conductivity: $\lambda_{\mathrm{wall}}=1\ \mathrm{J\,(s\,K\,m)^{-1}}$.

Energy flux along a temperature gradient (wall thickness $d=0.2\ \mathrm m$):

$$
J=E_{\mathrm{loss}}(A\times\Delta t)^{-1}=\lambda_{\mathrm{wall}}\Delta T d^{-1},
$$
$$
E_{\mathrm{loss}}=288.16\ \mathrm{m^2}\times(12\cdot60\cdot60\ \mathrm s)\times1\ \mathrm{J\,(s\,K\,m)^{-1}}\times25\ \mathrm K\times(0.2\ \mathrm m)^{-1}=1556\ \mathrm{MJ}.
$$

$E_{\mathrm{loss}}=1556\ \mathrm{MJ}$.



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-5">

### 1.5


Total energy and costs:

Total energy: $E_{\mathrm{tot}}=E_{\mathrm{water}}+E_{\mathrm{air}}+E_{\mathrm{loss}}=1316\ \mathrm{MJ}+12\ \mathrm{MJ}+1556\ \mathrm{MJ}=2884\ \mathrm{MJ}$.

$2884\ \mathrm{MJ}$ corresponds to $2.884\times10^6\ \mathrm{kJ}\times(3600\ \mathrm{s\,h^{-1}}\times9.981\ \mathrm{kJ\,s^{-1}\,m^{-3}}\times0.9)^{-1}=89.18\ \mathrm{m^3}$.

Volume of gas: $V=89.18\ \mathrm{m^3}$.

$2884\ \mathrm{MJ}$ correspond to a cost of: $0.40\ \mathrm{€\,m^{-3}}\times89.18\ \mathrm{m^3}=35.67\ \mathrm{€}$.

Rent for equipment: $150.00\ \mathrm{€}$.

Total cost of gas heating = $185.67\ \mathrm{€}$.

$2884\ \mathrm{MJ}$ correspond to a cost of $2.884\cdot10^6\ \mathrm{kJ}\times0.137\ \mathrm{€}\times(3600\ \mathrm{s\,h^{-1}}\times1\ \mathrm{kJ\,s^{-1}\,h})^{-1}=109.75\ \mathrm{€}$.

Rent for equipment: $100.00\ \mathrm{€}$.

Total cost of electric heating: $209.75\ \mathrm{€}$.



</ProblemSection>
