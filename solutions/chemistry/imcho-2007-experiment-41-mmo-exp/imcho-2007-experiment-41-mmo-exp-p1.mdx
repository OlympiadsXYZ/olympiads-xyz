The reversible reaction between dimethyl ketone and iodine in aqueous solution is catalyzed by protons:

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '36.1%', '--fig-max': '231px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/imcho-2007-experiment-41-mmo-exp/p1-fig1-astra-20260925.png" alt="Acetone iodination reaction with proton catalyst." width="723" height="121" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


The corresponding kinetic equation can be written as:

$$
w=-\frac{d[\mathrm{I_2}]}{dt}=k\cdot[\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3}]^x\cdot[\mathrm{I_2}]^y\cdot[\mathrm{H^+}]^z
$$

The initial rate is measured in order to determine the rate constant $k$ and the reaction orders $x$, $y$, and $z$ with respect to every reacting substance. The initial rate can be determined by following the decrease of iodine concentration in the reaction mixture during a definite period of time (15 min in the present experiment) after the reaction start. The curvature of the kinetic curve can be neglected, when the conversion degree of reagents is low. Thus, it is possible to treat the average rate during a short period as the initial reaction rate $w_0$:

$$
w_0=-\frac{d[\mathrm{I_2}]_0}{dt}=k\cdot[\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3}]_0^x\cdot[\mathrm{I_2}]_0^y\cdot[\mathrm{H^+}]_0^z,
$$

where $[\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3}]_0$, $[\mathrm{I_2}]_0$, and $[\mathrm{H^+}]_0$ are the initial concentrations of dimethyl ketone, iodine and hydrogen ions, respectively.

Sodium acetate is added to the reaction mixture in order to stop the reaction at the required time point. Acetate reacts instantly with $\mathrm{H^+}$ resulting in formation of acetic acid and decreasing the concentration of the $\mathrm{H^+}$ ions to the values, when the reaction takes place at a negligibly low rate. Since the reaction of iodination is not completely stopped (it is just slowed down), the reaction mixture must be titrated as fast as possible, once sodium acetate is added.

The excess of unreacted iodine in the reaction mixture is determined by titration with sodium thiosulfate using starch as an indicator. Calculate the initial molar concentrations of iodine, dimethyl ketone and HCl in flasks **A–D**, hypothetically taking the dimethyl ketone concentration in the 100 mL flask as 1 M.

8. Since the enol (E) formation is the stage limiting the entire process, we can suppose that $k_2/k_3\ll1$. Then, we can apply the quasi-stationary approach towards enol as an intermediate (A is acetone):

$$
\frac{d[\mathrm{E}]}{dt}=k_2[\mathrm{HA^+}]-k_3[\mathrm{E}][\mathrm{I_2}]=0,
$$

whereas $[\mathrm{HA^+}]=K_b[\mathrm{A}][\mathrm{H^+}]$, and the reaction rate $w=-\frac{d[\mathrm{I_2}]}{dt}=k_3[\mathrm{E}][\mathrm{I_2}]$, thus, taking into account the quasi-stationary state of the enol:

$$
w=k_3[\mathrm{E}][\mathrm{I_2}]=k_2[\mathrm{HA^+}]=k_2K_b[\mathrm{A}][\mathrm{H^+}].
$$

9. Since $k_2/k_3\ll1$, the effective constant is $k'=k_2K_b$.
