## Условие

**Problem 5. Methane pyrolysis (12 marks)**

| Question | 1 | 2 | 3 | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Points | 10 | 10 | 10 | **30** |
| Result |  |  |  |  |

One of the ways to produce hydrogen on an industrial scale is by thermal decomposition of methane according to the reversible reaction

$$
\mathrm{CH_{4(g)}=C_{(s)}+2H_{2(g)}}
$$

The raw material for the process is relatively cheap (it is natural gas) but the reaction is highly endothermic and requires high temperatures to proceed at a substantial rate. Luckily, methane can be also used as a fuel. When combusted in oxygen according to the equation

$$
\mathrm{CH_{4(g)}+2O_{2(g)}=CO_{2(g)}+2H_2O_{(g)}},
$$

it produces a lot of heat: the enthalpy of this reaction is $\Delta_\mathrm rH^\circ_{298}=-798\ \mathrm{kJ/mol}$. This heat can be used to convert methane into hydrogen and coke.

*Note: throughout the problem assume that enthalpies and entropies of all reactions and heat capacities of all compounds do not depend on temperature.*

**1** **Question 1: The equilibrium and the yield of methane pyrolysis**

Under isobaric conditions ($p_\mathrm{total}=2\ \mathrm{bar}$), 47% of methane is converted into hydrogen and carbon at $1000^\circ\mathrm C$ and 61% – at $1100^\circ\mathrm C$. What is the yield of the reaction at $1300^\circ\mathrm C$ under the same constant pressure?

Calculation

Yield = ______ % **[10 т.]**

If you failed to answer this question, use the following values for subsequent calculations: yield of methane is 70%, standard enthalpy of formation of methane is $\Delta_\mathrm fH^\circ_{298}=-90\ \mathrm{kJ/mol}$.

Assume that methane pyrolysis proceeds at constant pressure ($p_\mathrm{total}=2\ \mathrm{bar}$) and constant temperature ($1300^\circ\mathrm C$) in the setup schematically shown below.

Calculate the total quantities (in moles) of methane and oxygen required to produce one mole of hydrogen using the results from question 1 and assuming that a) thermal equilibrium is established between the combustion chamber and the pyrolysis chamber, b) no heat is dissipated into environment. The isobaric heat capacities of methane and oxygen are 36 and $29\ \mathrm{J/(mol\mathord\cdot K)}$, respectively.

Calculation

$n(\mathrm{CH_4})=$ ______ mol

$n(\mathrm{O_2})=$ ______ mol **[10 т.]**

If you failed to answer this question, use the following values for subsequent calculations: $n(\mathrm{CH_4})=1.21\ \mathrm{mol}$, $n(\mathrm{O_2})=1\ \mathrm{mol}$.

**3** **Question 3: Bookkeeping**

Cost planning is vital for industrial processes. In the question above we assumed that oxygen is used to burn methane but what if we use air? Pure oxygen comes at a cost of 1.50$ per kilogram while air is free. However, air contains only 21 mol.% of oxygen, thus some heat produced by combustion will be spent for heating inert air components.

Using the same assumptions as in Question 2, calculate the price of one kilogram of hydrogen in two cases: a) pure oxygen is used for combustion, b) air is used for combustion ($\mathrm{CH_4/O_2}$ ratio is stoichiometric). Assume that mole fraction of nitrogen in air is 79% (neglect argon) and its isobaric heat capacity is $29\ \mathrm{J/(mol\mathord\cdot K)}$. Methane price is 0.75$ per kilogram.

a) oxygen is used for combustion

Price($\mathrm{H_2}$) = ______ $/kg

b) air is used for combustion

Price($\mathrm{H_2}$) = ______ $/kg **[10 т.]**

*Hint.* Thermodynamic equations

Relation between equilibrium constant and thermodynamic functions:

$$
\Delta G^\circ=-RT\ln K_p=\Delta H^\circ-T\Delta S^\circ
$$

Enthalpy change during isobaric heating:

$$
\Delta H=nC_p(T_2-T_1)
$$

## Решение

### Official answers and grading scheme

**1** Calculation

Denote the yield as $Y$. For each mole of reactant, the resulting quantities of substances at equilibrium are:

| $\mathrm{CH_4}$ | = $\mathrm C$ | + $2\mathrm{H_2}$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $(1-Y)$ | $Y$ | $2Y$ |

1 pt.

The total quantity of gases is $(1+Y)$.

If the total pressure is 2 bar, then the partial pressures of the gases are:

$p(\mathrm{CH_4})=2*(1-Y)/(1+Y)$

$p(\mathrm{H_2})=2*2Y/(1+Y)$: 2 pts.

Expression for the equilibrium constant:

$K=p(\mathrm{H_2})^2/p(\mathrm{CH_4})$: 1 pt.

$K=(2*2Y/(1+Y))^2/(2*(1-Y)/(1+Y))=8Y^2/(1-Y^2)$

At 1273 K, $K=2.27$, $\Delta G^\circ=-8.63\ \mathrm{kJ/mol}$: 1 pt.

At 1373 K, $K=4.74$, $\Delta G^\circ=-17.76\ \mathrm{kJ/mol}$: 1 pt.

$\Delta S^\circ=(17760-8630)/100=91.3\ \mathrm{J/mol/K}$: 1 pt.

$\Delta H^\circ=-8630+1273*91.3=107.6\ \mathrm{kJ/mol}$: 1 pt.

At 1573 K, $K=\exp((1573*91.3-107600)/(8.314*1573))=15.7$: 1 pt.

Therefore, $Y=0.81$: 1 pt.

Yield = 81 % **[10 т.]**

**2** Calculation

Quantity of methane required to produce 1 mol of $\mathrm{H_2}$:

$n=1/2/\text{yield}=0.5/0.81=0.617\ \mathrm{mol}$: 1 pt.

Heat required to heat methane to $1300^\circ\mathrm C$:

$Q=(1300-25)*0.617*36=28.3\ \mathrm{kJ}$: 1 pt.

Heat required to decompose 0.5 mol of methane at $1300^\circ\mathrm C$:

$Q=0.5\Delta H=0.5*107.6=53.8\ \mathrm{kJ}$: 1 pt.

Total $Q=28.3+53.8=82.1\ \mathrm{kJ}$: 1 pt.

Heat released per mole of methane in a two-step process: heating methane-oxygen mixture to $1300^\circ\mathrm C$, then burning it isothermically:

$Q=798000-(1300-25)*(36+2*29)=678.2\ \mathrm{kJ}$: 3 pts.

(1 pt for total heat capacity of gas mixture, 1 point for each heat contribution)

Quantity of methane required to get the heat for pyrolysis:

$n=Q/\Delta H=82.1/678.2=0.121\ \mathrm{mol}$: 1 pt.

Quantity of oxygen

$n=0.121*2=0.242\ \mathrm{mol}$: 1 pt.

Total quantity of methane both for pyrolysis and combustion

$n=0.617+0.121=0.738\ \mathrm{mol}$: 1 pt.

$n(\mathrm{CH_4})=0.738\ \mathrm{mol}$

$n(\mathrm{O_2})=0.242\ \mathrm{mol}$ **[10 т.]**

**3** Methane price is 0.70$ per kilogram.

a) oxygen is used for combustion

Per 1 mol of hydrogen:

$m(\mathrm{CH_4})=nM=0.738*16=11.8\ \mathrm g$: 0.5 pts.

$m(\mathrm{O_2})=nM=0.242*32=7.74\ \mathrm g$: 0.5 pts.

Per 1 kg of hydrogen:

$m(\mathrm{CH_4})=11.8*1000/2=5.90\ \mathrm{kg}$: 0.5 pts.

$m(\mathrm{O_2})=7.74*1000/2=3.87\ \mathrm{kg}$: 0.5 pts.

$\text{Price}=5.9*0.70+3.87*1.50=9.94$ $/kg: 1 pt.

Price($\mathrm{H_2}$) = 9.94 $/kg

b) air is used for combustion

Heat released per mole of methane in a two-step process: heating methane-air mixture to $1300^\circ\mathrm C$, then burning it isothermically:

$Q=798000-(1300-25)*(36+2*29/0.21)=400.0\ \mathrm{kJ}$: 3 pts.

(1 pt for total heat capacity of gas mixture, 1 point for each heat contribution)

Quantity of methane needed to burn to get the heat for pyrolysis:

$n=Q/\Delta H=82.1/400.0=0.205\ \mathrm{mol}$: 1 pt.

Total quantity of methane both for pyrolysis and combustion

$n=0.617+0.205=0.822\ \mathrm{mol}$: 1 pt.

Per one mole of hydrogen:

$m(\mathrm{CH_4})=nM=0.822*16=13.2\ \mathrm g$: 0.5 pts.

Per 1 kg of hydrogen:

$m(\mathrm{CH_4})=13.2*1000/2=6.60\ \mathrm{kg}$: 0.5 pts.

$\text{Price}=6.60*0.70=4.62$ $/kg: 1 pt.

Price($\mathrm{H_2}$) = 4.62 $/kg **[10 т.]**
