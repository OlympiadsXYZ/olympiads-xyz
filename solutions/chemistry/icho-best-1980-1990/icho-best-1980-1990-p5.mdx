## Условие

Carbon monoxide is one of the most serious environmental hazards caused by automobiles and extensive investigation is being carried out to develop efficient catalysts for the conversion of CO present in the exhaust gases to CO₂. Consider a typical family car. It has four cylinders with a total cylinder volume of 1600 cm³ and a fuel consumption of 7.0 dm³ /100 km when driving at a speed of 90 km/h. During one second each cylinder goes through 25 burn cycles and consumes 0.400 g of fuel. Assume that the fuel is composed of 2,2,4-trimethylpentane, C₈H₁₈. The compression ratio of the cylinder is 1:8 (the ratio between the smallest and the largest volume within the cylinder as the piston moves to and fro).

**a)** Calculate the air intake of the engine (m³/s). The gasified fuel and air are introduced into the cylinder when its volume is largest until the pressure in the cylinder is 101.0 kPa. You may assume that the temperature of both the incoming fuel and air is 100.0 °C. (2.0 points)

Air contains 21.0% by volume of O2 and 79.0% by volume of N2. It is assumed that 10.0l% of carbon forms CO upon combustion and that nitrogen remains inert.

**b)** The gasified fuel and the air are then compressed until the volume in the cylinder is at its smallest. They are ignited. Calculate (1) the composition (% by volume) and (2) the temperature (K) of the exhaust gases immediately after the combustion (the exhaust gases have not yet started to expand). The following thermodynamic values are known. You can assume that both the enthalpies of formation and the molar heat capacities are independent of temperature and may be used in an approximate calculation of the temperature change. (5.0 points)

**c)** Calculate the final temperature of the exhaust gases leaving the cylinder assuming that the piston has moved to expand the gases to the maximum volume in the cylinder, the gas mixture obeys the ideal gas equation and that the final pressure in the cylinder is 200.0 kPa. (2.0 points)

**d)** To convert the CO(g) to CO₂(g) the exhaust gases are led through a bed of catalyst. The catalyst has the following work function:

$$
\left(\frac{n(\mathrm{CO})}{n(\mathrm{CO}_2)}\right)=\frac14\times k\left(\frac{n(\mathrm{CO})}{n(\mathrm{CO}_2)}\right)_i\times v\times e^{-T/T_0}
$$

where [n(CO)/n(CO₂)] is the molar ratio of CO and CO₂ leaving the catalyst, [n(CO)/n(CO₂)]ᵢ is the molar ratio before the catalyst, v is the flow rate of the exhaust gases (mol s⁻¹), T is the temperature of the exhaust gases entering the catalyst (assumed to be the same as the final temperature of the gases leaving the cylinder). T₀ is a reference temperature (373 K) and k is a constant (3.141 s mol⁻¹).

Calculate the composition (% by volume) of the exhaust gases leaving the catalyst. (3.0 points)

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи решението от архива">

Solution of problem No. 5:

<ProblemSection id="solution-key-a">

### a)

$M_f(\mathrm{C_8H_{18}})=114.0$.

Consider one cylinder. During each burn cycle:

$$
m_f=0.400/25\,\mathrm g=0.0160\,\mathrm g,\qquad n_f=1.4004\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol}.
$$

Cylinder volume:$V_0=4.00\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}$; pressure:$P_0=101.0\,\mathrm{kPa}=101000\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-2}}$; temperature:$T_0=373\,\mathrm K$.

$$
n_G=n_f+n_A=\frac{P_0V_0}{RT_0}=\frac{101000\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-2}}\times4\;4\;10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}}{8.314\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\times373\,\mathrm K}=0.0130\,\mathrm{mol}.
$$

$$
n_A=\text{air moles}=n_G-n_f=0.0130-1.404\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol}=0.0129\,\mathrm{mol}.
$$

Air intake(m³ s⁻¹)(one cylinder;25 burn cycles):

$$
V_A^1=25\times n_A\times\frac{RT_0}{P_0}=\frac{25\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\times0.0129\,\mathrm{mol}\times8.314\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}\times373\,\mathrm K}{101000\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-2}}}=9.902\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3\,s^{-1}}.
$$

The air intake(engine;m³ s⁻¹):

$$
V_A=4\times9.902\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3\,s^{-1}}=0.0396\,\mathrm{m^3\,s^{-1}}.
$$

Source note: the first printed fuel amount is 1.4004×10⁻⁴ and the displayed volume numerator has “4 4 10⁻⁴”; their later forms differ. Both original occurrences are retained.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-key-b">

### b)

1) Composition of the exhaust gases(consider one cylinder and one burn cycle).

Before combustion:

$$
n_{oxygen}=0.21\times n_A=2.709\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol},\quad n_{nitrogen}=0.79\times n_A=10.191\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol}.
$$

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
0.1\times&\mathrm{C_8H_{18}}+8.5\mathrm{O_2}\longrightarrow8\mathrm{CO}+9\mathrm{H_2O}\quad(10\%\mathrm C)\\
0.9\times&\mathrm{C_8H_{18}}+12.5\mathrm{O_2}\longrightarrow8\mathrm{CO_2}+9\mathrm{H_2O}\quad(90\%\mathrm C)
\end{array}
$$

$$
\mathrm{C_8H_{18}}+12.1\mathrm{O_2}\longrightarrow0.8\mathrm{CO}+7.2\mathrm{CO_2}+9\mathrm{H_2O}.
$$

|No. of moles|C₈H₁₈|O₂|CO|CO₂|H₂O|
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|before combustion|1.404×10⁻⁴|2.709×10⁻³|0|0|0|
|after combustion|0|10.10×10⁻⁴|1.123×10⁻⁴|10.11×10⁻⁴|12.63×10⁻⁴|

After combustion:$n_{nitrogen}=10.191\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol}$.

The composition of the gas after combustion:

|Component|mol×10⁻⁴|%|
|---|---|---|
|N₂|101.91|75.0|
|O₂|10.10|7.4|
|CO|1.12|0.8|
|CO₂|10.11|7.5|
|H₂O|12.63|9.3|
|nG|135.87|100.0|

2) The temperature of gas immediately after combustion:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta H&=n_f[0.8\times\Delta H_f(\mathrm{CO})+7.2\times\Delta H_f(\mathrm{CO_2})+9\times\Delta H_f(\mathrm{H_2O})-\Delta H_f(\mathrm{C_8H_{18}})]\\
&=1.40\times10^{-4}[0.8(-110.53)+7.2(-395.51)+9(-241.82)-(-187.82)]\\
&=-0.6914\,\mathrm{kJ}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
-\Delta H=\int_{373}^{T_1}[n_{CO}C_p(\mathrm{CO})+n_{CO_2}C_p(\mathrm{CO_2})+n_{H_2O}C_p(\mathrm{H_2O})+n_{N_2}C_p(\mathrm{N_2})+n_{O_2}C_p(\mathrm{O_2})]
$$

$$
=[1.12(29.14)+10.11(37.11)+12.63(33.58)+101.91(29.13)+10.10(29.36)]10^4(T_1-373)=691.4\,\mathrm J.
$$

$$
\therefore T_1=2060\,\mathrm K.
$$

Source note: the integral has no printed differential and the numerical heat-capacity multiplier is printed 10⁴; these are retained.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-key-c">

### c)

Temperature of the exhaust gas leaving the cylinder.

$P_2=200.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$, $V=4.00\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}$; $n_G=\text{exhaust gas moles in one cylinder}=0.01359\,\mathrm{mol}$.

$$
T_2=\frac{P_2V_0}{n_GR}=\frac{200000\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-2}}\times4.00\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}}{0.01359\,\mathrm{mol}\times8.314\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}}=708\,\mathrm K.
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-key-d">

### d)

Composition of the exhaust gas after the catalyst.

Mass stream of the exhaust gas from all four cylinders:

$$
v=4.0\times01359\,\mathrm{mol}\times25\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}=1.359\,\mathrm{mol\,s^{-1}}.
$$

$$
\left(\frac{n(\mathrm{CO})}{n(\mathrm{CO_2})}\right)=\frac14\times k\left(\frac{n(\mathrm{CO})}{n(\mathrm{CO_2})}\right)_i\times v\times e^{-T/T_0}
$$

$$
=3.141\frac{\mathrm s}{\mathrm{mol}}\times\frac{1.12\times10^{-4}}{10.11\times10^{-4}}\times1.359\frac{\mathrm{mol}}{\mathrm s}\times e^{-708/373}=0.0177.
$$

The molar ratio $(n(\mathrm{CO})/n(\mathrm{CO_2}))$ can be calculated considering the amounts of exhaust gas components from one cycle.

Catalysed reaction:$\mathrm{CO}+0.5\mathrm{O_2}\longrightarrow\mathrm{CO_2}$.

|moles×10⁴(from 4 cylinders)|CO|O₂|CO₂|
|---|---|---|---|
|Initial|4.48|40.40|40.44|
|Final|4.48−x|40.40−0.5x|40.44+x|

$$
\left(\frac{n(\mathrm{CO})}{n(\mathrm{CO_2})}\right)=0.0177=\frac{4.48-x}{40.44+x};\qquad\therefore x=3.70.
$$

Composition of the gas after the catalyst(one cycle):

|Component|moles×10⁴|=|value|%|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|N₂|407.64|=|407.64|75.26|
|O₂|40.40−0.5x|=|8.55|7.12|
|CO|4.48−x|=|0.78|0.15|
|CO₂|40.44+x|=|44.14|8.14|
|H₂O|50.52|=|50.52|9.33|
|total|541 63|||100.00|

Source note: the numerical substitution omits the preceding 1/4 factor; the flow line prints 01359; the final O₂ row prints 8.55 and total “541 63”. These native inconsistencies are retained.

</ProblemSection>
