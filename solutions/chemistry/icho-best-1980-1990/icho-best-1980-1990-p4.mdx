## Условие

The dissociation of (molecular) chlorine is an endothermic process, ∆H = 24.36 kJ mol–1. The dissociation can also be attained by the effect of light.

**1)** At what wavelength can the dissociating effect of light be expected?

**2)** Can this effect also be obtained with light whose wavelength is smaller or larger than the calculated critical wavelength?

**3)** What is the energy of the photon with the critical wavelength?

When light that can effect the chlorine dissociation is incident on a mixture of gaseous chlorine and hydrogen, hydrogen chloride is formed. The mixture is irradiated with a mercury UV-lamp (λ = 253.6 nm. The lamp has a power input of 10 watt. An amount of 2% of the energy supplied is absorbed by the gas mixture (in a 10 litre vessel). Within 2.5 seconds of irradiation, 65 millimoles of HCl is formed.

**4)** How large is the quantum yield (= the number of the product molecules per absorbed photon)?

**5)** How can the value obtained be (qualitatively) explained? Describe the reaction mechanism.

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи решението от архива">

Solution of problem No. 4:

<ProblemSection id="solution-key-1">

### 1)

$\lambda_1=c/\nu_1$, from $\Delta H=N_Ah\nu_1$ it follows that

$$
\lambda_1=\frac{cN_Ah}{\Delta H}=\frac{3\times10^8\times6.02\times10^{23}\times6.6\times10^{-34}}{2.436\times10^{-5}}=4.91\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm m=491\,\mathrm{nm}.
$$

Source note: the original question prints 24.36 kJ mol⁻¹, this denominator prints 2.436×10⁻⁵ and the key prints 491 nm. These conflicting native values are retained.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-key-2">

### 2)

Short-wave light is effective, as its photons have a greater energy than required, whereas the photons of longer-wavelength light are too poor in energy to effect the dissociation.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-key-3">

### 3)

$$
E_1=h\nu_1=\frac{hc}{\lambda_1}=\frac{6.6\times10^{-34}\times3\times10^8}{4.91\times10^{-7}}\,\mathrm J=4.03\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm J.
$$

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-key-4">

### 4)

The quantum yield(φ)=the number of HCl molecules formed/the number of absorbed photons.

Now the number of HCl molecules formed=$n_{\mathrm{HCl}}N_A$ and the number of photons absorbed=$E_{Total}/E_{photon}$, i.e.

$$
\varphi=\frac{n_{\mathrm{HCl}}N_A}{E_{Total}}\times E_{photon}=\frac{n_{\mathrm{HCl}}N_Ahc}{E_{Total}\lambda_2}
=\frac{6.5\times10^{-2}\times6.02\times10^{23}\times6.6\times10^{-34}\times3\times10^8}{(0.2\times2.5)\times2.536\times10^{-7}}=6.1\times10^4.
$$

$(E_{Total}=0.02\times10\times2.5\,\mathrm J)$.

</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-key-5">

### 5)

The observed quantum yield is based on a chain mechanism.

The start of the reaction chain:

$$
\mathrm{Cl_2}\xrightarrow{h\nu}2\mathrm{Cl}^{\bullet}
$$

The propagation of the chain:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2\mathrm{Cl}^{\bullet}+\mathrm{H_2}&\longrightarrow\mathrm{HCl}+\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}\\
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}+\mathrm{Cl_2}&\longrightarrow\mathrm{HCl}+\mathrm{Cl}^{\bullet}
\end{aligned}
$$

The chain termination, mainly by

$$
\begin{aligned}
2\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}&\longrightarrow\mathrm{H_2}\\
2\mathrm{Cl}^{\bullet}&\longrightarrow\mathrm{Cl_2}\\
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}+\mathrm{Cl}^{\bullet}&\longrightarrow\mathrm{HCl}
\end{aligned}
$$

Source note: the first propagation reaction’s coefficient 2 is printed in the source and retained.

</ProblemSection>


</Spoiler>
