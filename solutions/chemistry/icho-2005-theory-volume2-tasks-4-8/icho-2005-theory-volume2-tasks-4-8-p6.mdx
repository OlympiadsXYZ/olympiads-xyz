The capacity of water to accept $\mathrm{H^+}$ ions is called alkalinity. Alkalinity is important in water treatment and in the chemistry and biology of natural waters. Generally, the basic species responsible for alkalinity in water are $\mathrm{HCO_3^-}$, $\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}$, and $\mathrm{OH^-}$. At pH values below 7, $\mathrm{H^+}$ in water detracts significantly from alkalinity. Therefore, the complete equation for alkalinity in a medium where these are the only contributors can be expressed as

$$
\text{alkalinity}=[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]+2[\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}]+[\mathrm{OH^-}]-[\mathrm{H^+}].
$$

The contributions made by different species to alkalinity depend upon pH. Relevant chemical equations and equilibrium constants at $298\ \mathrm{K}$ are shown below:

| Equilibrium | Constant |
|---|---|
| $\mathrm{CO_2(g)}\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}$ | $K_{\mathrm{CO_2}}=3.44\times10^{-2}$ |
| $\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}+\mathrm{H_2O}\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{H_2CO_3}$ | $K_{\mathrm{H_2CO_3}}=2.00\times10^{-3}$ |
| $\mathrm{H_2CO_3}\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{HCO_3^-}+\mathrm{H^+}$ | $K_{a1}=2.23\times10^{-4}$ |
| $\mathrm{HCO_3^-}\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}+\mathrm{H^+}$ | $K_{a2}=4.69\times10^{-11}$ |
| $\mathrm{CaCO_3(s)}\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}+\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}$ | $K_{sp}=4.50\times10^{-9}$ |
| $\mathrm{H_2O}\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{H^+}+\mathrm{OH^-}$ | $K_w=1.00\times10^{-14}$ |

**Note: Calculations must be shown.**

### Native requests




**6.1**  
Natural waters (river or lake water) generally contain dissolved $\mathrm{CO_2}$. The ratio of $[\mathrm{H_2CO_3}]:[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]:[\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}]$ in water at $[\mathrm{H^+}]=1.00\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{M}$ will be $(a):1.00:(b)$. Calculate $(a)$ and $(b)$.



**6.2**  
Gaseous $\mathrm{CO_2}$ in the atmosphere can be regarded as a contributor to the alkalinity of water in equilibrium with air. Calculate the concentration of $\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}$ ($\mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}$) in pure water that is in equilibrium with the unpolluted air at $1.01\times10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}$ and $298\ \mathrm{K}$ containing $0.0360\%$ (molar ratio) $\mathrm{CO_2}$. Assume standard pressure $=1.01\times10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}$.

If you are unable to solve this problem, assume that the concentration of $\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}$ is equal to $1.11\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{M}$ for further calculations.

The solubility $S$ of $\mathrm{CO_2}$ in water can be defined as

$$
S=[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]+[\mathrm{H_2CO_3}]+[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]+[\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}].
$$

The solubility of atmospheric $\mathrm{CO_2}$ in water that is in equilibrium with the unpolluted air at $298\ \mathrm{K}$ and $1.01\times10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}$ will vary with alkalinity.



**6-3**  
Find the solubility of atmospheric $\mathrm{CO_2}$ in pure water ($\mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}$). Neglect dissociation of water.



**6.4**  
Find the solubility of atmospheric $\mathrm{CO_2}$ ($\mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}$) in a $1.00\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}$ $\mathrm{NaOH}$ solution.

At $298\ \mathrm{K}$, $1.01\times10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}$ unpolluted air is in equilibrium with natural water saturated with $\mathrm{CaCO_3}$. The following main equilibrium may exist:

$$
\mathrm{CaCO_3(s)}+\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}+\mathrm{H_2O}\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}+2\mathrm{HCO_3^-}.
$$



**6.5**  
Calculate the equilibrium constant for the above equation.

If you are unable to solve this problem, assume that equilibrium constant $K_{eq}=5.00\times10^{-5}$ for further calculations.



**6.6**  
Calculate the concentration of $\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}$ ($\mathrm{mg\,dm^{-3}}$) in $\mathrm{CaCO_3}$-saturated natural water that is in equilibrium with atmospheric $\mathrm{CO_2}$.

If you are unable to solve this problem, assume that concentration of $\mathrm{Ca^{2+}(aq)}=40.1\ \mathrm{mg\,dm^{-3}}$ for further calculations.



**6.7**  
Find the alkalinity ($\mathrm{mol/L}$) of the above solution.



**6.8**  
In an underground lake saturated with $\mathrm{CaCO_3}$, the water has a high content of $\mathrm{CO_2}$. The concentration of $\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}$ in this lake was found to be as high as $100\ \mathrm{mg\,dm^{-3}}$. Assume the lake and the air above is a closed system; calculate the effective pressure of $\mathrm{CO_2}$ ($\mathrm{Pa}$) in air which is in equilibrium with this $\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}$ content.



</ProblemSection>

## Решение

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-1">

### 6.1


$[\mathrm{H^+}]=1.00\times10^{-7}$.

$$
K_{a1}=\frac{[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}][\mathrm{H^+}]}{[\mathrm{H_2CO_3}]}=2.23\times10^{-4},\qquad\frac{[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]}{[\mathrm{H_2CO_3}]}=2.23\times10^3.
$$

$$
K_{a2}=\frac{[\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}][\mathrm{H^+}]}{[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]}=4.69\times10^{-11},\qquad\frac{[\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}]}{[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]}=4.69\times10^{-4}.
$$

$$
[\mathrm{H_2CO_3}]:[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]:[\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}]=4.48\times10^{-4}:1.00:4.69\times10^{-4}.
$$



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-2">

### 6.2


$$
p(\mathrm{CO_2(aq)})=1.01\times10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}\times3.60\times10^{-4}=36.36\ \mathrm{Pa},
$$

$$
[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]=K_{\mathrm{CO_2}}p(\mathrm{CO_2})=0.0344\times\frac{36.36\ \mathrm{Pa}}{1.01\times10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}}=1.24\times10^{-5}.
$$

If unable to solve, use $[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]=1.11\times10^{-5}$ for further calculations.



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-3">

### 6.3


**(a)**

$$
S=[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]+[\mathrm{H_2CO_3}]+[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]+[\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}]\approx[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]+[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}].
$$

Both $[\mathrm{H_2CO_3}]=[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]K_{\mathrm{H_2CO_3}}=2.48\times10^{-8}$ and

$$
[\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}]=\frac{K_{a2}}{[\mathrm{H^+}]/[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]}=K_{a2}=4.69\times10^{-11}
$$

can be neglected.

$$
\frac{[\mathrm{H^+}][\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]}{[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]}=K_{a1}K_{\mathrm{H_2CO_3}}=(2.23\times10^{-4})(2.00\times10^{-3})=4.46\times10^{-7}.
$$

From 6-2, $[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]=1.24\times10^{-5}$ and $[\mathrm{H^+}]=[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]=2.35\times10^{-6}$.

$$
S=1.24\times10^{-5}+2.35\times10^{-6}=1.48\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}.
$$

**(b)** Using $[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]=1.11\times10^{-5}$:

$$
S=[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]+[\mathrm{H_2CO_3}]+[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]+[\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}]\approx[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]+[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}].
$$

Both $[\mathrm{H_2CO_3}]=[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]K_{\mathrm{H_2CO_3}}=2.22\times10^{-8}$ and $[\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}]=K_{a2}/([\mathrm{H^+}]/[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}])=K_{a2}=4.69\times10^{-11}$ can be neglected.

$$
\frac{[\mathrm{H^+}][\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]}{[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]}=K_{a1}K_{\mathrm{H_2CO_3}}=4.46\times10^{-7}.
$$

From 6.2, $[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]=1.11\times10^{-5}$ and $[\mathrm{H^+}]=[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]=2.225\times10^{-6}$.

$$
S=1.11\times10^{-5}+2.225\times10^{-6}=1.34\times10^{-5}.
$$



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-4">

### 6.4


Using $[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]=1.24\times10^{-5}$, the solubility in $1.00\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}$ NaOH is much higher because

$$
\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}+2\mathrm{OH^-}\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}+\mathrm{H_2O},\qquad K=\frac{K_{\mathrm{H_2CO_3}}K_{a1}K_{a2}}{(1.00\times10^{-14})^2}=2.09\times10^{11},
$$

$$
\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}+\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}+\mathrm{H_2O}\rightleftharpoons2\mathrm{HCO_3^-},\qquad K=\frac{K_{\mathrm{H_2CO_3}}K_{a1}}{K_{a2}}=9.37\times10^3.
$$

Combining (1) and (2):

$$
\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}+\mathrm{OH^-}\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{HCO_3^-},\qquad K=4.43\times10^7.
$$

With such a large $K$ value all $\mathrm{OH^-}$ will finally be converted to $\mathrm{HCO_3^-}$.

$$
[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]=1.00\times10^{-3},\quad[\mathrm{OH^-}]=1.82\times10^{-6},\quad[\mathrm{H^+}]=5.49\times10^{-9},\quad[\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}]=8.54\times10^{-6}.
$$

$$
S\approx[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]+[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]+[\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}]=1.24\times10^{-5}+1.00\times10^{-3}+8.54\times10^{-6}=1.02\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}.
$$



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-5">

### 6.5


$$
K_{eq}=\frac{K_{sp}K_{\mathrm{H_2CO_3}}K_{a1}}{K_{a2}}=\frac{(4.50\times10^{-9})(2.00\times10^{-3})(2.23\times10^{-4})}{4.69\times10^{-11}}=4.28\times10^{-5}.
$$

If unable to solve, assume $K_{eq}=5.00\times10^{-5}$ for further calculations.



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-6">

### 6.6


Each printed branch uses the mass balance $[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]=2[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}]$ and its specified $[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]$ from 6.2.

**(a)** Using $K_{eq}=4.28\times10^{-5}$ and $[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]=1.24\times10^{-5}$, the printed equation is

$$
K=4.28\times10^{-5}=\frac{[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}][\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]}{[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]}=\frac{[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}](2[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}])^2}{[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]}.
$$

$$
[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}]=0.510\times10^{-3}=20.5\ \mathrm{mg\,dm^{-3}}.
$$

**(b)** Using $K_{eq}=5.00\times10^{-5}$ and $[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]=1.11\times10^{-5}$:

$$
K=5.00\times10^{-5}=\frac{[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}][\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]^2}{[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]}=\frac{[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}](2[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}])^2}{[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]},
$$

$$
[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}]=0.5177\times10^{-3}=20.75\ \mathrm{mg\,dm^{-3}}.
$$

**(c)** Using $K_{eq}=5.00\times10^{-5}$ and $[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]=1.24\times10^{-5}$:

$$
K=5.00\times10^{-5}=\frac{[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}][\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]^2}{[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]}=\frac{[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}](2[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}])^2}{[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]},
$$

$$
[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}]=0.5372\times10^{-3}=21.53\ \mathrm{mg\,dm^{-3}}.
$$

**(d)** Using $K_{eq}=4.28\times10^{-5}$ and $[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]=1.11\times10^{-5}$:

$$
K=4.28\times10^{-5}=\frac{[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}][\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]^2}{[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]}=\frac{[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}](2[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}])^2}{[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]},
$$

$$
[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}]=0.4916\times10^{-3}=19.70\ \mathrm{mg\,dm^{-3}}.
$$

The printed fallback says to assume $[\mathrm{Ca^{2*}}](\mathrm{aq})=40.1$ for further calculations.



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-7">

### 6.7


$\mathrm{HCO_3^-}$ is the major species in solution. Estimate

$$
\mathrm{pH}=\frac{\mathrm{p}K_{a1}+\mathrm{p}K_{a2}}2=\frac{3.65+10.33}2=6.99\approx7.00,
$$

where $K_{a1}$ and $K_{a2}$ are the dissociation constants of $\mathrm{H_2CO_3}$. At pH 7.00, both $[\mathrm{OH^-}]$ and $[\mathrm{H^+}]$ can be neglected. Besides, $[\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}]\ll[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]$ (from 6.1).

$$
\text{Alkalinity}=[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]+2[\mathrm{CO_3^{2-}}]+[\mathrm{OH^-}]-[\mathrm{H^+}]\approx[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}].
$$

From 6-6, mass balance gives $[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]=2[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}]$.

| Branch | Printed alkalinity | Input |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | $1.02\times10^{-3}$ | Ca from 6.6(a) |
| (b) | $1.035\times10^{-3}$ | Ca from 6.6(b) |
| (c) | $1.0744\times10^{-3}$ | Ca from 6-6(c) |
| (d) | $0.9831\times10^{-3}$ | Ca from 6-6(d) |
| (e) | $2.00\times10^{-3}$ | Assuming Ca concentration $40.1$ |

Alkalinity = (a) or (b) or (c) or (d) or (e).



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-8">

### 6.8


**(a)** Using $K_{eq}=4.28\times10^{-5}$:

$$
[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]=2[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}],\qquad[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}]=100\ \mathrm{mg\,dm^{-3}}=2.50\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}.
$$

The printed substitution is

$$
K_{eq}=4.28\times10^{-5}=\frac{[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}][\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]^2}{[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]}=\frac{4[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}]}{[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]}.
$$

The next printed value is $[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]=1.46\times10$ (no exponent is printed).

$$
p_{\mathrm{CO_2}}=\frac{[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]}{K_{\mathrm{CO_2}}}\times1.01\times10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}=4.28\times10^3\ \mathrm{Pa}.
$$

**(b)** Using $K_{eq}=5.00\times10^{-5}$:

$$
[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]=2[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}],\qquad[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}]=100\ \mathrm{mg\,dm^{-3}}=2.50\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}},
$$

$$
K_{eq}=5.00\times10^{-5}=\frac{[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}][\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]^2}{[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]}=\frac{4[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}]}{[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]},
$$

$$
[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]=1.25\times10^{-3},\qquad p_{\mathrm{CO_2}}=\frac{[\mathrm{CO_2(aq)}]}{K_{\mathrm{CO_2}}}\times1.01\times10^5\ \mathrm{Pa}=3.67\times10^3\ \mathrm{Pa}.
$$



</ProblemSection>
