**Problem 4. Radical decomposition (12 marks)**

Almost all halogen oxides are endothermic compounds, i.e. they are formed from elements with absorption of heat. Chlorine (I) oxide, a brownish-yellow gas, is not an exception. Upon mild heating it decomposes exothermically into chlorine and oxygen according to the equation
$$
2\mathrm{Cl_2O}\rightarrow2\mathrm{Cl_2}+\mathrm{O_2}.
$$
This reaction is not an elementary one. It proceeds through several consecutive elementary steps:
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1)\quad&\mathrm{Cl_2O+M\rightarrow Cl\cdot+ClO\cdot+M}&k_1\\
(2)\quad&\mathrm{Cl\cdot+Cl_2O\rightarrow Cl_2+ClO\cdot}&k_2\\
(3)\quad&2\mathrm{ClO\cdot\rightarrow Cl_2+O_2}&k_3
\end{aligned}
$$
($k_1$ – $k_3$ are the rate constants, M is any molecule)

**1** Using the Hess’ law calculate the reaction enthalpies for all elementary steps and for the overall reaction. For each case note whether the reaction is exothermic (write “$Q>0$”) or endothermic (“$Q<0$”). The necessary data are provided at the end of the problem.

Decomposition of oxygen difluoride, $\mathrm{OF_2}$, to fluorine and oxygen proceeds via a similar mechanism with the notable exception: reaction between two $\mathrm{FO\cdot}$ radicals yields two fluorine atoms rather than a difluorine molecule. This is due to the fact that the reaction
$$
2\mathrm{FO\cdot\rightarrow F_2+O_2}
$$
is highly exothermic, and the amount of heat released is enough to break the F–F bond. The fluorine atoms formed then either dimerize or react with $\mathrm{OF_2}$. So, the overall mechanism is as follows:
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1)\quad&\mathrm{OF_2+M\rightarrow F\cdot+FO\cdot+M}&k_1\\
(2)\quad&\mathrm{F\cdot+OF_2\rightarrow F_2+FO\cdot}&k_2\\
(3)\quad&2\mathrm{FO\cdot\rightarrow 2F\cdot+O_2}&k_3\\
(4)\quad&2\mathrm{F\cdot+M\rightarrow F_2+M}&k_4
\end{aligned}
$$
($k_1$ – $k_4$ are the rate constants, M is any molecule)

Note that this is a chain reaction: $\mathrm{F\cdot}$ radical produces $\mathrm{FO\cdot}$, then $\mathrm{FO\cdot}$ radical produces $\mathrm{F\cdot}$ and the process repeats itself. The radicals $\mathrm{F\cdot}$ and $\mathrm{FO\cdot}$ are the carriers of the chain process.

Under the certain conditions exothermic chain reactions can proceed with increasing rates. There are two possible reasons for this: a) self-heating of the reaction mixture as the reaction proceeds causes the increase of the rate constants which makes the reaction go faster and b) over a cycle of chain propagation the number of the chain carriers increases which leads to exponential growth of their concentrations. These two regimes are called *thermal mode* and *chain-branching mode*, respectively.

**4a** Using the law of mass action derive the expression for the change rate of the total concentration of chain carriers, [CC], in the reaction of $\mathrm{OF_2}$ decomposition. Do not express [M] in terms of concentrations of certain substances. Note that concentrations of radical species are negligible regardless of the reaction mode.
