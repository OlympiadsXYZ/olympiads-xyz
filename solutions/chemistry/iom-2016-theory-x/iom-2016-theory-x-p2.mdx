A gas with density $1.50\ \mathrm{g/L}$ (at $273\ \mathrm K$ and $1\ \mathrm{atm}$) was passed through a colorless solution containing $1.00\ \mathrm g$ of inorganic salt **X** that gives a violet flame test. After passage the density of the gas was reduced to $1.43\ \mathrm{g/L}$ (at $273\ \mathrm K$ and $1\ \mathrm{atm}$) but the volume remained unchanged. Evaporation of the obtained solution gave $1.289\ \mathrm g$ of a white powder **Y** that can also be obtained by oxidation of **X**. If the initial gas is passed through a solution of **X** in the presence of starch, a violet coloration appears.

### Official answers and grading scheme

**1** Calculations:

Molar mass of the initial gas: $M=\rho V_\mathrm m=1.50\cdot22.4=33.6\ \mathrm{g/mol}$. There is no individual gas with such molar mass, so we suppose it to be a gas mixture.

Molar mass of the gas after passage through the solution: $M=\rho V_\mathrm m=1.43\cdot22.4=32\ \mathrm{g/mol}$, this is pure dioxygen, $\mathrm{O_2}$. The initial mixture consists of ozone $\mathrm{O_3}$ and dioxygen $\mathrm{O_2}$.

Let $x$ be the molar fraction of $\mathrm{O_2}$, then

$32x+48(1-x)=33.6$, $x=0.9$.

Substances and their molar fractions: $\mathrm{O_2}$ – 90%, $\mathrm{O_3}$ – 10%

Lewis structures:

**Total – 6 pts**

**2** Calculations:

The violet flame coloration is typical for potassium salts, a violet coloration of starch indicates that the salt contains iodine. It can be potassium iodide (**X**). The quantity of this salt should be equal to that of another oxidation product, containing iodine.

So, $n(\mathrm{KI})=1.00/166=0.006024=n(\mathrm Y)$.

$M(\mathrm Y)=1.289/0{,}006024=214\ \mathrm{g/mol}$, it corresponds to potassium iodate $\mathrm{KIO_3}$.

**X** = $\mathrm{KI}$     **Y** = $\mathrm{KIO_3}$

Reaction equations:
$$
\mathrm{KI+3O_3=KIO_3+3O_2}
$$
$$
\mathrm{2KI+O_3+H_2O=I_2+O_2+2KOH}
$$

X and Y – 2 pts each (ZERO without calculations)

Reaction equations – 1 pt each (0.5 pts if not balanced)

**Total – 6 pts**

**5** Calculation:

The molar mass corresponding to 3 oxygen atoms: $3\cdot16/0.246=195\ \mathrm{g/mol}$. After subtracting molar mass of $\mathrm{IO_3}$ we get: $195-127-3\cdot16=20\ \mathrm{g/mol}$. This value is an average between K and H, so **Z** is an acid iodate $\mathrm{KIO_3\cdot HIO_3}$, or $\mathrm{KHI_2O_6}$

**Z** = $\mathrm{KHI_2O_6}$
