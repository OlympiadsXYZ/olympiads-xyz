**Problem 3. A Silk Purse out of a Sow’s Ear, or Drugs from Biomass (12 marks)**

*Dedicated to the blessed memory of Professor A. V. Butin*

A significant attention is now paid to the use of biomass as a source of organic chemicals. One of the most important products of biomass processing is furfural (furan-2-carbaldehyde). It can be easily transformed into other furan derivatives, including medicines, or linear alkanes which can be used as diesel fuel. One of the methods for furfural transformation into biofuel as well as preparation of bactericidal agent *furacin* from furfural are given in the scheme below.

**[14 т.]**

Furan derivatives can be efficiently converted into other heterocycles. Thus, in $1930^\mathrm{th}$ professor of Moscow State University Yu. K. Yuriev developed industrial transformation of furans into pyrroles under heating of furan with ammonia (amines) above $400^\circ\mathrm C$ in the presence of alumina. In a laboratory, the sequence of furan hydrolysis followed by Paal-Knorr reaction with ammonia (amine) is used for this transformation. This sequence can be realized as a two-step procedure or as a domino reaction. For example, $N$-(furfuryl)anthranylamide **G** ($\mathrm{R=CH_3}$) under treatment with conc. HCl in acetic acid afforded the new heterocyclic compound **J** *via* intermediates **H** and **I**. Nevertheless, compound **G** with $\mathrm{R=C(CH_3)_3}$ under the same conditions produced the corresponding product **H** only.

### Detailed solution

**1** The comparison of molecular formulas of furfural and compound **A** allows to conclude that at this step furan ring nitration is accompanied by addition of acetic anhydride. The last step in the synthesis of furacin is the reaction with semicarbazide, *i.e.* furacin has 4 nitrogen atoms (three from semicarbazide and 1 from nitro group). From the mass content of nitrogen and carbon we can determine the molecular formula of furacin as $\mathrm{C_6H_6N_4O_4}$. Therefore, formula of **B** is $\mathrm{C_5H_3NO_4}$, and the transformation of **A** to **B** is hydrolysis of diacetyl ketal moiety producing aldehyde function.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '97.3%', '--fig-max': '623px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/iom-2016-theory-x/k3-detailed-a-b-furacin.png" alt="Native complete furfural to A, B and furacin molecular sequence with all reaction arrows and reagent structures." width="1947" height="409" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


According to the text of the problem, compound **F** is a linear alkane. The content of carbon in **F** allows to conclude that this is $n$-decane ($\mathrm{C_{10}H_{22}}$), *i.e.*, in the formation of **F** one molecule of **C** reacts with one molecule of **D**. This reaction proceeds under acid catalysis. It is possible to suppose that this is Friedel-Crafts alkylation of the furan ring by furan-containing alcohol which is obtained from furfural by catalytic hydrogenation. Evidently, hydrogenation of side chain should proceed faster than hydrogenation of the furan ring itself. So, **C** is furfuryl alcohol. Hydrogenolysis of **C** produces then 2-methylfuran (**D**) which is alkylated by **C** under Friedel-Crafts conditions affording 5-methyldifurylmethane **E**. Its hydrogenation is accompanied by C–O bonds hydrogenolysis furnishing linear alkane **F**.

(**7 structures, 2 points for each, 14 points in total**).

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '90.4%', '--fig-max': '579px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/iom-2016-theory-x/k3-detailed-c-d-e-f.png" alt="Native detailed C/D/E/F molecular reaction network, full bonds, reagents and arrows." width="1809" height="430" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

**2** It is clear from the text of the problem, that the first step is hydrolysis of the furan ring. The product then enters the Paal-Knorr synthesis, producing pyrrole ring in **J**. This ring formation is a stepwise process: initially, nucleophilic amino group attacks the closest carbonyl function affording 7-membered ring in **I** (bicyclic compound). Then tautomerization of imine to enamine and attack of amino group on the second carbonyl function accomplished the pyrrole ring formation furnishing tricyclic product, pyrrolo[1,2-$a$]benzo[1,4]diazepine. The bulky *tert*-butyl group prevents cyclization of **I** to **J** due to steric repulsions. As a result, the intermediate imine **I** undergoes hydrolysis to diketone **H** instead of cyclization.

(**3 structures, 2 points for each, 6 points in total**).

<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/iom-2016-theory-x/k3-detailed-g-h-i-j.png" alt="Native detailed G/H/I/J molecular network with all heterocycle structures, hydrogen/charge labels and acid/hydrolysis arrows." width="1888" height="380" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

**3** Evidently, the last step of isocryptolepine synthesis is methylation of the nitrogen atom. Therefore, **N** is the corresponding desmethyl derivative. The fully aromatic indoloquinoline is the most stable tautomer for compound **N**.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '47.3%', '--fig-max': '303px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/iom-2016-theory-x/k3-detailed-n-isocryptolepine.png" alt="Native N to isocryptolepine methylation structures, complete reagents and bonds." width="947" height="301" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


Compound **K** is formed by diazotization of $o$-nitroaniline (6 carbon atoms) followed by the reaction of the formed diazonium salt with 2-methylfuran **D** (5 carbon atoms) in the presence of Cu(I) salt. This is Meerwein arylation furnishing 2-aryl-5-methylfuran. The structure can be also determined from the carbon content. Molecular weight of **K** is $132/0.65=203$ a.u. Compound **K** contains nitro group (46 a.u.) and furan oxygen atom (16 a.u.). Therefore, molecule of **K** has no more nitrogen atoms and has 9 hydrogen atoms. Furan is known to have excellent $\alpha/\beta$-selectivity in both radical and electrophilic reactions. So, 2-nitrophenyl group substitutes $\alpha$-hydrogen atom. From the carbon content in compound **L** we can find that its molecular weight is 173, *i.e.* 30 a.u. smaller than that of **K**. So, this is a result of reduction of nitro group leading to the corresponding aniline.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '94.2%', '--fig-max': '603px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/iom-2016-theory-x/k3-detailed-k-l.png" alt="Native diazotization/Meerwein arylation and reduction K/L reaction scheme with charge/counterion labels." width="1884" height="297" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


The next step is quite unusual. This is started by acid-catalyzed imine formation *via* reaction between the aldehyde and amine moieties. The protonation of the imine nitrogen increases electrophilicity of the imine carbon atom and it attacks the furan ring located in close proximity. For other arenes this attack proceeds onto *ortho*-carbon atom producing six-membered ring. However, if we try to write the corresponding product in this case, we will obtain compound **M′** which cannot produce **N** after reduction with iron and acetic acid.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '97.9%', '--fig-max': '627px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/iom-2016-theory-x/k3-path-a.png" alt="Native complete rejected path a to M prime, including curved mechanistic arrows, charges and crossed-out final conversion to N." width="1959" height="801" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


There is an alternative possibility – electrophilic attack of the protonated imine on the substituted $\alpha$-carbon atom (*ipso* attack). This attack is provided by the extremely high $\alpha/\beta$-selectivity of the furans in the reaction with electrophiles and leads to the spiro-intermediate which undergoes furan ring opening affording 2-(2-nitrophenyl)-3-(3-oxobut-1-en-1-yl)indole **M**. The reduction of nitro group in **M** produces the corresponding amino group which adds to $\alpha,\beta$-unsaturated ketone moiety (Michael addition) producing tetracyclic intermediate. It undergoes aromatization *via* elimination of acetone molecule (in its enol form) through the concerted six-membered transition state.

<figure className="problem-figure problem-figure--sized" style={{'--fig-w': '98.5%', '--fig-max': '631px'}}>
<img src="https://pub-43290baaaff14857b5dd59610ea438c7.r2.dev/problems/iom-2016-theory-x/k3-path-b.png" alt="Native complete path b via spiro intermediate and M to N, all curly arrows, intermediates, bracket endpoints and hydrogen labels." width="1971" height="805" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>


(**Structural formulas of K, L, N – 2 points for each. Structural formulae of M – 4 points. 10 points in total**).

Determination of the structure of **M** is a rather complex task. However, this structure contributes only 4 points of 30. Those students who are able to determine this structure will get much pleasure from the unusual transformations leading to **M** and from **M** to **N** as well as from their personal ability to find the solution.
