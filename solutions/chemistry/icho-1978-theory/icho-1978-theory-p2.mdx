in particular solutions of the problems set in the first years of the IChO competition that

## Условие


A sample of water under investigation had 10° of temporary hardness and 10° of permanent hardness. Hardness of the water was caused by cations Fe²⁺ and Ca²⁺ only.

A volume of 10.00 dm³ of the water was at disposal. From this volume 100.00 cm³ were taken for further procedure. The water was oxidised with a H₂O₂ solution and then precipitated with an aqueous ammonia solution. A brown precipitate was dried and after an appropriate heating 0.01432 g of an anhydrous product was obtained.

**2.1**   
Calculate the molar ratio of Fe²⁺ : Ca²⁺ in the water under investigation.



**2.2**   
In another experiment, 10.00 dm³ of the water was used again. The temporary hardness caused by cations Ca²⁺ was removed first and the permanent hardness caused by cations Fe²⁺ was removed by addition of Na₃PO₄. Calculate the mass of the precipitate (in its anhydrous form) on the assumption that only one half of cations Fe²⁺ was oxidised to Fe³⁺ in 10.00 dm³ of the water analysed. Calculation should be made with an accuracy of one hundredth. Give the molar ratio in integers.

1° of hardness = 10 mg CaO in 1 dm³ of water.

Relative atomic masses:

$$A_r(\mathrm{Ca})=40.08;\quad A_r(\mathrm{Fe})=55.85;\quad A_r(\mathrm C)=12.01;$$
$$A_r(\mathrm H)=1.01;\quad A_r(\mathrm P)=31.00;\quad A_r(\mathrm O)=16.00.$$



### Source notes

The key prints 1.293 g of FeO in earlier steps but 1.289 g in its molar-ratio fraction and 0.6445 g in its half-iron calculation. Those source values are retained.




## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи решението от архива">




<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-1">

### 2.1


Anhydrous product: Fe₂O₃.

$m(\mathrm{Fe_2O_3})=0.01432$ g from 100 cm³ of water, i.e. 1.432 g from 10 dm³.

$$1\ \mathrm{mol\ Fe_2O_3}\Longleftrightarrow2\ \mathrm{mol\ FeO}$$
$$n(\mathrm{Fe_2O_3})=\frac{1.432\ \mathrm g}{159.7\ \mathrm{g\ mol^{-1}}}\approx0.009\ \mathrm{mol}$$
$$m(\mathrm{FeO})=nM=2\times0.009\ \mathrm{mol}\times71.85\ \mathrm{g\ mol^{-1}}\approx1.293\ \mathrm g$$

1° of hardness = 10 mg CaO / dm³ of water.

$$1^\circ\text{ of hardness}=\frac{M(\mathrm{FeO})}{M(\mathrm{CaO})}\times10\ \mathrm{mg}=12.81\ \mathrm{mg\ FeO/dm^3\ of\ water}$$
$$\frac{1.293\ \mathrm{g\ FeO}}{0.1281\ \mathrm{g\ FeO}}\approx10^\circ\text{ of hardness}$$

Since the water has totally 20° of hardness, and 10° of hardness fall on FeO, the other 10° of hardness are attributed to CaO which corresponds to 1 g of CaO in 10 dm³ of the water.

Molar ratio:

$$
n(\mathrm{FeO}):n(\mathrm{CaO})=\frac{m(\mathrm{FeO})}{M(\mathrm{FeO})}:\frac{m(\mathrm{CaO})}{M(\mathrm{CaO})}
=\frac{1.289\ \mathrm g}{71.85\ \mathrm{g\ mol^{-1}}}:\frac{1\ \mathrm g}{56.08\ \mathrm{g\ mol^{-1}}}=1:1
$$



</ProblemSection>

### 2.2


A volume of 10.00 dm³ of the water contains so much iron that corresponds to 1.293 g of FeO. 50% of iron (0.6445 g of FeO) were oxidised to Fe(III), and therefore Fe₃(PO₄)₂ as well as FePO₄ are formed at the same time.

| FeO | Fe₃(PO₄)₂ |
|---|---|
| 3 mol | 1 mol |
| 215.55 g | 357.55 g |
| 0.6445 g | 1.0699 g |

| FeO | FePO₄ |
|---|---|
| 1 mol | 1 mol |
| 71.85 g | 150.85 g |
| 0.6445 g | 1.3542 g |

Mass of the precipitate:

$$1.0699\ \mathrm g+1.3542\ \mathrm g=2.4241\ \mathrm g$$



</ProblemSection>
