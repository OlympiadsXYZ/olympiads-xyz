In the previous forty-five years many known and unknown people - teachers, authors, pupils, and organizers proved their abilities and knowledge and contributed to the success of this already well known and world-wide competition. ## Условие

**Introduction**

Composition of a solution containing only MgCl₂ and NaCl can be determined by an indirect titration method, performing precipitation titration to determine total chloride followed by complex-formation titration to determine magnesium ions. A common precipitation technique is the Fajans method. In this argentometric procedure, silver nitrate precipitates chloride; the endpoint is detected by an adsorption indicator, typically dichlorofluorescein, a weak organic acid. Before the endpoint, silver chloride particles are negatively charged by adsorption of excess chloride. Indicator anions are repelled by their negative surface, imparting yellow-green colour. Beyond equivalence, silver chloride particles adsorb silver ions; the positively charged layer attracts dichlorofluoresceinate ions, displaying pink-red colour. Dextrin stabilizes silver chloride particles against coagulation.

Magnesium can be determined by complexometric titration with ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA). As a hexadentate ligand, EDTA forms chelates with all metal ions except alkali-metal ions in a 1:1 mole ratio regardless of cation charge. Eriochrome Black T (EBT) is common for EDTA titrations. At pH > 7.00, EBT is blue without metal ions and red when coordinated to metal ions. Here chloride in the MgCl₂/NaCl solution is determined by Fajans method and magnesium concentration by EDTA.

A 100 cm³ solution prepared by dissolving MgCl₂ and NaCl in water is the unknown sample. Determine the mass concentration of both salts in g/100 cm³.

**Reagents**

- Unknown solution, 100 cm³.
- Dextrin in three Eppendorf tubes in a zipper bag.
- Dichlorofluorescein indicator.
- AgNO₃ solution, c = 0.1 mol dm⁻³*, 100 cm³.
- EDTA solution, c = 0.01 mol dm⁻³*, 100 cm³.
- pH10 buffer (NH₃/NH₄Cl), 5 cm³.
- Eriochrome Black T indicator.

*The exact concentration is given on the label.

**A. Determination of total chloride concentration by Fajans method**

1. Use a 10 cm³ pipette to transfer a 10.0 cm³ aliquot from “unknown solution” to a 250 cm³ Erlenmeyer flask. Complete to approximately 100 cm³ with distilled water.
2. Take one Eppendorf tube from the zipper bag labelled “dextrin” and transfer all contents to the flask.
3. Add 5 drops of dichlorofluorescein indicator.
4. Record exact AgNO₃ standard-solution concentration.
5. Fill one burette with standard AgNO₃.
6. Titrate until the whole solution is pink-red.
7. Record AgNO₃ volume in cm³.
8. Use the same flask for repeat titration. First pour its contents into “Aqueous Waste” and rinse twice with distilled water.

**B. Determination of Mg²⁺ by direct titration with EDTA**

1. Fill the second burette with standard EDTA.
2. Record its exact concentration.
3. Use a 25 cm³ pipette to transfer a 25.0 cm³ aliquot to a 250 cm³ Erlenmeyer flask. Complete to approximately 100 cm³ with distilled water.
4. Use a 1 cm³ pipette to add 1.0 cm³ pH10 buffer.
5. Add 3–4 drops of EBT.
6. Titrate with standard EDTA until red changes to blue.
7. Record EDTA volume in cm³.
8. Use the same flask for repeats; pour into “Aqueous Waste” and rinse twice with water.

**Treatment of Data**

**1.1** Determine the amount of Cl⁻ ions in mmol in 100 cm³ of unknown solution.

**1.2** Determine the amount of Mg²⁺ ions in mmol in 100 cm³ of unknown solution.

**1.3** Calculate concentrations of both MgCl₂ and NaCl in g/100 cm³ in unknown solution.
