Preface  
This publication contains the competition problems (Volume 2) from the 21st – 40th  
International Chemistry Olympiads (IChO) organized in the years 1989 – 2008  and is a
continuation of the publication that appeared last year as Volume 1 and contained
competition problems from the first twenty IChOs.  The whole review of the competition
tasks set in the IChO in its fourty-year history is a contribution of the IChO International  
Information Centre in Bratislava (Slovakia) to the development of this world known
international competition. This Volume 2 contains 154 theoretical and 46 practical
competition problems from the mentioned years. The review as a whole presents
altogether 279 theoretical and 96 practical problems.  
In the elaboration of this collection the editor had to face certain difficulties because
the aim was not only to make use of past recordings but also to give them such a form
that they may be used in practice and further chemical education. Consequently, it was
necessary to make some corrections in order to unify the form of the problems. However,
they did not concern the contents and language of the problems.  
Unfortunately, the authors of the particular competition problems are not known and
due to the procedure of the creation of the IChO competition problems, it is impossible to
assign any author's name to a particular problem. As the editor I would appreciate many  
times some discussion with the authors about any critical places that occurred in the text.  
On the other hand, any additional amendments to the text would be not correct from the  
historical point of view. Therefore, responsibility for the scientific content and language of
the problems lies exclusively with the organizers of the particular International Chemistry  
Olympiads.    

Some parts of texts, especially those gained as scanned materials, could not be
used directly and thus, several texts, schemes and pictures had to be re-written or created
again. Some solutions were often available in a brief form and necessary extent only, just
for the needs of members of the International Jury.  

Recalculations of the solutions were made in some special cases only when the
numeric results in the original solutions showed to be obviously not correct. Although the
numbers of significant figures in the results of several solutions do not obey the criteria
generally accepted, they were left without change.      



In this publication SI quantities and units are used and a more modern method of
chemical calculations is introduced. Only some exceptions have been made when, in an
effort to preserve the original text, the quantities and units have been used that are not SI.  
There were some problems with the presentation of the solutions of practical tasks,
because most of the relatively simple calculations were based on the experimental results
of contestants. Moreover, the practical problems are accompanied with answer sheets in
the last years and several additional questions and tasks have appeared in them that were
not a part of the text of the original experimental problems. Naturally, answer sheets could
not be included in this publication and can only be preserved as archive materials.
  
When reading the texts of the IChO problems one must admire and appreciate the
work of those many known and unknown people – teachers, authors, pupils, and
organizers – who contributed so much to development and success of this important
international competition.

I am sure about the usefulness of the this review of the IChO problems. It may serve
not only as archive material but, in particular, this review should serve to both competitors
and their teachers as a source of further inspiration in their preparation for this challenging
competition.




Bratislava, July 2009  






  

                                    Anton Sirota, editor

### Acidity of the hydrated copper(II) ion and precipitation of the hydroxide


Consider a $1.00\times10^{-2}\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}$ solution of copper(II) nitrate. The pH of this solution is 4.65.



**2.1**  
Give the equation for the formation of the conjugate base of the hydrated $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$ ion.



**2.2**  
Calculate the $pK_a$ of the corresponding acid-base pair.

The solubility product of copper(II) hydroxide is $K_{\mathrm{sp}}=1\times10^{-20}$.



**2.3**  
At what pH value hydroxide $\mathrm{Cu(OH)_2}$ precipitates from the solution under consideration? Justify your calculation showing that the conjugate base of this hydrated $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$ ion is present in negligible quantity.



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="native-section-2">

### Disproportionation of copper(I) ions


The $\mathrm{Cu^+}$ ion is involved in two redox couples:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Couple 1:}\quad\mathrm{Cu^+}+\mathrm{e^-}&\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{Cu},
&E_1^0&=+0.52\ \mathrm{V},\\
\text{Couple 2:}\quad\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}+\mathrm{e^-}&\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{Cu^+},
&E_2^0&=+0.16\ \mathrm{V}.
\end{aligned}
$$



**2.4**  
Write down the equation for the disproportionation of copper(I) ions and calculate the corresponding equilibrium constant.



**2.5**  
Calculate the composition of the solution (in $\mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}$) obtained on dissolving $1.00\times10^{-2}\ \mathrm{mol}$ of copper(I) in $1.0\ \mathrm{dm^3}$ of water.



**2.6**  
Apart from $\mathrm{Cu^+}$ ions, name two chemical species which also disproportionate in aqueous solution; write down the equations and describe the experimental conditions under which disproportionation is observed.

Consider the stability of copper(I) oxide, $\mathrm{Cu_2O}$, in contact with a $1.00\times10^{-2}\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}$ solution of $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$ ions. The solubility product of copper(I) oxide is $K_{\mathrm{sp}}=[\mathrm{Cu^+}][\mathrm{OH^-}]=1\times10^{-15}$.



**2.7**  
Calculate the pH value at which $\mathrm{Cu_2O}$ becomes stable. Quote a simple experiment allowing the observation of the precipitation of $\mathrm{Cu_2O}$.



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="native-section-3">

### Complex formation involving copper(I) and copper(II) ions




**2.8**  
The dissociation constant of the complex ion $\mathrm{[Cu(NH_3)_2]^+}$ is $K_D=1\times10^{-11}$. Calculate the standard electrode potential of the couple:

$$
\mathrm{[Cu(NH_3)_2]^+}+\mathrm{e^-}
\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{Cu}+2\mathrm{NH_3}.
$$



**2.9**  
The standard electrode potential of the couple

$$
\mathrm{[Cu(NH_3)_4]^{2+}}+2\mathrm{e^-}
\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{Cu}+4\mathrm{NH_3}
$$

is $E_3^0=-0{,}02\ \mathrm{V}$. Calculate the dissociation constant for the complex ion $\mathrm{[Cu(NH_3)_4]^{2+}}$.



**2.10**  
Deduce from it the standard electrode potential of the couple:

$$
\mathrm{[Cu(NH_3)_4]^{2+}}+\mathrm{e^-}
\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{[Cu(NH_3)_2]^+}+2\mathrm{NH_3}.
$$

Does the disproportionation of the cation $\mathrm{[Cu(NH_3)_2]^+}$ take place?



</ProblemSection>

### Source notes

The 2.8 key labels its couple with $\mathrm{Cu^+}$ although the question and equation reduce to Cu. Native placement and labels are retained. ## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи решението от архива">




<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-1">

### 2.1


$$
\mathrm{[Cu(H_2O)_4]^{2+}}+\mathrm{H_2O}
\longrightarrow\mathrm{H_3O^+}+\mathrm{[Cu(OH)(H_2O)_3]^+}.
$$



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-2">

### 2.2


$$
K_a=
\frac{[\mathrm{H_3O^+}][\mathrm{[Cu(OH)(H_2O)_3]^+}]}{[\mathrm{[Cu(H_2O)_4]^{2+}}]}
=\frac{[\mathrm{H_3O^+}]^2}{[\mathrm{[Cu(H_2O)_4]^{2+}}]}
=\frac{(2.24\times10^{-5})^2}{1\times10^{-2}}
=5.01\times10^{-8},
\qquad pK_a=7.30.
$$



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-3">

### 2.3


$$
[\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}][\mathrm{OH^-}]^2=1\times10^{-20};
\quad[\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}]=1\times10^{-2}
\ \Longrightarrow\ [\mathrm{OH^-}]=1\times10^{-9};
\quad\mathrm{pH}=5.
$$

$$
[\mathrm{[Cu(OH)(H_2O)_3]^+}]:[\mathrm{[Cu(H_2O)_4]^{2+}}]
=K_a:10^{-\mathrm{pH}}
=1\times10^{-7.3}:1\times10^{-5}=1:200.
$$



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-4">

### 2.4


$$
2\mathrm{Cu^+}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}+\mathrm{Cu},
\qquad
K=\frac{[\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}]}{[\mathrm{Cu^+}]^2}.
$$

$$
0.52-0.16=0.059\log K\quad\text{(Nernst equation)}
\ \Longrightarrow\ K=1\times10^6.
$$



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-5">

### 2.5


At equilibrium:

$$
[\mathrm{Cu^+}]+2[\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}]=1\times10^{-2},
\qquad
[\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}]=1\times10^6[\mathrm{Cu^+}],
$$

so that the following equation is obtained:

$$
2\times10^6[\mathrm{Cu^+}]^2+[\mathrm{Cu^+}]-1\times10^{-2}=0,
$$

with the solution $[\mathrm{Cu^+}]=7.07\times10^{-5}$ and $[\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}]=4.96\times10^{-3}$.



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-6">

### 2.6


Other disproportionation reactions:

$$
2\mathrm{H_2O_2}\longrightarrow2\mathrm{H_2O}+\mathrm{O_2}
\quad\text{(catalyzed by }\mathrm{KMnO_4},\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}\text{ etc.)},
$$

$$
\mathrm{Cl_2}+\mathrm{OH^-}\longrightarrow\mathrm{HCl}+\mathrm{ClO^-}
\quad\text{(basic conditions)}.
$$



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-7">

### 2.7


$$
\mathrm{Cu_2O}+2\mathrm{H_3O^+}+2\mathrm{e^-}
\longrightarrow2\mathrm{Cu}+3\mathrm{H_2O},
\qquad
[\mathrm{Cu^+}]=\frac{1\times10^{-15}}{[\mathrm{OH^-}]}.
$$

$$
E_1=0.52+\frac{0.059}{2}\log\left([\mathrm{Cu^+}][\mathrm{H_3O^+}]^2\right)
=0.49-0.0885\,\mathrm{pH}.
$$

$$
2\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}+3\mathrm{H_2O}+2\mathrm{e^-}
\longrightarrow\mathrm{Cu_2O}+2\mathrm{H_3O^+},
$$

$$
E_2=0.16+\frac{0.059}{2}
\log\frac{1\times10^{-4}}{[\mathrm{Cu^+}][\mathrm{H_3O^+}]^2}
=0.07+0.0885\,\mathrm{pH}.
$$

$\mathrm{Cu_2O}$ is stable when $E_2>E_1$, i.e. $0.42<0.177\,\mathrm{pH}$, or $\mathrm{pH}>2.4$.

$\mathrm{Cu_2O}$ can be obtained by the reduction of $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$ in acid or basic media, e.g. by Fehling's solution or reducing sugars.



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-8">

### 2.8


$$
\mathrm{[Cu(NH_3)_2]^+}\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{Cu^+}+2\mathrm{NH_3},
\qquad
K_D=\frac{[\mathrm{Cu^+}][\mathrm{NH_3}]^2}{[\mathrm{[Cu(NH_3)_2]^+}]}
=1\times10^{-11}.
$$

Knowing $E^0(\mathrm{Cu^+/Cu})=0.52\ \mathrm{V}$, the printed $E^0(\mathrm{[Cu(NH_3)_2]^+/Cu^+})$ becomes:

$$
E_{\mathrm{f1}}=0.52-0.06\,pK_D=-0.14\ \mathrm{V}.
$$



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-9">

### 2.9


The standard emf of a $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}/Cu}$ cell is thus:

$$
E^0=\frac{0.5+0.16}{2}=0.33\ \mathrm{V},
\qquad E_3^0=0.33-0.03\,pK_2.
$$

Thereout:

$$
pK_2=\frac{0.33-E_3^0}{0.03}
=\frac{0.33-(-0.02)}{0.03}=12.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{[Cu(NH_3)_4]^{2+}}+2\mathrm{e^-}&\longrightarrow\mathrm{Cu}+4\mathrm{NH_3},
&E^0&=-0.02\ \mathrm{V},\\
\mathrm{[Cu(NH_3)_2]^+}+\mathrm{e^-}&\longrightarrow\mathrm{Cu}+2\mathrm{NH_3},
&E^0&=-0.14\ \mathrm{V},\\
\mathrm{[Cu(NH_3)_4]^{2+}}+\mathrm{e^-}&\longrightarrow\mathrm{[Cu(NH_3)_2]^+}+2\mathrm{NH_3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Since only $\Delta G^0$ is additive and from $\Delta G^0=-nFE^0$ it follows:

$$
E_{\mathrm{f2}}=2\times(-0.02)-(-0.14)=0.10\ \mathrm{V}.
$$



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-10">

### 2.10


$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{[Cu(NH_3)_2]^+}+\mathrm{e^-}&\longrightarrow\mathrm{Cu}+2\mathrm{NH_3},
&E_{\mathrm{f1}}&=-0.14\ \mathrm{V},\\
\mathrm{[Cu(NH_3)_4]^{2+}}+\mathrm{e^-}&\longrightarrow\mathrm{[Cu(NH_3)_2]^+}+2\mathrm{NH_3},
&E_{\mathrm{f2}}&=0.10\ \mathrm{V}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $E_{\mathrm{f1}}<E_{\mathrm{f2}}$ the $\mathrm{[Cu(NH_3)_2]^+}$ ion doesn't disproportionate (the emf would be $-0.14-0.10=-0.24\ \mathrm{V}$).



</ProblemSection>
