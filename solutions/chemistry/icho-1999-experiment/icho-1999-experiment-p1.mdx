The reaction between acetone and iodine in aqueous solution is catalyzed by H⁺.

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3(aq)+I_2(aq)}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{H^+}}
\mathrm{CH_3COCH_2I(aq)+H^+(aq)+I^-(aq)}.
$$

The rate law for this reaction is:

$$
-\frac{d[\mathrm{I_2}]}{dt}=k[\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3}]^x[\mathrm{I_2}]^y[\mathrm{H^+}]^z,\qquad
v_0=k[\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3}]_0^x[\mathrm{I_2}]_0^y[\mathrm{H^+}]_0^z.
$$

where H⁺ ions are the catalyst. In order to determine the rate constant $k$ and the kinetic orders $x$, $y$, $z$, the initial rate of reaction is measured, where $[\,]_0$ are the initial concentrations of acetone, I₂ and H⁺, respectively.

If the initial rates are measured for various initial concentrations of the reactants then the order with respect to each reactant can be obtained.

The initial rate is obtained by measuring the decrease in the I₂(aq) concentration after a short time interval (7.0 min. in this experiment) after the start of the reaction. Aqueous sodium acetate solution is added to stop the reaction after 7 minutes. The acetate ion reacts immediately with the H⁺ to produce acetic acid and so reducing the concentration of H⁺. The reaction is thus stopped as there is no catalyst present.

Since the reaction does not come to a complete halt, the solution should be titrated immediately after the addition of the sodium acetate solution.

The remaining iodine I₂(aq) is determined by titration with sodium thiosulphate, Na₂S₂O₃. As the end point of the titration is approached, starch indicator is added and the titration is continued until the blue colour disappears.

### A — Standardization of iodine solution




**1**  
Pipette 5.00 cm³ aqueous iodine into a clean 125 cm³ Erlenmeyer flask.



**2**  
Add 10 cm³ distilled water using a graduated cylinder.



**3**  
Titrate with standard 0.02 M sodium thiosulphate until pale yellow.



**4**  
Add 3–4 drops starch; continue until blue disappears.



**5**  
Record initial/final thiosulphate volumes and volume used on the answer sheet.



**6**  
Repeat steps 1–5 as necessary.



**7**  
Give the titre used for calculation on the answer sheet.



**8**  
Calculate iodine concentration.



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="native-section-3">

### B — Kinetic study




**1**  
Label stoppered flasks I–IV.



**2**  
Add these volumes, in cm³, and stopper each flask immediately.

| Flask | Water | 0.100 M HCl | 50% acetone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | 5.00 | 5.00 | 5.00 |
| II | 0.0 | 5.00 | 5.00 |
| III | 0.0 | 5.00 | 10.00 |
| IV | 0.0 | 10.00 | 5.00 |



**3**  
Measure 10 cm³ of 0.50 M sodium acetate in a graduated cylinder.



**4**  
Set the stopwatch to 0.0000.



**5**  
Pipette 5.00 cm³ iodine into flask I. Start timing as its first drop is added.



**6**  
Stopper and swirl continuously.



**7**  
Remove stopper just before 7.0 min. At 7.0 min immediately pour in the measured 10 cm³ sodium acetate; shake well.



**8**  
Titrate remaining iodine with standard thiosulphate.



**9**  
Record thiosulphate volume.



**10**  
Repeat steps 3–9 for II–IV, using iodine volumes 10.00, 5.00 and 5.00 cm³ respectively at step 5.



</ProblemSection>

### B — Calculations




**1** **(B-1)**


Calculate initial molar concentrations of iodine, acetone and HCl in I–IV, assuming additive volumes.



**2** **(B-2)**


Calculate iodine concentrations remaining after 7.0 min.



**3** **(B-3)**


Calculate initial reaction rates in M s⁻¹.



**4** **(B-4)**


The rate law is $-d[\mathrm{I_2}]/dt=k[\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3}]^x[\mathrm{I_2}]^y[\mathrm{H^+}]^z$. Calculate $x,y,z$ from rates and initial concentrations; round to nearest integers and fill the answer sheet. Write the rate equation.



**5** **(B-5)**


Calculate each flask's rate constant with proper units.



**6** **(B-6)**


Give the mean rate constant.



</ProblemSection>

In A, competitors use the bottle's standard thiosulphate concentration and their titres to calculate iodine concentration. Results were expected in an answer-sheet table. In B the collection lists calculations B1–B5:

- B1: initial concentrations in mixtures.
- B2: remaining iodine concentration at seven minutes.
- B3: initial iodine disappearance rate,

$$
-\frac{d[\mathrm{I_2}]}{dt}=\frac{c(\mathrm{I_2})_{\mathrm{initial}}-c(\mathrm{I_2})_{7\mathrm{min}}}{7\times60\ \mathrm{s}}.
$$

- B4: compare II:I, III:I and IV:I to find **$x=1,y=0,z=1$**, giving $v=k[\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3}][\mathrm{H^+}]$.
- B5: calculate each mixture's rate constant and their mean.




</Spoiler>
