## Условие

Nitrogen oxides, common pollutants in the ambient air, are primarily nitric oxide, NO, and nitrogen dioxide, NO₂. Atmospheric nitric oxide is produced mainly during thunderstorms and in the internal combustion engines. At high temperatures NO reacts with H₂ to produce nitrous oxide, N₂O, a greenhouse gas.

$$
2\,\mathrm{NO}(g)+\mathrm{H_2}(g)\to\mathrm{N_2O}(g)+\mathrm{H_2O}(g).
$$

To study the kinetics of this reaction at 820 °C, initial rates for the formation of N₂O were measured using various initial partial pressures of NO and H₂.

| Experiment | Initial p(NO) / torr | Initial p(H₂) / torr | Initial rate of N₂O production / torr s⁻¹ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 120.0 | 60.0 | 8.66·10⁻² |
| 2 | 60.0 | 60.0 | 2.17·10⁻² |
| 3 | 60.0 | 180.0 | 6.62·10⁻² |

Throughout this problem do not use concentrations. Use units of pressure (torr) and time in seconds.

**1.1** Determine the experimental rate law and calculate the rate constant.

**1.2** Calculate the initial rate of disappearance of NO, if NO with a pressure of 2.00·10² torr and H₂ with 1.00·10² torr are mixed at 820 °C. (If you have been unable to calculate the value for the rate constant, you can use the value of 2·10⁻⁷ in appropriate unit.)

**1.3** Calculate the time elapsed to reduce the partial pressure of H₂ to the half of its initial value, if NO with a pressure of 8.00·10² torr and H₂ with 1.0 torr are mixed at 820 °C. (If you have been unable to calculate the value for the rate constant, you can use the value of 2·10⁻⁷ in appropriate unit.)

A proposed mechanism for the reaction between NO and H₂ is given below:

$$
2\,\mathrm{NO}(g)\underset{k_{-1}}{\stackrel{k_1}{\rightleftharpoons}}\mathrm{N_2O_2}(g),\qquad\mathrm{N_2O_2}(g)+\mathrm{H_2}(g)\xrightarrow{k_2}\mathrm{N_2O}(g)+\mathrm{H_2O}(g).
$$

**1.4** Derive the rate law for the formation of N₂O from the proposed mechanism using the steady-state approximation for the intermediate.

**1.5** Under what condition does this rate law reduce to the experimentally determined rate law found in Part 1.1? Tick the relevant answer.

- If $k_{-1}\ll k_2p_{\mathrm{H_2}}$.
- If $k_{-1}\gg k_2p_{\mathrm{H_2}}$.
- If $k_{-1}>k_2$.
- If $k_1>k_{-1}$.

**1.6** Express the experimentally determined rate constant $k$ in terms of $k_1$, $k_{-1}$ and $k_2$.

**1.7** Select the schematic energy diagram that is consistent with the proposed reaction mechanism and experimental rate law.

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи решението от архива">

**1.1**

$$
R=k(p_{\mathrm{NO}})^a(p_{\mathrm{H_2}})^b.
$$

$$
\frac{R_1}{R_2}=\frac{8.66\cdot10^{-2}}{2.17\cdot10^{-2}}=3.99=\frac{k120^a60^b}{k60^a60^b}=2^a=3.99\Rightarrow a=2.
$$

$$
\frac{R_3}{R_2}=\frac{6.62\cdot10^{-2}}{2.17\cdot10^{-2}}=3.05=\frac{k60^a180^b}{k60^a60^b}=3^b=3.05\Rightarrow b=1.
$$

$$
R=k(p_{\mathrm{NO}})^2p_{\mathrm{H_2}},\qquad k=\frac{8.66\cdot10^{-2}}{120^2\times60}=1.00\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm{torr^{-2}\,s^{-1}}.
$$

**1.2**

$$
R=\frac{\Delta p_{\mathrm{N_2O}}}{\Delta t}=-\frac12\frac{\Delta p_{\mathrm{NO}}}{\Delta t}=1.0\cdot10^{-7}\times200^2\times100=0.40\,\mathrm{torr\,s^{-1}}.
$$

$$
-\frac{\Delta p_{\mathrm{NO}}}{\Delta t}=0.80\,\mathrm{torr\,s^{-1}}.
$$

**1.3** $R=k(p_{\mathrm{NO}})^2p_{\mathrm{H_2}}$. Since $p_{\mathrm{NO}}\gg p_{\mathrm{H_2}}$, $R=k'p_{\mathrm{H_2}}$, where $k'=k(p_{\mathrm{NO}})^2$.

$$
k'=1.0\cdot10^{-7}(8.00\cdot10^2)^2=0.064\,\mathrm{s^{-1}},\qquad t_{1/2}=\frac{\ln2}{k'}=10.8\,\mathrm s.
$$

**1.4**

$$
\frac{\Delta p_{\mathrm{N_2O}}}{\Delta t}=k_2p_{\mathrm{N_2O_2}}p_{\mathrm{H_2}}.
$$

Steady-state approximation for N₂O₂:

$$
\frac{\Delta p_{\mathrm{N_2O_2}}}{\Delta t}=0=k_1(p_{\mathrm{NO}})^2-k_{-1}p_{\mathrm{N_2O_2}}-k_2p_{\mathrm{N_2O_2}}p_{\mathrm{H_2}}=0.
$$

$$
p_{\mathrm{N_2O_2}}=\frac{k_1(p_{\mathrm{NO}})^2}{k_{-1}+k_2p_{\mathrm{H_2}}},\quad\frac{\Delta p_{\mathrm{N_2O}}}{\Delta t}=k_2p_{\mathrm{H_2}}\frac{k_1(p_{\mathrm{NO}})^2}{k_{-1}+k_2p_{\mathrm{H_2}}}.
$$

$$
R=k_1k_2\frac{(p_{\mathrm{NO}})^2p_{\mathrm{H_2}}}{k_{-1}+k_2p_{\mathrm{H_2}}}.
$$

**1.5** Correct answer: if $k_{-1}\gg k_2p_{\mathrm{H_2}}$.

**1.6** $k=k_1k_2/k_{-1}$.

**1.7** Energy diagram d is correct.


</Spoiler>
