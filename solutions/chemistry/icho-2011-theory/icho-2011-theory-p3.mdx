## Условие

At a temperature of 0 K, the total energy of a gaseous diatomic molecule AB is approximately given by $E=E_0+E_{\rm vib}$, where $E_0$ is the electronic energy of the ground state and $E_{\rm vib}$ is the vibrational energy. Allowed values of the vibrational energies are:

$$
E_{\rm vib}=(\nu+\tfrac12)\varepsilon,\quad\nu=0,1,2,\ldots,\quad\varepsilon=\frac{h}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{k}{\mu}},\quad\mu(\mathrm{AB})=\frac{m_Am_B}{m_A+m_B}.
$$

Here h is Planck’s constant, ν the vibrational quantum number, k the force constant and μ the reduced mass. At 0 K, ν may safely be assumed zero, and E₀ and k independent of isotopic substitution. Deuterium, D, is an isotope of hydrogen atom with mass number 2. For H₂, k = 575.11 N m⁻¹; isotopic molar masses of H and D are 1.0078 and 2.0141 g mol⁻¹. At 0 K, ε(H₂) = 1.1546 ε(HD) and ε(D₂) = 0.8167 ε(HD).

**3.1** Calculate the enthalpy change ΔH in kJ mol⁻¹ for H₂(g) + D₂(g) → 2 HD(g) at 0 K.

**3.2** Calculate the frequency in s⁻¹ of infrared photons that can be absorbed by HD molecule. (If you have been unable to calculate ε(HD), use 8.000·10⁻²⁰ J.)

The allowed electronic energies of H atom are $E=-R_H/n^2$, $n=1,2,\ldots$, where $R_H=13.5984\,\mathrm{eV}$ and 1 eV = 1.602·10⁻¹⁹ J. The total ground-state energy of H₂ is −31.675 eV, relative to the same reference as that of hydrogen atom.

**3.3** Calculate the dissociation energy DE in eV of a ground-state hydrogen molecule such that both H atoms are produced in their ground states.

**3.4** A molecule H₂ in the ground state dissociates into its atoms after absorbing a photon of wavelength 77.0 nm. Determine all possibilities for the electronic states of hydrogen atoms produced. For each case calculate the total kinetic energy KE in eV of the dissociated hydrogen atoms.

**3.5** Calculate the electron affinity EA of H₂⁺ ion in eV if its dissociation energy is 2.650 eV. (If you have been unable to calculate the dissociation energy of H₂, use 4.500 eV.)

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи решението от архива">

**3.1** H₂(g) + D₂(g) → 2 HD(g). $\Delta H=\Delta E+\Delta n_gRT$; $\Delta n_g=0$, so $\Delta H=\Delta E$.

$$
\Delta E=2E(\mathrm{HD})-E(\mathrm{H_2})-E(\mathrm{D_2}),\quad E_{\rm vib}=\tfrac12\varepsilon\quad(\nu=0\text{ at }0\,\mathrm K).
$$

$$
\Delta E=2(E_0+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{HD}}/2)-(E_0+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{H_2}}/2)-(E_0+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{D_2}}/2)=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{HD}}-\tfrac12(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{H_2}}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{D_2}}).
$$

$$
\Delta E=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{HD}}[1-\tfrac12(1.1546+0.8167)]=0.01435\varepsilon_{\mathrm{HD}}.
$$

$$
\mu(\mathrm{HD})=\frac{m_Hm_D}{m_H+m_D}=\frac{(1.0078\cdot10^{-3})(2.0141\cdot10^{-3})/N_A^2}{(1.0078\cdot10^{-3}+2.0141\cdot10^{-3})/N_A}=1.1154\cdot10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}.
$$

For one HD molecule:

$$
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{HD}}=\frac{h}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{k}{\mu}}=\frac{6.6261\cdot10^{-34}}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{575.11}{1.1154\cdot10^{-27}}}=7.5724\cdot10^{-20}\,\mathrm J.
$$

For 1 mol of HD, the key prints:

$$
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{HD}}=7.5724\cdot10^{-20}\,\mathrm J\times N_A=7.5724\cdot10^{-20}\times6.0221\cdot10^{23}=45\,600\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}.
$$

$$
\Delta H=\Delta E=0.01435\varepsilon_{\mathrm{HD}}=0.6544\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}.
$$

Source note: The printed 45 600 kJ mol⁻¹ intermediate unit/result conflicts with the following ΔH; it is preserved as printed.

**3.2** $h\nu=\Delta E$. $\Delta E=E_{\nu1}-E_{\nu0}=(3/2-1/2)\varepsilon_{\mathrm{HD}}=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{HD}}$; hence $\nu=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{HD}}/h$. From 3.1, ε(HD) = 7.5724·10⁻²⁰ J. The key prints:

$$
\nu=\frac{7.5724\cdot10^{-20}\,\mathrm J}{6.6261\cdot10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\,s}}=1.1428\cdot10^{-14}\,\mathrm s.
$$

Source note: The negative exponent and unit s are native printed discrepancies; no silent correction is made.

**3.3** H₂ → 2 H; for n = 1, ΔE = 2(−13.5984) − (−31.675) = 4.478 eV.

**3.4** H₂ + hν → H + H. The key first lists (n₁,n₂) = (1,1), (1,2), (2,1), (2,2), … . H₂ ground-state energy is −31.675 eV; λ = 77.0 nm.

$$
E_{\rm photon}=\frac{hc}{\lambda}=\frac{6.6261\cdot10^{-34}\times3.00\cdot10^8}{77.0\cdot10^{-9}}=2.58\cdot10^{-18}\,\mathrm J.
$$

The following two expressions are printed:

$$
E_{\rm photon}=\frac{6.6261\cdot10^{-34}}{1.602\cdot10^{-19}}=16.1\,\mathrm{eV},
$$

$$
\Delta E=E_{n1}+E_{n2}-E_{n\mathrm{H_2}}=-\frac{R_H}{n_1^2}-\frac{R_H}{n_2^2}-(31.675)<16.1\,\mathrm{eV}.
$$

Source note: The eV-conversion numerator and the sign before (31.675) are printed inconsistently with the preceding and following lines. Both native expressions are preserved.

For n₁ = n₂ = 1:

$$
\Delta E=-\frac{13.5984}{1^2}-\frac{13.5984}{1^2}+31.675=4.478\,\mathrm{eV},\quad\mathrm{KE}=16.1-4.478=11.6\,\mathrm{eV}.
$$

For (n₁,n₂) = (1,2) or (2,1):

$$
\Delta E=-\frac{13.5984}{1^2}-\frac{13.5984}{2^2}+31.675=14.677\,\mathrm{eV},\quad\mathrm{KE}=16.1-14.677=1.4\,\mathrm{eV}.
$$

For n₁ = n₂ = 2:

$$
\Delta E=-\frac{13.5984}{2^2}-\frac{13.5984}{2^2}+31.675=24.880\,\mathrm{eV}>16.1\,\mathrm{eV}.
$$

The key then prints “K.E. = 16.1 − 14.677 = 1.4 eV” again. Source note: This is a copied expression under the forbidden case, retained explicitly. The key concludes that the possibilities are (1,1), (1,2), (2,1).

**3.5** The native ionization-potential expression prints:

$$
\mathrm{IP(H)}=\Delta E_{n\to\infty}=-\frac{13.5984}{\infty^2}-\frac{13.5984}{1^2}=13.598\,\mathrm{eV}.
$$

Source note: The second minus sign conflicts with the stated positive result; both are printed on native124 and retained.

| Process | Energy relation |
|---|---|
| H₂⁺ + e → H₂ | EA(H₂⁺) = −IP(H₂) |
| H₂⁺ → H⁺ + H | DE(H₂⁺) = 2.650 eV |
| H → H⁺ + e | IP(H) = 13.598 eV |
| H₂ → H + H | DE(H₂) = 4.478 eV |

$$
\mathrm{EA(H_2^+)}=\mathrm{DE(H_2^+)}-\mathrm{IP(H)}-\mathrm{DE(H_2)}=2.650-13.598-4.478=-15.426\,\mathrm{eV}.
$$

Electron affinity H₂⁺ = −15.426 eV.


</Spoiler>
