## Условие

For sustainable energy, hydrogen appears to be the best energy carrier. The most efficient way of using hydrogen is generation of electrical energy in a fuel cell. However, storing hydrogen in large quantities is a challenge in fuel cell applications. Among chemical hydrides considered as solid hydrogen storage materials, sodium borohydride (NaBH₄), being non-toxic, stable and environmentally benign, appears to be the most promising one. Its hydrolysis releases H₂ gas slowly at ambient temperature and needs to be catalyzed.

NaBH₄(aq) + 2 H₂O(l) → Na⁺(aq) + BO₂⁻(aq) + 4 H₂(g), with a catalyst.

Colloidal ruthenium(0) nanoclusters are the most active catalysts in this hydrolysis even at room temperature and lead to complete H₂ release. Kinetic studies show first order with respect to the catalyst but zero order with respect to the substrate. The rate of hydrogen production per mole of ruthenium is 92 mol H₂ (mol Ru)⁻¹ min⁻¹ at 25 °C.

**4.1** Calculate the amount of ruthenium catalyst (in mg) which must be added to 0.100 dm³ of NaBH₄ solution with concentration 1.0 mol dm⁻³ to supply hydrogen at 0.100 dm³ min⁻¹ at 25 °C and 1.0 atm, as required for a portable fuel cell.

**4.2** For how many minutes will this system supply hydrogen gas at this rate?

**4.3** The Arrhenius activation energy is Ea = 42.0 kJ mol⁻¹. Calculate the temperature required to achieve the same rate of hydrogen evolution using half the amount of ruthenium catalyst used at 25.0 °C.

**4.4** A fuel cell (see figure) is made up of three segments sandwiched together: the anode, electrolyte and cathode. Hydrogen is fuel and oxygen oxidant. Two chemical reactions occur at the interfaces:

O₂(g) + 2 H₂O(l) + 4 e⁻ → 4 OH⁻(aq),

H₂(g) + 2 OH⁻(aq) → 2 H₂O(l) + 2 e⁻.

Net: 2 H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2 H₂O(l).

Hydrogen is supplied from sodium-borohydride hydrolysis. Calculate the standard cathode half-reaction potential if the anode standard reduction potential is −0.83 V and ΔfG°(H₂O(l)) is −237 kJ mol⁻¹.

**4.5** Calculate the volume of air at 25 °C and 1.0 atm needed to generate a constant current of 2.5 A for 3.0 hours in this cell. Assume air contains 20% by volume O₂(g).

**4.6** Fuel-cell efficiency is the ratio of work produced to heat dissipated by the cell reaction: $\eta_{\rm fuel\ cell}=\mathrm{work}/\mathrm{heat}$. Calculate the maximum efficiency at 25 °C and standard pressure using:

| Species | S° / J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ |
|---|---|
| H₂(g) | 130.7 |
| O₂(g) | 205.2 |
| H₂O(l) | 70.0 |

The second law of thermodynamics states that it is impossible to convert all heat qH from a high-temperature reservoir TH into work. Some energy qC must be transferred to a low-temperature reservoir TC. Thus a 100% efficient heat engine is thermodynamically impossible. Reversible operation, as in a Carnot cycle, gives maximum efficiency.

For a reversible engine between two reservoirs, $q_H=w+q_C$ and $q_H/T_H=q_C/T_C$.

**4.7** What should be the hot-reservoir temperature TH of a Carnot engine to maintain the fuel-cell efficiency calculated in 4.6 if cold-reservoir TC is 40 °C? (If unable to calculate the efficiency, use 0.80.)

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи решението от архива">

**4.1**

$$
n(\mathrm{H_2})=\frac{0.100\,\mathrm{dm^3\,min^{-1}}\times101.325\,\mathrm{kPa}}{8.314\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}K^{-1}}\times298\,\mathrm K}=4.1\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol\,min^{-1}}.
$$

$$
n(\mathrm{Ru})=\frac{4.1\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol\,H_2\,min^{-1}}}{92\,\mathrm{mol\,H_2\,min^{-1}}/(1\,\mathrm{mol\,Ru})}=4.5\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{mol}.
$$

$m(\mathrm{Ru})=4.5\cdot10^{-5}\times101.07=4.5\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm g=4.5\,\mathrm{mg}$.

**4.2** $n(\mathrm{NaBH_4})=1.0\,\mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}\times0.100\,\mathrm{dm^3}=0.10\,\mathrm{mol}$. The key prints “0.04 mol of H₂ are released from 0.10 mol NaBH₄”, followed by:

$$
t=\frac{0.40\,\mathrm{mol\,H_2}}{4.1\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol\,H_2\,min^{-1}}}=98\,\mathrm{min}.
$$

Source note: The prose 0.04 and numerator 0.40 are both printed; neither is silently replaced.

**4.3** $\mathrm{Rate}=k[\mathrm{Ru}]=Ae^{-E_a/(RT)}[\mathrm{Ru}]$.

$$
\frac{e^{-E_a/(R\times298)}}{e^{-E_a/(RT)}}=\frac12,\quad-\frac{E_a}{R}\left(\frac1{298}-\frac1T\right)=\ln\frac12.
$$

$$
\frac{4.20\cdot10^4\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}}}{8.314\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}K^{-1}}}\left(\frac1{298}-\frac1T\right)=\ln2.
$$

T = 311 K or 38 °C.

**4.4** Since $\Delta G^\circ=-nFE^\circ$, $2(-2.37\cdot10^5)=-4\times96485\times E^\circ_{\rm cell}$; $E^\circ_{\rm cell}=1.23\,\mathrm V$. $1.23=E^\circ_{\rm cathode}-(-0.83)$; $E^\circ_{\rm cathode}=0.40\,\mathrm V$.

**4.5** $2.5\,\mathrm A\times3\times3600\,\mathrm s=27000\,\mathrm C$.

$$
n(\mathrm{O_2})=27000\,\mathrm C\times\frac{1\,\mathrm{mol}}{4\times96485\,\mathrm C}=0.070\,\mathrm{mol},\quad V(\mathrm{O_2})=\frac{0.070\times8.314\times298}{101.325}=1.7\,\mathrm{dm^3}.
$$

V(air) = 8.6 dm³.

**4.6** $\Delta_{\rm rxn}G^\circ=\Delta_{\rm rxn}H^\circ-T\Delta_{\rm rxn}S^\circ$.

$$
\Delta_{\rm rxn}S^\circ=2S^\circ(\mathrm{H_2O}(l))-[2S^\circ(\mathrm{H_2}(g))+S^\circ(\mathrm{O_2}(g))]=2\times70.0-(2\times130.7+205.2)=-326.6\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}K^{-1}}.
$$

$$
\Delta_{\rm rxn}H^\circ=\Delta_{\rm rxn}G^\circ+T\Delta_{\rm rxn}S^\circ=-474+298.15(-326.6\cdot10^{-3})=-571.4\,\mathrm{kJ}.
$$

Maximum $w=\Delta_{\rm rxn}G^\circ=-474\,\mathrm{kJ}$; $\eta=(-474000\,\mathrm J)/(-571400\,\mathrm J)=0.83$.

**4.7** $\eta_{\rm engine}=w/q_H=(q_H-q_C)/q_H=1-q_C/q_H$. Since $q_H/T_H=q_C/T_C$, $q_C/q_H=T_C/T_H$.

$$
\eta_{\rm engine}=1-\frac{T_C}{T_H},\quad0.83=1-\frac{313}{T_H},\quad T_H=1.8\cdot10^3\,\mathrm K\ \text{or}\ T_H\approx1.5\cdot10^3\,{}^\circ\mathrm C.
$$


</Spoiler>
