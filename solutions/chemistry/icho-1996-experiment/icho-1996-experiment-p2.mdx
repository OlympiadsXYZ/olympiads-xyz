This publication contains the competition problems (Volume 2) from the 21st – 40th  
International Chemistry Olympiads (IChO) organized in the years 1989 – 2008  and is a
continuation of the publication that appeared last year as Volume 1 and contained
competition problems from the first twenty IChOs.  The whole review of the competition
tasks set in the IChO in its fourty-year history is a contribution of the IChO International  
Information Centre in Bratislava (Slovakia) to the development of this world known
international competition. This Volume 2 contains 154 theoretical and 46 practical
competition problems from the mentioned years. The review as a whole presents
altogether 279 theoretical and 96 practical problems.  
In the elaboration of this collection the editor had to face certain difficulties because
the aim was not only to make use of past recordings but also to give them such a form
that they may be used in practice and further chemical education. Consequently, it was
necessary to make some corrections in order to unify the form of the problems. However,
they did not concern the contents and language of the problems.  
Unfortunately, the authors of the particular competition problems are not known and
due to the procedure of the creation of the IChO competition problems, it is impossible to
assign any author's name to a particular problem. As the editor I would appreciate many  
times some discussion with the authors about any critical places that occurred in the text.  
On the other hand, any additional amendments to the text would be not correct from the  
historical point of view. Therefore, responsibility for the scientific content and language of
the problems lies exclusively with the organizers of the particular International Chemistry  
Olympiads.    

Some parts of texts, especially those gained as scanned materials, could not be
used directly and thus, several texts, schemes and pictures had to be re-written or created
again. Some solutions were often available in a brief form and necessary extent only, just
for the needs of members of the International Jury.  

Recalculations of the solutions were made in some special cases only when the
numeric results in the original solutions showed to be obviously not correct. Although the
numbers of significant figures in the results of several solutions do not obey the criteria
generally accepted, they were left without change.      



In this publication SI quantities and units are used and a more modern method of
chemical calculations is introduced. Only some exceptions have been made when, in an
effort to preserve the original text, the quantities and units have been used that are not SI.  
There were some problems with the presentation of the solutions of practical tasks,
because most of the relatively simple calculations were based on the experimental results
of contestants. Moreover, the practical problems are accompanied with answer sheets in
the last years and several additional questions and tasks have appeared in them that were
not a part of the text of the original experimental problems. Naturally, answer sheets could
not be included in this publication and can only be preserved as archive materials.
  
When reading the texts of the IChO problems one must admire and appreciate the
work of those many known and unknown people – teachers, authors, pupils, and
organizers – who contributed so much to development and success of this important
international competition.

I am sure about the usefulness of the this review of the IChO problems. It may serve
not only as archive material but, in particular, this review should serve to both competitors
and their teachers as a source of further inspiration in their preparation for this challenging
competition.

Three organic compounds are most widely used as pain relieving drugs: acetylsalicylic acid(ortho-acetoxybenzoic acid), phenacetine(para-ethoxyacetanilide), and paracetamol(para-hydroxyacetanilide). The1996 examination introduction calls paracetamol “now the most popular”, the base of many patented pharmaceuticals(panadol,solpadeine,coldrex,calpol,efferalgan,etc.), and “the safest and highly efficient drug”. These are attributed historical source statements.

You receive an unknown drug claiming to contain paracetamol. Prove or disprove this experimentally by preparing authentic para-hydroxyacetanilide and running thin-layer chromatography.

<ProblemSection id="native-section-1">

### Reagents


- Para-aminophenol,3.10g in a weighing beaker.
- Acetic anhydride,$4.00\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ in an Erlenmeyer flask.
- Ethanol.
- Eluent heptane:ethyl acetate:ethanol=$41:47:12$ by volume.
- Unknown drug in a test tube,1% solution in ethanol.
- Water: use tap water for all purposes.



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="native-section-2">

### Preparation of para-hydroxyacetanilide


A $50\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ round-bottom flask is equipped with reflux condenser and installed on a laboratory stand over a hot plate. Leave1–1.5cm between plate top and flask bottom. Use two clamps to support flask and condenser. Remove the hot plate. Do not switch it on until reagents are added and apparatus reassembled.

Temporarily remove the condenser. Add3.10g para-aminophenol through a funnel, pushing it with a glass rod if necessary. Add $10\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ water through the same funnel. Remount the condenser and carefully pour $4.00\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ acetic anhydride through it. **Attention:** its smell is strongly irritating. In case of spill wash hands immediately with water and ask the supervisor to help dispose of the spilled compound.

Carefully stir by slightly relieving clamps and waving the flask2–3times. Be careful: reaction heat makes mixture and flask very hot. Replace and switch on the hot plate. Heat15min from switching it on. Switch off and remove heater. Cool by immersing in a cold-tap-water bath; the source permits doing this immediately because the flask is highly durable glass. After about5min detach the condenser and pour into an empty $100\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ Pyrex beaker. Put the beaker in a metal dish containing ice and water. Carefully rub its walls with a spatula and observe crude product crystallize as small white crystals.

Assemble suction filtration: fit a frit filter onto a rubber ring and heavy-wall flask. Connect to a suction vacuum pump and turn on pump water at the tap. **Attention:** never turn off the water while the device is under vacuum. First disconnect the flask by carefully pulling rubber tubing off the inlet.

Quantitatively transfer crystals to the filter with a spatula. Wash remaining solid with the smallest possible portions of ice-cold water: the compound is appreciably water-soluble, so dissolution losses must not outweigh incomplete-transfer losses. Carefully wash the product with2–3portions of $2\text{–}3\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ cold water by a) opening the suction flask to atmosphere,b) adding water and carefully mixing precipitate with a spatula,c) reapplying vacuum,d) pressing precipitate with a flat glass-rod tip to remove as much water as possible.

Use several crystals for chromatography below. Move all other precipitate to filtering paper in a Petri dish, spread it thinly and dry on a shelf to avoid accidental spill. For rapid drying spread as thinly as possible, break large pieces, and stir/re-spread every3–5min to expose wet crystals. Repeated procedure trials established no more than5% water after30min; this wetness is considered insignificant for evaluating preparation.



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="native-section-3">

### Chromatography test


If preparation failed, obtain a chromatography sample from the supervisor. Dissolve several still-wet crystals in $1\text{–}2\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ ethanol. The unknown is supplied as1% ethanol solution. Use both for TLC.

Prepare a silica-covered chromatography plate. With a sharp pencil draw a start line and spot marks. Use a capillary to place a small spot of each solution. Dry spots1–2min. Immerse the plate in a beaker of eluent and allow elution. Use forceps to move it in and out. After elution remove it, mark the solvent front, and dry under the hood5min. Examine under UV in a special cabinet. Outline any dark spots with a sharp pencil.



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="native-section-4">

### Weighting of sample


After chromatography the product is usually nearly dry and ready for weighting. Ask the supervisor to weigh an empty weighing beaker. Put the dry product into the weighed beaker and give it to the supervisor to weigh. The supervisor writes weights on your answer sheet. Calculate product weight.

Do the following and fill in the answer sheet form:



**2.1**  
Draw structures of the three drugs above.



**2.2**  
Draw the para-hydroxyacetanilide preparation equation. Calculate stoichiometric reagent amounts. How much acetic anhydride exceeds stoichiometry? Its density is $1.08\ \mathrm{g\,cm^{-3}}$.



**2.3**  
Calculate your product yield.



**2.4**  
Calculate and compare $R_f$ of unknown drug and product.



**2.5**  
Is paracetamol likely present in the drug?



</ProblemSection>
