## Условие

The chemical oxygen demand (COD) refers to the amount of oxidizable substance, such as organic compounds, in a sample solution, and it is used as an indication of water quality in seas, lakes, and marshes. For example, the COD of service water is kept below 1 mg dm⁻³. The COD (mg dm⁻³) is represented by mass of O₂ (mg) which accepts the same amount of electrons which would be accepted by the strong oxidizing agent when 1 dm³ of a sample solution is treated with it. An example of the operation is presented below.

**Analytical Operation**

A sample solution with a volume of 1.00 dm³ was acidified with a sufficient amount of sulphuric acid, and chloride ions were removed by the addition of silver nitrate solution. A volume of $1.00\cdot10^{-1}\,\mathrm{dm^3}$ of potassium permanganate solution ($c=5.00\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}$) was added to the sample solution, and the mixture was heated for 30 min. Further, a volume of $1.00\cdot10^{-1}\,\mathrm{dm^3}$ of disodium oxalate (Na₂C₂O₄ or NaOOC–COONa) standard solution ($c=1.25\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}$) was added, and the mixture was stirred well. Oxalate ions that remained unreacted were titrated with potassium permanganate solution ($c=5.00\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}$). A volume of $3.00\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm{dm^3}$ of the solution was used for the titration.

**3.1** Give the equation of the redox reaction of potassium permanganate and disodium oxalate.

**3.2** Calculate the mass of O₂ (in mg) that will oxidize the same number of moles of oxidizable substance as $1.00\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{dm^3}$ of potassium permanganate solution with a concentration of $5.00\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}$ does.

**3.3** From the following choices, select the most appropriate reason for the removal of chloride ions:

- [A] Some of the chloride ions react with potassium permanganate, resulting in an error in COD.
- [B] Some of the chloride ions react with disodium oxalate, resulting in an error in COD.
- [C] Some of the chloride ions react with organic compounds in the sample solution, resulting in an error in COD.
- [D] A colour is developed during titration, resulting in an error in COD.

**3.4** Calculate the COD (mg dm⁻³) of the sample solution described in the analytical operation above.

## Решение

<Spoiler title="Покажи решението от архива">

**3.1**

2 KMnO₄ + 5 Na₂C₂O₄ + 8 H₂SO₄ → 2 MnSO₄ + 5 Na₂SO₄ + K₂SO₄ + 10 CO₂ + 8 H₂O,

or 2 KMnO₄ + 5 H₂C₂O₄ + 3 H₂SO₄ → 2 MnSO₄ + K₂SO₄ + 10 CO₂ + 8 H₂O,

or 2 MnO₄⁻ + 5 C₂O₄²⁻ + 16 H⁺ → 2 Mn²⁺ + 10 CO₂ + 8 H₂O.

**3.2** The reactions of potassium permanganate and O₂ are as follows:

MnO₄⁻ + 8 H⁺ + 5 e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4 H₂O;

O₂ + 4 H⁺ + 4 e⁻ → 2 H₂O.

$$
n(\mathrm{MnO_4^-})=1.00\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{dm^3}\times5.00\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}=5.00\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{mol},
$$

$$
n(\mathrm{O_2})=5.00\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{mol}\times5/4=6.25\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{mol},
$$

$$
m(\mathrm{O_2})=6.25\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{mol}\times32\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}=2.00\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm g=2.00\cdot10^{-1}\,\mathrm{mg}.
$$

**3.3** The correct answer is [A].

**3.4** The amounts of electrons used for reduction or oxidation are equal. Then:

$$
5\times5.00\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}\times(1.00\cdot10^{-1}+A)\,\mathrm{dm^3}=2\times1.25\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}\times1.00\cdot10^{-1}\,\mathrm{dm^3}+X.\qquad (1)
$$

Here A (cm³, as printed) is an amount of potassium permanganate used for the final titration, and X (mol) is the amount of electrons for the oxidizable substance. Equation (1) gives $X=2.50\cdot10^{-2}A$. When $A=3.00\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm{dm^3}$, $X=7.50\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol}$. Hence,

$$
\mathrm{COD}=(32/4)\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\times7.50\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol}\times10^3\,\mathrm{mg/g}\times1/1\,\mathrm{dm^{-3}}=6.00\,\mathrm{mg\,dm^{-3}}.
$$

**Alternative:** The amount of potassium permanganate consumed by the oxidizable substance, B (cm³, as printed), is

$$
5\times5.00\cdot10^{-3}\times(1.00\cdot10^{-1}+A-B)=2\times1.25\cdot10^{-2}\times1.00\cdot10^{-1}.
$$

At $A=3.00\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm{dm^3}$, B equals $3.00\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm{dm^3}$. From the solution to question 3.2 above:

$$
\mathrm{COD}=\frac{2.00\cdot10^{-1}}{1.00\cdot10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{(mg\,dm^{-3})}\times3.00\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm{(dm^3)}\times1/1\,\mathrm{(dm^{-3})}=6.00\,\mathrm{mg\,dm^{-3}}.
$$

Source note: The key labels A and B cm³ but substitutes dm³. Both complete alternatives and their printed unit labels are retained.
