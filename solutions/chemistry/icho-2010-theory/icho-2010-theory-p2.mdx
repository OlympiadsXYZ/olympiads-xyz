## Условие

**Crystal structure of alkali metal halide**

In crystals of ionic compounds, cations are generally arranged in the interstices of the closest packed lattice of anions. The structure of an ionic crystal such as sodium chloride becomes stable when the cations are in contact with the nearest anions.

**2.1** In the crystal of sodium chloride, both Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions form a face-centred cubic lattice. Give the numbers of Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions in a unit cell and the coordination numbers of Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions in sodium chloride crystal.

**2.2** The ionic radii of Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions in the crystal of sodium chloride are 0.102 nm and 0.181 nm, respectively. Calculate the density [kg m⁻³] of the sodium chloride crystal.

**Born-Haber cycle and lattice enthalpy**

In ionic inorganic compounds such as sodium chloride, the heat of lattice formation from gaseous ions is very high, and the contribution of the change in entropy is small. Therefore, the lattice formation enthalpy is estimated from enthalpy data by using a Born-Haber cycle.

**2.3** The figure below shows the Born-Haber cycle of NaCl. The labels “g” and “s” represent “gas” and “solid” states, respectively. Show chemical equations in the A and F steps.

**2.4** Calculate the enthalpy of the lattice formation of NaCl [kJ mol⁻¹] by using the following enthalpy data of the respective steps in the above Born-Haber cycle.

| Step | Enthalpy / kJ mol⁻¹ |
|---|---|
| Formation of NaCl(s) | −411 |
| Sublimation of Na(s) | 109 |
| Ionization of Na(g) | 496 |
| Dissociation of Cl₂(g) | 242 |
| Electron gain by Cl(g) | −349 |

**Synthesis of sodium carbonate by the ammonia-soda process (Solvay process)**

Sodium carbonate (anhydrous soda ash) is a raw material in the manufacture of glass, medicaments, alkaline detergents, etc.

**2.5** The total chemical reaction in the ammonia-soda process is represented as follows:

$$
2\,\mathrm{NaCl}+\mathrm{CaCO_3}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Na_2CO_3}+\mathrm{CaCl_2}.
$$

This reaction between sodium chloride and calcium carbonate does not proceed directly. The process comprises the following five reactions involving ammonia:

$$
\mathrm{CaCO_3}\xrightarrow{\Delta}[A]+[B],
$$

$$
\mathrm{NaCl}+\mathrm{NH_3}+[B]+\mathrm{H_2O}\longrightarrow[C]+[D],
$$

$$
2[C]\xrightarrow{\Delta}\mathrm{Na_2CO_3}+\mathrm{H_2O}+[B],
$$

$$
[A]+\mathrm{H_2O}\longrightarrow[E],
$$

$$
[E]+2[D]\longrightarrow\mathrm{CaCl_2}+2\mathrm{H_2O}+2\mathrm{NH_3}.
$$

Here Δ represents applying heat treatment. Insert the chemical formulas of the appropriate compounds instead of [A]–[E] in the above reactions.

## Решение

**2.1**

| Quantity | Na⁺ | Cl⁻ |
|---|---|---|
| Number of ions |4|4|
| Coordination number of ions |6|6|

**2.2** Length of lattice: l = 0.102 × 2 + 0.181 × 2 = 0.566 nm. Density:

$$
\rho=\frac{(22.99+35.45)\times4}{(0.566\cdot10^{-9})^3\times6.022\cdot10^{23}}=2.1408\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{g\,m^{-3}}=2.14\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}.
$$

**2.3** A: Na(s) + ½ Cl₂(g) → NaCl(s). F, as printed: NaCl(s) → Na(g) + Cl⁻(g).

Source note: Native key65 omits the positive charge on Na in F. The printed equation is retained.

**2.4** Enthalpy conservation condition: −A + B + C + D/2 = F − E. From the above equation: −(−411) + 109 + 496 + (242/2) = F + 349. Thus F = 788. Lattice formation enthalpy of NaCl is −F, thus −788 kJ mol⁻¹.

**2.5** A: CaO; B: CO₂; C: NaHCO₃; D: NH₄Cl; E: Ca(OH)₂.
