due to the procedure of the creation of the IChO competition problems, it is impossible to
assign any author's name to a particular problem. As the editor I would appreciate many  
times some discussion with the authors about any critical places that occurred in the text.  
On the other hand, any additional amendments to the text would be not correct from the  
historical point of view. Therefore, responsibility for the scientific content and language of
the problems lies exclusively with the organizers of the particular International Chemistry  
Olympiads.    

Some parts of texts, especially those gained as scanned materials, could not be
used directly and thus, several texts, schemes and pictures had to be re-written or created
again. Some solutions were often available in a brief form and necessary extent only, just
for the needs of members of the International Jury.  

Recalculations of the solutions were made in some special cases only when the
numeric results in the original solutions showed to be obviously not correct. Although the
numbers of significant figures in the results of several solutions do not obey the criteria

Determination of water by the classical Fischer method involves titration of a sample solution(or suspension) in methanol by a methanolic iodine solution, containing also an excess of SO2 and pyridine(C5H5N, Py)—Fischer reagent. The following reactions occur during the titration:

SO2+CH3OH+H2O+I2→2HI+CH3OSO3H;

Py+HI→PyH+I−;

Py+CH3OSO3H→PyH+CH3OSO3−.

Iodine content is usually expressed in mg of water reacting with1 cm3 of the titrant solution(hereunder T, mg cm−3), which equals the mass of water(mg) reacting with1.00 cm3 of the iodine solution. T is determined experimentally by titration of a sample with a known water content. The sample may be, for example, a hydrated compound or a standard solution of water in methanol. In the latter case it should be taken into account that methanol itself can contain certain amount of water.

In all calculations please use the atomic masses accurate to2 decimal points.

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-1">

### 4.1

Equation: I2+SO2+2H2O+4Py→2PyHI+(PyH)2SO4.



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-2">

### 4.2

M(Na2C4H4O6·2H2O)=230.05 g mol−1;2M(H2O)=36.04 g mol−1.

$$m(\mathrm{H_2O})=\frac{1.3520\times36.04}{230.05}=0.2118\;\mathrm{g}=211.8\;\mathrm{mg}.$$

$$T=\frac{211.8}{12.20}=17.36\;\mathrm{mg\,cm^{-3}}.$$



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-3">

### 4.3

Volume of iodine consumed for10 cm3 of pure CH3OH=2.20×10.00/25.00=0.88 cm3.

$$T=\frac{21.537\times0.01\times10^3}{22.70-0.88}=9.87\;\mathrm{mg\,cm^{-3}}.$$

More exactly,10.00 cm3 of the solution contains(1000−21.5)×10.00/1000=9.785 cm3 of methanol. Volume of iodine consumed for9.785 cm3 of pure CH3OH=2.20×9.785/25.00=0.86 cm3.

$$T=\frac{21.537\times0.01\times10^3}{22.70-0.86}=9.86\;\mathrm{mg\,cm^{-3}}.$$

The source then repeats T=9.87 mg cm−3.



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-4">

### 4.4

Approach1. Let1 cm3 of CH3OH contain x mg H2O; then1 cm3 of A contains[(1.000−0.006)x+5.624] mg H2O.

15.00T=22.45(0.994x+5.624)—1st titration;

10.00T=25.00x+10.79(0.994x+5.624)—2nd titration.

Hence,x=1.13 mg cm−3,T=10.09 mg cm−3(10.10 without taking into account0.994 factor).

Approach2. Let y cm3 of B be consumed for titration of water contained in1 cm3 of CH3OH.

$$T=\frac{22.45\times5.624}{15.00-22.45\times0.994y}\quad(\text{1st titration})=\frac{10.79\times5.624}{10.00-25.00y-10.79y}\quad(\text{2nd titration}).$$

Hence,y=0.1116 and T=10.10 mg cm−3. T=10.09 mg cm−3(10.10 without taking into account0.994 factor).



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-5">

### 4.5

CaO+SO2→CaSO3;

2CaO+2I2→CaI2+Ca(OI)2;

6CaO+6I2→5CaI2+Ca(IO3)2.

(Instead of CaO,Ca(OH)2 may be written.)



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-6">

### 4.6

Fe2(SO4)3+2HI→2FeSO4+I2+H2SO4;

Fe2(SO4)3+H2O+SO2+CH3OH→2FeSO4+CH3OHSO3+H2SO4.

(Or in ionic form.)



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-7">

### 4.7

Fe2(SO4)3·xH2O+(x−1)I2+xSO2+xCH3OH→2FeSO4+xCH3OHSO3+H2SO4+2(x−1)HI.



</ProblemSection>

<ProblemSection id="solution-native-key-8">

### 4.8

The composition of the crystallohydrate is:

M(Fe2(SO4)3·xH2O)=399.9+18.02x;

$$m(\mathrm{H_2O})=\frac{0.6387\times18.02x}{399.9+18.02x}.$$

$$m(\mathrm{H_2O})=10.59\;\mathrm{cm^3}\times15.46\;\mathrm{mg\,cm^{-3}}\times0.001\;\mathrm{g\,mg^{-1}}\times\frac{x}{x-1}.$$

0.1637×(399.9+18.02x)=11.51x−11.51;

x=8.994≈9. Formula: Fe2(SO4)3·9H2O.



</ProblemSection>
